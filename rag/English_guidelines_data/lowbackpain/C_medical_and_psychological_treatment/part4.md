# Secondary Prevention of Work-Related Disability in Nonspecific Low Back Pain

## Effectiveness of Interventions for Low Back Pain (LBP)

### Fear-Avoidance Behaviors and Physical Therapy

In patients undergoing treatment for low back pain, interventions targeting fear-avoidance behaviors are recommended over physical therapy alone to improve outcomes in the first six months. George et al. conducted a randomized controlled trial comparing standard physical therapy and fear-avoidance-based physical therapy for patients with acute LBP. The study demonstrated that the fear-avoidance therapy group showed significant improvements in disability and pain intensity, as well as reduced fear-avoidance beliefs compared to the standard therapy group. This study provides Level II evidence that fear-avoidance-based physical therapy is more effective for acute LBP in patients with elevated fear-avoidance beliefs at baseline.

### Graded Exercise vs. Graded Exposure for Low Back Pain

Later, George et al. conducted another randomized controlled trial comparing physical therapy combined with graded exercise or graded exposure in patients with acute and subacute LBP. The results showed that adding graded exercise or exposure to physical therapy did not significantly improve outcomes. Both groups exhibited improvements in disability, fear-avoidance beliefs, and pain intensity, with no significant differences between the groups. This study provides Level I evidence that graded activity or exposure does not improve outcomes in the development of chronic LBP.

### Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT) in Multidisciplinary Programs

Monticone et al. evaluated a CBT-based multidisciplinary program for nonspecific chronic LBP. Patients who received both exercise and CBT experienced significant improvements in disability, fear-avoidance beliefs, pain, and quality of life compared to the exercise-only group. This study provides Level I evidence that CBT combined with exercise is superior to exercise alone in managing chronic LBP.

### Graded In Vivo Exposure for Fear-Avoidance Behavior

Woods et al. conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the effectiveness of a graded in vivo exposure (GivE) therapy program for chronic LBP. The results showed significant improvements in psychological measures, including fear-avoidance beliefs and pain catastrophizing, in the GivE group compared to the graded activity and wait-list control groups. This study provides Level I evidence that graded in vivo exposure is effective in improving psychological outcomes in patients with chronic LBP.

## Future Directions for Research

The work group recommends longer-term follow-up studies (one year or more) to determine the longitudinal benefits of treatments targeting fear-avoidance behaviors. Additionally, future studies should explore the role of cognitive-behavioral therapy and graded exposure in the management of LBP.

## Psychological Factors Influencing Low Back Pain Outcomes

### Kinesiophobia and Prognosis in Low Back Pain Treatment

Foster et al. conducted a prospective cohort study to evaluate psychological factors that influence LBP treatment outcomes. Their study found that kinesiophobia (fear of movement) was a significant negative predictor of recovery from LBP. The study provides Level III evidence that psychological factors such as pain self-efficacy, illness identity, and personal control are predictive of poor clinical outcomes in LBP patients.

### Psychological Factors in Chronic LBP Recovery

Helmhout et al. studied the relationship between chronic LBP recovery and baseline psychological factors. Their study indicated that patients with high levels of fear-avoidance behavior and disability were at risk for poor long-term recovery from LBP. This study provides Level II evidence that kinesiophobia can negatively impact LBP recovery.

### Psychosocial Predictors of Quality of Life in Chronic LBP

Keeley et al. assessed the relationship between psychosocial factors and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in chronic LBP patients. The study found that anxiety, depression, and fear-avoidance beliefs were significant predictors of poor physical HRQoL and increased health service utilization. This study provides Level III evidence that psychological factors are critical in determining the health outcomes of chronic LBP patients.
