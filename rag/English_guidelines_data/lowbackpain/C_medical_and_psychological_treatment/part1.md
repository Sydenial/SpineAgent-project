## Med/Psych Question 1: Is smoking cessation effective in decreasing the frequency of low back pain episodes?

A systematic review of the literature yielded no studies to adequately address this question.

## Future Directions for Research

The work group recommends further population-based observational studies that investigate the association between low back pain (LBP) and smoking. Specifically, a study comparing the frequency of LBP episodes between patients who have quit smoking and those who continue to smoke would be useful.

## Med/Psych Question 2: In patients with low back pain, is pharmacological treatment effective in decreasing the duration of pain, decreasing intensity of pain, increasing functional outcomes of treatment, and improving the return-to-work rate?

### Treatment Comparisons:
- **a. No treatment**
- **b. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and/or psychosocial intervention alone**
- **c. Patient education alone**

Recommendations were developed based on specific definitions, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and the resulting literature, which excluded conditions such as the presence of neurological deficit or leg pain experienced below the knee, among others. Given the exclusion criteria, these guideline recommendations address subtypes of low back pain care rather than low back pain in its entirety. ### Study on Topiramate for Chronic Low Back Pain

Muehlbacher et al. studied the efficacy of topiramate for the treatment of chronic LBP in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Participants with chronic LBP were randomized into the topiramate group (n=48) or placebo group (n=48). The participants in the topiramate group had significantly greater improvements in pain scores compared to the placebo group. Adverse events such as severe somnolence, vision problems, and dizziness were reported, but no serious side effects occurred. The authors concluded that topiramate is safe and effective in treating chronic LBP.

### Antidepressants for Low Back Pain

#### Grade of Recommendation: A  
Atkinson et al. conducted a randomized controlled trial to compare the efficacy of noradrenergic and serotonergic antidepressants for treating chronic LBP in patients without depression. The trial found that noradrenergic agents such as maprotiline were more effective than selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors. However, the methodology was critiqued for rapid dose escalation and less than 80% follow-up. This study provides Level II evidence that noradrenergic agents are more effective than SSRIs for chronic LBP.

#### Paroxetine in Chronic LBP

Dickens et al. conducted a randomized controlled trial to determine if paroxetine is more effective at reducing depression and pain than placebo in patients with LBP. The study found no significant differences between the paroxetine and placebo groups in reducing pain or depression. This study provides Level I evidence that paroxetine is no better than placebo for chronic LBP.

### Non-Selective NSAIDs for the Treatment of Low Back Pain

#### Grade of Recommendation: B
The evidence is insufficient to make a recommendation for or against the use of selective NSAIDs for the treatment of low back pain.

### Etoricoxib for Low Back Pain

Birbara et al. conducted a randomized, placebo-controlled trial comparing 60 mg and 90 mg doses of etoricoxib to a placebo. Both etoricoxib doses significantly improved back pain intensity after 4 and 12 weeks. The study concluded that etoricoxib provided significant improvement in chronic LBP-related symptoms and disability compared to placebo.

### Opioids for Low Back Pain

It is suggested that the use of opioid pain medications should be cautiously limited and restricted to short duration for the treatment of low back pain.

Buynak et al. conducted a randomized controlled trial to compare the efficacy and safety of tapentadol ER and oxycodone CR for chronic LBP. Both medications significantly reduced pain intensity compared to placebo, with tapentadol having fewer gastrointestinal side effects.

Cloutier et al. studied controlled-release oxycodone/CR naloxone for chronic LBP and found it to be effective for treating moderate to severe LBP, though adverse events were common.

### Hydromorphone ER for Chronic LBP

Hale et al. conducted a randomized controlled trial comparing hydromorphone ER to placebo for opioid-tolerant patients with chronic LBP. The hydromorphone ER group had significantly reduced pain intensity compared to placebo, but the study's high dropout rate and lack of validated outcome measures limited its findings.

## Future Directions for Research

The work group recommends development of studies that identify specific groups of patients who respond to a particular opioid medication with minimal dependence risk.
