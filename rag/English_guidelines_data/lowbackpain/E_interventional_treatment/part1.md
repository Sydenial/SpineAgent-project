## Interventional Question 1

### In patients with low back pain, do fluoroscopically-guided epidural steroid injections decrease the duration of pain, decrease the intensity of pain, increase the functional outcomes of treatment, and improve the return-to-work rate?

There is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against the use of caudal epidural steroid injections in patients with low back pain.  
**Grade of Recommendation**: I  

Southern et al. (2003) investigated the efficacy of fluoroscopic caudal epidural steroid injections for chronic lumbar discogenic pain. Patients with chronic low back pain (LBP) and evidence of disc pathology without stenosis received at least one fluoroscopically-guided caudal epidural injection with 12 mg of betamethasone and 8 cc of 0.5% lidocaine. After follow-up, 19 patients were classified as successes and 65 as failures. Patient satisfaction was 45%. The study concluded that patient satisfaction exceeded the reported efficacy of caudal epidural steroid injections in chronic lumbar discogenic pain.  
This study provides Level IV evidence that about 20% of patients with chronic discogenic LBP will experience at least a 50% reduction in pain following caudal epidural steroid injections.

Lee et al. (2010) conducted an observational study to determine the effect of fluoroscopic interlaminar epidural steroid injection (ESI) for axial LBP. 81 patients with LBP received an interlaminar ESI and followed up within the first month and again 1-2 years later. The procedure was considered effective if the patient's reported pain score reduced by >50%. 63 out of 81 procedures were deemed effective at short-term follow-up, and 37% reported greater than 6 months' symptom relief. The study concluded that fluoroscopic interlaminar ESI was effective for axial LBP.  
This study provides Level IV evidence that interlaminar epidural steroid injections in patients with LBP can provide at least a 50% reduction in pain in 40% of patients at 6 months or greater.

## Interventional Question 2

### When evaluating fluoroscopically-guided intra-articular lumbar facet joint injections in patients with acute or chronic low back pain:
- **a.** What is the diagnostic utility of this procedure?
- **b.** From a therapeutic standpoint, does this procedure decrease the duration of pain, decrease the intensity of pain, increase the functional outcomes of treatment, and improve the return-to-work rate?

There is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against the use of facet joint injections for the diagnosis and treatment of low back pain.  
**Grade of Recommendation**: I

### Interventional Question 3

In patients with low back pain, do medial branch blocks have a role in defining treatment for low back pain?
- **a.** Does the duration of pain, intensity of pain, functional outcomes, and return-to-work status vary when candidates for neurotomy are determined by single vs. comparative medial branch blocks?
- **b.** Is there a threshold for the magnitude of relief from diagnostic facet nerve blocks that predict outcomes to neurotomy?
- **c.** Does the duration of pain, intensity of pain, functional outcomes, and return-to-work status vary when candidates for neurotomy are determined by diagnostic facet nerve blocks vs. intra-articular facet joint injections?
- **d.** What is the therapeutic utility of medial branch blocks?
- **e.** Does technical accuracy of medial branch blocks (e.g., contrast use) affect its validity and effectiveness for subsequent neurotomy?

**Grade of Recommendation**: I  
There is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against the use of medial branch blocks for facet joint pain diagnosis and treatment.

## Future Directions for Research

1. The workgroup recommends further randomized controlled trials to assess the efficacy of facet joint infiltration and neurotomy for the treatment of chronic low back pain.
2. A comparison of outcomes between dual diagnostic blocks with different durations of action and response rates to various local anesthetics is needed.
