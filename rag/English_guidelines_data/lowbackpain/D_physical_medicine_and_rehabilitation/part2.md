# Patient Characteristics and Spinal Manipulation Therapy (SMT) for Low Back Pain (LBP)

## Are there specific patient or treatment characteristics that predict improved outcomes with SMT following an episode of low back pain?

### Definition: SMT Defined

Spinal Manipulation Therapy (SMT) involves the application of manual thrust techniques to the spine, aiming to improve function and reduce pain.

### Recommendations

There is conflicting evidence regarding the predictive characteristics of symptoms above the knee, low Fear-Avoidance Beliefs Questionnaire (FABQ) scores, the presence of hypomobile segments, and hip internal rotation greater than $35^{\circ}$ for patients with acute low back pain (LBP).

#### Grade of Recommendation: I

### Flynn et al. Study (2002)

Flynn et al. conducted a prospective study to develop a clinical prediction rule (CPR) for identifying patients likely to improve with spinal manipulation. Patients referred to physical therapy for LBP ($n = 71$) underwent an initial examination that included pain assessment (11-point scale), disability (Modified Oswestry Disability Questionnaire), and beliefs about activity (FABQ). 

Spinal manipulation was performed during the first session, and follow-up sessions occurred after 2–4 days. Statistical analyses identified the following clinical predictors of successful treatment:
- Symptoms less than 16 days.
- Symptoms above the knee.
- Low FABQ work subscale score (<19).
- At least one hypomobile lumbar segment.
- Greater than $35^{\circ}$ of internal hip rotation.

This study provides Level II prognostic evidence, showing that these predictors are associated with a $>50\%$ improvement in function within one week for patients receiving SMT and exercise.

### Childs et al. Study (2004)

Childs et al. aimed to validate Flynn et al.'s CPR in a multicenter randomized controlled trial. Patients with LBP were randomly assigned to receive either exercise alone ($n = 61$) or exercise plus spinal manipulation ($n = 70$). 

The CPR required at least four out of five criteria:
- Duration of LBP less than 16 days.
- No symptoms distal to the knee.
- FABQ work subscale score <19.
- At least one hypomobile segment in the lumbar spine.
- At least one hip with internal rotation >$35^{\circ}$.

Participants who met these criteria and received spinal manipulation showed greater improvement in both pain and disability at all follow-up points. This study provides Level II prognostic evidence that the CPR can assist with decision-making for patients with LBP.

### Dougherty et al. Study (2014)

Dougherty et al. evaluated a modified CPR in a randomized controlled trial for chronic LBP. Participants ($n = 92$) were randomized to receive either SMT or active exercise therapy ($n = 89$). A modified CPR included:
- Pain above the knee.
- Hip internal rotation >$35^{\circ}$.
- At least one hypomobile lumbar segment.
- FABQ work subscale score <19.

The study concluded that the modified CPR could not predict which patients would benefit most from SMT. This study provides Level I prognostic evidence, suggesting that these predictors are not strongly associated with pain and functional outcomes for chronic LBP patients receiving SMT.

### Hancock et al. Study (2008)

In this multicenter randomized controlled trial in Sydney, Australia, Hancock et al. investigated the CPR developed by Flynn et al. for patients with LBP presenting in outpatient general practice clinics. The study found that the clinical predictors (symptoms less than 16 days, above the knee, low FABQ, and internal rotation of the hip >$35^{\circ}$) were not associated with improvements in pain or function.

This study provides Level II prognostic evidence that these clinical predictors are not valid for predicting outcomes in a primary care setting.

### Hallegraef et al. Study (2009)

Hallegraef et al. assessed the efficacy of manipulative therapy for acute nonspecific LBP. Patients with LBP meeting three criteria of the CPR (symptom duration <16 days, no pain distal to the knee, and age >35) were randomized into two treatment arms: manipulative therapy plus physical therapy ($n = 31$) or physical therapy alone ($n = 33$). 

The study found that manipulative therapy showed greater improvement in disability compared to physical therapy alone, but no significant differences in pain or mobility. The study provides Level I prognostic evidence that manipulative therapy offers added benefits over physical therapy alone for disability, but the CPR was not predictive of response to treatment.

### Cook et al. Study (2013)

Cook et al. analyzed the predictive value of various prognostic factors in patients with LBP receiving manual therapy. The study found that a positive CPR at baseline (defined as meeting at least four of five criteria) was predictive of success in treatment. 

Factors included:
- No pain below the knee.
- Duration of symptoms less than 16 days.
- FABQ-W score <19.
- One or more hypomobile lumbar segments.
- At least one hip with internal rotation >$35^{\circ}$.

The study provides Level II prognostic evidence that the CPR can predict success in treatment with SMT.

### Cecchi et al. Study (2012)

In a retrospective analysis of data from a randomized controlled trial, Cecchi et al. identified predictors of response to various interventions for chronic LBP. Patients were randomly assigned to receive back school ($n = 68$), individual physiotherapy ($n = 68$), or spinal manipulation ($n = 69$). The study concluded that there were no specific predictive factors for success with spinal manipulation.

### Cook et al. Study (2012)

Cook et al. analyzed data from a multicenter randomized clinical trial to determine if changes in pain during manual therapy sessions were associated with outcomes in patients treated for LBP. Participants who reported a $>2$ point reduction in pain on the Numerical Pain Rating Scale (NPRS) after two sessions were more likely to experience functional recovery.

### Donaldson et al. Study (2013)

Donaldson et al. studied outcomes in patients with LBP who preferred thrust or nonthrust manual therapy. The study found no significant differences in pain or disability between patient preference groups, concluding that patient preference did not influence outcomes for LBP treatment.

### Fritz et al. Study (2005)

Fritz et al. investigated the predictive value of lumbar spine segmental mobility assessments for determining treatment strategies. Patients with hypomobility had better outcomes with spinal manipulation, while those with hypermobility benefited more from stabilization exercises. The study offers Level I prognostic evidence for hypomobile patients benefiting from SMT.

### Haas et al. Study (2004)

Haas et al. conducted a trial to evaluate the effect of treatment visits frequency (1, 2, 3, or 4 visits per week) on pain and disability outcomes. The study found that patients who received 3–4 visits per week showed substantial pain and disability relief.

### Hoehler et al. Study (1983)

Hoehler et al. examined the predictive value of the Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI) for response to spinal manipulation in patients with acute or chronic LBP. Psychological factors like hypochondriasis and hysteria were associated with poor outcomes, providing Level III prognostic evidence that psychosomatic factors may influence the recurrence of LBP.

### Niemisto et al. Study (2004)

Niemisto et al. studied the predictive value of sociodemographic factors in chronic LBP patients receiving SMT or physician consultation. Severe affective distress was identified as a risk factor for poor response to SMT.

### Underwood et al. Study (2007)

The UK BEAM Trial analyzed predictors for response to SMT in subacute LBP patients, finding no significant baseline characteristics that predicted treatment outcomes.

## Future Directions for Research

1. Larger randomized controlled trials focusing on subgroups could identify specific populations that are responders to SMT.
2. More studies are needed to evaluate clinical predictors in chronic LBP, including thrust versus nonthrust techniques, mobility versus stability, socioeconomic and psychological factors, and the optimal number of treatment visits.
