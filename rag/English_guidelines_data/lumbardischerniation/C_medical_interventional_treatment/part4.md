# What is the role of epidural steroid injections (ESI) for the treatment of lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy?

Transforaminal epidural steroid injection is recommended to provide short-term (2-4 weeks) pain relief in a proportion of patients with lumbar disc herniations with radiculopathy.

## Grade of Recommendation: A

Ghahreman et al. reported results from a prospective randomized controlled trial assessing the efficacy of transforaminal injection of steroid and local anesthetic, local anesthetic alone, normal saline alone, intramuscular injection of steroid or normal saline on radicular pain secondary to lumbar disc herniation. Of the 150 consecutively assigned patients, 28 received transforaminal steroid and local anesthetic, 27 had transforaminal local anesthetic, 27 received transforaminal normal saline, 30 had intramuscular steroid, and 28 received intramuscular normal saline. Outcomes were assessed at one month and 12 months using Numeric Rating Scale, Roland Morris, SF-36, and several other criteria. Of the transforaminal epidural steroid group, 54% experienced greater than 50% radicular pain relief at one month after treatment. This outcome was statistically significant compared to the transforaminal normal saline, transforaminal local anesthetic, intramuscular normal saline, and intramuscular steroid groups. The transforaminal steroid group also showed improvements in function and disability. The authors concluded that transforaminal epidural steroid injection is a viable alternative to surgery for lumbar radicular pain due to disc herniation.

Karppinen et al. (2001) performed a randomized controlled trial to test the efficacy of periradicular corticosteroid injection for sciatica. Outcomes were assessed at two and 12 months. For contained disc herniations, leg pain at four weeks and emotional scores at three months were significantly better for the transforaminal epidural steroid injections compared to transforaminal normal saline. The study provided evidence that transforaminal epidural steroid injection is effective for short-term (four weeks) pain relief in patients with symptomatic lumbar disc herniations.

## Interlaminar epidural steroid injections may be considered in the treatment of patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy

## Grade of Recommendation: C

Manchikanti et al. described a prospective randomized controlled trial comparing interlaminar epidural corticosteroid injection to interlaminar epidural local anesthetic injection. The study concluded that both groups showed significant improvement in pain and disability scores at three, six, and 12 months, with the steroid group having a greater improvement at six months. The authors concluded that interlaminar epidural steroid injection provides better relief of pain and disability compared to local anesthetic in the treatment of patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy.

## Future Directions for Research

The work group suggests randomized, controlled trials comparing the use of individual interventional spine procedures to a control, preferably blinded, in patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy. Further studies should also compare automated percutaneous discectomy and percutaneous endoscopic discectomy with open surgical techniques.

## Recommendation #1

A large double-blinded, randomized controlled clinical trial with at least one-year follow-up in patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy treated by fluoroscopically-guided interlaminar or caudal epidural steroid injections, with a control group receiving saline placebo injections. Subgroup analyses should be provided for responders and nonresponders.

## Recommendation #2

A large double-blinded, randomized controlled clinical trial with at least one-year follow-up comparing patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy treated by fluoroscopically guided transforaminal, interlaminar, and caudal epidural steroid injections.

# What is the role of interventional spine procedures such as intradiscal electrothermal annuloplasty (IDET) and percutaneous discectomy (chemical or mechanical) in the treatment of lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy?

## Grade of Recommendation: I (Insufficient Evidence)

There is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against the use of intradiscal ozone in the treatment of patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy.

## Endoscopic percutaneous discectomy may be considered for the treatment of lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy

## Grade of Recommendation: C

Ahn et al. described a retrospective case series assessing the clinical outcome of percutaneous endoscopic lumbar discectomy for upper lumbar disc herniation. The study showed that percutaneous endoscopic discectomy can be effective for upper lumbar disc herniations, with a combined excellent or good outcome rate of 77.8% at follow-up.

## Endoscopic percutaneous discectomy is suggested for carefully selected patients to reduce early postoperative disability and reduce opioid use compared with open discectomy in the treatment of patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy

## Grade of Recommendation: B

Hermantin et al. conducted a study comparing the results of endoscopic percutaneous lumbar discectomy with open discectomy in patients with lumbar disc herniation and radiculopathy. The study found that patients treated with endoscopic discectomy had significantly less postoperative disability and required less opioid use compared to those treated with open discectomy.

## Automated percutaneous discectomy may be considered for the treatment of lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy

## Grade of Recommendation: C

Alo et al. reported results from a prospective case series examining outcomes of patients treated with automated percutaneous lumbar discectomy. The study showed a significant reduction in preoperative pain scores, with 74% of patients reporting a reduction in analgesic intake and 90% reporting improvement in post-decompression functional status.

## There is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against the use of automated percutaneous discectomy compared with open discectomy in the treatment of patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy

## Grade of Recommendation: I (Insufficient Evidence)

Haines et al. conducted a prospective randomized controlled trial to estimate the success rates of automated or endoscopic percutaneous discectomy compared with conventional discectomy. The study found no statistical difference between the two groups for primary and secondary outcomes.

## There is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against the use of plasma disc decompression/nucleoplasty in the treatment of patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy

## Grade of Recommendation: I (Insufficient Evidence)

Cohen et al. reported a case series evaluating the efficacy of nucleoplasty in patients with lumbar radicular pain secondary to a herniated disc. The study concluded that nucleoplasty was not an effective long-term treatment for lumbar radiculopathy.

## There is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against the use of plasma disc decompression compared with transforaminal epidural steroid injections in patients with lumbar disc herniation who have previously failed transforaminal epidural steroid injection therapy

## Grade of Recommendation: I (Insufficient Evidence)

Gerszten et al. conducted a trial comparing plasma disc decompression and transforaminal epidural steroid injections in patients with radicular pain due to a lumbar disc herniation. The study found that plasma disc decompression provided greater improvements in pain and quality of life scores compared to repeated transforaminal epidural steroid injections.

## There is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against the use of intradiscal high-pressure saline injection in the treatment of patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy

## Grade of Recommendation: I (Insufficient Evidence)

Kanai et al. evaluated the efficacy of intradiscal high-pressure saline injection in patients with lumbar radiculopathy due to lumbar disc herniation. The study found good outcomes in patients with extruded or sequestered discs, but less effectiveness in patients with protruded discs.

## There is insufficient evidence to make a recommendation for or against the use of percutaneous electrothermal disc decompression in the treatment of patients with lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy

## Grade of Recommendation: I (Insufficient Evidence)

Schaufle et al. described a study on the effect of percutaneous electrothermal disc decompression for symptomatic lumbar disc herniation. The study showed moderate improvements in pain and function, but the effect size was small.
