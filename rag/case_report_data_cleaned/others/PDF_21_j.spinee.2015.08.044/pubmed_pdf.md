# Posterior epidural cyst associated with Baastrup disease

## Case Report

A 57-year-old man presented with a several-year history of midline lower back pain radiating down both legs. The pain worsened with extension. On physical examination, he had an antalgic gait of the left lower extremity from the hip to the mid leg, and brisk bilateral deep tendon reflexes. There was lateral lumbar paraspinal tenderness to palpation.

Lateral radiographs (Fig. 1) demonstrated apposition of the dorsal spinous processes, which is characteristic of Baastrup disease. Magnetic resonance imaging (Fig. 2) demonstrated bursal fluid between the L4 and L5 spinous processes, as well as a posterior epidural cyst resulting in significant canal stenosis.

The patient underwent an L4–L5 laminectomy and excision of the epidural cyst. Intraoperatively, a cyst was encountered between the L4 and L5 interspace extending under the lamina of L4, with severe compression of the thecal sac. Postoperatively, the patient experienced resolution of his back and leg pain, and his left lower extremity strength improved to 5/5.
