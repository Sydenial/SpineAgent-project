# A Rare Case of Noncontiguous Multiple Spinal Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis Involving Atlantoaxial Instability

## Abstract
A 14-year-old girl was referred to our department who presented with occipitocervical pain for 1 month, without numbness or weakness of four limbs. Physical examination of the patient showed tenderness of occipitocervical region, no hypesthesia region, or weak myodynamia of four limbs was detected. The results of laboratory examinations showed that erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) was 56mm/h, C-reactive protein (CRP) was 38.5 mg/L, carbohydrate antigen (CA)72–4 12.7 U/mL, bone alkaline phosphatase (B-ALP) 58.4 ug/L. Whole spine sagittal magnetic resonance imaging (T2) showed C2, T1, T5, T12, and L4 vertebral body destruction without compression of spinal cord.

## Introduction
Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) is a rare disorder characterized by the proliferation of Langerhans cells. This case discusses a patient presenting with multiple vertebral involvement and atlantoaxial instability.

## Methods
Physical examination and laboratory tests were conducted to assess the patient's condition. Imaging techniques, including whole spine sagittal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), cervical MRI, and three-dimensional computed tomography (CT) reconstruction, were used to evaluate the spinal damage.

## Results
Whole spine sagittal MRI (T2) showed vertebral body destruction at multiple levels, including C2, T1, T5, T12, and L4, without spinal cord compression. The cervical MRI revealed deconstruction of the C2 right side vertebral body by a tumor, with bony wall penetration and paravertebral extension. T1 and T5 vertebral bodies showed signal changes. Three-dimensional CT reconstruction of the C1-C2 region revealed asymmetric distance between the odontoid and the bilateral C1 lateral mass, resulting in atlantoaxial instability.

## Discussion
After admission, the patient underwent occipital-jaw traction and limited activity. Anterior tumor curettage and iliac bone grafting were performed due to atlantoaxial instability, without fixation. Postoperative pathology confirmed Langerhans cell histiocytosis. The other involved spinal segments were stable, and no further surgical intervention was needed. The patient showed partial relief of occipitocervical pain after surgery and fully recovered after wearing a skull-neck-thorax brace for 3 months.

## Conclusion
This case highlights the rare presentation of noncontiguous multiple spinal involvement in Langerhans cell histiocytosis, complicated by atlantoaxial instability. Early diagnosis and appropriate surgical intervention lead to significant improvement in the patient's condition.
