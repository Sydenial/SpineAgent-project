# Response of Aneurysmal Bone Cyst to Denosumab

## Abstract
Aneurysmal bone cysts (ABCs) are expansile osteolytic lesions that typically contain blood-filled spaces separated by fibrous septa. Standard treatment includes surgical resection or curettage and packing; however, for some spinal lesions, the standard approach is not optimal. This report describes a unique case of large sacral ABCs treated with denosumab, a human monoclonal antibody to RANKL, and reviews the literature on this rare entity.

## Introduction
Aneurysmal bone cysts (ABCs) are expansile osteolytic lesions that typically contain blood-filled spaces separated by fibrous septa. The treatment usually includes surgical resection or curettage and packing, but for some spinal lesions, these approaches may result in serious morbidity. One therapeutic strategy involves using an agent that targets pathways dysregulated in diseases with similar pathophysiology. In ABCs, bone destruction is mediated by receptor activator of nuclear factor k-B ligand (RANKL) produced by tumor cells. Denosumab, a human monoclonal antibody to RANKL, has been effective in treating giant cell tumors of bone and is explored here for ABC.

## Methods
We report a case of a large sacral ABC in a 27-year-old male who presented with increasing back pain. Imaging revealed a lytic lesion in the sacrum with no clear solid component and regions where the cortex was difficult to identify. ABC was diagnosed on biopsy. Given the high morbidity of surgery or radiation, denosumab was administered using the regimen for giant cell tumors of bone (120 mg monthly with a loading dose).

## Results
The patient's pain gradually resolved after two months of treatment. New bone formation and a more clearly defined cortex were evident on computed tomography at 16 weeks and continued to show improvement at 7 and 12 months. Biopsy at 12 months revealed a hypocellular fibrous stroma with new bone formation and no giant cells.

## Discussion
Denosumab functions by binding RANKL, preventing its binding to RANK, and reducing osteoclast accumulation in tumors. This decreases bone destruction and may lead to new bone formation. The treatment is generally well-tolerated, although it carries risks, including osteonecrosis of the jaw and potential changes in bone growth in young patients. Denosumab may be particularly useful in treating ABC in cases not amenable to surgical interventions. Further studies are needed to determine the long-term effects and optimal treatment duration.

## Case Report
A 27-year-old male developed low back pain after falling at work. Imaging revealed a $5.6\times4.1\times3.5$ cm lytic lesion in the right sacrum. Biopsy confirmed the diagnosis of ABC. The patient received denosumab treatment following the regimen used for giant cell tumors of bone. Treatment led to significant pain reduction and the formation of new bone, with subsequent imaging showing clear signs of healing.

## Conclusion
Denosumab shows promise in treating ABC, especially in cases where traditional surgical options are not suitable. It results in both symptomatic and radiological improvement, with further investigation necessary to fully understand its long-term benefits.

## Key Points
- Standard treatment with surgical resection/curettage and packing for some spinal aneurysmal bone cysts (ABCs) is not optimal.
- Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody to RANKL, which mediates bone destruction in ABC.
- Denosumab may be useful for the treatment of select ABC cases.
