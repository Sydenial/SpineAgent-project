# Posterior Unilateral Exposure and Stability Reconstruction with Pedicle and Lamina Screw Fixation for the Cervical Dumbbell Tumorectomy: A Case Report and Biomechanical Study

## Abstract

**Purpose** Cervical dumbbell tumor is usually removed via a posterior approach and may require spinal fixation. A new technique of unilateral exposure and stability reconstruction with pedicle and lamina screw fixation for posterior cervical dumbbell tumorectomy was described and compared with conventional techniques.  
**Methods** Posterior unilateral exposure, hemi-laminectomy, and facetectomy were performed in one patient with a cervical dumbbell tumor between C3 and C4. Stability was reconstructed by unilateral pedicle and lamina screws fixation (UPLS), and a strip of shaped allograft bone was implanted between the superior and inferior lateral mass. Biomechanical stability tests of this new technique were performed using seven fresh-frozen human cervical spine specimens (C4–C7) and compared with unilateral pedicle screw (UPS) and bilateral pedicle screw fixation (BPS) techniques. A continuous pure moment of ±2.0 Nm was applied to the specimen in flexion, extension, lateral bending, and axial rotation.  
**Results** The cervical dumbbell tumor was removed completely, and bone fusion with continuous trabecular bone was maintained at 18 months postoperatively. Biomechanical stability tests revealed that the range of motion of the UPLS fixation plus graft bone implant was similar to the BPS fixation in flexion (1.8° vs 1.5°, p = 0.58) and extension (2.3° vs 2.2°, p = 0.73), but significantly larger in lateral bending (3.9° vs 1.0°, p < 0.001) and axial rotation (6.8° vs 3.8°, p = 0.002), which were smaller than the UPS fixation in all directions (all p < 0.001).  
**Conclusions** For the treatment of cervical dumbbell tumors, posterior unilateral exposure and stability reconstruction with pedicle and lamina screw fixation following hemi-laminectomy and facetectomy appear to provide stable and less traumatic outcomes.

## Introduction

Spinal dumbbell tumors, first described as intra/extraspinal tumors of neural origin, usually grow across the bony neural foramen and the dura of the nerve root sleeve. These tumors account for 13–17% of spinal cord tumors, with a significant portion located at the cervical spine. The majority of dumbbell tumors are schwannomas.  
To eliminate cervical dumbbell tumors, complete resection of the lesion and maximal reduction in neurological symptoms is necessary. Spinal deformity occurs in up to 18% of adult patients after laminectomy, with a higher incidence when combined with facetectomy. Spinal fixation and fusion are often required when more than 50% of the facet joint is resected. In cases with malignant tumors or bone destruction, fusion with instrumentation may be necessary to prevent complications such as pain, deformity, or loss of neurological function.  
There are two common fixation methods after cervical dumbbell tumor resection:  
1. Ipsilateral pedicle screw/lateral mass screw and rod/plate fixation, with a higher risk of instability.  
2. Bilateral pedicle screw/lateral mass screw and rod fixation, which requires larger soft tissue exposure and results in a more significant bony defect.  
This study introduces a new technique combining posterior unilateral exposure, stability reconstruction with pedicle and lamina screws fixation, and bone graft implantation to restore spinal stability, hypothesizing that it would offer better biomechanical stability and less trauma.

## Materials and Methods

### Case

A 50-year-old woman experienced neck pain and numbness on her left side for three years. Preoperative MRI showed a crumbly tumor at the C3 and C4 levels, extending into the vertebral body and both intra- and extradural regions. A coronal CT scan revealed the lesion involved the C3 and C4 vertebrae, neuroforamina, and posterior facet joints on the left side.  
Surgery was performed by two experienced surgeons. After general anesthesia, the patient was placed prone with the head in a neutral position using a three-pin head holder. A midline incision was made, and subperiosteal dissection was performed on the affected side. Muscle dissection was carried out to expose the distal tumor. Left hemi-laminectomy and unilateral facetectomy were performed. A strip of allograft bone was placed between the lateral masses of C2 and C5. The stability was reconstructed with unilateral pedicle screws (C2, C5) and laminar screws (C2–C5), connected with a titanium rod.

### Biomechanical Study

Seven fresh-frozen human cadaveric cervical spine specimens (C4–T1) were used. The specimens were all male, aged 35–70 years (average 44.3 years). Radiographs were taken to rule out obvious conditions, and bone mineral density (BMD) was measured using dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry.  
For pedicle screw placement, the entry points were located using standard landmarks, followed by high-speed drilling. The screws were 26–32 mm long. For lamina screw placement, the entry point was at the lamina's initiation from the spinous process. The procedure was performed under oblique fluoroscopy to ensure correct screw placement.  
Biomechanical testing involved applying a pure moment of ±2.0 Nm to the top vertebra in flexion, extension, lateral bending, and axial rotation. Each specimen underwent six testing configurations, which included:  
1. Intact  
2. Injured  
3. Unilateral pedicle screws fixation (UPS)  
4. Bilateral pedicle screws fixation (BPS)  
5. Unilateral pedicle and lamina screws fixation (UPLS)  
6. Unilateral pedicle and lamina screws fixation with graft bone implant (UPLS+G)

### Statistical Analysis

Data were processed using SPSS software (SPSS Inc, Chicago, IL). Differences in range of motion (ROM) and neutral zone (NZ) among different conditions were analyzed using repeated measures analysis of variance at a 95% significance level. Post-hoc tests were performed with Student-Newman-Keuls analysis.

## Results

### Case

Postoperatively, pathological reports confirmed the tumor was a schwannoma. MRI and CT scans indicated complete tumor resection and adequate cervical spine reconstruction. At the 18-month follow-up, the patient's symptoms resolved, and bone fusion was observed without instrumentation failure.

### Biomechanical Study

The ROMs of the intact specimens were:
- Flexion: $23.1^{\circ} \pm 4.9^{\circ}$
- Extension: $13.6^{\circ} \pm 5.4^{\circ}$
- Lateral bending: $29.4^{\circ} \pm 4.5^{\circ}$
- Axial rotation: $23.0^{\circ} \pm 5.7^{\circ}$

After injury, ROM increased in all directions. The ROMs for UPS fixation were significantly smaller than UPLS+G and BPS fixations. The UPLS+G fixation showed ROMs similar to BPS in flexion and extension, but significantly greater ROMs in lateral bending and axial rotation compared to both UPS and UPLS fixations.

## Discussion

Dumbbell tumors connect multiple regions such as the intradural, epidural, and paravertebral areas. The surgical approaches to these tumors are challenging and controversial. The posterior approach, often used for cervical dumbbell tumors, involves hemi-laminectomy or laminectomy combined with facetectomy. This study demonstrates that unilateral exposure with stability reconstruction using pedicle and lamina screws, along with graft bone implantation, provides effective results with less trauma compared to bilateral approaches. The new fixation technique preserves more soft tissue and bone structures, reducing surgical time, bleeding, and postoperative complications.

## Conclusion

Posterior unilateral exposure and stability reconstruction with pedicle and lamina screw fixation, combined with graft bone implantation, is a feasible and effective technique for treating cervical dumbbell tumors. It offers good biomechanical stability with minimal trauma, making it a viable alternative to more invasive fixation methods.
