# Prolonged Hoarseness Caused by Arytenoid and Fusion Dislocation After Anterior Cervical Corpectomy

## Abstract
Prolonged hoarseness is a common postoperative complication of cervical surgeries, especially in the anterior approach. Postoperative hoarseness is usually associated with paresis of the recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN). However, other causes such as arytenoid dislocation, which is often misdiagnosed as RLN palsy, should not be ignored. This case report emphasizes that arytenoid dislocation could be a possible cause of prolonged hoarseness in patients after anterior cervical corpectomy and fusion (ACCF).

## Introduction
Prolonged hoarseness is a well-recognized complication following cervical surgeries, particularly those involving anterior approaches. The common cause of hoarseness is RLN palsy, but other factors such as arytenoid dislocation, though rare, can also lead to similar symptoms. In this paper, we present a case of arytenoid dislocation after ACCF and review related literature to emphasize the importance of differential diagnosis.

## Methods
We reported a case of arytenoid dislocation after ACCF and reviewed the related literature. The patient underwent laryngoscopy and three-dimensional CT scans, which confirmed the diagnosis of arytenoid dislocation. A closed reduction of the cricoarytenoid joint was performed under general anesthesia, and the patient's vocal fold motion returned to nearly normal post-surgery.

## Results
A 64-year-old male patient who underwent ACCF of C5 developed postoperative hoarseness. Initial concerns pointed to general throat discomfort, but laryngoscopy and CT scans revealed right arytenoid dislocation. A closed reduction was performed, and the patient's hoarseness resolved with significant improvement in vocal fold motion. The patient was satisfied with the clinical outcome, and follow-up evaluations showed successful reduction of the dislocation.

## Discussion
Arytenoid dislocation, though rare, can be a cause of prolonged hoarseness after anterior cervical procedures. The cricoarytenoid joint's role in vocal fold motion makes it susceptible to dislocation, particularly during surgeries that require manipulation of the airway. Misdiagnosis of arytenoid dislocation as RLN palsy can delay appropriate treatment. Diagnostic tools such as CT scans, vocal cord electromyography (EMG), and fiberoptic laryngoscopy are essential for identifying arytenoid dislocation and distinguishing it from other causes of hoarseness. Early diagnosis and reduction of the dislocation are crucial for restoring normal vocal fold function.

## Case Report
A 64-year-old male patient with cervical spondylotic myelopathy underwent ACCF at C5. His medical history included diabetes, abdominal aortic dissection, and benign prostatic hyperplasia. The procedure was completed without incident, and the RLN was not visualized during surgery. However, the patient developed hoarseness postoperatively, which worsened over the next 24 hours. Laryngoscopy and three-dimensional CT scans confirmed the presence of arytenoid dislocation. A closed reduction under general anesthesia was performed, leading to near-normal vocal fold motion and resolution of hoarseness. The patient was satisfied with the outcome.

## Conclusion
Arytenoid dislocation should be considered in the differential diagnosis of prolonged postoperative hoarseness after ACCF. Early diagnosis through imaging and EMG, followed by prompt treatment, can lead to favorable outcomes for patients with this condition.

## Key Points
- A rare case of arytenoid dislocation after ACCF was reviewed.
- Arytenoid dislocation should never be ignored in the differential diagnosis of prolonged postoperative hoarseness after ACCF.
- Once the diagnosis is established, appropriate treatment should be considered immediately.
