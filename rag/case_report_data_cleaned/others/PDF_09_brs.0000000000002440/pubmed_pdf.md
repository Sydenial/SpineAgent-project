# Recommencement of Denosumab for Unresectable Giant Cell Tumour of the Cervical Spine: A Case Report

## Abstract

### Study Design
Case report.

### Objective
To highlight rapid disease recurrence of giant cell tumour of bone (GCTB) with cessation of denosumab in the management of unresectable GCTB, even in cases with prior demonstration of excellent disease control and tumour reduction. The close proximity of unresectable GCTB to vital structures makes it prudent that these patients are monitored closely given its locally aggressive nature.

### Summary of Background Data
Cervical spine GCTB is extremely rare. Unresectable GCTB has historically been challenging to treat due to the lack of prospective, randomized clinical trials to guide treatment. Radiotherapy has fallen out of favour because of the risk of malignant transformation, especially since most GCTB patients are young.

In recent years, improved understanding of the receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB ligand (RANKL) in the pathophysiology of GCTB has led to the use of denosumab in patients with recurrent, unresectable, or metastatic GCTB and in patients for whom surgical resection carries high morbidity. To date, the optimal dosage and duration of therapy in the treatment of GCTB remain unknown.

### Methods
We report a case of cervical spine GCTB in a 53-year-old male with positive surgical margins managed with denosumab.

### Results
This is the first report demonstrating recurrence of disease after cessation of denosumab despite earlier satisfactory disease control with the drug.

### Conclusion
Denosumab is an excellent option in patients with unresectable GCTB or when surgery would result in excessive morbidity. However, further studies are required to determine optimal dosing, treatment duration, side effect profile, and whether denosumab is able to achieve partial or complete disease remission in the long term.

### Keywords
Denosumab; monoclonal antibody; optimal dosing; duration of treatment; side effects.

## Introduction

Giant cell tumour of bone is a benign but locally aggressive tumour, with a peak incidence between the ages of 20 and 30.

Surgery is the treatment of choice for resectable GCTB, with curative intent for localized disease. When surgery is contraindicated, various other treatment modalities have been used. Although radiotherapy has been reported to achieve good long-term local control, it is limited by the risk of malignant transformation.

Denosumab is highly effective in the treatment of GCTB and was approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration in 2013 for recurrent, unresectable, or metastatic GCTB, or for patients in whom surgery would result in excessive morbidity. However, the optimal dosage and duration of denosumab therapy are unknown.

## Case Report

### Case Presentation
A 53-year-old Indian male presented with atraumatic acute axial neck pain without neurological symptoms to a tertiary hospital in December 2011. Examination revealed midline spinal tenderness over the mid-cervical region with full range of motion of the neck. Neurological examination was normal. Plain radiographs showed a possible C3 pathological fracture. Haematological and biochemical results, together with tumour markers, were normal except for a mildly raised erythrocyte sedimentation rate of 16.

Cross-sectional imaging showed a C3 vertebral body lytic lesion extending into both pedicles, with no evidence of metastases.

### Operation
C3 anterior corpectomy with tubular cage reconstruction and C2–C4 anterior fixation was performed in January 2012. Intraoperatively, the tumour was found to be highly vascular with collapse of the vertebral body. Complete resection of the tumour was not possible, and corpectomy of C3 extending to the lateral body was performed.

Subsequent computed tomography and magnetic resonance angiography at 3.5 months postoperatively showed local extension of residual tumour invading the right transverse foramen with partial encasement and displacement of the vertebral artery, without compromise of arterial patency. Denosumab was chosen over further surgical resection due to the high risk of vertebral artery injury and posterior circulation stroke.

Subcutaneous denosumab 120 mg injections were commenced monthly at 4 months postoperatively. Regular computed tomography scans at 3-month intervals demonstrated good response to treatment. The dosing interval was subsequently reduced from monthly to every 2 months for 1 year. Repeat imaging at 6-month intervals showed no disease progression despite dose reduction, and denosumab was ceased after 21 months of therapy.

Interval disease progression was observed 6 months after cessation of denosumab, prompting recommencement of monthly subcutaneous 120 mg injections. Good radiological response was again demonstrated on subsequent imaging.

At latest follow-up, the patient reported complete resolution of neck pain since 3 months post-surgery and experienced no adverse events related to denosumab.

## Discussion

Denosumab is an excellent treatment option for patients with unresectable GCTB or for those in whom surgery would result in excessive morbidity. However, the optimal dose and duration of therapy remain unclear, and there are no long-term studies assessing whether GCTB continues to remain sensitive to denosumab or whether its efficacy diminishes with prolonged use.

Although denosumab is able to control disease to a large extent, it may not completely eliminate residual tumour, underscoring the importance of maximal surgical clearance when feasible. In cases where complete resection is not possible, denosumab remains a valuable adjunct, even when initiated postoperatively, as demonstrated by disease control achieved in this case when treatment was started 4 months after surgery.

The long-term effects of denosumab on normal bone density are unknown, which is particularly relevant given that most patients with GCTB are young. Reports of profound osteopetrosis in RANK-deficient mice and osteopetrotic-like bone changes in a young patient after prolonged denosumab therapy raise concerns regarding long-term safety. In this case, disease stability was maintained despite dose reduction, suggesting that dose de-escalation after disease stabilization may be a potential strategy to reduce long-term adverse effects.
