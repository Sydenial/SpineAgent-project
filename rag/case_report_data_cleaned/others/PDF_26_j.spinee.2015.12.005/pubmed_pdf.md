# Anterior Retroperitoneal Approach for Removal of L5/S1 Foraminal Nerve Sheath Tumor—Case Report

## Abstract

BACKGROUND CONTEXT: Extradural lumbar schwannomas are a rare form of nerve sheath tumors (NSTs). The typical management approach for extradural foraminal NSTs is total gross resection, which involves a midline incision and muscle exposure, followed by laminectomy and facetectomy to access the tumor for resection. Following tumor removal, spinal fusion is often indicated to reduce postoperative deformity, pain, and neurologic deficits.

PURPOSE: We report the case of a 34-year-old woman who presented with a 2-year history of progressive dysesthesia and left foot drop. Magnetic resonance imaging revealed a lesion involving the lateral L5/S1 foramen. A novel anterior retroperitoneal approach was used to access the tumor via muscle splitting, retraction of the peritoneum medially, and the psoas muscle or iliac vessels laterally.

STUDY DESIGN/SETTING: Case report of a novel surgical approach for extradural lumbar schwannomas.

METHODS: Description of the surgical approach and reporting of clinical findings.

RESULTS: The schwannoma was successfully resected without requiring additional fusion surgery. The patient recovered uneventfully and was discharged on postoperative day 2.

CONCLUSION: The anterior retroperitoneal approach is a viable technique for resection of lumbar foraminal NSTs without the need for fusion surgery.

## Introduction

Neoplasms of the spinal canal comprise a range of tumors which may involve the spinal cord, theca, and nerves. Nerve sheath tumors (NSTs) are the most frequent form, accounting for 25%–35% of primary spinal neoplasms in Western populations. These tumors are classified according to location as intradural, combined intradural–extradural, or purely extradural [1]. Intramedullary tumors such as astrocytomas and ependymomas are more common than extramedullary tumors, which are more likely to be NSTs including schwannomas, neurofibromas, and meningiomas.

Spinal extradural foraminal NSTs are rare and more commonly found in the cervical and thoracic regions than in the lumbar spine [1]. The standard management approach is gross total resection using a posterior midline approach with laminectomy and facetectomy, often followed by spinal fusion to reduce postoperative instability, pain, and neurologic deficits [2–4]. Minimally invasive posterior techniques have been described [3,5,6]. Here, we report a case of L5/S1 foraminal schwannoma resection using an anterior retroperitoneal approach.

## Case Report

A 34-year-old previously healthy woman presented with a 2-year history of progressive dysesthesia and left-sided foot drop. Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a lesion in the lateral aspect of the left L5/S1 foramen with lateral deflection of the exiting L5 nerve root. Nerve conduction studies confirmed an L5 nerve root lesion, with normal common peroneal nerve conduction.

The patient was initially scheduled for a posterior approach involving total facetectomy, tumor resection, and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion at an outside institution. A non-fusion anterior retroperitoneal approach was subsequently selected to access and resect the lesion.

Under general anesthesia, a linear incision was made medial to the anterior superior iliac spine and lateral to the rectus abdominis. A muscle-splitting approach through the external and internal oblique muscles exposed the retroperitoneal plane. A retractor system was used to retract the peritoneum medially and the psoas muscle or common iliac vessels laterally, providing anterior exposure of the L5/S1 foramen.

The lesion was readily identified at the L5/S1 foramen, with the L5 nerve root displaced laterally. Using microsurgical techniques, the tumor capsule was incised and the lesion was completely resected. Histopathological examination confirmed a schwannoma without atypical features.

The patient had an uneventful postoperative recovery and was discharged on postoperative day 2. She returned to work within 2 weeks. Imaging at 9-month follow-up demonstrated complete tumor resection.

## Comment

Extradural foraminal schwannomas of the lumbar spine are exceptionally rare, with reported prevalence ranging from 0.7% to 4.2% of all extradural schwannomas [1,3]. The traditional treatment approach involves total resection through a posterior approach with laminectomy, facetectomy, and often fusion to prevent postoperative instability [1,7,8]. To our knowledge, this is the first reported case of lumbar extradural foraminal schwannoma resection using an anterior retroperitoneal approach.

Posterior approaches provide access to posterior spinal elements and facilitate simultaneous fusion if required [9–12]. However, they are associated with disadvantages including longer operative time, extensive muscle dissection, prolonged nerve root retraction, and increased risk of iatrogenic instability requiring fusion [13–17]. Although minimally invasive posterior techniques have reduced some of these risks [3,18–21], they are not suitable for all extradural schwannomas, particularly those extending laterally through the foramen.

The anterior retroperitoneal approach offers direct access to the intervertebral foramen without the need for laminectomy or facetectomy [4,13,22–24]. This minimizes the risk of postoperative instability and eliminates the need for fusion. Additional advantages include reduced muscle trauma, lower blood loss, reduced postoperative pain, faster recovery, and shorter hospitalization.

Potential risks of the retroperitoneal approach include abdominal ileus, injury to the psoas muscle, and vascular injury to the iliac vessels [25,26]. Vascular surgical support should therefore be readily available. An alternative approach for select cases is the far-lateral extraforaminal approach, which may also allow tumor resection without fusion.

## Conclusion

Anterior retroperitoneal access represents a viable alternative to traditional posterior approaches for the removal of lumbar extradural foraminal schwannomas, allowing complete resection while avoiding the need for additional fusion surgery.
