# Denosumab: a potential treatment option for aneurysmal bone cyst of the atlas

## Abstract

**Purpose:** Aneurysmal bone cysts (ABCs) of the spine are traditionally treated with en-bloc resection or intralesional excision/curettage and reconstruction/filling of defects with bone cement. For the treatment of upper cervical ABCs, en-bloc resections are often not desirable due to the risk/benefit ratio, while the risk of recurrence after intralesional excision is high. Hence, alternative management options are often necessary. We describe our clinical experience with one such treatment alternative—denosumab—for the treatment of ABC of the Atlas.

**Methods and Results:** We present the case of a 16-year-old boy who presented with neck pain and limited neck movements. A large lytic lesion with multiple fluid-fluid interfaces involving the vertebral arch of the atlas was identified. The lesion was encasing the right vertebral artery, and biopsy confirmed the diagnosis of ABC. After no response to initial treatment with intralesional calcitonin and methylprednisolone, the patient was treated with denosumab (120 mg SC once a month) for 12 months. His symptoms resolved within 7 months of treatment, and serial CT scans over the treatment period showed complete ossification of the lesion. There was no recurrence at the 12-month follow-up.

**Conclusion:** Our case report contributes to the accumulating evidence on the effectiveness of denosumab for the treatment of spinal ABCs. However, long-term safety, the risk of recurrence, optimal duration of treatment, and consistency of denosumab are yet to be determined.

**Keywords:** Spine · Atlas · Aneurysmal bone cyst · Denosumab · Methylprednisolone and calcitonin injection

## Introduction

Aneurysmal bone cysts (ABCs) are rare benign tumors that comprise about 1% of all primary benign bone tumors. About 10–30% of ABCs are found in the mobile spine, and 11–41% of these are seen in the cervical spine. ABCs of the Atlas are rare and comprise about 10% of cervical ABCs. Radiologically, ABCs appear as lytic blood-filled lesions with multiple fluid-fluid interfaces separated by fibrous septa. Histologically, ABCs are characterized by multinucleated giant cells with hypercellular stromal cells. The natural history of ABCs is often unpredictable. Some ABCs may be slow-growing, while others may be rapidly expanding and destructive. Due to this unpredictability, conventional treatment practices involve curettage and filling defects with cement or wide surgical resection with reconstruction. However, these treatments are not always feasible in ABCs of the spine due to the proximity of vital neural and/or vascular structures. Surgical resections are associated with a complication rate of 15–30%, and recurrence following incomplete excision varies from 10 to 44%.

An ideal treatment for spinal ABC should preserve spinal stability without compromising mobility, prevent recurrence, and minimize risk to neurovascular structures. Given the risks associated with surgical intervention, there is growing interest in non-invasive or minimally invasive treatments for ABCs in the upper cervical spine. Although there is limited evidence to recommend these newer treatment options, they are worth considering in cases where the risks of surgical resection are unusually high. 

## Methods and Results

A 16-year-old boy presented with progressive severe neck pain of 2 months duration, localized to the sub-occipital region. The visual analogue scale (VAS) pain score was 9, and the Neck Disability Index (NDI) was 45. Radiographs and CT scans revealed an expansile lytic lesion in the C1 posterior arch and lamina with paper-thin cortices. MRI confirmed these findings and showed an expansile, multiloculated cystic lesion involving the C1 vertebrae. A CT-guided core biopsy confirmed the diagnosis of ABC. Digital subtraction angiography revealed a hypervascular lesion with small arterial feeders, predominantly from the right V3 segment of the vertebral artery.

Under CT guidance, a mixture of methylprednisolone (80 mg) and calcitonin (400 IU) was injected into the lesion. A follow-up CT scan at 2 months showed no significant change, and there was no clinical improvement. Following a discussion with a multidisciplinary team, the decision was made to treat the patient with denosumab. The patient received 120 mg of denosumab subcutaneously once a month for 12 months, with daily oral supplements of calcium and vitamin D. Six weeks into treatment, the patient's symptoms began to improve, and at 7 months, he regained full neck movement with an NDI of 5. There were no signs of recurrence at the 12-month follow-up.

## Discussion

Treatment of atlanto-axial ABCs is a therapeutic challenge. En-bloc resections at upper cervical levels are associated with major risks such as massive intraoperative bleeding, stroke, neural deficit, and even death. The void left by excision often requires extensive reconstruction or cement filling, which can lead to significant complications. Furthermore, incomplete excision increases the risk of recurrence.

Several treatment alternatives have been considered, including selective arterial embolization (TAE), sclerotherapy, and intralesional injections. While TAE has been successful in some cases, it poses significant risks, especially for intracranial embolization. Similarly, sclerotherapy is not suitable for patients with eroded cortices, and radiotherapy is avoided due to the associated radiation risks. In our case, despite trials with intralesional calcitonin and methylprednisolone, there was no improvement, which led to the use of denosumab.

Denosumab, a human monoclonal antibody, works by binding to the receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa B ligand (RANKL) and preventing the activation of osteoclasts. This mechanism is particularly relevant for ABCs, where giant cells secrete RANKL, promoting bone resorption. The use of denosumab in ABCs, especially in cervical cases, has been reported in a few studies, showing positive results in tumor regression and stabilization.

Our case is the first to report the effectiveness of denosumab in the treatment of ABC of the Atlas. The patient showed significant clinical and radiological improvement, with complete ossification of the lesion and no recurrence after 12 months of treatment.
