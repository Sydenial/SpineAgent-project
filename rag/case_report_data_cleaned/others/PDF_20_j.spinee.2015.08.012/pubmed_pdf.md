# Severe thoracic kyphosis

## Case Report

A 64-year-old man presented with severe weakness of the lower limbs after falling 3 meters 3 days earlier. Physical examination showed hypoesthesia below the costal arch, grade 1 weakness of the lower limbs, absent deep tendon reflexes, and a negative Babinski sign. Radiographs, computed tomography reconstruction images, and magnetic resonance imaging revealed severe kyphosis with a Cobb angle of 180° and an associated rotational deformity. T10 and T11 were the apical vertebrae, and there was spinal stenosis from T8 to L1 with spinal cord compression.

The patient was treated with decompression of the spinal canal at T10 and T11 and screw internal fixation. Sensory and motor deficits were unchanged at the 3-month follow-up.
