# Intraspinal Canal Rod Migration Causing Late-Onset Paraparesis 8 Years After Scoliosis Surgery

## Abstract

### Introduction
Complete intraspinal canal rod migration with posterior bone reconstitution has never been described in the adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) population. We present an unusual but significant delayed neurological complication after spinal instrumentation surgery.

### Case Report
A 24-year-old woman presented with lower limb weakness (ASIA D) 8 years after posterior instrumentation from T2 to L4 for AIS. CT scan and MRI demonstrated intra-canal rod migration with complete laminar reconstitution. The C-reactive protein was slightly elevated (fluctuated between 10 and 20 mg/l). Radiographs showed the convex rod had entered the spinal canal. The patient was taken into the operating room for thoracic spinal decompression and removal of the convex rod. This Cotrel-Dubousset rod, which had been placed on the convexity of the thoracic curve, had completely entered the canal from T5 to T10 and was totally covered by bone, with the eroded laminae entirely healed and closed. There was no pseudarthrosis. Intra-operatively, the fusion mass was opened along the whole length of this rod and the rod carefully removed and the spinal cord decompressed. The bacteriological cultures returned positive for *Propionibacterium acnes*. The patient recovered fully within 2 months post-operatively.

### Conclusion
We opine that the progressive laminar erosion with intra-canal rod migration resulted from mechanical and infectious-related factors. The very low virulence of the strain of *Propionibacterium acnes* is probably involved in this particular presentation where the rod was trapped in the canal, owing to the quite extensive laminar reconstitution.

## Case Presentation

A 14-year-old female (Risser 3) underwent a T2–L2 posterior arthrodesis in 1998 for AIS. As a result of pull-out of the superior hooks 8 months later, she underwent a reoperation necessitating removal of proximal hooks and shortening of rods. Subsequently, due to progressive kyphosis, an extension of the arthrodesis from T2 to L4 was performed in 1999.

The patient continued to experience diffuse mid- and low-back pain, present at rest and worsened after physical activity, with a moderate effect on daily activities. Subsequent radiographs were inconclusive, and the C-reactive protein oscillated between 10 and 20 mg/l.

In 2002, a spinal CT scan from T11 to L5 and bone scintigraphy did not show any abnormalities. The pain was attributed to irritation of the 11th intercostal nerve, and local anaesthetic infiltration was performed, resulting in significant symptom relief for 3 years. A second infiltration was performed in 2007 but did not improve symptoms.

In January 2008, the patient was admitted to the emergency department complaining of difficulty with ambulation and lower limb paraparesis for 2 days (ASIA D), predominantly on the right side. Clinical examination revealed spastic paraparesis with positive Babinski signs bilaterally. Repeat CT scan showed intra-canal migration of the convex rod with overlying bony growth from T5 to T10, without progression of the thoracic curve. MRI demonstrated thoracic spinal cord compression by the rod, with a hyperintense signal from T7 to T9 on T2-weighted imaging. Laboratory evaluation revealed an elevated C-reactive protein level of 37 mg/l.

## Diagnostic Imaging

CT and MRI imaging demonstrated intra-canal migration of the convex rod with associated thoracic spinal cord compression.

## Historical Review, Epidemiology, Diagnosis, Pathology, and Differential Diagnosis

Severe neurological complications related to spinal instrumentation for AIS usually occur during or shortly after surgery and are typically due to implant malposition or postoperative mechanical failure. Delayed neurological complications are most commonly caused by adjacent level disease. Late-onset spinal cord compression by epidural abscess or epidural metallosis has been reported [1–4], but rod-related spinal cord compression has not previously been described.

Delayed neurological complications due to junctional problems are generally evident on radiographs, showing adjacent disc degeneration and instability. Metallosis has been reported as a cause of paraparesis due to compressive intra-canal granuloma [1–3]. In previously reported cases, metallosis was associated with implant loosening, often at the distal end of long fusion constructs. The role of low-virulence organisms has not been systematically investigated in these cases.

In the present case, there was no evidence of corrosion or metallosis, and the inflammatory reaction to *Propionibacterium acnes* was minimal, with no abscess or pseudomembrane formation. However, the organism may have contributed to laminar erosion and intra-canal rod migration by accelerating bone remodeling. At the apex of the scoliosis, the convex rod exerts compressive forces on the posterior arch, and micromotion between implants may increase mechanical stress on the laminae. Concurrent low-grade infection may facilitate laminar erosion while still allowing bone formation, resulting in progressive enclosure of the rod within the spinal canal.

Similar mechanisms have been described in a case reported by Tribus and Garvey [5], although in their patient infection was the dominant clinical feature and resulted in laminar erosion without reconstitution.

## Rationale for Treatment

Late infections following spinal instrumentation occur in approximately 2.6–6.9% of cases [6, 7] and may present with minimal clinical signs. As demonstrated in this patient, delayed diagnosis can have severe consequences. The combination of pain, paraparesis, mildly elevated inflammatory markers, and advanced imaging findings prompted urgent surgical intervention.

## Operative Procedure

Emergency spinal cord decompression and instrumentation removal were performed. A Cotrel-Dubousset rod on the convexity of the thoracic curve had completely migrated into the spinal canal from T5 to T10 and was entirely covered by fusion mass. All levels from T4 to L4 were fully fused, without evidence of bony erosion or pseudarthrosis. Although chronic infection was suspected intra-operatively, no abscess or pseudomembrane was identified.

The fusion mass was opened along the entire length of the rod to allow careful removal of the instrumentation and decompression of the spinal cord. Surrounding tissues were debrided, and thorough irrigation was performed.

Fifteen days postoperatively, bacteriological cultures were positive for *Propionibacterium acnes* in four of five samples. Histopathological examination showed nonspecific inflammatory changes without metallosis. Based on infectious disease consultation, the patient received 2 weeks of intravenous antibiotics followed by 10 weeks of oral therapy.

## Clinical Outcome

Complete neurological recovery (ASIA E) was achieved within 2 months, with full resolution of back pain and ribcage symptoms. This case represents an exceptional cause of delayed spinal cord compression occurring 8 years after spinal instrumentation for AIS. The progressive laminar erosion with intra-canal rod migration likely resulted from a combination of mechanical stress and low-grade infection. The low virulence of *Propionibacterium acnes* may explain the unique presentation, in which the rod became trapped within the spinal canal due to progressive laminar reconstitution.
