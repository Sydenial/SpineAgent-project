# Five-level cervical corpectomy for neurofibromatosis-associated spinal deformity: case report

Scott L. Parker · Jean Paul Wolinsky · Anthony P. Tufaro · Ziya L. Gokaslan · Timothy F. Witham

Received: 2 September 2014 / Revised: 10 November 2014 / Accepted: 13 November 2014 © Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 2014

## Abstract

**Purpose**: To describe a successful five-level cervical corpectomy and circumferential reconstruction in a patient with a plexiform neurofibroma causing a severe kyphotic deformity.  
**Methods**: Case report.  
**Results**: A 43-year-old man with a history of Neurofibromatosis presented with signs and symptoms of myelopathy, including spastic lower extremities and gait difficulties. Imaging studies revealed a severe kyphotic deformity of the cervical spine with cord compression caused by an anteriorly positioned plexiform neurofibroma. A two-stage surgical procedure was planned: Stage I consisted of tracheostomy, transmandibular, circumglossal approach to the cervical spine, C2-C6 corpectomies, and C1-C7 reconstruction with a custom titanium cage/plate. Stage II involved suboccipital craniectomy, C1-C2 laminectomies, and occipital-cervical-thoracic instrumented fusion (O-T8). The patient experienced a small pulmonary embolism postoperatively but made significant recovery after rehabilitation.  
**Conclusions**: A five-level cervical corpectomy, including C2, can be safely and successfully performed via a transmandibular, circumglossal approach. Circumferential reconstruction with a custom titanium cage and plate system optimized for the patient’s anatomy can improve myelopathy, correct deformity, and enhance quality of life.

## Introduction

Severe deformities of the cervical spine remain a challenging pathology to manage, regardless of etiology. Successful surgical outcomes are the result of extensive preoperative planning, adequate decompression, spinal reconstruction using instrumented constructs tailored to the specific pathology, and ultimately bony fusion. Cervical corpectomy in the setting of multilevel myelopathy allows for extensive neurologic decompression and has been shown to be associated with excellent clinical results [1]. Reports of cervical corpectomies extending over as many as four levels have been reported in the literature [2–4]. There have also been reports of six-level corpectomy that extend across the cervico-thoracic junction [5, 6].

However, five-level corpectomy in the cervical spine has not been reported in the literature to date. Such extensive surgical reconstruction can be associated with a high risk of early and late complications; in particular, hardware failure is an important possible early complication and pseudoarthrosis a potential late risk following multi-level cervical corpectomy. We describe a successful five-level cervical corpectomy and circumferential reconstruction in a patient with a plexiform neurofibroma causing a severe kyphotic deformity.

## Case report

### History

A 43-year-old male with a known history of Neurofibromatosis type 1 presented with a three-month history of progressive myelopathy. Symptoms included bilateral upper and lower extremity stiffness, balance difficulties, and a fall due to transient paralysis and numbness. The patient also reported dizziness, constipation, back pain, and neck pain, but denied radicular complaints. Past medical history was significant for asthma and parathyroid disease, both of which were well controlled.

### Physical exam

Physical exam revealed mild weakness in bilateral upper extremities: deltoids 4+, right hand intrinsics 4+, and left hand intrinsics 4+ out of five motor strength. His left lower extremity showed weakness with dorsiflexion 4+ and extensor hallucis longus 3/5 motor strength. Spasticity was present in all four extremities, and the patient walked with a spastic gait.

### Imaging

Subsequent imaging studies revealed a severe kyphotic deformity of the cervical spine with C2-C7 cord compression due to an anteriorly positioned plexiform neurofibroma. The neurofibroma measured $10.2 \times 5.3 \times 9.3~\mathrm{cm}$ (transverse × AP × craniocaudal), was centered within the left neck from the left mastoid process to the level of the thyroid cartilage, and completely encased the left internal carotid and vertebral arteries. The mass entered the cervical canal at multiple levels via left-sided neural foramina and caused remodeling of C2-C4 vertebral bodies. The kyphotic deformity was centered at C3-C4 with approximately 90° of angulation.

### Operation

A two-staged procedure was planned: an anterior approach first to resect the plexiform tumor and reduce the cervical kyphosis, followed by a posterior approach to stabilize the occipital-cervical-thoracic spine.

#### Stage 1

The patient was intubated using an awake, fiberoptic technique. Continuous motor- and sensory-evoked potential monitoring was employed. He was positioned supine on the operating room table with his head placed in a halo ring with traction in extension to correct the deformity. A tracheostomy and exposure to the spine via a transmandibular, circumglossal approach was performed. A midline incision from the patient's lower lip through the submental area to nearly the right mastoid process was made. Subcutaneous tissue and muscle flaps were elevated, and dissection continued anterior to the mandible. An osteotomy was performed just anterior to the mental nerve. The mandible was retracted, and the tongue base was repositioned. A curvilinear incision along the sternocleidomastoid muscle allowed access to the cervical spine from C1 to C7. The esophagus was mobilized, and a 5 cm incision in the posterior pharyngeal wall allowed visualization of the neurofibroma. A large portion of the tumor was resected, exposing the anterior spine from C1 to T1. A total resection was not planned.

Corpectomies at C2-C6 were performed using a high-speed air drill. The posterior longitudinal ligament was dissected off the dura to decompress the spinal cord. A custom titanium cage filled with allograft bone and demineralized bone matrix was placed between C1 and C7, and the plate was fastened with screws at C1 and C7. Intraoperative radiographs showed excellent positioning of the cage-plate construct. The patient remained in a halo postoperatively for three days until Stage 2.

#### Stage 2

Three days later, the patient was positioned prone on the operating room table with the halo secured to the Mayfield frame. The posterior spine was exposed from occiput to T8. A small suboccipital craniectomy and C1-C2 laminectomies were performed for further spinal cord decompression. Pedicle screws were placed bilaterally in T1-T3 and T5-T8. Lateral mass screws were placed bilaterally in C3-C6, except for C3 on the left. An occipital plate was placed in the midline, and tapered rods were placed from occiput to T8. A third rod was added for additional stability. The decorticated surfaces were supplemented with bone morphogenic protein, autograft, allograft, and demineralized bone matrix. Intraoperative radiographs confirmed proper positioning of the instrumentation. The estimated blood loss was 800 mL.

## Post-operative course

The patient's length of stay was 20 days, including 10 days in the neuro-critical care unit. He developed a pulmonary embolism on postoperative day two, which was managed with heparin. On postoperative day seven, the patient developed ventilator-associated pneumonia, treated with vancomycin, piperacillin, and tazobactam. The patient spent two weeks in inpatient rehabilitation following discharge.

## Follow-up/imaging

Patient-reported outcome questionnaires assessing pain (visual analog scale), disability (neck disability index), and quality of life (EQ-5D) demonstrated significant improvement. The short-term improvement observed at the 4-month follow-up was maintained at the 24-month follow-up. The patient resumed normal activities and returned to work. He has full motor strength and a normal gait. The patient reports only minimal pain, relieved with ibuprofen. Radiographs show stable positioning of the hardware from the occiput to T8 at long-term follow-up.

## Discussion

There have been multiple reports of circumferential instrumented fusion for cervical kyphotic deformity [8–10]. However, this is the first five-level cervical corpectomy with circumferential reconstruction described. Severe cervical deformities pose a unique challenge to successful reduction. In this case, a two-stage procedure was planned: an anterior approach for tumor resection and cervical kyphosis reduction, followed by a posterior approach to stabilize the occipital-cervical-thoracic spine. Halo immobilization was essential to maintain alignment between surgeries.

Multi-level cervical corpectomy, especially greater than two levels, involves complex exposure and is associated with complications such as graft migration and pseudoarthrosis [3, 11–13]. A custom titanium cage and plate system was utilized to optimize fit and minimize cage displacement. Anterior plate fixation has been shown to augment construct stability [1, 2, 16]. In this case, posterior fixation with pedicle screws was added to further reduce the risk of complications. 

The patient experienced a postoperative pulmonary embolus, a known complication of extensive surgery, but has made a favorable recovery. Patient-reported outcomes showed significant and durable improvement in pain, disability, and quality of life. Radiographically, the hardware remains stable with no signs of graft-related complications.

Five-level cervical corpectomy, including C2, can be performed safely via a transmandibular, circumglossal approach. Long segment occipital-cervical-thoracic reconstruction is recommended for similar cases. Using this technique, improvement in myelopathy, correction of deformity, and enhanced quality of life can be achieved.
