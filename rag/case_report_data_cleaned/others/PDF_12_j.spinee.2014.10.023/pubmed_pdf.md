# Identification of Arachnoid Web with a Relatively Novel Magnetic Resonance Imaging Technique

## Introduction

Arachnoid webs are described as intradural extramedullary transverse bands of arachnoid tissue that extend to the dorsal surface of the spinal cord, causing mass effect and dorsal indentation, and they are often associated with syringomyelia [1]. Although the precise mechanism of syrinx formation is unknown, dissection of the arachnoid web shrinks the syrinx and improves symptoms [2,3]. Arachnoid webs are not easily visualized with conventional magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or computed tomography (CT) myelography because of their comparatively thin width relative to other structures [2]. Other institutions have described the use of cardiac-gated phase-contrast cine-mode MRI in multiple axial planes to better identify these entities radiographically [2]. We report the use of a heavily T2-weighted constructive interference in steady state (CISS) sequence to identify these lesions preoperatively.

## Case Report

A 65-year-old man presented with worsening gait imbalance and exhibited evidence of myelopathy on physical examination. He had two significant neck injuries in his medical history but was never diagnosed with a cervical fracture.

MRI of the cervical spine revealed abnormal T2 hyperintensity within the lower cervical and upper thoracic spinal cord, extending from C6/C7 to T2. This was thought to reflect a combination of syrinx and either spinal cord edema or myelomalacia. There was also a focal deformity of the thoracic spinal cord at the T2 level dorsally. The thoracic spinal cord at the T1–T2 level was positioned abnormally ventrally within the thecal sac, with a focal curvilinear density identified posteriorly that was believed to represent a thoracic dorsal arachnoid cyst.

For further evaluation of the focal deformity, a myelogram with postmyelographic CT was performed. This revealed complete and immediate opacification of the subarachnoid space up to the T1 level, excluding the possibility of an arachnoid cyst. However, there was a transient holdup of contrast at the cervicothoracic junction that appeared related to narrowing of the dorsal subarachnoid contrast column. A subtle differential contrast density above and below T2 was noted on sagittally reconstructed postmyelographic CT images. Although there were no direct findings of a membrane, these findings were suggestive of a dorsal arachnoid web.

A repeat MRI with CISS sequences was therefore obtained. This imaging revealed a transversely oriented arachnoid membrane extending from the posterior dura to the pial margin of the spinal cord, consistent with an arachnoid web. The patient underwent a T1–T2 laminotomy, during which a thick arachnoid web was visualized and resected. Pulsatile flow was restored to the spinal cord intraoperatively. The patient was ambulating, and significant improvement in gait was noted by postoperative day 2.

## Discussion

“Idiopathic” syringomyelia may be secondary to the presence of an arachnoid web. Increased suspicion for an arachnoid web should arise if a CT myelogram demonstrates any decrease in cerebrospinal fluid flow or dorsal indentation of the spinal cord. A heavily T2-weighted CISS sequence is a useful tool to further clarify the level of cerebrospinal fluid flow obstruction [3] and can assist in preoperative planning.
