# Danger of Injudicious Use of Tui-na Therapy in Ankylosing Spondylitis

## Abstract

Tui-na is a very important component of Chinese medicine. It is a well-respected treatment modality known to be helpful and safe for a wide range of conditions. It can be considered as a certain kind of massage which is performed at acupoints, meridians, and collaterals by pushing, finger twisting, grasping, thumb waving, pressing, patting, palm twisting, and other manipulation techniques. At present, it is extensively used for relieving pain and stiffness associated with ankylosing spondylitis in China, even though there is a lack of evidence to support its validation and feasibility. The patient in this case was treated by tui-na massage at an acute flare-up of ankylosing spondylitis and ended up with catastrophic results.

Keywords: Ankylosing spondylitis · Cervical dislocation · Tui-na therapy

## Introduction

Ankylosing spondylitis (AS), also known as rheumatoid spondylitis, is characterized not only by reduced but also increased bone formation, which contributes to joint ankylosis and syndesmophyte formation, especially in advanced stages [1]. Osteoporosis is considered a common feature of AS even in the early period of the disease [2]. Tui-na is an important part of Traditional Chinese Medicine and is alternatively used in combination with acupuncture and other manipulation techniques to treat AS in China [3, 4]. In this case, a patient with AS was injudiciously treated with tui-na therapy for neck pain and stiffness and ended up with catastrophic results.

## Case Report

A 34-year-old male was admitted to the Emergency Department with quadriplegia (power 0 of 5) and labored breathing. Radiographs showed fracture dislocation at C4–C5 involving all three columns, with distraction and angulation of the spine between C4 and C5. After history taking, it was found that he was a known case of ankylosing spondylitis and had received long-term tui-na therapy to relieve chronic neck pain and stiffness. During the massage process 12 hours before admission, he complained of sudden severe neck pain and then became tetraplegic. He was quickly transported to the hospital for further treatment. However, due to respiratory failure and cardiovascular dysfunction that emerged sequentially and deteriorated rapidly, the patient died 7 days after admission.

## Discussion

Tui-na, as part of Traditional Chinese Medicine, is an ancient therapeutic tradition commonly used in China because of its reported success in treating specific musculoskeletal disorders, such as neck and back pain, sciatica, and arthritis, as well as chronic stress-related disorders of multiple systems. It involves various technical manipulations conducted by the practitioner’s finger, hand, elbow, knee, or foot applied to muscle or soft tissue at specific body locations. Tui-na also incorporates many principles of acupuncture, including the use of acupoints. Manual techniques such as pushing, rubbing, kneading, or high-intensity, high-frequency patting are often used to clear energy blocks along specific meridians associated with particular conditions [5].

The infinite variations of tui-na application are derived from skilled use of depth, pressure, drag, direction, speed, rhythm, frequency, and duration, resulting in variations in the quality of touch. All manipulations introduce mechanical forces into the soft tissues, which stimulate various physiological responses [6]. The five kinds of forces that can affect body tissues are compression, tension, bending, shear, and torsion. Compressive forces occur when two structures are pressed together, with force applied perpendicularly. This depth of pressure is used therapeutically to affect circulation, sensory and autonomic nerve stimulation, nerve chemicals, and connective tissue pliability. Therefore, tui-na is alternatively used in combination with acupuncture and other manipulation techniques to treat AS in China [3, 4].

Ankylosing spondylitis is a seronegative autoimmune inflammatory arthritis that predominantly affects the spine and sacroiliac joints [7]. The interaction between chronic inflammation and the spine is primarily characterized by progressive ossification of spinal ligaments and facet joints, eventually leading to a fixed and stiff spine accompanied by vertebral osteoporosis [8, 9]. With disease progression, the ankylosed spine—most commonly the cervical spine—becomes prone to fracture even after trivial trauma [10]. Thus, in general, compression above the thoracic spine should be avoided, and ankylosed joints should not be forcibly mobilized by tui-na in patients with AS [11]. In this case, cervical hyperextension induced by a compressive force vector produced bilateral fracture dislocation and wide angulation between the C4 and C5 vertebrae, involving both bony and ligamentous components.

Although tui-na can be an alternative and effective method for treating patients with ankylosing spondylitis, practitioners must be extremely cautious in the choice of manipulation techniques. Compression above the thoracic spine should be avoided in patients with AS.

## Conclusion

This case highlights the potential danger of injudicious tui-na manipulation in patients with ankylosing spondylitis, particularly involving the cervical spine. Careful patient selection and avoidance of high-risk maneuvers are essential to prevent catastrophic complications.
