# Abdominal Vascular Injury During Posterior Lumbar Discectomy, Experience from Three Cases and Review of Literature

## Abstract
Vascular injury during lumbar discectomy is a rare but serious complication. We report three cases of vascular injury during posterior lumbar disc surgery, two of which occurred during open discectomy, and one during endoscopic discectomy. These cases highlight the importance of early diagnosis and prompt intervention to prevent morbidity and mortality. All three patients were managed with endovascular stenting and followed for a minimum period of 2 years without further complications.

## Introduction
Abdominal vascular injury during posterior lumbar discectomy was first reported in 1945 by Linton and White. Despite its rarity, the complication can have a rapid downhill course unless diagnosed early and treated emergently. The mortality rate, if left untreated, ranges from 15% to 61%. Vascular injury can lead to laceration of major abdominal vessels, forming a false aneurysm or arteriovenous fistula that may remain undetected for extended periods. This paper presents three cases of vascular injury during lumbar disc surgery, with one case occurring during an endoscopic procedure, which has not been previously reported.

## Case Reports

### Case 1
A 34-year-old man presented with low back pain radiating to the left lower limb for 1 year. MRI revealed a sequestered disc at the L4-L5 vertebral level. After failed conservative treatment, endoscopic discectomy was performed. Intraoperatively, a sudden hemorrhage occurred with a drop in blood pressure, which was controlled with sustained pressure. Postoperatively, the patient developed hypotension and tachycardia, which responded to blood transfusion and vasopressors. CT angiography showed an arteriovenous fistula between the right common iliac artery and vein. Endovascular stenting was performed, and the patient’s anemia and jaundice improved. At a 7-year follow-up, there were no complications.

### Case 2
A 28-year-old woman with a prolapsed L4-L5 disc and cauda equina syndrome underwent open posterior discectomy. During surgery, resistance was encountered with the pituitary rongeur, followed by acute hemorrhage and a drop in blood pressure. A bruit was detected in the right iliac fossa, and CT angiography revealed a pseudo-aneurysm in the common iliac artery. Endovascular stenting was performed, and at a 3-year follow-up, the patient had no complications.

### Case 3
A 31-year-old man presented with low back pain and bilateral radiculopathy for 3 years. MRI suggested a central extruded disc at L4-L5. Open posterior discectomy was planned. During surgery, there was a brief obstruction followed by a gush of bright red blood, and the patient became hemodynamically unstable. CT angiography confirmed a pseudo-aneurysm in the internal iliac artery. Endovascular stenting was performed, and at a 2-year follow-up, the patient had no complications.

## Discussion
Abdominal vascular injuries following posterior lumbar disc surgery are serious complications with high morbidity and mortality. The incidence of these injuries during conventional discectomies is between 0.01% and 0.05%, and the mortality rate can be as high as 61%. The most commonly affected vessels are the left common iliac artery and vein, although in our cases, the right-sided vessels were involved. The main cause of injury was the pituitary rongeur, which perforated the anterior structures during discectomy. Early diagnosis is critical, and CT angiography is the diagnostic tool of choice. Endovascular stenting has proven to be an effective treatment with lower morbidity and mortality compared to open surgery.

## Conclusion
Abdominal vascular complications during posterior lumbar disc surgery are rare but associated with high rates of morbidity and mortality. The key to reducing these complications is early recognition and prompt intervention. Endovascular stenting provides a less invasive and effective treatment option with a lower risk of complications compared to traditional open surgery.

## Key Points
- Pituitary rongeur should be used only to pick loose fragments from the disc space, and curettes should be used to loosen the disc fragments.
- Control of hemorrhage with packing is a life-saving step and acts as a bridge to definitive vascular surgery.
- CT angiography is the diagnostic tool of choice to confirm the presence of vascular injury.
- Endovascular stenting is a less invasive treatment option with lower morbidity and mortality compared to open surgery.
