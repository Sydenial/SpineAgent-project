# Traumatic Brachial Plexus Root Avulsion and Cervical Spine Epidural Hematoma in an 18-Year-Old Man

## Case

An 18-year-old, otherwise healthy man, was an unrestrained passenger in a vehicle that collided with a tree at high speed. Subsequent clinical and radiographic evaluation revealed nonspinal injuries including splenic laceration, multiple left-sided rib fractures, left pulmonary contusions, left acetabulum fracture, right tibia pilon fracture, and right mandibular fracture.

On limited examination, his left upper extremity was flaccid without any movement. A computed tomography scan of his cervical spine demonstrated evidence of a right occipital condyle fracture, right C1 lateral mass fracture, and a cervical epidural hematoma from levels C4–C7. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) confirmed these findings and depicted a left-sided hematoma extending from the inferior aspect of C2 to the T4/T5 levels, compressing the spinal cord with nearly complete effacement from C6 to T1. On axial MRI sequences, an asymmetric shift of the spinal cord was also noted.

The patient was taken to the operating room for laminoplasty and decompression of the epidural hematoma. Preoperatively, somatosensory-evoked potentials produced no signals from the left upper extremity. The hematoma was decompressed using irrigation and suction. After further exposure, the left-sided nerve roots from C3 to C7 were found to be either partially or completely avulsed from their origin at the level of the spinal cord. There was no active extravasation or cerebrospinal fluid leak identified intraoperatively.

## Discussion

Early and accurate diagnosis of cervical spine injuries is crucial. In this case, both computed tomography and MRI revealed a large cervical epidural hematoma located posterolateral to the spinal cord, extending from C2 to T4/T5. In retrospect, the asymmetric shift of the spinal cord observed on axial MRI sequences may have represented underlying nerve root avulsions. Surgical exploration confirmed partial and complete avulsions of the left C4–C7 nerve roots.

As noted by Harper et al. [1], nerve root avulsions cannot be reliably excluded based solely on negative MRI studies. Although the epidural venous plexus has been speculated as a common source of traumatic spinal epidural hematomas, in this case its contribution was directly observed intraoperatively.

This case represents a unique injury complex involving both traumatic spinal epidural hematoma and cervical nerve root avulsions. Laminoplasty proved to be an effective treatment option, allowing adequate exposure and thorough decompression of the epidural hematoma.
