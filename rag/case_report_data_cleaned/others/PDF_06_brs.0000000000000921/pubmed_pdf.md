# Posterior Vertebral Column Resection for Severe and Rigid Spinal Deformity Associated With Neurological Deficit After Implant Removal

## Abstract
Objective: To investigate the safety and efficacy of posterior vertebral column resection for severe and rigid spinal deformity associated with neurological deficit after implant removal following posterior instrumented fusion.

Summary of Background Data: Loss of correction after implant removal in patients with posterior instrumented fusion has been previously reported. However, to our knowledge, posterior vertebral column resection (PVCR) for severe and rigid spinal deformity associated with neurological deficit after implant removal following posterior instrumented fusion has not been reported.

## Introduction
Spinal deformities are complex conditions, and the correction of severe and rigid deformities presents significant challenges, particularly after the removal of posterior spinal instrumentation. This case report explores the outcome of posterior vertebral column resection (PVCR) in a patient who developed severe and rigid spinal deformity with neurological deficits after the removal of spinal implants following a posterior instrumented fusion.

## Materials and Methods
An 18-year-old female had undergone posterior spinal fusion with pedicle screw fixation at 16 years of age in another hospital. Her radiographs revealed that the coronal and thoracic kyphosis Cobb's angles were 75° and 80°, respectively, before the removal of the implants. Her implants were removed after one year due to back pain, and she began to experience weakness in her lower limbs. Seven months prior to admission to our hospital, the patient reported an inability to stand or walk and had to use a wheelchair.

Complete physical examination showed skin hypoesthesia below the umbilical plane, and lower limbs spasticity. The patient had weakness of the intrinsic musculature of lower limbs, and her Babinski sign was positive. Achilles and knee tendons showed hyper-reflexia.

Preoperative radiographs revealed that the coronal and thoracic kyphosis Cobb's angles were 100° and 95°, respectively. The distance between the C7 plumb line and central sacral vertical line (Coronal Trunk Balance, CTB) was 28 mm, and the distance between the posterior superior corner of S1 and C7 plumb line (Sagittal Trunk Balance, STB) was 34 mm. The whole spine 3D-computed tomography scans showed spinal fusion, and magnetic resonance imaging of the spinal canal revealed stenosis. Electrodiagnostic testing showed neurological deficits.

The pedicle screws were inserted using free-hand technique. The patient underwent a VCR at T8, and posterior spinal fusion from T2 to L4. Spinal cord monitoring, including motor evoked potentials (MEP), somatosensory evoked potential (SEP), and wake-up test, were performed during the surgery.

## Results
The surgery duration was 420 minutes, with a blood loss of 4000 mL. There were no changes in the signals of MEP and SEP, and the wake-up test was normal. Postoperatively, the coronal main curve was corrected to 40° and the sagittal kyphosis curve to 35°; the CTB and STB improved to 10 mm and 17 mm, respectively. Three months after PVCR, the patient's neurological status improved to Frankel E. At 32 months of follow-up, there was no deterioration of neurological status, no instrumentation failure, and solid fusion was radiologically evident.

## Discussion
Instrumented posterior spinal fusion is the most common surgical treatment for progressive scoliosis. With advancements in the pedicle screw rod technique and PVCR, correction of severe deformities has improved. However, neurological deficits following spinal deformities present a challenge. Some studies have described the need for implant removal due to infection and late surgical site pain. In 2014, Hilary et al. reported indications for removal of spinal implants, including pain (57%), infection (28%), hardware failure (8%), and prominent hardware (7%). However, neurological deficits following implant removal have not been reported previously. In our case, the patient successfully underwent PVCR and re-instrumentation.

Postoperative long-standing scoliosis radiographs showed good correction, and within three months, the patient's neurological status returned to normal.

## Conclusion
Our study suggests that PVCR is a safe and efficacious option for treating severe and rigid spinal deformity associated with neurological deficits after implant removal following posterior instrumented fusion.

## Key Points
- There is a risk for neurological deficit after the removal of an implant.
- This is the first case report of PVCR for severe and rigid spinal deformity associated with neurological deficit after implant removal following posterior instrumented fusion.
- The patient's neurological status successfully improved from Frankel C to E after PVCR.
