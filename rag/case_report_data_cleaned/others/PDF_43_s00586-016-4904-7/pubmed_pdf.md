# Aggressive osteoblastoma of the cervical spine involving the canal and vertebral artery: a case report

## Abstract

**Objective**  
We present a case of aggressive osteoblastoma of the cervical spine. We describe its complicated clinical progression, hoping to shed light on the surgical strategy for this complex tumor.  

**Methods**  
A 25-year-old man was diagnosed with aggressive osteoblastoma involving the C6–7 vertebrae. The lesion encroached upon the radicular foramina and was located adjacent to the canal of the vertebral artery. Preoperative embolization was performed to reduce intraoperative bleeding and to prevent injury to the vertebral artery during surgery.  

**Results**  
Pathological examination showed osteoblasts suggestive of osteoblastoma. At a 2-year follow-up, bony union was achieved, and no recurrence was observed on a CT scan.  

**Conclusion**  
En bloc total resection for highly vascular osteoblastomas is ideal. However, this case demonstrates that piecemeal total resection following preoperative embolization can be a viable surgical option for highly expansive osteoblastomas.

**Keywords**  
Aggressive osteoblastoma, Cervical spine, Canal and vertebral artery involvement

## Introduction

Osteoblastomas are relatively rare, osteoid-producing benign primary bone tumors, accounting for 1% of all primary bone tumors and 3% of benign bone tumors. These tumors are less common than osteoid osteomas, with an incidence of 10–25% of primary osseous spine tumors. Osteoblastomas typically involve the posterior elements of the spine, but due to their larger size, they may extend into the anterior vertebral body and canal. In 1956, Lichtenstein and Jaffe first reported a series of osteoblastomas.  

By definition, the lesion size of an osteoblastoma at diagnosis is above 2 cm. In the Enneking staging system, stage 3 osteoblastomas are defined as tumors with fully lytic lesions eroding the cortex and invading the canal or surrounding soft tissues. These tumors are referred to as aggressive osteoblastomas. The aggressive nature is illustrated in a study by Raskas et al., where 56.6% of osteoblastomas invaded the epidural space and required dissection of the dura mater.  

Resection is the standard treatment for osteoblastoma, but local recurrence after surgery, especially subtotal resection, occurs in approximately 50% of Enneking stage 3 spine lesions, and en bloc resection is recommended for aggressive osteoblastomas. However, the cervical spine has complex anatomical constraints, such as the dura mater of the spinal cord, spinal roots, and vertebral artery, which complicate en bloc resection. Additionally, aggressive osteoblastomas are highly vascular, further complicating complete resection.

## Case report

A 25-year-old man presented with neck pain, gradually progressive difficulty in walking, and numbness in both hands for 6 months. The lesion had been detected incidentally at another hospital after a traffic accident, and he was referred to our hospital for further management. Preoperative examination revealed mild hyperesthesia in both hands and atrophy in the right-hand intrinsic muscles. The patient could perform the one-leg standing test on each leg but only for 2 seconds. He had hyporeflexia in TTR and hyperreflexia in the lower limbs, with no bilateral Babinski response. His preoperative Japanese Orthopaedic Association (JOA) score was 10/17.  

Routine laboratory blood tests were normal. Chest X-ray was unremarkable. Plain cervical X-rays revealed a radiolucent lesion in the pedicle and facet on the right side, visible in the anteroposterior view of C6–C7. Reconstructed CT showed an expansive and highly vascular osteolytic lesion with a thin rim of cortex involving the spinous process, lamina, pedicle, superior articular process, transverse process, and vertebral bodies of C6–7. The lesion encroached upon the radicular foramina and was adjacent to the vertebral artery canal. Secondary spinal canal narrowing was also noted.  

Sagittal cervical MRI showed an expansile, well-demarcated, inhomogeneous mass that extended into the canal at the C4–T1 levels. Gadolinium-enhanced images showed destruction mainly on the right side of C6 and C7 vertebrae, pedicle, lamina, spinous process, and facet joint. Axial MRI displayed encircling of the right vertebral artery. Pre-surgical open biopsy revealed osteoblastoma without malignant features.  

The patient underwent vertebral artery embolization before surgery to reduce blood loss.  

Two-stage surgery was planned. In the first operation, a midline incision was made, and the paravertebral musculature was gently dissected from the tumor mass. The spinous processes, laminae of C4 and T2, and lateral masses were exposed, identifying the tumor's border. The tumor was removed piecemeal, exposing the dura and right C7 nerve root. The embolized vertebral artery was also exposed. Posterior instrumentation of C4–T2 was performed with screws in C4, C5, C7, T1, and T2, and a high-speed burr was used for shaving the remaining lateral masses.  

After 10 days, total resection of the remaining bony mass was performed via an anterior approach. The C6–C7 vertebral bodies were reconstructed using iliac crest bone graft. The patient did not require a blood transfusion in either procedure.

## Pathologic examination

The pathological examination showed osteoblasts suggestive of osteoblastoma. None of the tumors exhibited significant mitotic activity, and the MIB-1 indices were all <5%.

## Follow-up

The postoperative course was uneventful. The patient was immobilized with a cervical collar for 3 months. Neck pain disappeared soon after the operation. Motor weakness and sensory disturbances improved within 3 months, except for the C7 dermatome and atrophy of the right-hand intrinsic muscles. At the 2-year follow-up, the patient had full neurological recovery, bony union was achieved, and there was no evidence of recurrence on a CT scan. The JOA score improved to 15/17.

## Discussion

This case presents aggressive osteoblastoma with cervical myelopathy due to tumor canal involvement. Osteoblastoma is histologically similar to osteoid osteoma but occurs in slightly older patients, has a greater propensity for the spine, and is more biologically aggressive. Expansively growing cervical spine osteoblastomas are rare but can cause severe neurological damage due to their anatomical proximity to nerve structures.  

The rate of malignant transformation is 12–25%. Rapid growth over months has been described for aggressive osteoblastomas. Surgery is the treatment of choice for symptom relief, halting aggressive behavior, and preventing malignant transformation.  

In a review of 40 stage 3 osteoblastomas treated over 26 years, recurrence rates were 0% after wide/marginal en bloc resection and 7% after intralesional excision with or without radiotherapy. However, good results can be achieved with intralesional excision. In this case, we performed intralesional total resection rather than en bloc resection due to the complexity revealed by gadolinium-enhanced MRI. A two-stage surgical approach was employed because of the tumor's expansive osteolytic nature.  

Aggressive osteoblastomas are highly vascular, complicating complete resection. Preoperative embolization was performed to reduce bleeding and prevent injury to the vertebral artery. To minimize the risk of tumor residue, the lesion was exposed through normal tissue, and piecemeal resection was performed. Adjuvant radiotherapy should be considered in the case of recurrence after intralesional excision.

## Conclusion

We report a case of cervical aggressive osteoblastoma involving two vertebral bodies and posterior elements, resulting in myelopathy. En bloc total resection is ideal for highly vascular osteoblastomas, but this case shows that piecemeal resection following preoperative embolization can be a viable surgical option for highly expansive osteoblastomas.
