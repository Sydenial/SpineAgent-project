# Successful Surgery for a Neuromuscular Scoliosis Patient by Pulmonary Rehabilitation with Forced Vital Capacity Below 30%

KaiHan Yog, Sginu Ciug

Received: 4 September 2016 / Accepted: 3 December 2017 © Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2018

## Abstract

A rare case of a 15-year-old male patient with neuromuscular scoliosis with forced vital capacity (FVC) below 30%, who underwent successful surgery without any pulmonary complications, is reported herein. The patient had obvious asymmetric shoulders and poor exercise tolerance. The Cobb's angle of the main thoracic curve was 62.8°, and FVC in the sitting position was 18% of the predictive value. After skull traction and pulmonary rehabilitation, the FVC was still below 30%, and he finally underwent surgery under this serious condition. By early pulmonary rehabilitation using a home ventilator, he successfully recovered without any pulmonary complications. The patient had complete symptom remission, and no deterioration of Cobb's angle was found during follow-up.

**Keywords**: Neuromuscular scoliosis · Pulmonary rehabilitation · Forced vital capacity · Home ventilator

## Introduction

Neuromuscular scoliosis (NMS), mainly caused by Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) or spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), often causes respiratory failure due to weaknesses of the respiratory muscles. Thus, NMS can pose serious risks to anesthesia and is considered to be a relative contraindication to surgery. In the past decades, scoliosis correction could only be performed in patients with forced vital capacity (FVC) above 30%; otherwise, the increased pulmonary complications could be fatal. However, preoperative pulmonary rehabilitative support, such as a home ventilator, can remarkably reduce surgical risks and postoperative respiratory complications. In the past 10 years, five studies of operations were reported in patients with neuromuscular disease with low FVC below 30% of predictive value. However, there are few reports of successful operations without any postoperative respiratory complications in the previous studies.

In this study, we report on a patient with SMA who developed respiratory failure early in the course of his condition. This complicates the management of the spinal deformity. We present the result of scoliosis surgery in this patient with respiratory failure and on nocturnal ventilator support. There are very few reports of successful scoliosis correction with no respiratory complications achieved by active and comprehensive pulmonary rehabilitation in an SMA patient with FVC below 30% of predictive value.

## Case History

A 15-year-old male patient, who had been found to have obvious asymmetric shoulders and poor exercise tolerance for the past 2 years, was brought to the First Affiliated Hospital, Sun Yat-Sen University by his father. He was very thin and weak, weighing only 43 kg. He had no symptoms of dyspnea during daily life. However, when jogging in the morning at school, he would suffer severe dyspnea and could not continue for even 1 minute. This phenomenon had become more pronounced over the past 2 years. The parents wanted to relieve the child's pain in appearance and improve his quality of life, regardless of the cost. The patient himself was also willing to take the chance. Preoperative examination showed that Cobb's angle of the main thoracic curve was 62.8°. In the evaluation of pulmonary function, FVC in the sitting position was 18% of the predicted value, and pulmonary capacity was 1065.49 ml, which was significantly lower than the normal value.

A skull traction bracket was used for 3 days before an acute coma occurred, and the blood oxygen saturation ($\mathrm{S_aO_2}$) dropped to 60%. He was immediately transferred to the intensive care unit (ICU). In the arterial blood gas analysis, the partial pressure of carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{PaCO_2}$) was 48 mmHg and the partial pressure of oxygen ($\mathrm{PaO_2}$) was 241 mmHg when the fraction of inspired oxygen ($\mathrm{FiO_2}$) was 40%. After 3 days of respirator-assisted ventilation in the ICU, the arterial blood analysis showed CO2 retention, but the patient did not experience any discomfort. He was transferred to the normal ward and received training for noninvasive intermittent positive pressure ventilation (NIPPV) in the assist-controlled ventilation (ACV) mode. He was prescribed a home ventilator, and the ventilator settings were the mode of ACV, a respiration rate of 18–20 times per minute, and a pressure support level of 10 and 4 cmH2O during inspiration and expiration, respectively. Based on the daytime monitoring of $\mathrm{PaCO_2}$, after about 4 hours of discontinuation of the home ventilator, hypercapnia redeveloped; he was thus educated to apply the home ventilator while sleeping and additionally during the daytime. After the application of the home ventilator, the symptoms of hypercapnia during sleep were resolved.

After being discharged, the patient had no complaints related to respiratory failure but suffered from the appearance of severe scoliosis: Cobb's angle of the main thoracic curve increased to 72.7°. After 4 months of halo-gravity traction and pulmonary rehabilitation, he was admitted to the Department of Spine Surgery for surgery. The traction weight was as high as 20 kg for about 10 weeks. In the evaluation of pulmonary function, FVC in the sitting position was 23% of the predicted value, and pulmonary capacity was 1043.16 ml, which was still significantly lower than the normal value. He was considered a high risk for scoliosis surgery due to very poor pulmonary function. After a serious case discussion, the decision was made to proceed with surgery for posterior fixation from the first thoracic vertebra to the third lumbar vertebra on December 14, 2015. During the operation, his vital signs remained stable. From the time of admission, noninvasive PaCO2 monitoring was conducted during sleep. The blood loss was 800 ml, and he had self-blood transfusion for about 400 ml. When the surgery ended successfully, he was transferred to ICU, and NIPPV via a home ventilator was applied immediately after the removal of the intubation tube. Upon confirmation that there were no complications or hypercapnia, he was transferred to the general ward.

At 9 days after the surgery, intravenous antibiotics were administered due to hematuria and odynuria. On January 4, 2016, 26 days after the operation, all stitches were removed, and the wound healed very well. Cobb's angle was reduced to 31.1° postoperatively. The shoulders were almost symmetric when standing. He was then discharged and continued pulmonary rehabilitation using the home ventilator. At 6 months postoperatively, pulmonary function was reevaluated: FVC was 19% of the predicted value and pulmonary capacity was 1414.67 ml. He was able to lead a normal daily life without the home ventilator, both during the daytime and during sleep.

## Discussion

A high incidence of postoperative complications has been noted in most studies focusing on NMS surgery. The mean incidence of complications, including deep surgical-site infections, cardiopulmonary complications, and intraoperative neurologic complications, was 33%. This has led patients with NMS to be considered high risk for surgery. They require significant preoperative planning and preparation to prevent any complications.

Pulmonary complications, such as pneumonia, pneumothorax, and prolonged trachea cannula, are most frequently observed in patients with NMS. According to Rawlins et al., 19% of NMS patients with FVC below 40% may suffer respiratory complications after surgery. In the past, scoliosis surgery was contraindicated in patients with FVC below 30% of the predictive value. However, in studies over the past 10 years, no significant difference in surgical risk was found even if FVC was below 30% of the predictive value.

If respiratory muscles are weakening in patients with NMS, the tidal volume decreases and the respiratory rate increases. Accordingly, fatigue of the respiratory muscles occurs and induces CO2 retention. The patient in this case had a very low FVC below 30% of the predictive value. Due to hypercapnia, he required assistance through NIPPV, and normal ventilatory states were maintained during sleep. After applying NIPPV for more than 4 months, he was able to transition from intubation to NIPPV immediately after surgery without any difficulty.

In our patient, despite having a very low FVC below 30% of the normal predictive value, surgical scoliosis correction was performed successfully without operation-related complications, with pulmonary rehabilitative support (NIPPV) provided using a home ventilator.
