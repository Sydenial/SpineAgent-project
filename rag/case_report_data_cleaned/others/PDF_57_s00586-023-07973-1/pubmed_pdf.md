# Treatment of a Symptomatic Cervical Cerebrospinal Fluid Fistula After Full Endoscopic Cervical Foraminotomy With CT-Guided Epidural Fibrin Patch

## Abstract

### Background
There is only limited data on the management of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) fistulas after cervical endoscopic spine surgery. We investigated the current literature for treatment options and present a case of a patient who was treated with a CT-guided epidural fibrin patch.

### Methods
We present the case of a 47-year-old female patient with a suspected CSF fistula after endoscopic decompression for C7 foraminal stenosis. She was readmitted 8 days after surgery with dysesthesia in both upper extremities, orthostatic headache, and neck pain, which worsened during mobilization. A CSF leak was suspected on spinal magnetic resonance imaging. A CT-guided epidural blood patch was performed with short-term relief. A second CT-guided epidural fibrin patch was executed, after which the patient improved and was discharged home without sensorimotor deficits or sequelae. We investigated the current literature for complications after endoscopic spine surgery and for treatment of postoperative CSF fistulas.

### Results
Although endoscopic and open revision surgery with dural repair have been described in previous studies, dural tears in endoscopic surgery are frequently treated conservatively. In our case, the patient was severely impaired by a persistent CSF fistula. We opted for a less invasive treatment and performed a CT-guided fibrin patch, which resulted in complete resolution of the patient’s symptoms.

### Discussion and Conclusion
CSF fistulas after cervical endoscopic spine procedures are rare complications. Conservative treatment or revision surgery are the standard of care. A CT-guided epidural fibrin patch was an efficient and less invasive option in our case.

## Introduction

CSF fistulas after endoscopic spine surgery are rare complications that can be difficult to treat and result in severe symptom burden, while there are only limited reports on their occurrence and standard treatment [6]. In lumbar spinal endoscopic procedures, the reported incidence ranges from 1% to 4% [6,7]. Dural sac injury in endoscopic interlaminar approaches may occur due to dural tears during insertion of the working tube. These injuries mostly originate from small defects and can often be successfully closed with sealants [7]. However, some authors recommend conversion to open dural repair in cases of extensive durotomies with prolapsing rootlets [3,4].

There is scarce information regarding the management of CSF fistulas and pseudomeningoceles after posterior endoscopic cervical foraminotomy. We present the case of a patient suffering from a CSF fistula after endoscopic foraminotomy and its successful management using a CT-guided epidural fibrin patch.

## Case Report

A 47-year-old female patient presented to the outpatient neurosurgical clinic of the University Hospital of Augsburg with a history of cervical spine trauma seven years prior to admission. Since then, she had suffered from numbness in the index and middle fingers of the left hand. Over several weeks, she experienced an acute exacerbation of radiating pain in the left shoulder blade, ventrolateral upper arm, and medial forearm extending to the wrist, accompanied by tingling paresthesia of the middle and ring fingers, consistent with a left-sided C7 radiculopathy.

Conservative treatment, including oral opioid and analgesic medication as well as facet joint infiltration, failed to provide sufficient pain relief. Magnetic resonance imaging of the cervical spine revealed cervical spondylosis at the C6/C7 level with a left-sided paramedian disc herniation and consecutive foraminal stenosis.

After confirmation via a left-sided fluoroscopy-guided C7 periradicular injection that rendered the patient approximately 80% pain-free for 24 hours, an endoscopic cervical foraminotomy was recommended. The procedure lasted 68 minutes and was performed using a 6.9 mm endoscopic system without noticeable intraoperative complications. Radicular pain resolved immediately after surgery, and the patient reported only mild left-sided C7 hypesthesia. She was discharged home on the first postoperative day after an uneventful course.

On the eighth postoperative day, the patient presented to the emergency department with tingling dysesthesia in both upper extremities, orthostatic headache, and neck pain. These symptoms had started a few days after surgery and intensified with standing and walking, rendering her largely immobile. The surgical wound was dry without signs of irritation, and inflammatory blood markers were within normal limits. Cervical MRI demonstrated sufficient decompression of the left C7 neuroforamen and a fluid collection within the paravertebral musculature, suspicious for a CSF fistula. The symptoms were attributed to a CSF leak.

A CT-guided epidural blood patch using 10 mL of autologous blood was performed, resulting in significant but transient symptom relief lasting approximately 24 hours. Four days later, a second CT-guided epidural fibrin patch was applied. A total of 4 mL of fibrin sealant (1 mL thrombin and 3 mL blood) was injected into the cervical epidural space via an 18-gauge Quincke spinal needle at the C6/7 level after confirmation of correct epidural placement with contrast agent. The patient improved gradually and was discharged home two days later without sensorimotor deficits or sequelae.

At follow-up examinations one month and three months after surgery and intervention, the patient reported complete satisfaction with the outcome. She was pain-free, fully mobile, and without neurological deficits.

## Discussion

Incidental durotomy is a rare but challenging complication in endoscopic spine surgery and may lead to prolonged hospitalization, immobilization, neurological deterioration, and persistent CSF leakage [10]. Persistent CSF fistulas can cause chronic radicular or axial pain as well as postural headaches [2]. A recent survey among spine surgeons revealed that 79.4% preferred the application of a sealant patch with fibrin glue or similar products, while 69.7% favored single-suture repair in cases of incidental durotomy [1].

Endoscopic dural closure techniques using autologous muscle grafts combined with fibrin sealant have been described as safe alternatives in lumbar spine surgery [7]. Nevertheless, prevention of dural tears remains difficult and relies on appropriate patient selection, surgeon experience, meticulous technique, and suitable instrumentation. Full endoscopic dural suture repair has also been reported, but its feasibility depends on the size and location of the defect and is associated with a steep learning curve [11]. In cases of extensive durotomies, conversion to minimally invasive tubular or open surgery may be required.

Unlike lumbar procedures, data on cervical durotomies are scarce. Previous reports in cervical trauma cases describe the use of fibrin glue without lumbar drainage, combined with early drain removal and mobilization [5]. Systematic reviews indicate that dural tears are uncommon in full endoscopic posterior cervical foraminotomy and are often managed conservatively without sequelae [13].

In the present case, the patient was severely impaired by a symptomatic CSF fistula and unable to mobilize independently. After thorough discussion, a less invasive approach was chosen to avoid revision surgery. While an epidural blood patch provided only short-term relief, complete symptom resolution was achieved after CT-guided epidural fibrin patch application. Injectable fibrin sealants have been reported to be effective in cases where blood patching fails or is inappropriate [8,9].

Revision surgery might offer direct visualization and definitive closure of the dural defect but would have been more invasive and potentially associated with increased postoperative pain. CT-guided fibrin patching represents a minimally invasive alternative for small dural defects without nerve root prolapse. However, this technique is limited by the indirect targeting of the suspected leak site, as small dural tears may not be clearly visible on imaging. To our knowledge, this is the first report of a CSF fistula after endoscopic cervical foraminotomy successfully treated using this technique.

## Conclusion

CSF fistulas after endoscopic spine procedures are rare but potentially debilitating complications. In symptomatic patients with small dural defects, a CT-guided epidural fibrin patch may represent a viable and less invasive therapeutic option.
