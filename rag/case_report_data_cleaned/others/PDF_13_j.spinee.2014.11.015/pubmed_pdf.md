# Spontaneous Atlantoaxial Rotatory Dislocation in a Patient with Ankylosing Spondylitis

## Case Report

In July 2012, a 24-year-old man with a 13-year history of ankylosing spondylitis presented with cervical pain and weakness of both the upper and lower extremities. These conditions had initially developed four months earlier and had progressed rapidly over the preceding two months, resulting in dyspnea on admission.

Physical examination revealed decreased muscle strength throughout the limbs (Grade 2 of 5) and generalized hyperreflexia. A lateral plain radiograph showed severe anterior dislocation of the atlas on the axis that markedly compromised the spinal canal.

A sagittal computed tomographic scan revealed marked anterior and vertical migration of the dens with narrowing of the spinal canal at the level of C1, resulting in substantial cervicomedullary compression. A coronal computed tomographic reconstruction showed marked displacement of the C1–C2 facet joints concurrent with rotatory subluxation.

Halo-dependent traction followed by occipitocervical arthrodesis was performed according to the guidance of Chien et al. [1]. Postoperatively, the patient experienced improvement in muscle power and sensory function and was discharged from the hospital 16 days after the initial surgery. At the two-year follow-up, he was able to walk with the aid of a walker, along with a favorable radiologic outcome demonstrating solid occipitocervical fusion without loss of correction.
