# Epidural Chronic Inflammatory Mass of the Lumbar Spine in an HIV-Positive Patient with a History of Repeated Epidural Injections

This study presents a rare case of an epidural chronic inflammatory mass of the lumbar spine in an HIV-positive patient with a history of 12 epidural injections over 3 years due to chronic low back pain (LBP). A 61-year-old man had a 10-year history of LBP and buttock pain. He had been HIV-positive for 4 years. The patient underwent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the lumbar region, which showed degenerated disc bulging, mild stenosis, and hypertrophied ligament flavum at multiple levels in the lumbar spine (Fig. 1). His LBP was treated with epidural triamcinolone and ropivacaine injections, a total of 12 treatments at a pain clinic, but 3 years later, his back pain and bilateral buttock pain aggravated. Follow-up lumbar MRI was performed.

There was a thecal sac compression caused by a $17\times12\times9$ mm-sized, oval-shaped posterior epidural mass at the L4–L5 ligament flavum on the MRI. The lesion showed heterogeneous hypointense signals on T1-weighted images, heterogeneous hyperintense signals on T2-weighted images, and rim enhancement. There was no bony abnormality (Fig. 2). The patient underwent an L4 laminectomy and total excision of the mass, which involved the ligament flavum and epidural adipose tissue. After removal of the mass, the patient's symptoms resolved immediately. Postoperative follow-up MRI showed the complete removal of the epidural mass. Histopathological examination revealed chronic inflammation with hemorrhage and fibrous necrosis throughout the specimen, with no evidence of tumorous lesions or mycobacterial and fungal infections. Some authors suggest that this pathology could result from prior surgery, trauma, immune disturbances, or infection [1,2]. This is the first reported case of an epidural chronic inflammatory mass in the lumbar spine caused by repeated epidural injections in an HIV-infected patient.
