# Clinical Features and Treatment Strategy of Paraspinal Arteriovenous Shunt (PAVS): A Systematic Review with Individual Participant Data Meta-Analysis

## Abstract

**Background**  
Because of the rarity of the disease, paraspinal arteriovenous shunt (PAVS) is not well recognized, and therapeutic options remain controversial. To introduce this rare disease and demonstrate its etiology, clinical features, treatment options, and outcomes, we present a case report and conduct a systematic review and individual participant data (IPD) meta-analysis.

**Methods**  
Studies regarding PAVS were integrated, and IPD were obtained, including patients’ demographics, disease etiology, clinical and radiologic features, clinical courses, and outcomes. Clinical manifestations and treatment outcomes were reviewed, and comparative analyses (cervical versus thoracolumbar) were performed. Logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify poor prognostic factors (incomplete obliteration).

**Results**  
Fifty-two articles were selected, and 88 patients were enrolled. General and location-specific characteristics of PAVS were identified: three-quarters isolated and one-quarter associated etiology; bruit, thrill, or murmur (cervical) and weakness (thoracolumbar) as common symptoms; 40% multiple feeders; and 22% intradural venous involvement. Endovascular treatment was usually preferred. Eighteen patients (20.5%) showed incomplete obliteration. In multivariate analysis, etiologies of systematic genetic dysplasia (p = 0.031) and trauma (negatively, p = 0.038) were significantly associated with incomplete obliteration. Multiple feeders and combined approach were significant only in univariate analyses.

**Conclusion**  
General as well as location-specific characteristics of PAVS are demonstrated. Approximately 20% show incomplete obliteration, particularly in cases associated with genetic dysplasia, trauma (negative association), combined approach (negative association), and multiple feeders.

**Keywords**: Spinal; Paraspinal; Arteriovenous; Fistula; Shunt

## Introduction

Over several decades, clinicians have made efforts to classify spinal arteriovenous shunts (AVSs) with respect to their location, venous drainage pattern, and etiology [1–5]. To date, paraspinal (or paravertebral) AVS (PAVS) has received limited attention owing to its rare incidence and unfamiliarity. These lesions were previously considered epidural or extradural types of spinal AVS until Rodesch first described them using the term “PAVS” [5].

PAVSs are usually located outside the spinal canal and can cause neurological symptoms due to cord compression by ectatic veins, venous congestion, or arterial steal [5]. A new classification of PAVSs based on origins and drainage pathways has been proposed (Stuttgart classification) [6]. Because of the pathophysiological and locational differences between spinal and paraspinal AVSs, a tailored approach to PAVS is mandatory. However, existing studies are largely limited to case reports, and the etiology, clinical manifestations, and treatment strategies remain uncertain.

Herein, we introduce a patient with a large lumbar PAVS and integrate available literature to demonstrate the etiology, clinical manifestations, and treatment strategies of PAVS through a systematic review and IPD meta-analysis.

## Illustrative Case

A 21-year-old male presented with back pain. Preoperative lumbar MRI and CT angiography revealed engorged vascular structures along the L4 vertebral body extending into the left L4 neural foramen. The PAVS was fed by the left L4 lumbar segmental artery originating from the abdominal aorta and draining into epidural venous structures. The feeding artery diameter was 13 mm.

Catheter angiography demonstrated arterialized veins forming a stepladder pattern through the foramina. No additional feeders were identified preoperatively. Given the single visible feeder and concerns about endovascular risk due to large diameter and uncertain margins, surgical ligation was performed via a retroperitoneal approach. Postoperative imaging revealed occlusion of the proximal feeder but persistent retrograde filling of distal venous pouches, indicating additional feeders.

A second-stage embolization was performed targeting extraforaminal venous pouches via collateral vessels. Seventeen detachable coils were deployed, achieving complete obliteration. The patient recovered well, and follow-up MRI at six months showed collapsed venous structures and decompressed thecal sacs.

## Methods

### Study Design

A systematic review and IPD meta-analysis were conducted following the PRISMA-IPD statement [7].

### Eligibility Criteria and Search Strategy

All English-language publications reporting patients with PAVS were included, encompassing case reports, technical notes, and original articles. Studies were required to provide clinical, radiologic, treatment, and outcome data. Articles were excluded if patients were misdiagnosed as other spinal AVS types, written in other languages, or published in the pre-embolization era.

Searches were performed using combinations of terms related to paraspinal or paravertebral arteriovenous fistulae or malformations. Reference lists were manually reviewed to identify additional studies.

### Study Selection and Data Extraction

Two reviewers independently screened abstracts and full texts, with discrepancies resolved by a third reviewer. Extracted data included demographics, etiology, clinical presentation, radiologic features, treatment modality, and outcomes.

### Risk of Bias Assessment

All included studies were case reports or technical notes without comparative designs. As individual participant data were available, no significant risk of bias from study selection was identified.

### Patient Classification and Outcome Definitions

After exclusions and inclusion of the present case, 88 patients were analyzed. Outcomes were classified as complete obliteration without neurologic deficit, complete obliteration with neurologic deficit, incomplete obliteration, or not determined. Incomplete obliteration included failed initial treatment, partial embolization, or recurrence.

Patients were categorized by lesion location (cervical vs. thoracolumbar) and prognosis (complete vs. incomplete obliteration).

### Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation, and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Comparative analyses used appropriate parametric or nonparametric tests. Logistic regression analyses identified factors associated with incomplete obliteration, with significance set at p < 0.05.

## Results

Eighty-eight patients from 53 articles were included [8–59]. The mean age was 29 years, with no gender predominance. PAVSs were located in the cervical spine in 50 patients and thoracolumbar spine in 38.

Key features included 27.3% associated etiology, common symptoms of bruit or murmur (cervical) and weakness (thoracolumbar), 40% with multiple feeders, and 21.6% with intradural venous involvement. Endovascular treatment was performed in 75% of cases. Incomplete obliteration occurred in 18 patients (20.5%).

Comparative analysis showed cervical PAVSs were more frequently associated with female gender, traumatic etiology, bruit or murmur, and cranial symptoms, whereas thoracolumbar PAVSs were associated with male gender, acquired etiology, weakness, deformity, bowel or bladder dysfunction, and multiple feeders. Treatment modality preference differed, but incomplete obliteration rates were similar.

Univariate analysis identified traumatic etiology (negative association), congenital disease (negative), systematic genetic dysplasia, multiple feeders, and combined treatment as significant factors. Multivariate analysis confirmed systematic genetic dysplasia as a poor prognostic factor and traumatic etiology as inversely related to incomplete obliteration.

## Discussion

This study presents the largest reported lumbar PAVS case and the first IPD meta-analysis focusing on PAVS. Approximately one-quarter of cases were associated with metameric or genetic etiologies, which showed higher rates of treatment failure. Traumatic PAVSs, often cervical and related to catheterization injuries, generally exhibited simpler hemodynamics and favorable outcomes.

Clinical presentation varied by location: cervical PAVSs frequently presented with audible vascular signs and cranial symptoms, whereas thoracolumbar lesions often caused myelopathic symptoms such as weakness and bladder or bowel dysfunction. Intradural venous involvement was less frequent than in typical spinal AVSs, reflecting the predominantly extradural location of PAVSs.

## Treatment Strategy

Overall, 22.2% of treated patients experienced incomplete obliteration. Poor outcomes were associated with etiology, presence of multiple feeders, and treatment modality. Genetic or hereditary-associated PAVSs often demonstrated complex hemodynamics and larger lesions, contributing to treatment difficulty. Multiple feeders increased complexity and risk of incomplete treatment. While endovascular therapy is the current first-line approach, combined surgical and endovascular strategies may be advantageous in complex cases.

## Limitations

The study is limited by heterogeneity across a long publication period, potential selection bias inherent to meta-analysis, and evolving definitions of PAVS that may have led to missed cases.

## Conclusion

This systematic review and IPD meta-analysis clarify the general and location-specific characteristics of PAVS. Incomplete obliteration occurs in approximately 20% of cases, particularly those associated with systematic genetic dysplasia and complex vascular anatomy. Careful evaluation of etiology, hemodynamics, and treatment strategy is essential for optimal outcomes.
