# Congenital lordoscoliosis and stenosis of the external ostium of the foraminal canal induced by a nonsegmented transversal bony bar associated with rachischisis and meningocele

## Case Report

### Clinical Presentation
A female patient presented for medical checkup at the age of 5 years because of hypoplasia of the left lower limb and a minor lordotic deformity of the lumbar spine. The decision at that time was simple clinical monitoring. The patient neglected the progressive worsening of the limb hypoplasia and lumbar lordosis and presented again at the age of 15 years with a left L3 radicular syndrome.

Preoperatively, the lordotic deformity was unaesthetic, and the radicular syndrome intensified and became permanent. The lumbar region presented characteristic skin marks, including hypertrichosis, pigmented spots, and a dermal sinus.

### Imaging Findings
X-rays and magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a T11–L3 thoracolumbar scoliotic curve of 25° Cobb and a lumbar hyperlordosis of 62.1°. Normal values for the age of 16 years are reported as 36.7 ± 7.6° [1,2]. 

Computed tomography revealed a longitudinal obliquely oriented bony bar unifying the posterior somatic part of the L3 vertebra with the left pedicle, the base of the L3 transverse process, and the L4 pedicular-lamellar junction. Three-dimensional computed tomography in a posterolateral view showed a trapezoidal L3 vertebra and a significant narrowing of the intervertebral space due to synostosis of the posterolateral portions of the L3 and L4 vertebrae.

A marked stenosis of the external ostium of the left L3 foraminal canal was observed, with a funnel-shaped configuration from inside to outside. The posterior thoracolumbosacral spinal segment distal to T11 presented rachischisis with defects of formation caused by nonunion of the primary lateral posterior ossification centers. Magnetic resonance imaging further highlighted a fusiform meningocele and the presence of diplomyelia corresponding to the T12–L1 spinal segment.

### Surgical Treatment
Surgical management consisted of a posterior approach extending from T10 to S2, followed by left L3 foraminoplasty, repair of the meningocele, and posterior spinal instrumentation with correction of the scoliotic curve and lumbar hyperlordosis.

### Follow-up
Clinical and radiological follow-up at 1 year postoperatively demonstrated complete resolution of the L3 radicular syndrome. Lumbar lordosis was restored to the normal range, measuring 35° Cobb, with appropriate frontal and sagittal spinal balance.
