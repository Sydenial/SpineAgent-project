# A Comprehensive Review of Adult Onset Spinal Teratomas: Analysis of Factors Related to Outcomes and Recurrences

## Abstract

A comprehensive review along with an illustrative case managed at the author's institute has been presented. Materials and methods: An online database search was performed for literature review. Results: A 26-year-old male presented with acute onset neurological deficits and imaging revealed features of a conus teratoma. Subtotal resection was performed and the patient achieved fair recovery. Including the present one, a total of 146 cases have been reported and were analysed. Mean age was 39.6 years (range 18–85 years) and males predominated. Limb weakness and backache were the commonest symptoms. Majority of the lesions were intramedullary and located in the thoracolumbar region. Complete resection (CR) was achieved in 45% of cases. All, except one, were mature teratomas. Recurrences were noted in nine (6.1%) cases. Outcome was good/excellent in 86 (60%) and fair/poor in 26 (18%) cases. The presence of pain, absence of limb weakness and CR were significantly associated with good outcomes. Furthermore, subtotal resection (STR) had significantly higher recurrence rates than CR. Four deaths (2.7%) were noted. Conclusions: Total surgical resection is the standard treatment and appears to be beneficial both in terms of outcomes and recurrences. Overall, recurrences are rare and may be managed by re-surgery. In addition, the present report is the eighth case of adult spinal teratoma with an acute onset presentation.

## Introduction

Spinal teratomas are rare tumours, constituting less than 1% of central nervous system tumours [1–3]. They are mostly noted in the paediatric age group and most commonly in the sacrococcygeal spine. However, the thoracolumbar region is the most frequent location in adults, and spinal teratomas presenting in adults are a rare entity. Till date, less than 150 cases of adult spinal teratomas have been reported in the literature [1–100].

The most common symptoms include pain, progressive limb weakness and bowel/bladder disturbances. An acute onset presentation has been rarely described [22, 28, 32, 33, 40]. In the present report, a rare case of a mature cystic teratoma of the conus-cauda region in a 26-year-old male is described. The patient presented with an acute onset paraplegia and bowel/bladder dysfunction and was managed by surgical resection. In addition, a thorough literature review has been presented on adult spinal teratomas reported in the world literature till date.

## Materials and Methods

All previously published reports (case reports, letters to the editor, review articles and original articles) of adult-onset spinal teratomas were retrieved and analysed. Adult onset was defined as age $\geq 18$ years. The first case reported in 1888 and all cases reported until the present date were included. Cases with missing or inaccessible data were excluded. Sacrococcygeal teratomas and paediatric-onset teratomas were excluded.

The following keywords were searched using online databases (PubMed, Scopus, EMBASE, Google Scholar): “teratoma”, “spinal”, “adult”, “intramedullary”, “intradural”, “extradural”, “mature teratoma”, “cystic teratoma” and “review”. Hospital records were retrieved for the present case. Data analysed included demographics, clinical presentation, duration of symptoms, spinal and axial location, associated anomalies, radiological and histopathological findings, extent of resection, recurrence, time to recurrence, and outcome at last follow-up.

Acute or rapid progression was defined as symptom evolution within 2 weeks. Outcomes were defined as good/excellent when functional or complete motor recovery was achieved, and fair/poor when recovery was non-functional, absent, or worsened. Chi-square testing was used for statistical analysis, with $p < 0.05$ considered significant.

## Results

### Case Report

A 26-year-old male presented with acute onset weakness over 48 hours affecting both lower limbs, associated with bowel and bladder dysfunction. Examination revealed complete paraplegia with flaccid tone and absent reflexes. MRI demonstrated a heterogeneous lesion at the conus-cauda region extending from L1 to L2, with intramedullary and extramedullary components. Imaging features were suggestive of a fat-containing lesion consistent with a mature cystic teratoma.

L1–L2 laminectomy and tumour resection were performed. The extramedullary cystic component was totally excised, while the intramedullary portion was subtotally resected due to poor cleavage planes. Postoperatively, the patient showed partial neurological improvement. Histopathology confirmed a mature teratoma. At 15-month follow-up, the patient had modest motor improvement but persistent bladder dysfunction.

### Pooled Analysis

Including the present case, 146 adult spinal teratomas were analysed. Mean age was 39.6 years (range 18–85), with male predominance. Limb weakness and pain were the most common symptoms. Acute presentations were noted in eight cases.

Most lesions were intramedullary and located in the thoracolumbar region. Complete resection was achieved in 45% of cases, while 37% underwent subtotal resection. All but one tumour were mature teratomas. Mean follow-up was 37 months.

Nine recurrences (6.1%) were reported. Subtotal resection was significantly associated with higher recurrence rates compared to complete resection ($p = 0.029$). Outcomes were good/excellent in 60% and fair/poor in 18%. Four deaths (2.7%) were reported.

Statistical analysis showed that the presence of pain, absence of limb weakness, and complete resection were significantly associated with good outcomes. Extramedullary tumours were more likely to undergo complete resection.

## Discussion

### Epidemiology and Clinical Features

Spinal teratomas are extremely rare neoplasms, accounting for 0.2–0.5% of spinal tumours. Adult-onset cases are uncommon and predominantly affect the thoracolumbar region. Clinical presentation is usually insidious, although acute presentations may occur rarely.

### Imaging

MRI is the modality of choice. Typical features include heterogeneous lesions with fat, cystic and calcified components. Fat-containing regions often appear hyperintense on T1-weighted images. Contrast enhancement is variable and not characteristic of mature teratomas.

### Embryology and Pathogenesis

Multiple theories have been proposed, including dysembryogenic differentiation and misplaced germ cell migration. The exact pathogenesis remains uncertain, particularly in adult cases without overt dysraphism.

### Histopathology

Teratomas consist of tissues derived from all three germ layers. Mature teratomas are benign and composed of differentiated tissues, whereas immature and malignant variants are aggressive and rare in adults. In this review, all but one tumour were mature teratomas.

### Treatment and Outcome

Surgical resection is the treatment of choice. Complete resection should be attempted whenever safely possible, particularly in extramedullary lesions. In intramedullary tumours, subtotal resection may be justified to preserve neurological function. Recurrences are uncommon and usually delayed, highlighting the need for long-term follow-up. Adjuvant radiotherapy has no role in mature teratomas and should be reserved for malignant or immature variants.

## Limitations

Despite being the most comprehensive review to date, some cases may have been missed due to language barriers and indexing limitations.

## Conclusions

Adult-onset spinal teratomas are rare lesions with generally favourable outcomes following surgery. Total resection offers the best chance for good neurological recovery and lower recurrence rates. Subtotal resection is associated with higher recurrence but may be appropriate in selected intramedullary cases. Long-term follow-up is essential due to the slow-growing nature of these tumours.
