# A Case Report of Atypical Long Segmental Thoracic Hypertrophic Pachymeningitis with Ossification of Ligamentum Flavum and Literature Review

## Abstract

**Background:** Thoracic spinal stenosis caused by ossification of ligamentum flavum (OLF) is frequently seen, but long segmental thoracic spinal cord compressed posteriorly by consequent thickened ligament is rarely reported.

**Objective:** To report a case of thoracic spinal cord compression caused by atypical long segmental thoracic hypertrophic pachymeningitis (HP) with OLF.

**Methods:** A 55-year-old woman presenting with weakness and numbness in the lower extremities was admitted to our department. Combined with physical examination and MRI results, a diagnosis of HP with OLF was considered. Due to progressive neurological symptoms, thoracic decompression with internal fixation was performed.

**Results:** The patient felt reduced numbness and improvement in motor function 5 days after surgery. Pathological examination suggested the diagnosis of HP with OLF.

**Conclusions:** HP is a rare condition characterized by thickening and enhancement of the dura mater on contrast-enhanced MRI and chronic inflammatory hyperplasia changes on biopsy. A case of atypical HP complicated with OLF is described. Chondrocyte infiltration on histological examination indicates the potential of ossification in HP.

**Keywords:** Myelopathy; Thoracic spine; Hypertrophic pachymeningitis; Ossification of ligamentum flavum

## Introduction

Long segmental thoracic spinal cord compression posteriorly by consequent thickened ligament is rare. Hypertrophic pachymeningitis (HP) can lead to this presentation and is identified by thickening and enhancement of the dura mater on contrast-enhanced MRI or chronic inflammatory changes on biopsy. Here, a case of chronic myelopathic symptoms in a 55-year-old woman whose thoracic spinal cord was compressed by long consequent ligament and fibrous tissue is presented. MRI and histological examination suggested a possible diagnosis of atypical thoracic hypertrophic pachymeningitis.

## Case Report

A 55-year-old woman had suffered weakness and numbness in the left lower extremity for 1 year, then developed the same symptoms in the right lower extremity half a year ago. She found it difficult to walk unaided when admitted. The patient had no history of back trauma.

Physical examination revealed 4/5 muscle strength in the lower limbs. Decreased pin-prick and touch sensation below the bilateral knees was detected. Knee tendon reflex was normal. Patellar and ankle clonus were negative. Babinski sign was not present.

A computed tomographic (CT) scan showed a striped lesion located posteriorly within the spinal canal from T3 to L1 levels, with non-enhancement on contrast images by iodinated agents. Multiple ossification of ligamentum flavum (OLF) was also observed (Fig. 1).

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with gadopentetate dimeglumine (Gd-DTPA) of the spine presented an epidural lesion located between T3 and L1 compressing the spinal cord posteriorly, with low signal intensity on T1- and T2-weighted images and non-enhancement on T1-weighted enhanced images (Fig. 2).

Due to progressive neurological symptoms, the patient underwent a T3–L1 laminectomy, and consequent white-colored fibrous tissue was stripped. The pia mater was visible (Fig. 3). Histological examination was performed afterwards. Pathological changes showed ligament and fibrous tissue with no inflammatory cell infiltration. No obvious differences were observed between the spinal cord side and lamina side of the specimen. In histological examination of pathological dura mater adhering to ossified ligamentum flavum, chondrocytes were found infiltrated (Fig. 4).

Five days after surgery, the patient felt reduced numbness and improvement in motor function.

## Discussion

Myelopathy caused by OLF has been reported frequently, but long segmental thoracic spinal cord compression posteriorly by consequent thickened ligament is rare. The ligamentum flavum connects adjoining two laminae and is discontinuous in the lamina part, with bilateral flaps separated at the midline. In this case, a consequent lesion was observed compressing the spinal cord posteriorly at the midline. In the operation, thickened dura mater was demonstrated, and the gross specimen presented as a long consequent fibrous tissue. Therefore, HP was given priority for consideration.

HP is an inflammatory process of the dura mater characterized by thickening and enhancement of the dura mater on contrast-enhanced MRI and chronic inflammatory hyperplasia changes on biopsy. According to etiology, HP is classified as idiopathic and secondary types. Autoimmune disorders have been identified as secondary causes of HP in previous reports, such as rheumatoid arthritis [1], multifocal fibrosclerosis [2], Wegener's granulomatosis [3], Neuro-Behçet's disease [4], sarcoidosis [5], ANCA-related diseases [6–8], IgG4-related diseases [9–12, 23], and antiphospholipid syndrome [13]. Infectious diseases caused by bacteria [14, 37], fungi [15], and spirochetes [16, 17] have also been reported in association with HP. Idiopathic hypertrophic pachymeningitis (IHP) should be a diagnosis of exclusion. In this case, diagnostic evidence and potential causal relationships were attempted but not found. Serum CRP, ESR, ANCA, and IgG levels were within normal ranges, and ANA and RF were negative.

Epidemiology of HP has been rarely reported. In a nationwide survey of 159 cases in Japan [18], a prevalence of 0.949/100,000 population was reported. The mean age at onset was 58.3 ± 15.8 years. ANCA-related HP was found in 54 cases (34.0%).

MRI findings of HP can present as a peripheral enhancement pattern, representing more active inflammation in the peripheral zone than in the central zone of fibrosis [7, 19, 20]. Homogeneous enhancement has been reported frequently in IHP [21–23]. A case of IHP with non-enhancement on MRI was also reported [24]. Similarly, in this case, contrast MRI presented low signal intensity on T1- and T2-weighted images and non-enhancement on T1-weighted enhanced images. This enhancement pattern is considered to be associated with a lack of inflammatory reaction in pathological dura mater tissue [20, 24, 25]. Moreover, in some previously reported cases of HP, hyperostosis [26, 27] or destruction [24, 28] of adjacent bone tissue was observed.

Histological examination is key to the diagnosis of HP. Typical pathological manifestations of HP are dense fibrous tissue with a moderate infiltrate of inflammatory cells. In our case, no obvious infiltration of inflammatory cells was observed among hyperplastic fibroblasts on histology, neither on the spinal cord side nor on the lamina side. This pattern might be due to lack of inflammatory process and poor response to immunosuppressive therapy [7, 25].

Obvious OLF was also observed in this case. In previous case reports, thickening or ossification of dura mater was confined to the tissue attached to ossified ligamentum flavum [32, 33]. In the present case, thickened dura mater from T3 to T6 without ossified ligamentum flavum compressing posteriorly was also observed. Therefore, thickening of dura mater and ossification of ligamentum flavum may represent two independent processes in this case.

Histological examination of dura mater adherent to ossified ligamentum flavum revealed chondrocyte infiltration. This finding is similar to that of ligamentum flavum in the process of ossification [29]. This change might be caused by mechanical stimulation from ossified ligamentum flavum. The role of mechanical compression in ligamentum flavum hyperplasia and ossification has been reported [29–31]. A possible mechanism and pathological outcome of HP with ossification of ligamentum flavum was proposed: when ossified ligamentum flavum compresses the dura mater, relative friction during spinal activity leads to adhesion; osteogenic cytokines transfer to the dura mater via adherent tissue and eventually lead to chondrocyte differentiation. Several studies reported dural ossification in OLF patients with an incidence ranging from 15.3 to 40% [32–34]. No previous study had shown the potential of ossification in HP; in this case, chondrocytes were found infiltrated in a particular area on histology.

Because of low morbidity and lack of long-term follow-up studies, there is no optimal management plan for HP currently. In some HP cases associated with infection, antimicrobial therapy was effective [35]. Steroid [7, 20, 25], cyclophosphamide [36], and methotrexate [37] therapies have shown limited efficacy in some reports. For patients with progressive neurologic deficit, decompression surgery should be considered. In the present case, due to progressive myelopathic symptoms, laminectomy and pathological dura mater resection were performed.

## Conclusion

HP is a rare condition characterized by thickening and enhancement of the dura mater on contrast-enhanced MRI and chronic inflammatory hyperplasia changes on biopsy. A case of atypical HP complicated with OLF is described. MRI showed non-enhancement on T1-weighted enhanced images. Histological examination showed no infiltration of inflammatory cells. Chondrocyte infiltration indicates the potential of ossification in HP.
