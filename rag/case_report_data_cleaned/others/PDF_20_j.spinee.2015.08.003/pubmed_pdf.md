# Lumbar Pedicle Lesion Causing Radicular Pain

## Case Report

A twenty-six-year-old male presented with a history of right lower limb radicular pain, paresthesias in the right thigh, and backache for two years. He had an antalgic gait and lumbosacral scoliosis with wasting of paraspinal muscles on the right side.

Computed tomography (CT) showed a lytic lesion with surrounding sclerosis in the right L3 pedicle. There was a soft tissue mass lesion arising inferomedially and causing foraminal stenosis. On magnetic resonance (MR) imaging, the lesion was hypointense in both T1- and T2-weighted images, measuring 2.3 × 2 cm. There was moderate contrast-enhancing soft tissue extending into the right L3–4 neural foramen, compressing the exiting root. Marrow edema was seen on short tau inversion recovery (STIR) images.

The patient underwent a right L3 hemilaminectomy and excision of the tumor. The lesion had both bony and soft tissue components. The L3 inferior facet was excised. The postoperative period was uneventful, and the radicular pain subsided. Postoperative dynamic X-rays showed no instability. The patient was advised follow-up and was informed about the possible requirement of instrumentation in the future if instability occurred.

The lesion was sent for histopathological examination. Histopathology revealed osteoid formation with intervening fibrovascular stroma. There were foci of osteoclastic giant cells with hemosiderin-laden macrophages. Overall features were consistent with osteoblastoma, a benign tumor resulting in remodeling of osteoid tissue and bone [1]. At ten months of follow-up, the patient was doing well.
