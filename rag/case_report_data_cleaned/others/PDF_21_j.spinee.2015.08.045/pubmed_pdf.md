# Gorham's disease—vanishing bone of the cervical spine

## Case Report

A 25-year-old male patient presented with neck pain. Cervical spine magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a mass isointense to muscle on T1- and T2-weighted images with moderate enhancement in the C6 posterior elements. A small right scapula cystic lesion was also seen. Computed tomography revealed the C6 lesion as a destructive vanishing bone tumor, as well as small lucent lesions in the vertebrae, right scapula, and second right anterior costa. Fat density in the second right anterior costa lesion suggested benignity. A very low density mass compatible with lymphangioma was found in the anterior mediastinum. The radiological diagnosis was Gorham's disease. Resection of the C6 vertebral lesion was performed, and pathological examination demonstrated an angiomatous lesion.

## Discussion

Gorham's disease (idiopathic massive osteolysis, vanishing bone disease, or disappearing bone disease) is an aggressive form of osseous angiomatosis and is very rare in the spine [1].
