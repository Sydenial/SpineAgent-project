# Severe Vertebra Collapse Due to Vertebra Osteonecrosis After Percutaneous Vertebroplasty

## Case Report

A 70-year-old woman complained of a 2-week history of severe back pain and restricted motion following a traffic accident. A magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the lumbar spine showed a T12 compression fracture (Figure A) and severe osteoporosis (T score: −3.4); the visual analogue scale (VAS) score was 9. Bilateral percutaneous vertebroplasty was performed, and a total of 4.5 mL of polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) bone cement was injected (Figures B, C). The patient was completely free of back pain, and the VAS score improved to 3 at 1 week after surgery. She was prescribed risedronate sodium (5 mg/day) and thoracolumbar hyperextension brace fixation for 1 month. However, on the 86th day after surgery, she complained of increased back pain without any specific incident. A physical examination showed a VAS score of 5, and plain radiographs revealed a T12 vertebra collapse and kyphoscoliosis (Figure D). Sagittal computed tomography (Figure E) and MRI (Figure F) demonstrated a T12 intravertebral vacuum cleft sign, suggesting the possibility of T12 vertebral osteonecrosis. We recommended percutaneous vertebroplasty for further treatment, but the patient refused.

## Figures

- **Figure A**: MRI T2-weighted images showing high signal intensity on the T12 vertebra with compression (white arrow).
- **Figures B and C**: A total of 4.5 mL of PMMA bone cement was injected, and cement diffusion occurred evenly, achieving end plate-to-end plate (white arrow).
- **Figure D**: Plain radiograph showing T12 vertebra collapse with a kyphosis angle of 45° (white arrow).
- **Figure E**: CT reconstruction showing the T12 vertebral body collapse with intravertebral vacuum (white arrow).
- **Figure F**: MRI T2-weighted images demonstrating low signal intensity on T12 (white arrow).
