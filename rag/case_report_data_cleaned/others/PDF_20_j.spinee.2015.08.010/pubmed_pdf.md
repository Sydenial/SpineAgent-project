# Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis of the Cervical Spine

## Case Report
A 31-year-old with LCH involving the left mastoid base status post resection and radiation presented with progressive severe right-sided neck pain. CT and MRI revealed an osteolytic lesion at C5 (Figure 1). Due to concern for mechanical instability, the patient underwent a two-stage anterior C5-6 corpectomy and posterior cervical fusion with instrumentation (Figure 2). Final pathology of the surgical tissue confirmed LCH (Figure 3). Postoperatively, the patient's pain improved and he continues to undergo chemotherapy for his systemic disease.

## Discussion
LCH is a rare disease commonly affecting the bones, and spinal involvement occurs in 6.5-25% of all skeletal cases, with 1% affecting the cervical spine. LCH of the spine may be treated conservatively with observation, immobilization, NSAIDs, and casting, or with radiation, steroids, or chemotherapy, especially in systemic disease. Surgical intervention (i.e., curettage with or without bone grafting) is indicated in cases of severe mechanical instability, deformity, and/or neurological deficits caused by compression [1,2].
