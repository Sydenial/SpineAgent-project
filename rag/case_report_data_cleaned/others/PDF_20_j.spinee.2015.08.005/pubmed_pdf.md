# Treatment of Sacral Aneurysmal Bone Cyst with Percutaneous Sacroplasty

## Abstract

A 32-year-old male patient was admitted with pain unresponsive to analgesics, reflecting to a sacral aneurysmal bone cyst. The lesion was detected to be hypovascular. Twenty cc of cement was injected into both sides of the lesion under fluoroscopy guidance. The cement remained within the borders of the sacrum on control CT images. The Visual Analog Scale score was 10 before the procedure and regressed to 2 after the procedure. Symptoms improved significantly.

## Introduction

Aneurysmal bone cysts (ABCs) are benign, expansile bone lesions typically affecting the long bones and vertebrae. These cysts can be symptomatic, causing pain, swelling, and pathological fractures. When ABCs affect the sacrum, they present particular challenges due to the surrounding anatomical structures and the complexity of surgical intervention. This study investigates the efficacy of percutaneous sacroplasty in treating sacral aneurysmal bone cysts.

## Methods

A 32-year-old male patient with a sacral aneurysmal bone cyst was evaluated. Imaging studies, including CT and MRI, were performed to assess the lesion. A fluoroscopy-guided biopsy confirmed the diagnosis. The patient was then treated with percutaneous sacroplasty, during which 20 cc of cement were injected into both sides of the lesion.

## Results

Post-procedural CT images demonstrated that the cement remained confined within the sacral borders. The patient reported a significant reduction in pain, with a decrease in the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) score from 10 to 2. The procedure was well-tolerated, and no complications were observed.

## Discussion

Percutaneous sacroplasty has proven to be a safe and effective treatment for sacral aneurysmal bone cysts. This minimally invasive procedure provides pain relief and stabilization of the lesion without the need for open surgery. The use of cement as a filler material helps in maintaining structural integrity and alleviating symptoms.

## Conclusion

Percutaneous sacroplasty is a viable treatment option for patients with sacral aneurysmal bone cysts. It offers significant pain relief and lesion stabilization with minimal risk and recovery time.
