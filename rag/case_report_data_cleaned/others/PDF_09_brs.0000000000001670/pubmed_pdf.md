# Pseudoarthrosis Repair Using Autologous Cultured Osteoblasts in Complex Type-1 Neurofibromatosis Spinal Deformity: A Case Report and Review of the Literature

## Abstract

Neurofibromatosis type-1 (NF-1) is a well-known genetic disorder that is commonly characterized by spinal deformities, including kyphoscoliosis. Late dislocation of the vertebra resulting from progressive dural ectasia after surgical correction of NF-1 scoliosis is a rare occurrence, and pseudoarthrosis frequently develops after surgical intervention for this complex spinal deformity. This report presents a case of a 32-year-old female with NF-1 scoliosis who developed lumbar spine dislocation and implant failure due to massive progressive dural ectasia. Autologous cultured osteoblasts were used for pseudoarthrosis repair, and a three-dimensional printing technique was employed to understand the surgical anatomy of the dislocated lumbar spine.

## Introduction

Neurofibromatosis type-1 (NF-1) is characterized by spinal deformities, including kyphoscoliosis and dural ectasia. Dural ectasia can result from cerebrospinal fluid pulsation, leading to spinal instability. This condition is particularly rare but presents significant challenges in the management of spinal deformities in NF-1 patients. Surgical correction and biologic treatments, such as autologous cultured osteoblasts, may offer promising solutions for complex cases like the one described here.

## Case Report

In 1992, a 32-year-old female patient with NF-1 underwent surgical correction for dystrophic NF kyphoscoliosis. Seventeen years later, she developed lumbar spine dislocation and implant failure due to progressive dural ectasia. Multiple revision surgeries were performed, including posterior instrumented fusion (PIF) and anterior interbody fusion (AIF) with autogenous iliac bone grafts. Despite these interventions, pseudoarthrosis persisted. For the final surgery, autologous cultured osteoblasts were used to repair the non-union, with preoperative three-dimensional printing to better understand the bony defects. Postoperative radiographs showed successful bone union and fusion.

## Methods

The patient underwent posterior instrumented fusion (PIF) followed by anterior interbody fusion (AIF) using autogenous iliac bone grafts. Despite multiple revisions, pseudoarthrosis persisted. For the final operation, 3D printing was used to plan the surgical approach. Autologous cultured osteoblasts, isolated from bone marrow aspirates, were transplanted to the non-union site after decortication. A fibular graft was also used to support the fusion. Postoperative CT scans demonstrated bridging bone formation at the non-union site, suggesting successful fusion.

## Results

After the final operation, bone union was achieved and confirmed by clinical and radiological examination. The patient reported no neurological deficits, although she experienced persistent low back pain, attributed to the multiple spinal surgeries. Radiographic evidence showed that fusion had progressed without implant failure.

## Discussion

Dural ectasia in NF-1 is commonly associated with spinal deformities such as spinal canal and neural foramina enlargement, bony erosion, and instability. In this case, massive dural ectasia developed 17 years after the initial surgery, causing progressive spinal instability and pseudoarthrosis. The use of autologous cultured osteoblasts and 3D printing proved effective in treating this complex case. While biologic treatments like BMP have been explored, their use is limited due to safety concerns. Autologous cell therapy offers a promising alternative for pseudoarthrosis repair in spinal deformities associated with NF-1.

## Conclusion

Spinal deformities in NF-1, combined with late destabilization due to pulsatile dural ectasia, can lead to high rates of pseudoarthrosis. Autologous cultured osteoblasts represent a promising modality for pseudoarthrosis repair, offering an effective solution for complex spinal deformities in NF-1 patients.
