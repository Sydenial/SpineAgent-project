# Opioid-free Analgesia for Posterior Spinal Fusion Surgery Using Erector Spinae Plane (ESP) Blocks in a Multimodal Anesthetic Regimen

## Abstract

The aim of this study was to report the use of erector spinae plane (ESP) blocks as part of an opioid-free multimodal anesthetic regimen and its impact on postoperative pain and opioid requirements following spine surgery. Posterior spinal fusion surgery is highly painful and usually requires significant amounts of opioids for adequate perioperative analgesia; this is commonly associated with adverse effects, including opioid-induced hyperalgesia, nausea, and ileus. The ESP block is a novel ultrasound-guided regional anesthetic technique involving local anesthetic injection into the musculofascial plane between the erector spinae muscle and transverse processes. This safe and simple technique blocks dorsal rami of spinal nerves and can thus provide opioid-sparing analgesia for spine surgery.

## Introduction

Posterior spinal fusion surgery is known for its high pain intensity, necessitating significant opioid use for effective analgesia. However, this reliance on opioids is associated with various adverse effects, including opioid-induced hyperalgesia, nausea, and ileus. In this case report, the efficacy of ESP blocks, integrated into a multimodal anesthetic regimen, is explored as an opioid-free alternative for postoperative pain management following spine surgery.

## Methods

A 35-year-old woman with a previous T3-pelvis fusion for neuromuscular scoliosis underwent revision surgery involving T2-T8 decompression and fusion. She refused the use of perioperative opioids due to intolerable adverse effects during previous surgeries. Analgesia was provided by preoperative bilateral ESP blocks at T4 with 20mL 0.25% bupivacaine and epinephrine 5 μg/ml on each side, and intraoperative infusion of ketamine and dexmedetomidine. Postoperatively, oral acetaminophen 1g every 6 hours was administered, along with baclofen 10 mg every 8 hours to manage muscle spasms.

## Results

The patient had adequate analgesia without the use of opioids during her anesthetic or hospital stay.

## Discussion

The ESP block has been successfully used for lumbosacral spine surgery, but this report marks its novel application in major revision surgery of the thoracic spine. A single injection blocks spinal nerves at multiple levels due to extensive cranial-caudal local anesthetic spread in the musculofascial plane deep to the erector spinae muscle. ESP blocks are feasible even in the presence of prior instrumentation, as the injection site remains lateral to the fusion area. Additionally, the procedure is safe, with minimal adverse effects.

The multimodal anesthetic regimen employed here included preemptive analgesic administration of dexamethasone, dexmedetomidine, and ketamine, alongside avoidance of potent intraoperative opioids. These agents can help prevent opioid-induced hyperalgesia and modulate nociceptive processing in the dorsal horn, improving postoperative analgesia while reducing the need for opioids.

## Conclusion

The combination of intraoperative conduction blockade with ESP blocks, modulation of nociceptive processing, and avoiding opioid-induced hyperalgesia may explain the prolonged duration of effective analgesia observed in this case. This multimodal anesthetic approach shows potential for significantly improving postoperative analgesia following spinal fusion surgery and reducing both acute and chronic opioid use.

## Key Points

- The ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane (ESP) block is a novel, safe, and simple technique that anesthetizes dorsal rami of spinal nerves and provides effective perioperative analgesia in spine surgery.
- The injection site, superficial to the tips of the transverse processes, makes the ESP block a simple and safe technique without the risk of neuraxial or pleural injury, even in the presence of previous spinal instrumentation.
- The use of ketamine and dexmedetomidine infusions contributes to intraoperative analgesia and modulates nociceptive processing in the dorsal horn, preventing central sensitization and improving postoperative analgesia.
- Minimizing or eliminating the use of potent opioids during surgery reduces the risk of opioid-induced hyperalgesia.
- A preemptive, multimodal, intraoperative anesthetic regimen combining conduction blockade, modulation of nociceptive processing, and avoidance of opioid-induced hyperalgesia may significantly reduce the need for postoperative opioid analgesia following posterior spinal fusion surgery.
