# Treatment of Early-Onset Scoliosis with a Hybrid of a Concave Magnetic Driver (MCGR) and a Contralateral Passive Sliding Rod Construct with Apical Control: Preliminary Report on 17 Cases

## Abstract

### Background Context
Magnetically controlled growing rods (MCGRs) are increasingly popular for the surgical treatment of early-onset scoliosis (EOS), because they allow for non-invasive extensions with good growth maintenance. We combined a MCGR with a contralateral passive sliding rod construct with apical control on the convex side to improve efficiency in terms of costs and 3D correction.

### Purpose
To investigate the feasibility, 3D correction, spinal growth, and complications of the apical control MCGR/sliding rod hybrid.

### Study Design
Two-center retrospective cohort study.

### Patient Sample
A consecutive series of 17 EOS children from two European spine centers were treated with the hybrid principle: 13 primary cases and 4 conversion cases from other growth instrumentation. Median age at surgery was 9 years (range 6-18). Median follow-up time was 24 months (range 12-31).

### Outcomes
Cobb angles (frontal Cobb, kyphosis, lordosis), rotation, spinal length gain, growth rate, and complications.

## 1. Introduction
Early-onset scoliosis (EOS) is a potentially life-threatening condition that may require surgical intervention to ensure pulmonary function and development. Several technical solutions have been developed in recent years aimed to allow for growth in a stabilized and corrected spine, thereby retaining thoracic growth potential and pulmonary function. Traditional distraction-based growing rod systems require frequent surgical lengthening procedures. Other systems, such as the Luque Trolley and the Shilla system, have their disadvantages, including multiple planned surgical lengthening procedures, unpredictable lengthening capacity, and a high requirement for operations. The worldwide application of magnetic-controlled growth rods (MCGR), which allow for non-invasive lengthening, has increased in recent years. Early results from several studies suggest the efficacy and cost-effectiveness of the system. We have utilized a hybrid technique, using a single MCGR to drive the lengthening on the scoliosis concave side combined with a passive sliding system with apical control on the convex side.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### Study Design
Retrospective cohort study at two European spine centers.

### Surgical Techniques
Standard surgical techniques were used on all patients. The patients were placed in a balanced prone position without traction. Cell saver and intraoperative neuromonitoring were used according to local procedure guidelines. Proximal and distal anchors were created through separate skin incisions, each consisting of at least 2 consecutive vertebrae. An apical anchor was created unilaterally on the convex side by one or more pedicle screws. The anchor vertebrae were decorticated, facet joints removed, and local or autologous bone graft placed to stimulate fusion. On the concave side, an MCGR was inserted under distraction. On the convexity, the sliding system was fixed to the apex and both rods were contoured proximally in kyphosis and distally in lordosis.

### Post-operative Care
Patients stayed in the hospital for at least 24 hours, and no braces or restraints were applied except for precautions related to heavy lifting. The MCGR was extended by external magnetic stimulation on an outpatient basis, at approximately 2.5-3 month intervals. Biplanar radiographs were taken post-operatively and at follow-up intervals.

### Data Collection
Demographics, medical history, pre- and post-operative parameters were recorded from patient files. Scoliosis deformity measurements were performed on standard digital biplanar scoliosis radiographs. Measurements included the Cobb angle of the main curve, kyphosis and lordosis Cobb angles, and the rotation according to the well-described Nash-Moe method. Height gains were measured after the radiographs were calibrated using the MCGR actuator diameter.

## 3. Results

### Patient Demographics
Our series consisted of 17 patients, 9 from Utrecht and 8 from Aarhus: 13 primary cases and 4 conversion cases from other growth rod instrumentation systems. The etiology was 53% neuromuscular, 29% idiopathic, and 18% syndromic. One patient was 18 years old when her growth instrumentation was converted to MCGR lengthening, but she was small for age with delayed skeletal maturity.

### Surgical Parameters
Mean surgery time was 192 minutes (range: 96-278). No intraoperative complications occurred. Mean admission time was 5.4 days (range: 1-12).

### Radiographic Outcomes
The frontal Cobb angle was reduced from a mean of 59° preoperatively to 30° postoperatively and was maintained throughout follow-up. The rotation of the apical vertebra improved from 27° to 20°. Kyphosis decreased, and lordosis remained largely unaltered.

### Spinal Length Gain
The average T1-S1 height increased from 309 mm preoperatively to 334 mm postoperatively and grew to 347 mm at 1 year follow-up. The instrumented height increased from 237 mm preoperatively to 258 mm postoperatively and grew to 273 mm at 1 year follow-up. The length gains per year indicated a steady growth of approximately 1 cm/year in all length parameters.

### Complications
One defect MCGR was encountered before implantation, but no procedure-related adverse events were registered during implantation. Four instrumentation-related complications occurred after implantation, including one case of progressive trunk shift that was resolved by revising the sliding system. In three occasions, the MCGR failed to distract during a lengthening session, but subsequent lengthenings were successful.

## 4. Discussion
These preliminary results suggest that a combination of a single MCGR as a growth engine and a contralateral passive sliding system with apical control is feasible, although some complications were identified. The growth rates observed were within the expected range for spinal growth, which is estimated to be between 1 and 1.8 cm/year.

## 5. Conclusions
This preliminary report suggests that the application of a single MCGR combined with a contralateral sliding rod construct with apical control is feasible with few complications. Curve correction and spinal growth were on par with dual MCGRs. In seventeen patients, there was one unplanned surgery due to an adverse event not specifically related to this hybrid technique, and one definitive fusion is planned earlier because of spontaneous facet fusion.
