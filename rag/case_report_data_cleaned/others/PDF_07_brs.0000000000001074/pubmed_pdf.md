# Obstructive Hydrocephalus Due to Intraventricular Hemorrhage Following Incidental Durotomy during Lumbar Spine Surgery

## Abstract

**Study Design:** Case report and review of the literature.  
**Objective:** To present an exceedingly rare occurrence of obstructive hydrocephalus secondary to intraventricular hemorrhage after incidental durotomy during lumbar spine surgery.  
**Summary of Background Data:** Incidental durotomies are uncommon but well-recognized complications associated with spine surgery. While mostly considered benign with no untoward clinical sequelae, they can be symptomatic and present with spinal headaches, pseudomeningoceles, wound infection, meningitis, and rarely intracerebral hemorrhage.  
**Methods:** A 76-year-old woman underwent L3–S1 laminectomies and fusion for lumbar spondylosis and stenosis. Intraoperatively, a small incidental durotomy was encountered and primarily repaired.  
**Results:** The patient developed altered mental status on postoperative day 2. Computed tomography of the brain revealed obstructive hydrocephalus and intraventricular hemorrhage. The patient was immediately transferred to the neurosurgical intensive care unit, and an external ventricular drain was placed emergently with high opening pressure. Her mental status improved immediately after cerebrospinal fluid diversion. The external ventricular drain was successfully removed after 8 days. The patient made a full recovery and was discharged in stable condition.  
**Conclusions:** Obstructive hydrocephalus following intraventricular hemorrhage is an exceptionally rare but potentially life-threatening complication of incidental durotomies. Spine surgeons should be aware of this rare but serious complication.

## Introduction

Incidental durotomies are an uncommon but well-recognized complication associated with spine surgery, with an estimated incidence ranging from 1.6% to 17.4% [4]. Fortunately, most patients with incidental durotomies are asymptomatic and have a benign postoperative course [1,2,5,6]. Symptomatic patients usually present with spinal headaches, nausea and vomiting, cerebrospinal fluid leak, cerebrospinal fluid–cutaneous fistula, pseudomeningocele, wound infection, and rarely meningitis or intracranial hemorrhage [1,2,7]. Obstructive hydrocephalus is an exceedingly rare complication. We report a case of obstructive hydrocephalus secondary to intraventricular hemorrhage following an inadvertent durotomy during lumbar fusion surgery.

## Case Report

A 76-year-old woman with low back and leg pain underwent L3–S1 laminectomies and posterolateral fusion for diffuse lumbar spondylosis and stenosis. Intraoperatively, a small incidental durotomy occurred at the L4 level during bone removal with a Kerrison rongeur. The dural defect was primarily repaired with 6-0 Prolene, and a watertight closure was confirmed with a Valsalva maneuver. The remaining portions of the surgery were uneventful.

On postoperative day 2, the patient developed acute altered mental status. Computed tomography of the brain demonstrated enlargement of the lateral and third ventricles with intraventricular hemorrhage obstructing the cerebral aqueduct and fourth ventricle, consistent with obstructive hydrocephalus.

The patient was immediately transferred to the neurosurgical intensive care unit, and an external ventricular drain was placed with high opening pressure. The patient experienced dramatic improvement in mental status following cerebrospinal fluid diversion and returned to her neurological baseline. Vascular studies of the entire neural axis were negative for any vascular abnormality. Cerebrospinal fluid drainage was continued for 8 days. The external ventricular drain was removed after the patient successfully passed a clamp trial without the need for ventriculoperitoneal shunt placement. The patient recovered completely and was discharged in stable condition.

## Discussion

Incidental durotomies are a well-recognized complication of spine surgery. Although management strategies vary among surgeons, the optimal duration of postoperative recumbency has not been well defined. Recent studies have demonstrated increased morbidity associated with prolonged bed rest and favorable clinical outcomes with early mobilization within 24 hours [1,12].

While most patients with incidental durotomies have a benign course, very few reports describe intracranial hemorrhage following durotomy. The true incidence is difficult to estimate, as many cases may remain asymptomatic. This discrepancy is evident when comparing the reported incidence of remote cerebellar hemorrhage (approximately 0.08%) with the much higher incidence of incidental durotomies (1.6% to 17.4%) [12].

Hydrocephalus following incidental durotomy during lumbar spine surgery has been reported previously [10,15]. In those cases, the proposed mechanism involved cerebellar infarction with hemorrhagic transformation and posterior fossa mass effect. In contrast, our patient developed hydrocephalus due to obstruction of cerebrospinal fluid pathways from blood clot formation within the ventricular system, without any intraparenchymal hemorrhage. This represents a different pathophysiological mechanism from previously reported cases.

The exact pathophysiology of this exceedingly rare complication remains unclear. One possible mechanism is migration of blood into the ventricular system secondary to intracranial hypotension following cerebrospinal fluid leakage. Another hypothesis involves changes in transmural pressure across the ventricular wall after incidental durotomy, leading to spontaneous intraventricular hemorrhage and subsequent hydrocephalus. This latter explanation is less likely, given that intentional durotomies are routinely performed for intradural spinal procedures without reports of similar complications.

This case highlights the fact that rare but potentially life-threatening complications can occur following incidental durotomies. Clinicians involved in postoperative care of spine surgery patients should remain vigilant for neurological deterioration, as early recognition and prompt treatment can significantly reduce morbidity and mortality.

## Conclusion

Obstructive hydrocephalus associated with intraventricular hemorrhage is a rare but dangerous complication following incidental durotomy during spine surgery. Early recognition and prompt treatment of obstructive hydrocephalus can lead to complete recovery. Spine surgeons should be aware of this rare complication.
