# Effect of Denosumab on Recurrent Giant Cell Reparative Granuloma of the Lumbar Spine

## Abstract

A case of recurrent giant cell reparative granuloma (GCRG) of the lumbar spine successfully treated with denosumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody against the receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa B (RANK) ligand (RANKL), is reported. This is the first case of recurrent GCRG of the lumbar spine treated with denosumab.

## Introduction

GCRG is a non-neoplastic osteofibrous lesion usually found in the maxilla and mandible but rarely in the spine. It is clinically distinct from giant cell tumor of bone (GCTB), although common histological characteristics such as the proliferation of spindle-shaped stromal cells and multinucleated giant cells are shared. Denosumab has recently been reported to be effective for unresectable GCTB; however, there is only one report of its effect on GCRG. Moreover, the effect of denosumab on GCRG of the spine is unknown.

## Methods

The clinical course, radiological features, pathology, and treatment outcome of a patient with recurrent GCRG of the lumbar spine treated with denosumab are documented.

## Results

Denosumab treatment was used for this patient with unresectable recurrent GCRG of the lumbar spine. Follow-up lumbar radiography showed significant bone formations in the tumor lesion after 3 months of treatment. On follow-up computerized tomography of osteolytic lesions, the formation of cortical-like bone tissue was clearly identified.

## Case Report

A 69-year-old female first noticed low back pain in 2007 when she pulled weeds by hand from a squatting position. She presented to the department of orthopedic surgery with progressive back pain and paresthesia of the right leg. No relevant history was noted, although she had complications of diabetes mellitus and hyperlipidemia.

Radiological imaging revealed osteolysis in the right half of the L3 vertebra, including the right side of the pedicle and transverse process. Sagittal and axial magnetic resonance images showed a lesion in the L3 vertebra. The lesion exhibited low intensity on T1-weighted images, intermediate-to-high intensity on T2-weighted images, and diffuse enhancement on contrast-enhanced T1-weighted images. No abnormal laboratory test findings were identified.

The histological diagnosis was GCRG.

In February 2008, the patient underwent resection of the L3 tumor and placement of a hydroxyapatite block with spinal instrumentation. Three years and 10 months later, the patient developed left leg pain, and osteolytic bone lesions on the left L2 and L3 vertebrae were found. The patient underwent decompression of the L2–L3 lamina, resection of the L2 tumor, and placement of a hydroxyapatite paste with spinal instrumentation. Histological analysis of the L2 and L3 lesions confirmed the diagnosis of GCRG.

In May 2013, the patient began treatment with denosumab (120 mg, monthly subcutaneous injection), along with daily supplements of calcium and vitamin D. After the second surgery, the patient's symptoms improved, and no recurrence appeared during denosumab treatment. Three months after starting treatment, follow-up lumbar radiographs showed bone formation around the right L3 transverse process. This radiographical change progressed over time, and marked bone formation was observed in the L3 vertebra at 6 and 12 months after starting denosumab.

## Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical analysis of tissues from the lumbar lesion confirmed the expression of RANK in multinucleated giant cells and RANKL in the spindle-shaped mononuclear stromal cells. These results suggest that denosumab may have an antitumor effect on GCRG.

## Discussion

GCRG is a reactive lesion to intraosseous hemorrhage from jaw trauma. It is histologically and clinically distinct from GCTB. GCRG, although containing multinucleate giant cells, is distinguished from GCTB by its granulomatous appearance, characterized by fibroblasts and multinucleated giant cells surrounding foci of hemorrhage. Osteoid production, an unusual feature of GCTB, is also commonly identified in GCRG. Local recurrence occurs in 22%-50% of patients.

GCRG involvement of the spine is rare, and the pathogenesis of GCRG is still debated. Denosumab, a human monoclonal antibody directed against RANKL, has been used for the treatment of osteoporosis, bone metastasis, and unresectable GCTB. The effectiveness of denosumab in treating GCRG of the spine is a novel approach, and the results of this case suggest that denosumab can effectively treat unresectable lesions and promote new bone formation in the spine.

## Conclusion

This is the first report of recurrent GCRG of the spine successfully treated with denosumab. Treatment with denosumab induced significant bone formation in the unresectable lumbar lesion, with stable clinical improvement during the 12-month follow-up period and no serious complications. Denosumab shows promise as a new alternative treatment for osteoclastic giant cell-rich tumors, such as GCRG, especially for unresectable lesions in the spine.

- A case of recurrent GCRG of the lumbar spine treated with denosumab is reported. Treatment induced significant bone formation in the unresectable lumbar lesion and showed stable clinical improvement during the 12-month follow-up period without apparent complications.
