# Selective Hemivertebrae Resection in a Congenital Scoliosis Patient with Multiple Hemivertebrae Deformities

## Abstract

**Purpose**  
To report the surgical experience of selective hemivertebrae resection for a case of congenital scoliosis with multiple hemivertebrae deformities.

**Methods**  
A 14-year-old male presented with progressive rib hump, tilted torso, and spinal deformity and was admitted to our department. No abnormalities were detected on neurological examination. Comprehensive imaging demonstrated congenital scoliosis with multiple hemivertebrae at T5, T10, L1, and L3. Treatment commenced with 10 days of skin traction prior to surgery. Selective resection of hemivertebrae at T5 and L1 was performed with segmental fusion from T3 to L2.

**Results**  
After surgery, the patient achieved good coronal and sagittal balance with satisfactory correction of the spinal curve. At 18-month postoperative follow-up, there was no significant loss of correction. No device-related complications, such as implant loosening or failure, or neurological complications occurred. Shoulder balance was further improved, and coronal balance was maintained within a normal range.

**Conclusion**  
Multiple factors must be considered in clinical decision-making for congenital scoliosis patients with multiple hemivertebrae deformities, with particular emphasis on the type and location of hemivertebrae and patient age. Selective hemivertebrae resection may be a more suitable approach for such patients.

**Keywords**  
Congenital scoliosis; hemivertebrae; pedicle screw fixation; spinal fusion; hemivertebrae resection

## Case Presentation

A 14-year-old male presented with progressive rib hump, tilted torso, and spinal deformity and was admitted to the orthopedic department. The malformations had been identified 3 years earlier and had progressed since then, as confirmed by whole-spine radiography, despite unsuccessful conservative treatment. The patient denied any other congenital abnormalities in his medical history. Physical examination revealed shoulder imbalance, rib hump, and a tilted torso. Neurological examination was normal.

## Diagnostic Imaging

Erect whole-spine radiography demonstrated a thoracic curve with a Cobb angle of 42° and a coronal imbalance of 3.9 cm to the left. The left shoulder was 3 cm higher than the right. Lateral radiographs showed thoracic kyphosis of 42° and a positive sagittal balance of 2.4 cm. The Risser sign was grade I. Hemivertebrae deformities were observed at L1 and L3. Bending radiographs revealed a rigid thoracic curve. Computed tomography with three-dimensional reconstruction and magnetic resonance imaging of the whole spine were performed. MRI showed no spinal cord abnormalities. CT demonstrated segmentation failure at C5–6, a butterfly vertebra at T6, and hemivertebrae deformities at T5, T10, L1, and L3.

## Historical Review

Congenital scoliosis is a common etiology of scoliosis and results from failure of vertebral formation or segmentation, leading to asymmetric spinal growth and deformity [1]. Hemivertebrae are the most common cause, accounting for approximately 46% of cases [2]. Numerous studies have reported good correction outcomes in congenital scoliosis patients treated with one-stage hemivertebra resection and posterior pedicle screw instrumentation, with maintained results on long-term follow-up [3,4]. However, unlike cases with single-level hemivertebrae, patients with multiple hemivertebrae deformities require individualized surgical strategies to determine which hemivertebrae should be excised or preserved. Limited literature has addressed detailed treatment strategies for multiple hemivertebrae deformities. This report describes a case of congenital scoliosis caused by multiple hemivertebrae and outlines the surgical strategy employed.

## Rationale for Treatment and Evidence-Based Literature

In patients with congenital scoliosis caused by a single hemivertebra, early resection with short fusion is often appropriate for mild, flexible curves without structural compensatory curves, in order to preserve spinal growth potential [3–7]. In contrast, treatment strategies for multiple hemivertebrae are far more complex due to variations in distribution and type. Rational selection of hemivertebrae for resection is therefore critical.

McMaster et al. [8] suggested that progression of congenital scoliosis depends on four factors: the type, location, number, and relationship of hemivertebrae, as well as patient age. Similar factors guide the selection of hemivertebrae for resection. Except for non-segmented or incarcerated types, most hemivertebrae possess normal growth plates and are prone to progression. Location is also critical: upper thoracic hemivertebrae (T1–4) significantly affect shoulder balance; lower thoracic and thoracolumbar hemivertebrae (T5–L1) often require early intervention due to a high risk of progression; lumbosacral hemivertebrae (L1–S1) can influence pelvic and coronal balance and should be removed if necessary [8,9]. Hemivertebrae located at the apical or end vertebrae of a curve should be resected because asymmetric stress can exacerbate scoliosis. Early intervention before the development of rigidity and secondary structural changes is advocated [7,10].

In this case, considering deformity progression, spinal malalignment, and the patient’s aesthetic concerns, the primary goals were to eliminate the main causes of progression, restore spinal alignment, and partially correct the scoliosis. The semi-segmented hemivertebra at L1 was resected first because it was located at the end vertebra of the thoracic curve and imposed asymmetric stress on T12. Its removal significantly improved coronal balance, with modest curve correction. The hemivertebra at T5 was located at the apex of the thoracic curve; its excision reduced curve magnitude and improved shoulder balance. Due to poor thoracic curve flexibility and patient age, long fusion was selected instead of local fusion.

The hemivertebrae at T10 and L3 were preserved. Radiographic evaluation showed that the lumbar curve (L3–5) had the same direction as the thoracic curve, indicating that it was not secondary. The poor flexibility of the lumbar curve suggested a structural curve that could serve as a stable base. Resection of the L3 hemivertebra would have disrupted this balance. The hemivertebra at T10 was judged to have minimal influence on overall alignment and was left intact. The final outcome demonstrated good coronal balance and satisfactory thoracic curve correction. Erect radiographs at 18-month follow-up confirmed the stability of the lumbar curve and the absence of significant progression.

## Surgical Procedure

Treatment began with 10 days of skin traction prior to surgery. Selective resection of hemivertebrae at T5 and L1 was performed with pedicle screw fixation from T3 to L2. Through a posterior midline incision, subperiosteal exposure of posterior elements from T3 to L2 was achieved. Pedicle screws were placed discontinuously on both convex and concave sides. Wedge-shaped resection of the T5 and L1 hemivertebrae was performed. After complete resection, two precontoured rods were applied, and residual gaps were gradually closed using posterior compression. Somatosensory evoked potential monitoring and wake-up testing revealed no neurological changes. Posterior decortication and fusion from T3 to L2 were then completed.

## Outcome and Follow-Up

Postoperative erect radiographs showed satisfactory coronal and sagittal balance with good curve correction. The thoracic curve improved to 30°, and thoracic kyphosis decreased to 33°. Coronal imbalance improved to 1.2 cm, sagittal balance to 2.1 cm, and shoulder imbalance to 1.5 cm. Photographs taken 2 weeks postoperatively demonstrated marked improvement in torso and shoulder balance. At 18-month follow-up, no significant loss of correction was observed. The thoracic curve further decreased to 25°, thoracic kyphosis increased slightly to 34°, and coronal and sagittal balance remained within normal ranges. Shoulder imbalance improved to 1 cm. No implant-related or neurological complications occurred.

## Conclusion

Clinical decision-making in congenital scoliosis patients with multiple hemivertebrae deformities must account for multiple factors, particularly the type and location of hemivertebrae and patient age. Selective hemivertebrae resection represents a suitable and effective strategy for achieving satisfactory spinal balance and deformity correction in such complex cases.
