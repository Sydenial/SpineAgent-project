# Multiple Posttraumatic Cervical Pseudomeningoceles
## Case Report
A 4-year-old girl presented with weakness and wasting of the left upper limb following a traction injury 3 months earlier. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the spine (Fig. 1) demonstrated multiple, discrete, non-enhancing cystic lesions suggestive of pseudomeningoceles along the dorsal and ventral nerve root sleeves on the left side from the C3 to C7 vertebral levels. The spinal cord was displaced to the right side at some levels by the intraspinal components of these lesions (Fig. 2). There was no evidence of altered signal intensity within the cord, and no fracture or instability of the vertebral column. There was significant atrophy of the shoulder muscles, trapezius, and paraspinal muscles on the left side (Fig. 2A). Electromyographic evaluation demonstrated denervation from C5 to T1, with no sensory involvement. The diagnosis was consistent with an extensive preganglionic brachial plexus injury, with the formation of posttraumatic pseudomeningoceles caused by avulsion of multiple roots from the spinal cord. She was referred to another center for nerve transfers.
