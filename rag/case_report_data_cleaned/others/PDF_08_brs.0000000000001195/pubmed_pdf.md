# Treatment of Extreme Tuberculous Kyphosis Using Spinal Osteotomy and Halo-Pelvic Traction: A Case Report  

## Abstract  
This case report describes the treatment of extreme tuberculous kyphosis using spinal osteotomy and halo-pelvic traction. Spinal tuberculosis causes destruction, deformity, and paraplegia, with long-standing kyphosis progressing in children, leading to respiratory insufficiency and neurological deficits. Surgical intervention can prevent or reverse neurological deterioration and improve pulmonary function in cases with significant spinal deformity. The aim of this study was to outline the treatment process and outcome for a patient with extreme tuberculous kyphosis.

## Introduction  
Spinal tuberculosis can cause significant spinal deformities such as kyphosis and paraplegia. Long-standing kyphosis can progress and result in severe respiratory and neurological complications. The goal of treatment is to correct the deformity, restore function, and prevent further deterioration.

## Methods  
The patient was a 24-year-old female diagnosed with tuberculous angular kyphosis. She presented with bilateral lower extremity paresis and severe dyspnea. Radiographs revealed a Cobb's angle of 180° from T3 to T9. A halo-pelvic apparatus was used for distraction over a period of 10 months, followed by posterior release surgery due to inadequate kyphosis flexibility. Pedicle subtraction osteotomy and pedicle screw fixation were performed to achieve final correction after the Cobb's angle decreased to about 80°. The patient’s height increased by approximately 30 cm, and the kyphosis was corrected to a Cobb's angle of 30°.

## Results  
The patient showed significant improvement. After treatment, the angular kyphosis was corrected to a Cobb's angle of 30°. There were no complications or neurological deterioration during the treatment process. The patient was followed for two years, during which time the correction angle and sagittal balance remained stable.

## Discussion  
Spinal tuberculosis often leads to severe deformities, including kyphosis, which can worsen over time. Surgical correction combined with traction is an effective treatment method. The halo-pelvic apparatus applies slow, consistent corrective forces, which is particularly beneficial for extreme kyphotic deformities. This case demonstrates the successful use of halo-pelvic traction in combination with surgical release and osteotomy to correct severe spinal deformities.

### Advantages of Halo-Pelvic Traction  
Halo-pelvic traction offers the advantage of gradual, controlled correction of deformities. Although it can be burdensome and may limit daily activities, it is safer than rapid, one-stage correction methods, reducing the risk of serious complications such as neurological injuries and large-vessel damage.

### Surgical Procedure  
The patient underwent a posterior release surgery due to insufficient kyphosis flexibility. A high-speed drill was used to create bone grooves on the fused vertebral lamina. After the release, halopelvic traction continued for another 4 months, achieving a Cobb's angle of 80°. Final correction was achieved through pedicle subtraction osteotomy and pedicle screw fixation.

## Conclusion  
Halo-pelvic traction, when combined with spinal osteotomy, is an effective and safe treatment for severe, rigid kyphotic deformities due to spinal tuberculosis. The technique allows for gradual correction without the risk of spinal cord compression, and it provides a stable, long-term correction.

## Key Points  
- A 24-year-old female with extreme tuberculous kyphosis (Cobb’s angle of 180°) was treated using a multi-step approach, including halo-pelvic traction and spinal osteotomy.
- The kyphosis was successfully corrected to a Cobb’s angle of 30° without complications.
- The patient had no neurological deterioration during the treatment, and the correction was maintained during a 2-year follow-up.
