# Growing Rod Technique for the Treatment of Traumatic Spino-Pelvic Dissociation: A Technical Trick

## Abstract

**Background Context:** Traumatic spino-pelvic dissociation, sometimes referred to as a U-shaped sacral fracture, is a very rare high-energy trauma. The surgical management of spino-pelvic dissociation includes decompression, reduction, and fixation.

**Purpose:** We report a novel surgical technique for the treatment of spino-pelvic dissociation that uses growing rods and a pedicle screw system, which is often used to treat patients with early onset scoliosis.

**Study Design:** Technical report of spino-pelvic dissociation surgery using spino-pelvic fixation and the growing rod technique.

**Outcome Measures:** Radiographic outcomes, including plain X-ray, three-dimensional (3D) computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans.

**Methods:** Radiographic outcomes were compared preoperatively, postoperatively, and at the 1-year follow-up after confirmation of bony union.

**Results:** The growing rod technique improved traumatic sacral angulation, displacement, and canal encroachment, and provided sufficient structural support.

**Conclusions:** The growing rod technique for spino-pelvic dissociation under intraoperative neurophysiologic monitoring could be a useful alternative surgical option, especially in patients without neurologic deficit.

**Keywords:** growing rod, spino-pelvic dissociation, sacrum

## Introduction

Traumatic spino-pelvic dissociation, sometimes referred to as a U-shaped sacral fracture, is a very rare high-energy trauma [1]. The sacrum is the mechanical nucleus of the axial skeleton and serves as the base for the spinal column and the keystone for the pelvic ring. Surgical management of spino-pelvic dissociation includes decompression, reduction, and fixation [2,3]. We report a novel surgical technique for the treatment of spino-pelvic dissociation that uses growing rods and a pedicle screw system, which is often used to treat patients with early onset scoliosis [4,5].

## Case Presentation

A 28-year-old woman was injured by a fall from a height of 10 meters, which occurred while she was hallucinating under the influence of anti-psychotic medication. She sustained hemothorax accompanied by flail chest, lung contusion, and hemoperitoneum due to spleen and liver rupture. She underwent emergency surgery for hemoperitoneum and received a chest tube for hemothorax management.

The patient remained in the surgical intensive care unit and underwent spino-pelvic surgery 13 days post-trauma once her vital signs stabilized. Neurologic examination revealed intact motor and sensory function below the level of the sacral fracture. Preoperative CT scans showed no evidence of intraforaminal bone fragments that could injure the nerve root during fracture reduction [1]. Therefore, a growing rod system was selected for fracture reduction and fixation.

## Surgical Technique

During preoperative planning, the insertion level of the proximal pedicle screws was determined using lateral plain radiographs, taking into consideration distal rod bending and the minimal length of the growing rods. The preferred insertion level was near the thoraco-lumbar junction.

The entire back and upper buttock region was prepared and draped in a sterile orthopedic manner. Skin incisions were made over both iliac screw entry points bilaterally and along the posterior midline at the designated proximal lumbar vertebra level. Proximal pedicle screws were inserted using a free-hand technique or under C-arm guidance. Iliac screws were then inserted under C-arm guidance from the posterior superior iliac crest to maximize fixation force. Screws with an 8.5 mm diameter and 90–95 mm length were preferred, with tips positioned within 1 cm of the sciatic notch.

Submuscular growing rods were inserted and assembled. Under lateral C-arm guidance, bilateral simultaneous distraction of the growing rods was performed by the surgeon and assistant. After confirmation of adequate distraction and reduction of the proximal sacral fragment, the rods were locked in position. Motor evoked potential (MEP) monitoring was performed before and after reduction to ensure neurologic safety.

## Postoperative Care

Postoperative motor and sensory function remained intact. The patient was encouraged to sit upright at 45 degrees starting on postoperative day 3 and to ambulate with walking aids at 6 weeks postoperatively due to a concomitant pelvic ring fracture. Hardware removal was performed at 1 year postoperatively after confirmation of bony union.

At the 1-year follow-up, MRI scans showed no degenerative changes in the involved intervertebral discs compared with preoperative images.

## Discussion

The usefulness of spino-pelvic fixation for transverse sacral fractures has been reported previously [3,7–9]. Our technique differs from conventional fixation techniques by employing the powerful reduction force of saw-tooth geared growing rods for displaced and angulated sacral fragments. The mean distraction force of the growing rod system is higher and more controllable than manual distraction using hand grip strength. The average hand grip power of men in their thirties is approximately 47 ± 9.7 kg, equivalent to about 460 N [10]. In contrast, the distraction force generated by growing rods can exceed 500 N and is maintained by locking the saw-toothed gear [11].

In this case, the patient’s body weight was approximately 50 kg, and the distraction force was sufficient to lengthen and support the trunk. The reduction mechanism of the growing rod system reverses the gravitational and muscular forces, particularly from the psoas muscle, which otherwise increase sagittal rotation and kyphotic deformity of the proximal sacral fragment [1]. The proximal pedicle screw insertion point at the thoraco-lumbar junction is above the origin of the psoas muscle, thereby satisfying fundamental principles of fracture reduction [12].

Additionally, posterior skin tenting around the fracture site improved after reduction, likely due to tightening of the posterior ilio-sacral ligament complex during rod lengthening. This technique also facilitates restoration of spino-pelvic alignment at the time of surgical stabilization. Intraoperative fluoroscopic assessment is necessary due to the lack of intact cortical bone to guide reduction [1,3,13–15]. Modulation of iliac screw entry points and lordotic bending of distal rods contributed to improved alignment and rotational stability [16].

Importantly, this minimally invasive technique avoids direct exposure of the fracture site, thereby reducing the risk of massive bleeding and cerebrospinal fluid leakage. The saw-tooth geared lengthening device also allows quantitative control of reduction under fluoroscopic guidance. Neurologic safety was ensured with continuous MEP monitoring, and additional exploration would be considered in the event of signal changes.

## Conclusion

This novel surgical technique for spino-pelvic dissociation using growing rods under MEP surveillance may serve as a useful alternative surgical option, particularly for patients without neurologic deficit.
