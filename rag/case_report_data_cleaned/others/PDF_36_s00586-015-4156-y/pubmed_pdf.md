# Unusual Presentation of Vertebral Endplate Modic Changes in Congenital Scoliosis Associated with Pain: A Report of Two Cases

## Abstract

Congenital scoliosis is not normally associated with pain in young children. We are presenting two cases of young patients with congenital scoliosis and moderate to severe pain. There were no spinal cord abnormalities found in these patients. Magnetic resonance imaging and computed tomography revealed disc degeneration with Modic changes at the apex of the congenital scoliosis. We hypothesized that the mechanical instability resulting from poor spinal element formation associated with congenital scoliosis was responsible for causing the disc degeneration and endplate changes. Modic changes have been reported to be associated with pain in degenerative conditions of the spine. Both patients underwent posterior spinal fusion and instrumentation, which relieved their pain immediately.

Keywords: Congenital scoliosis; Modic changes; Disc degeneration

## Case Presentation

The first case was a 10-year-old girl presenting with scoliosis and back pain. Her family history was negative for scoliosis. She described daily pain in the upper thoracic area for several months and found that this pain was related to her activities. She also had night pain but no numbness or weakness in her bilateral lower extremities. Physical examination showed a symmetrical waist, no pelvic obliquity, and no midline skin abnormalities. She had a hump on the right upper thoracic spine while performing the Adam forward bending test. There was tenderness on palpation of the upper thoracic spine. Neurological examination was normal.

Radiographic evaluation revealed a right thoracic scoliosis of 30° with a hemivertebra at T7 on the right side. Lateral plain radiographs revealed thoracic kyphosis of 39° and lumbar lordosis of 46°. The remainder of the spine appeared normal both proximally and distally to the involved area. A left-side bending film was used to further evaluate the hemivertebra. Computed tomography imaging showed a fully segmented hemivertebra at T7 with disc space narrowing and sclerosis at the lower endplate of left T6 and the upper endplate of left T8 without endplate destruction. The sclerotic changes were located only on the concave side in an asymmetrical distribution. Magnetic resonance imaging of the entire spine showed no spinal cord abnormalities. Sagittal views demonstrated hypointense signal on T1-weighted images and intermediate signal on T2-weighted images at the lower endplate of left T6 and upper endplate of left T8, representing Modic type 3 changes [1].

The second case was an 8-year-old girl who presented with scoliosis and back pain. She had pain in the upper thoracic area during the day and night for one year, which increased with physical activity. Physical examination revealed a hump on the right upper thoracic spine during the Adam forward bending test and marked tenderness on palpation of the thoracic spine. Neurological examination did not demonstrate any focal deficits. Radiographic evaluation revealed a right thoracic scoliosis of 48° due to a hemivertebra at T11 on the right side. Computed tomography showed a fully segmented hemivertebra at T11 with disc space narrowing and sclerosis of the lower endplate of left T10 and the upper endplate of left T12 without endplate destruction. These sclerotic changes were also located only on the concave side. Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated Modic type 3 endplate changes similar to the first case.

In both cases, a hemivertebrectomy was performed through a posterior approach. The hemivertebrae and degenerative discs were removed. After surgery, thoracic back pain resolved in both patients. The first patient had more than one year of follow-up, and the second patient had more than four years of follow-up, with continued resolution of pain and return to full physical activities.

## Discussion

Modic changes are signal changes of the vertebral endplate observed on magnetic resonance imaging [1–3]. These changes were first described by Modic et al. and classified into three types. Modic type 1 changes are hypointense on T1-weighted sequences and hyperintense on T2-weighted sequences, corresponding pathologically to fissuring of the cartilaginous endplate and increased vascularity within the subchondral bone. Modic type 2 changes are hyperintense on T1-weighted sequences and either hyperintense or intermediate on T2-weighted sequences, reflecting fatty replacement of adjacent bone marrow. Modic type 3 changes are hypointense on both T1- and T2-weighted sequences and represent sclerotic changes within the vertebral bodies.

Modic changes are common in adult degenerative spinal diseases, and most studies have investigated their relationship with chronic low back pain in the lumbar spine. However, no prior investigations have reported Modic changes in congenital scoliosis of the thoracic spine. In this report, we describe Modic type 3 changes in two young girls with congenital thoracic scoliosis. The Modic changes observed in our cases were asymmetrically distributed and occurred only on the concave side of the curve at the apex vertebrae, a pattern similar to that seen in adult degenerative scoliosis [4]. This distribution can be explained by asymmetrical biomechanical loading at the endplates resulting from scoliosis, with compression forces on the concave side and tension forces on the convex side.

Pain is not typically a primary complaint in patients with congenital scoliosis and is not usually a priority when considering operative management because of its rare presentation. In contrast to adult scoliosis, congenital scoliosis is generally not associated with pain. However, in these two cases, relatively minor congenital scoliosis was associated with significant back pain. Pain in scoliosis patients can result from various pathologies, including disc degeneration, facet arthrosis, muscular imbalance, occult syrinx, spinal cord tumors, and neuromuscular disorders [6,7]. Many studies have demonstrated an association between Modic changes and low back pain [1,3,8–10].

We hypothesized that mechanical instability due to poor spinal element formation leads to increased stress in the disc space, resulting in disc degeneration and Modic endplate changes. These Modic changes secondary to instability are likely the cause of pain in patients with congenital scoliosis. Previous studies have suggested that Modic changes reflect an imbalance of biomechanical stress or intervertebral instability [8] and represent a response to degenerative processes involving disc degeneration [9]. Increased intervertebral shear forces resulting from instability in congenital scoliosis may contribute to endplate trauma and promote disc and endplate degeneration [4].

We believe the endplate changes observed in these cases are due to instability from abnormal spinal element formation rather than other etiologies such as infection. One patient had a history of cardiac surgery but no evidence of infection at the time of surgery or during follow-up. Neither patient had constitutional symptoms such as fever, night sweats, weight loss, or severe pain suggestive of discitis. Magnetic resonance imaging did not demonstrate increased signal within the disc space indicative of infection. Although a burned-out infection may not show fluid, discitis was considered unlikely given the clinical history.

In congenital scoliosis, intervertebral discs may be abnormally formed, lacking proper growth plates and normal internal architecture, contributing to deformity progression. Instability resulting from abnormal formation of discs and bony elements can produce endplate reactions similar to Modic changes seen in degenerative disc disease. Although the etiology and initiating factors differ, the radiographic appearance of the endplate changes is similar.

Postoperative follow-up demonstrated complete resolution of pain at one year in the first patient and at four years in the second patient. These cases highlight that painful small curves in congenital scoliosis may arise from instability-induced endplate changes and that stabilization can effectively relieve pain.

## Conclusion

This is the first study reporting asymmetrical Modic type 3 changes on the concave side of the thoracic spine in young girls with congenital scoliosis and pain. Instability resulting in Modic endplate changes appears to be the cause of pain in these patients. Surgical stabilization resolved pain in both cases.
