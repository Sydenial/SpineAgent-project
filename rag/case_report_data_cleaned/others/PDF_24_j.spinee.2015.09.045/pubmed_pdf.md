# Spinal venous hypertension secondary to pelvic extra-spinal arteriovenous fistula – A previously unreported cause of Congestive Myelopathy

## Abstract
A 65-year-old male presented with sub-acute onset of myelopathy with paraparesis and bladder involvement. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) revealed dorsal cord edema with multiple dorsal, extra-medullary flow voids. With a provisional diagnosis of Spinal Dural Arteriovenous Fistula (SDAVF), a spinal angiography was performed, which revealed normal intercostal and lumbar arteries. Right internal iliac angiogram revealed an ectatic internal iliac artery (IA) with a fistulous communication with the internal iliac vein (IIV). There was opacification of the spinal radicular veins across the fistula with reflux into spinal peri-medullary veins. The patient underwent coil embolization of the fistula with complete angiographic occlusion and partial clinical improvement.

## Introduction
Congestive myelopathy is a condition that often arises from vascular malformations, with Spinal Dural Arteriovenous Fistulas (SDAVF) being the most common cause. Other spinal vascular malformations, such as spinal arteriovenous malformations and epidural arteriovenous fistulas, can also demonstrate flow voids on MRI. This case highlights a rare cause of congestive myelopathy due to an extra-spinal arteriovenous fistula between the pelvic internal iliac arteries and veins.

## Methods
### Imaging and Diagnosis
The patient underwent an MRI, which revealed flow voids in the posterior subarachnoid space along with cord edema. Spinal angiography was done to confirm the diagnosis. The right internal iliac artery angiogram showed a fistulous communication between the internal iliac artery and vein, leading to the opacification of the spinal radicular veins. Angiographic imaging demonstrated the reflux of blood into the spinal peri-medullary veins.

### Treatment
The patient underwent coil embolization of the fistula, which resulted in complete angiographic occlusion. Partial clinical improvement was observed following the procedure.

## Discussion
SDAVF is the most common cause of congestive myelopathy and presents with flow voids in the spinal subarachnoid space on MRI. The presence of flow voids is a hallmark of vascular malformations, including both spinal arteriovenous malformations and epidural arteriovenous fistulas. This case describes a previously unreported cause of congestive myelopathy, originating from an extra-spinal arteriovenous fistula between the pelvic internal iliac arteries and veins, which was successfully treated with coil embolization.

## Conclusion
This case emphasizes the need for careful diagnostic imaging when evaluating patients with congestive myelopathy, as it can be caused by uncommon vascular malformations such as the extra-spinal arteriovenous fistula. Prompt diagnosis and treatment, such as coil embolization, can lead to significant clinical improvement.
