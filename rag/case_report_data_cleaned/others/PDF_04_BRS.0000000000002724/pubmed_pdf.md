# Congenital Unilateral Atlanto-Occipital Rotatory Subluxation – Rare Cause of C1 Neuralgia

## Abstract

**Study Design:** Case report that presented with left C1 neuralgia.  

**Summary of Background Data:** Secondary occipital neuralgia is commonly attributed to pathologies of the atlanto-axial joint and C2/C3 nerve involvement. Our case depicts a model of slow creeping atlanto-occipital subluxation due to a rare left C1 superior articular facet dysplasia with C1 foraminal stenosis presenting as C1 neuralgia. We discuss the etiology and pathoanatomy of this rare, previously undescribed presentation.  

**Methods:** A 42-year-old gentleman presented with deteriorating and intractable left occipital headache of 6 months’ duration. The neck disability index (NDI) was 64%. Cervical MRI/CT scan showed unilateral C1 facet dysmorphism with left-sided C1 foraminal bony compression. There was no central canal stenosis.  

**Results:** Posterior left C1 arch excision and decompression of the C1 foramen with occipito-cervical fusion relieved the C1 neuralgia.  

**Conclusion:** This case depicts a model of slow creeping deformation due to left C1 superior articular facet dysplasia. An abnormal facet slope allowed the occipital condyle to migrate posteriorly and medially, leading to crowding of the left C1 foramen. Although the etiology was congenital, neck spasm was delayed until the fourth decade. Secondary C1 foraminal stenosis led to C1 occipital neuralgia presenting as an intractable headache.

**Keywords:** congenital; craniovertebral junction; foraminal compression; facet subluxation; instability; cervical pain; facet dysplasia; facet dysmorphism; occipital neuralgia; occiput  

**Level of Evidence:** 5

## Introduction

We report a case of congenital unilateral rotatory atlanto-occipital subluxation that presented with left occipital neuralgia. To our knowledge, this has not been reported in the literature. Posterior left C1 arch excision and decompression of the C1 foramen along with occipito-cervical fusion relieved the C1 neuralgia.

## Case Report

A 42-year-old gentleman presented with deteriorating and intractable left occipital headache for 6 months. There was no history of significant trauma. He was misdiagnosed as having migraine and treated with medications and physiotherapy. Sleep was disturbed. There was a left torticollis, and attempted correction induced severe spasm. Slow flexion and extension movements were possible with terminal spasm. The NDI was 64%. C2 dermatomal sensation (greater occipital nerve) was normal. Cranial nerve examination was normal, and there was no myelopathy. Markers for infection (CRP, ESR) and inflammatory spondyloarthropathy (RA, HLA-B27, anti-CCP) were normal.

Open-mouth and lateral radiographs showed normal soft tissue and bony relationships. MRI demonstrated posterior subluxation of the left occipito-cervical joint with left C1 foraminal stenosis. CT scan revealed left C1 superior articular facet dysplasia. The left facet sloped 49° posteriorly and $45^{\circ}$ medially (25° and 15°, respectively, on the right). The left occipital condyle showed mild deformation and had migrated posteromedially, causing C1 foraminal stenosis. The posterior C1 arch was bifid. Degenerative changes were observed around the occiput and dens. Reduced dens-to-left occiput–C1 joint distance on coronal CT images implied rotatory subluxation. Atlanto-axial articulation was normal. Dynamic MRI ruled out sagittal instability.

Posterior C1 arch excision with occipito–C2 fixation was performed. The patient experienced dramatic resolution of neuralgia. At 2-year follow-up, the NDI was 6%, with painless limitation of neck rotation and terminal flexion–extension. He resumed normal activities.

## Discussion

The superior articular facets of the atlas, shaped like a chalice, support the convex occipital condyles. This orientation permits sagittal movements (flexion–extension) while restricting rotation and lateral bending. Impingement of the dens against the basion limits flexion, while locking of the facets limits extension. This case depicts a model of slow creeping deformation due to a rare left C1 superior articular facet dysplasia.

An abnormal facet slope allowed the occipital condyle to migrate posteriorly and medially. Surprisingly, no neck pain ensued initially. Altered weight-bearing vectors produced secondary ossification around the tip of the dens, suggesting chronicity, while the contralateral occiput–C1 and C1–C2 articulations remained normal. C1 foraminal stenosis due to the displaced occipital condyle produced C1 neuralgia. To the authors’ knowledge, unilateral C1 facet dysmorphism has not been reported in the literature.

Although the C1 nerve has no cutaneous distribution, its dorsal ramus supplies sensory innervation to deep somatic tissues in the suboccipital region, including the short muscles of the occipital triangle. The ventral ramus supplies the sternocleidomastoid, trapezius, and occiput–C1 joint. The sinuvertebral nerve of C1 innervates the medial C1–C2 joint, dura, and vertebral artery in conjunction with the sinuvertebral nerves of C2 and C3. Hollinshead described the Cruveilhier plexus as a neural interconnection among the upper cervical nerves (sensory branches of C1, C2, and C3). The mechanism by which a C1 root produces occipital headache remains unclear. Although the etiology was congenital, neck spasm was delayed until the fourth decade. The authors hypothesize that C1 radiculopathy occurred through this plexus and presented as occipital neuralgia. Common causes of occipital neuralgia include trauma, myositis, atlas fracture, C1–C2 lateral mass arthritis, and tumors affecting the C2/C3 nerve roots.

Altered left occiput–C1 joint morphology inhibited anatomical reduction. Posterior C1 arch excision relieved C1 root compression. Posterior fixation was extended to C2 because of a hypoplastic left C1 lateral mass.

## Conclusion

This case depicts unilateral superior articular facet dysplasia of C1 with posterior rotatory subluxation causing secondary C1 foraminal stenosis. A rare C1 occipital neuralgia ensued, presenting as an intractable headache. Diagnosis was delayed because of lack of suspicion and subtle radiological signs.

Left C1 foraminal decompression with prophylactic occiput–C2 fixation resolved the left occiput–C1 neuralgia.
