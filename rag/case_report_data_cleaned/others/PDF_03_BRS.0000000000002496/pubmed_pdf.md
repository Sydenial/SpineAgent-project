# MRI characteristics of spinal ependymoma in WHO grade II: a review of 59 cases

## Abstract

**Study Design:** Retrospective multicenter study.

**Objective:** To review MRI data from a large series of surgically proven cases and determine the characteristic imaging features of spinal ependymoma.

**Summary of Background Data:** Common spinal intramedullary tumors are mostly ependymomas and comprise the majority of pathological diagnoses. However, these tumors exhibit a variety of MRI findings.

**Methods:** MRI records were examined for 59 patients who underwent surgery for spinal cord ependymoma and had a pathological diagnosis of cellular ependymoma (WHO grade II). Lesions were evaluated for tumor localization; components (intratumoral or rostral/caudal cysts) or reactive dilatation of the central canal (syringomyelia); evidence of hemorrhage; length of involvement; signal characteristics; gadolinium-enhancement pattern; surrounding cord edema; and the hemorrhage “cap sign.”

**Results:** All cases were isointense or hypointense on T1-weighted MRI, and 55 (93%) were hyperintense on T2-weighted MRI. Tumors were located centrally in all cases. Fifty (85%) showed surrounding cord edema; 36 (61%) had rostral or caudal cysts; 10 (17%) had intratumoral cysts; and 6 (10%) had syringomyelia. Seventeen (29%) had a “cap sign,” a rim of extreme hypointensity around the tumor on T2-weighted images due to hemosiderin deposition. On gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted MRI, all cases were enhanced; 27 (46%), 16 (27%), 11 (19%), and 5 (8%) cases showed homogeneous, heterogeneous, rim, and nodular enhancement, respectively.

**Conclusion:** Hypointense changes on T2-weighted MRI and hemosiderin deposition reflect easy bleeding. Tumors are associated with various types of cysts, and gadolinium-enhancement patterns reflect a variety of intratumor cellular components. In cases in which the whole tumor shows gadolinium enhancement on MRI, a rostral or caudal cyst and a cap sign with hemorrhage are characteristics of grade II classical ependymoma.

**Key Words:** spinal ependymoma, MRI, diagnosis, enhancement, cyst

## Introduction

Spinal ependymomas exhibit a variety of MRI findings. Thus, the purpose of this study was to determine the characteristic imaging features of spinal ependymoma through a review of MRI data from a large series of surgically proven cases.

## Materials and Methods

The subjects were 59 patients (35 males, 24 females) who underwent surgery for spinal cord ependymoma in the Nagoya Spine Group between June 2000 and December 2015. Surgery was performed by five board-certified spine surgeons. Reliable and detailed information was obtained from a comprehensive prospective registry. The average age was 51 years (range 19–77).

The pathological diagnosis in all 59 cases was confirmed by two pathologists at each institution as cellular ependymoma based on surgical biopsy. Based on histology of microsections stained with hematoxylin and eosin, all tumors were WHO grade II (tumors derived from ependymal cells; excluding grade I).

MRI was performed using T1-weighted sequences, T2-weighted sequences, and gadolinium-enhanced contrast (0.1 mmol/kg Gd-DTPA) T1-weighted sequences. All MRI data were evaluated by two board-certified neurosurgeons and two radiologists, who reviewed all images and characterized abnormalities by consensus.

Criteria used to evaluate MRI scans included tumor localization; components (intratumoral or rostral or caudal cysts) or reactive dilatation of the central canal (syringomyelia); evidence of hemorrhage; length of involvement; signal characteristics; gadolinium-enhancement pattern; surrounding cord edema; and hemorrhage “cap sign.” Tumor localization in contrast images was examined to understand the relationship between the tumor and spinal cord and to determine if the tumor was central.

## Results

Of the 59 ependymomas, 28 were in the cervical spine and 34 were in the thoracic spine, including 3 conus lesions. The extent of involvement ranged from 1 to 6 vertebral segments, with a mean of 2.5. All cases showed isointense or hypointense lesions on T1-weighted MRI, and 55 (93%) were hyperintense on T2-weighted MRI.

Tumors were located centrally in all cases; 50 (85%) showed surrounding cord edema; 44 (75%) had a clear boundary; 36 (61%) had rostral or caudal cysts; 10 (17%) had intratumoral cysts; and 6 (10%) had syringomyelia. Seventeen (29%) had a “cap sign,” a rim of extreme hypointensity due to hemosiderin at the poles of the tumor on T2-weighted images; and 4 (7%) had formed a niveau inside the cyst.

On gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted images, all cases were enhanced, and 27 (46%), 16 (27%), 11 (19%), and 5 (8%) cases showed homogeneous, heterogeneous, rim, and nodular enhancement, respectively.

As an illustrative case, a 19-year-old male presented with slight numbness of the upper extremities without neck pain or motor deficit. At onset, MRI showed spinal cord swelling and gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted images showed a homogeneous tumor from T4 to T6. Two years later, the tumor size had increased and cystic components were present on the rostral and caudal sides. Gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted MRI showed a heterogeneous signal. Numbness of the hands worsened, and surgery was performed. The pathological finding was cellular ependymoma of WHO grade II.

## Discussion

Intramedullary spinal cord tumors are most frequently diagnosed as ependymoma; other diagnoses include astrocytoma, hemangioblastoma, and myxopapillary ependymoma. The most common intramedullary spinal cord tumors are grade II ependymomas, comprising about 60% of all such tumors.

Ependymomas are usually well circumscribed and relatively amenable to complete removal, whereas astrocytomas are more infiltrative. Treatment strategy depends on tumor grade and imaging and pathological findings.

Previous studies have reported a high incidence of cysts. Three distinct types of cysts have been identified: intratumoral cysts, rostral or caudal cysts, and syringomyelia. In this series, these three types were present at rates of 17%, 61%, and 10%, respectively. Intratumoral cysts may reflect necrosis and liquefaction within the neoplasm and contain a mixture of protein, old hemorrhage, and necrotic tumor tissue; this heterogeneous composition can lead to variable signal characteristics on MRI. Rostral and caudal cysts do not show contrast enhancement around their borders and are likely related to partial obstruction of the central canal by the tumor.

Spinal ependymomas have a well-known tendency to bleed. In this series, 29% (17/59) of cases had a “cap sign,” a rim of extreme hypointensity on T2-weighted images thought to be secondary to hemorrhage with hemosiderin deposition.

For signal characteristics, we focused on spinal cord swelling, signal intensity, contrast effects, and tumor location for MR-based diagnosis. Almost all intramedullary spinal cord tumors show at least some contrast effect with gadolinium-based contrast agents. In this series, ependymomas were isointense or hypointense on T1-weighted images and relatively hyperintense on T2-weighted images. On contrast-enhanced MRI, these tumors showed strong but variable enhancement patterns. Sequential changes in gadolinium-enhanced MRI in an illustrative case indicated changes in intratumor cellular components.

There are several limitations in this study. First, the number of cases was limited. Second, MRI was performed using various pulse sequences and parameters. However, because grade II tumors are the most frequently diagnosed ependymoma and spinal ependymomas exhibit a variety of MRI findings, summarizing MRI variation may be helpful for better diagnosis and surgical planning based on improved preoperative information.

## Conclusion

Most tumors have clear boundaries and are located in the center of the spinal cord. Hypointense changes on T2-weighted images and hemosiderin deposition reflect easy bleeding. These tumors are associated with various types of cysts, and gadolinium-enhancement patterns reflect a variety of intratumor cellular components. In a case in which the whole tumor shows gadolinium enhancement on MRI, a rostral or caudal cyst and a cap sign with hemorrhage are characteristics of grade II classical ependymoma.
