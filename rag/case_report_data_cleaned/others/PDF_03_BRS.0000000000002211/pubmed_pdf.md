## Study Design
Case Report

## Objective
Cell salvage (ICS) in a neuromuscular scoliosis patient with sickle cell trait (SCT).

## Summary of Background Data
Hemolysis associated with acute renal failure following ICS in patients with SCT has not been previously reported. Sickle cell disease is regarded as a relative contraindication for ICS due to the risk of red blood cell sickling following blood processing, as reported in general surgery.

## Methods
A 14-year-old female with Group I neuromuscular scoliosis underwent a navigated T3-S1 posterior spinal instrumentation fusion. 300 ml of blood collected by ICS was reinfused intraoperatively along with two units of packed red blood cells.

## Results
Post-operatively, the patient had delayed emergence from the general anesthetic, and gross hematuria was observed in the urinary catheter bag. The patient was transferred to the intensive care unit and was treated successfully for hemolysis-induced renal failure.

## Conclusions
Given the potential seriousness of hemolysis-associated acute renal failure, we recommend against the use of ICS in patients with SCT.

## Introduction
Sickle cell trait (SCT) is often associated with electrolyte imbalances and transient hematuria.

## Case Report
A 14-year-old female with neuromuscular scoliosis secondary to perinatal hypoxic ischemic encephalopathy resulting in choreo-athetoid cerebral palsy amenable to posterior spinal instrumentation (PSI) was seen in clinic and consented for surgery. A pre-operative sickle cell screen was positive for sickling hemoglobin. Routine pre-operative bloodwork showed normal blood count and renal function.

A navigated T3-S1 fusion was performed under general anesthesia. Intraoperative blood loss was controlled, and no neuromonitoring concerns were reported. 300 ml of blood was salvaged and transfused intraoperatively along with two units of packed red blood cells.

Postoperatively, the patient had delayed emergence from anesthesia, and gross hematuria was observed in the urinary catheter bag. The patient’s hemoglobin was 6.4 g/dL, hematocrit 20%, platelet count 107x10³/L, with hyperkalemia (6.8 mmol/L) and elevated creatinine (17.6 mg/dL). Urinalysis showed hematuria (6-10 RBC/hpf). She was transferred to the pediatric intensive care unit (PICU). Specific lab tests ruled out a blood transfusion reaction, and kidney ultrasounds showed no abnormalities. The patient was diagnosed with hemolysis-induced renal failure, likely due to the ICS process, which resulted in increased hemolysis of sickled cells. She was successfully discharged on postoperative day seven after treatment for her renal failure.

## Discussion
Neuromuscular scoliosis is a challenging condition to manage surgically, with several factors influencing the complexity of the procedure. Excess free hemoglobin from hemolysis is nephrotoxic and can lead to acute renal failure. The hypoxic environment in the cell saver reservoir may predispose red blood cells to sickling, and reinfusion could precipitate sickle cell crisis. At present, no clinical trials have addressed the safety of ICS use in patients with SCT. Given the risks of hemolysis-induced acute renal failure, we recommend against the use of ICS in patients with SCT.
