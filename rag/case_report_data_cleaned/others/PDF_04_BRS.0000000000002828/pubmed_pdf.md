# Posterior Spinal Fusion in a Scoliotic Patient With an Intraoperative Technical Difficulty

## Abstract

### Study Design
Case report.

### Summary of Background Data
Congenital complete heart block requires pacemaker implantation at birth through thoracotomy, which can result in scoliosis. Corrective surgery in this patient was challenging. Height gain after corrective surgery may potentially cause lead dislodgement. The usage of monopolar electrocautery may interfere with the function of the implanted cardiac device.

### Methods
A 17-year-old boy was referred to our institution for the treatment of right thoracic scoliosis of 70°. He had underlying complete congenital heart block secondary to maternal systemic lupus erythematosus. A pacemaker was implanted through thoracotomy at birth and later changed four times. Posterior spinal fusion (PSF) was performed by two attending surgeons with temporary pacing inserted before surgery. A monopolar electrocautery device was used throughout the procedure.

### Results
The PSF was successfully performed without technical issues or complications. Postoperatively, his permanent pacemaker was functioning normally. Three days later, he was recovering well and was discharged home.

### Conclusion
This case indicates that PSF can be performed successfully with thoughtful anticipation of technical difficulties in a pacemaker-dependent patient with underlying congenital heart block.

## Introduction

Congenital heart block has a prevalence of 1% to 2% in pregnant women with anti–Sjogren's-syndrome–related antigen A antibodies. It carries substantial mortality during the neonatal period and requires pacing. Thoracotomy, which is the standard surgical approach for pacemaker implantation in infants with congenital heart block, can result in scoliosis.

To prevent worsening of scoliosis, posterior spinal fusion (PSF) is recommended in postpubertal patients. The use of monopolar electrocautery in PSF introduces electromagnetic interference (EMI), which can alter or damage pacemaker function. Instant height gain during surgical correction may also cause lead dislodgement. Therefore, this report describes a pacemaker-dependent patient with congenital heart block and right thoracic scoliosis who successfully underwent PSF.

## Case Presentation

A 17-year-old boy with underlying glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency and congenital heart block on pacemaker was referred for surgical correction of right thoracic scoliosis measuring 70°. He was diagnosed with complete congenital heart block at birth and underwent epicardial pacemaker implantation via left thoracotomy. Family history revealed maternal systemic lupus erythematosus. During cardiology follow-up, his pacemaker was replaced four times. The current dual-chamber pacemaker was inserted two years before scoliosis surgery with a remaining battery longevity of eight years.

On clinical examination, a left thoracotomy scar was present. He had right thoracic scoliosis with marked rib cage prominence, waistline asymmetry, lateral shoulder imbalance, and a C7 plumb line deviation of 10 mm to the right. Other examinations were unremarkable.

Cobb angle measurements were 38° from T1–T5 and 70° from T6–T12. The Risser sign was grade 5. Magnetic resonance imaging of the whole spine was normal. Blood tests were normal except for polycythemia. Cardiology evaluation showed good cardiac function with 100% ventricular pacing and 20% atrial pacing. Chest radiography revealed minimal redundancy of pacemaker leads.

Before surgery, a temporary pacemaker was inserted as a preemptive measure against intraoperative lead dislodgement. A magnet was applied to the chest to convert the permanent pacemaker to asynchronous mode at 80 beats per minute, while the temporary pacemaker was set at 60 beats per minute as backup pacing. Surgery was performed by two senior attending surgeons using a monopolar electrocautery device with grounding pads applied to the thighs. Somatosensory-evoked potential monitoring was used throughout the procedure.

There was a 15% drop in blood pressure with a total blood loss of 1654 mL after surgical correction, which responded well to fluid resuscitation and phenylephrine boluses. The patient successfully underwent PSF from T2 to L2 with an intact permanent pacemaker. All pacemaker and lead parameters remained normal. The temporary pacemaker was removed 24 hours later after close observation in the intensive care unit.

Clinical and radiological height gain from T1 to S1 measured 3.6 cm. The patient was discharged home three days later without complications. Postoperatively, he had balanced shoulders, a more symmetrical waistline, and reduced rib hump.

## Discussion

Congenital complete atrioventricular heart block has an incidence of 1 in 15,000 to 20,000 live births and carries a fetal mortality rate of 20%. Pacemaker implantation via thoracotomy is the standard approach in infants, and postoperative scoliosis following thoracotomy has been reported.

Essential factors in perioperative management of pacemaker-dependent patients include pacemaker indication, model, programming, battery longevity, lead type, and functionality. Introduction of EMI from monopolar electrocautery can result in pacemaker inhibition, oversensing, and damage to the pulse generator. Electrocautery-induced asystole in pacemaker-dependent patients during scoliosis surgery has been reported. Bipolar electrocautery, minimal power settings, and strategic positioning of grounding electrodes more than 6 inches away from the pulse generator and leads are recommended.

Placing a magnet over the pulse generator to achieve an asynchronous pacing state can minimize pacemaker inhibition, but access to the pulse generator must be ensured in the prone position. If central venous access is required, care must be taken to avoid lead dislodgement. Somatosensory-evoked potential monitoring is considered safe, whereas transcranial electrical stimulation for motor-evoked potentials may interfere with pacemaker function. Postoperative pacemaker evaluation is mandatory.

Growth-related lead problems are a concern in pacemaker patients undergoing scoliosis surgery, as substantial height gain may result in inadequate lead redundancy and possible dislodgement. Height increment has been proposed to equal Cobb angle correction multiplied by 0.77. Undercorrection using polyaxial screws, reduced screw density, and controlled rod contouring may limit excessive height gain. Temporary pacing should be prepared to mitigate risks associated with intraoperative lead dislodgement.

Hemodynamic changes due to blood loss are another concern. In this patient, the pacemaker base rate was increased to 80 beats per minute to compensate for blood loss and minimize instability. Although bipolar cautery was ineffective for dissection, monopolar cautery was safely used throughout the procedure. A dual attending surgeon approach may reduce operative time and blood loss.

## Conclusion

With thorough perioperative evaluation, meticulous intraoperative management, and careful postoperative monitoring, scoliosis surgery via posterior spinal fusion can be safely performed in pacemaker-dependent patients.

## Key Points

- Posterior spinal fusion in pacemaker-dependent patients with congenital heart block presents technical challenges.
- Electromagnetic interference from monopolar electrocautery can be mitigated through careful planning and intraoperative management.
- Pacemaker lead dislodgement related to instant height gain can be avoided through undercorrection strategies and temporary pacing.
- A dual attending surgeon approach may reduce operative duration and blood loss, minimizing hemodynamic instability.
