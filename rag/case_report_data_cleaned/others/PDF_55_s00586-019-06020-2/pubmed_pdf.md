# Management of a pseudarthrosis with sagittal malalignment in a patient with ochronotic spondyloarthropathy

## Abstract

The case of a 56-year-old female with pseudarthrosis after L4L5 laminectomy and subsequent severe low back pain and sagittal malalignment is presented. The diagnosis of L4L5 pseudarthrosis with associated severe sagittal malalignment in the context of ochronotic spondyloarthropathy was confirmed. Surgical intervention with a posterior-only approach and long-segment pedicle screw construct resulted in satisfactory clinical and radiological outcomes at 2 years.

## Case presentation

A 56-year-old female developed progressive forward imbalance over four years following L4L5 laminectomy for central canal stenosis. She had a history of generalized joint pain and chronic low back pain. X-rays revealed a widely opened L4L5 intervertebral disc space, with major vacuum phenomena and sclerotic margins. CT scans confirmed multiple levels of disc calcification, while MRI showed hypointensity at L4L5, indicating bone marrow oedema. Alkaptonuric ochronosis was confirmed by the presence of homogentisic acid in the urine.

Given the patient's exhaustion from non-operative therapies, a single-stage posterior correction with 360-degree fusion was proposed.

## Diagnostic imaging section

Due to the severe forward imbalance, full standing spine X-rays could not be obtained in a single image, so they were divided into two parts, revealing significant sagittal malalignment with a positive sagittal vertical axis and thoracolumbar kyphosis. MRI showed multiple levels of central intradiscal low signal, consistent with disc calcification, and bone marrow oedema in adjacent vertebrae.

## Historical review, epidemiology, diagnosis, pathology and differential diagnosis

Alkaptonuria is a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by the accumulation of homogentisic acid in tissues, leading to ochronosis. It causes progressive cartilage degeneration, calcification, and eventual breakdown. The pathophysiology includes four stages: inflammatory, early disc calcification, fibrous ankylosis, and bony ankylosis, which may resemble ankylosing spondylitis. The diagnosis is confirmed by the presence of homogentisic acid in the urine.

## Rationale for treatment

There is no specific treatment for ochronosis; management is symptomatic. Surgical options for pseudarthrosis with sagittal malalignment in ochronotic spondyloarthropathy include anterior, posterior, or combined approaches. The posterior-only approach is preferred due to its ability to achieve 360-degree fusion while avoiding the need for an anterior approach, thus reducing operative time and complications.

For the patient in this case, a long-segment pedicle screw construct from T10 to the pelvis with transforaminal insertion of a high cage at L4L5 was performed.

## Operative procedure

Under general anesthesia, the patient was placed in a prone position. A posterior midline incision was made, and subperiosteal exposure from T1 to the sacrum was achieved. Inferior facetectomies were performed, and pedicle screws were placed from T11 to the sacrum. The interspinous ligament was removed and found to be pigmented, confirming ochronosis. Ponte osteotomies were performed at L1L2, L2L3, and L4L5. Disc material at L4L5 was removed, revealing brown-dark pigmentation, consistent with ochronosis.

Two cobalt chrome rods were inserted for spinal correction, and autologous bone graft was used to support the fusion. Histopathological examination confirmed ochronosis.

## Clinical outcome

The patient began walking on day 2 with the help of a walking frame and was discharged on day 6. She showed significant improvement in sagittal alignment. Postoperative X-rays confirmed correct cage placement and correction of thoracolumbar kyphosis. At 2 years, the patient exhibited stable alignment with a successful long-term fusion.
