# A Silver Medal Winner at the 13th World Wushu (武术) Championship 2015, 17 Months After Selective Thoracic Fusion for Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis: A Case Report

## Abstract
Study Design: Case report.

Objective: To report the successful rehabilitation and training progression of an elite high-performance martial arts exponent after selective thoracic fusion for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS).

Summary of Background Data: Posterior spinal fusion (PSF) for AIS results in loss of spinal flexibility. Rehabilitation after PSF for AIS remains controversial, and reports of return to elite athletic performance after PSF are scarce.

Methods: A 25-year-old national Wushu Taolu (exhibition) exponent underwent selective thoracic fusion (T4-T12) using alternate-level pedicle screw placement augmented with autogenous local bone graft in June 2014. She resumed training at 3 months post-surgery with progressive intensity after 6 months. Follow-up to 2 years post-surgery monitored clinical and radiographic outcomes.

Results: Post-fusion training emphasized speed training, core and limb strengthening, and flexibility exercises. At 17 months post-operation, she competed in the 13th World Wushu Championship 2015 and won a silver medal. No instrumentation failure or loss of correction was observed at 2-year follow-up.

Conclusion: Return to elite high-performance martial arts is possible after selective thoracic fusion for AIS. Accelerated and intensive training did not lead to instrumentation failure or complications.

Keywords: Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, instrumentation, martial arts, selective thoracic fusion, silver medal, spinal flexibility, sports, Taolu exponent, World Wushu Championship 2015.

## Introduction
Posterior spinal fusion for AIS reduces spinal flexibility, creating challenges for athletes returning to sport. Fabricant et al. identified distal fusion level and Lenke curve type as independent predictors of return to equal or higher athletic activity postoperatively [3]. Rehabilitation protocols after PSF for AIS vary widely. In a survey of 23 deformity surgeons, 76% allowed return to contact sports by 6 months and 77% allowed return to collision sports by 1 year [4]. However, there are no detailed reports describing the rehabilitation process of an elite high-performance athlete (martial arts exponent) after PSF for AIS. We report the training progression of a national Wushu exponent culminating in a silver medal at the 13th World Wushu Championship, 17 months after selective thoracic fusion for AIS [5].

## Case Report
A 25-year-old national Wushu Taolu exponent competed in the "Duilian" (dual demonstrative combat) event. Diagnosed with AIS at age 12, she presented with progressive worsening in early 2014. Preoperative whole-spine erect radiograph showed a Lenke 1BN curve with a 55-degree Cobb angle (T4-T11). She strongly wished to return to international-level competition.

She underwent selective thoracic fusion (upper instrumented vertebra T4, lower instrumented vertebra T12) using alternate-level pedicle screw placement and autogenous local bone graft in June 2014. Computed tomography at 4.5 months post-operation demonstrated fusion at the proximal and distal anchor sites. Training emphasized speed, core, limb strengthening, and flexibility work.

Training timeline and milestones:
- 3 months post-operation: Training resumed; emphasis on low-impact speed work, foundational core activation, supported lower-limb strengthening, balance, and gentle flexibility.
- 6 months onward: Progressive increase in intensity and volume.
- 12 months: Participation in local competitions.
- 17 months: Participation in the 13th World Wushu Championship 2015; won a silver medal.
- 24 months: No loss of correction, implant loosening, or failure on follow-up.

Training components (examples):
- Speed: Walking progressing to jogging.
- Core: Sit-ups, "pushing to the sky," reverse back exercises, reaching for the knees, bridges (with and without fitness ball).
- Limb strength: Squats with back support; squats with a fitness ball.
- Balance: Single-leg balance with squat; opposite arm-leg raise; single-leg balance on a fitness ball with shoulder press.
- Flexibility: Face-to-chest position; fingers-to-toes stretch (sitting).
- Shoulder mobility/others: Latissimus pull; finger climb.

## Discussion
Wushu (武术) integrates striking, sweeping, and throwing techniques with acrobatic elements that demand balance and flexibility. In Taolu, it is an exhibition sport and may be considered mixed contact/collision. The Brazilian Olympic Wushu team demonstrated requirements for substantial flexibility, leg power, lumbar isometric strength, and moderate arm power [6].

Return-to-sport guidance after PSF for AIS is heterogeneous. Rubery and Bradford reported that 60% of surgeons prohibited collision sports and 13% recommended against contact sports after surgery; 20% allowed return to contact sports at 6 months and 61% at 1 year, with distal fusion level influencing decisions for 44% of respondents [7]. Another survey noted instrumentation type and lowest instrumented vertebra as important considerations; some surgeons would not recommend surgery if athletes aimed to return to collision sports in the immediate next season [4].

A widely publicized example is professional golfer Stacy Lewis, who underwent scoliosis surgery at a younger age and returned to peak competition many years later [8]. In contrast, this case shows an accelerated progression to elite performance after selective thoracic fusion, with safe return 17 months post-operation. Selective thoracic fusion preserves lumbar motion segments, helping maintain near-normal lumbar flexibility, which likely facilitates athletic function in sports requiring dynamic spinal motion.

## Conclusion
Return to elite high-performance martial arts competition is achievable after selective thoracic fusion for AIS. In this case, an accelerated, intensive, and structured rehabilitation and training program enabled a silver-medal performance at a world championship without evidence of loss of correction or instrumentation complications at 2-year follow-up.

## Key Points
- Return to elite sports after PSF for AIS is challenging due to reduced spinal flexibility.
- A 25-year-old national Wushu exponent underwent selective thoracic fusion (T4-T12) and began intensive training 3 months post-surgery.
- At 17 months post-surgery she won a silver medal at the 13th World Wushu Championship 2015; at 2 years there was no loss of correction or implant failure.
