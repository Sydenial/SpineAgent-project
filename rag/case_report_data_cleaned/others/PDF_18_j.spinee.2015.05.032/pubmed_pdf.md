# Lumbar Osteochondroma Presented with Low Back Pain

## Abstract

A 24-year-old woman presented to the neurosurgery clinic with lower back pain. Computed tomography scans revealed an osseous mass at L4 with medullary continuity and bony projections (Figure 1). The mass was diagnosed as an osteochondroma by radiology and confirmed by pathology. Spinal osteochondromas are very rare tumors that are usually asymptomatic. However, a symptomatic osteochondroma of the spine has been reported. [1]

## Introduction

Osteochondromas are benign cartilage-capped bony tumors that primarily affect long bones. However, they can also arise in the spine, albeit rarely. Symptoms are uncommon and often occur due to mechanical irritation or compression of adjacent structures. In this case, the osteochondroma was located in the lumbar spine and presented with low back pain, leading to its diagnosis.

## Case Report

### Patient Presentation

A 24-year-old woman presented to the neurosurgery clinic with a history of lower back pain. The pain had been persistent for several weeks and was localized to the lower lumbar region. The patient had no significant past medical history and was otherwise healthy.

### Imaging Findings

Computed tomography (CT) scans revealed an osseous mass at the L4 vertebra, which had medullary continuity and bony projections. These features were consistent with the diagnosis of osteochondroma. The mass was well-defined and appeared to arise from the posterior aspect of the vertebral body. 

### Pathological Confirmation

The diagnosis of osteochondroma was confirmed upon pathological examination. The specimen showed a bony excrescence covered by a cartilage cap, typical of an osteochondroma.

## Discussion

Osteochondromas of the spine are exceedingly rare, with only a few cases reported in the literature. These tumors are usually asymptomatic, but symptoms can occur when the tumor compresses adjacent structures or irritates nerve roots. The patient’s case is notable because it presented with low back pain, which is an uncommon symptom for spinal osteochondromas.

The treatment of symptomatic spinal osteochondromas typically involves surgical excision. In this case, surgical removal of the mass was recommended to alleviate the patient's symptoms.

## Conclusion

Spinal osteochondromas are rare tumors that are typically asymptomatic. However, when symptomatic, they can cause pain and neurological deficits. This case highlights the importance of considering osteochondroma in the differential diagnosis of low back pain, especially in young patients without a clear history of trauma.
