# Type II Klippel-Feil Syndrome with Accompanying Rare Costa Deformity: Rib Fusion

## Case Report

A 17-year-old male patient was admitted to our neurosurgery department with limitation of head and neck movements. His neurological examination was normal. Physical examination revealed short stature, a short neck, a low posterior hairline, and limited neck movements. There was no history of trauma.

Magnetic resonance imaging was performed. The images showed a congenital fusion of the first, second, and third vertebral bodies. In addition, computed tomography was performed. Computed tomography images demonstrated fusion of the second and third ribs, basilar invagination, occipitalization of the atlas, and vertebral anomalies.

Klippel-Feil syndrome (KFS) is a complex syndrome of osseous and visceral anomalies. Its classical clinical triad includes a short neck, a low posterior hairline, and limitation of head and neck movements. Fusion of two or three vertebrae with associated hemivertebrae, occipito-atlantoid fusion, or other cervical spine abnormalities indicates Type II KFS. Cervical ribs are noted for their association with KFS; however, fusion of ribs is rarely reported.

In our case, there was no Chiari malformation associated with tonsillar herniation; therefore, surgical treatment was not considered.
