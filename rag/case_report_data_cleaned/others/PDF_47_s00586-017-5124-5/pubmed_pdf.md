# Paraspinal hibernoma: Grand Round presentation of a rare benign adipocytic tumor  

## Abstract 

We report an uncommon case of paraspinal hibernoma with a T12–L1 foraminal extension and discuss the potential differential diagnoses of paraspinal adipocytic tumors.

### Introduction
A 32-year-old woman consulted our department with a right subscapular and paraspinal mass. There was no associated neurological deficit. The MRI revealed a paraspinal adipocytic tumor with a T12–L1 right foraminal extension. Percutaneous biopsy suggested a diagnosis of hibernoma.

### Materials and Methods
Hibernoma is a rare and benign adipocytic tumor arising from embryologic remnants of brown fat. Specific MRI findings are discussed to differentiate hibernoma from other soft-tissue tumors. A planned marginal resection was undertaken with the final histopathology confirming the diagnosis of hibernoma.

### Results
The patient underwent a planned marginal resection. Histopathology confirmed the diagnosis of hibernoma, and there was no recurrence at 3-year follow-up.

### Conclusion
We discuss a rare case of paraspinal hibernoma with a foraminal component. The patient showed no recurrence at 3-year follow-up, and the tumor was successfully excised.

## Case Presentation 

A 32-year-old woman with no prior medical history presented with a right subscapular and paraspinal mass in January 2013. The mass had been slowly growing over the past few years. The primary motive to consult was anxiety as the mass had become unsightly. There was no associated skin lesion. A deep, subcutaneous, homogeneous and smooth mass was palpated, with no associated neurological deficit.

## Diagnostic Imaging

An initial MRI performed in 2012 showed a homogeneous lesion of 165 mm in the long axis. This paraspinal, intramuscular mass extended from the T12–L1 right intervertebral foramen. The lesion had a fatty signal and was well circumscribed. It contained vascular structures and regular fibrous septae in its inferior portion. An intraforaminal extension to the duramater on the right side was noted with no mass effect or deviation of the dural sac. There was no associated morphological or signal anomaly on the spinal cord. A follow-up MRI showed the stability of the lesion 10 months later.

A percutaneous biopsy with ultrasound guidance was performed under local anesthesia. Histological analysis suggested the diagnosis of hibernoma, ruling out malignancy.

## Procedure 

The patient was positioned in the prone position under general anesthesia. A paraspinal approach on the long axis of the tumor was performed. Following blunt dissection, a brown subfascial mass was exposed with meticulous hemostasis using bipolar cautery. Its vascular pedicle was ligated, and a marginal resection was performed. Histological analysis confirmed the diagnosis of hibernoma. Layered closure was performed, and a closed suction drainage was used. The patient’s recovery was uneventful, and she was discharged 3 days post-surgery.

## Histological Analysis 

Upon microscopic inspection, the final pathological analysis revealed the presence of well-differentiated mature adipose tissue consisting of both white and brown adipocytes, in favor of a hibernoma.

## Outcome and Follow-Up

At a 3-year follow-up, the patient reported no functional complaints and showed no sign of local recurrence. No additional documentation was required.

## Description of the Condition (Epidemiology, Diagnosis, Pathology, and Differential Diagnosis) 

Brown fat, found in newborns, plays a role in non-shivering thermoregulation and represents 4% of their total body weight. It is typically located in superficial regions (posterior neck, interscapular region) or deep regions (peri-carotid, retrosternal, pericardiac, perirenal, and intervertebral) and gradually decreases with age.

Hibernoma is a rare benign adipocytic tumor that arises from embryologic remnants of brown fat. First described by Merkel in 1906, it was later recognized by Gery in 1914 for its similarity to tissues from hibernating animals. It commonly occurs in patients between 20 and 40 years old. The largest cohort study included 170 cases, with no recurrence, metastasis, or death at an average follow-up of 7.7 years.

Most cases present as well-defined, painless, slow-growing masses, although they can suddenly increase in size. They may also present with local heat due to hypervascularity. Hibernomas typically show a homogeneous, heterogeneous, or septated mass on MRI, and MRI is the imaging modality of choice for diagnosis.

MRI findings of hibernoma include:
- A well-defined lesion with fatty content
- Slight hypointensity to subcutaneous fat on T1-weighted images
- Hyperintensity to normal muscle
- Inhomogeneous enhancement after contrast injection

These findings distinguish hibernomas from other soft tissue tumors, such as lipomas and atypical lipomatous tumors.
