# An Acute Abdominal Syndrome Reveals a Postoperative Ilio-Iliac Arteriovenous Fistula: About One Case

## Abstract

Postoperative ilio-iliac arteriovenous fistula is an unusual but recognized complication after lumbar surgery. We report the case of a 74-year-old patient who presented to the emergency department with an intense acute abdominal syndrome, which revealed a postoperative common ilio-iliac arteriovenous fistula five years after a lumbosacral arthrodesis from L3 to S1. The patient was treated with an endovascular arterial stent-graft, with immediate vascular and clinical improvement. Arteriovenous fistula should be considered as a possible etiology of acute abdominal syndrome in patients with a history of lumbar or abdominopelvic surgery.

## Introduction

Acute abdominal syndromes are among the most frequent indications for computed tomography (CT) scan requests by emergency physicians. Etiologies vary depending on patient age. The most frequent causes include cholecystitis, appendicitis, diverticulitis, acute pancreatitis, bowel obstruction, or peritonitis. We report the case of a patient presenting with an acute abdominal syndrome that revealed a postoperative common ilio-iliac arteriovenous fistula.

## Case Report

A 74-year-old patient presented to the emergency department with intense abdominal pain and vomiting that had been worsening over two days. He had moderate obesity (body mass index: 33 kg/m²) and a medical history significant for deep venous thrombosis 15 years earlier, appendectomy during adolescence, and lumbosacral arthrodesis from L3 to S1 performed five years earlier for lumbar arthritis. He was not receiving any daily treatment.

Due to the severity of pain, a triple-phase abdominal CT scan was immediately performed to rule out mesenteric ischemia. On the non-contrast phase, no usual abdominal cause of pain was identified. However, marked dilation of pelvic vessels was observed near the right S1 arthrodesis screw. During the arterial phase, the right iliac vein and perivesical veins demonstrated the same density as the iliac arteries. The two iliac vessels were in close proximity to the right S1 arthrodesis screw, and the common iliac vein was dilated just beneath it, with numerous bilateral pelvic varicosities.

The patient exhibited no symptoms of heart failure, which is the typical presentation of this condition. Furthermore, all clinical, biological, and bacteriological assessments were normal.

The hypothesis of a postoperative common ilio-iliac arteriovenous fistula was confirmed two days later by dedicated CT angiography using a 2 ml/kg injection of Iomeron 400 at a rate of 4 ml/min. The patient was subsequently transferred to cardiac surgery and treated with an endovascular arterial stent-graft, resulting in immediate vascular, clinical, and imaging improvement.

## Discussion

Vascular complications are uncommon but well-documented after lumbar surgery, with an estimated incidence ranging from 0.05% to 0.1% in patients undergoing lumbar disc surgery [1, 2].

Ilio-iliac arteriovenous fistula is most often caused by iatrogenic injury, particularly following lumbar disc surgery [3], laminectomy [2], appendectomy [4], or cholecystectomy [5]. It may also occur after traumatic abdominal injury [6] or spontaneously, such as in the context of an iliac artery aneurysm [7]. In most cases, the right common iliac artery is involved [2].

Clinical discovery may be incidental or prompted by findings such as an abdominal vascular murmur [2], heart failure, dyspnea, palpitations [6], acute abdominal syndrome [5], or lower limb edema [8]. In iatrogenic cases, the interval between surgery and diagnosis of ilio-iliac arteriovenous fistula ranges from one to fifteen years.

Although arteriography remains the reference standard for definitive diagnosis, CT angiography is increasingly used and provides reliable diagnostic information [9].

Treatment options include surgical suture [4], embolization [8], endovascular arterial stent-grafting [10], or combined approaches. Therapeutic results are usually immediate, although recurrence has been reported in some cases.

## Conclusion

Arteriovenous fistula should be recognized as a possible differential diagnosis of acute abdominal syndrome, particularly in patients with a history of lumbar or abdominopelvic surgery. Clinical manifestations are often nonspecific, but available treatments generally provide rapid and effective results.
