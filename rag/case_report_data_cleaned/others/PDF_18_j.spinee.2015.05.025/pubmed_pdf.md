# Diastematomyelia with the Owl Sign (Type I Split Cord Malformation)

## Case Report

A 4-year-old female patient was admitted to our neurosurgery department with complaints of weakness in her left leg, difficulty in walking, and limping while walking. She had a sacral dimple since birth, which had not been previously investigated. Physical examination revealed a sacral dimple, scoliosis, and slight dorsal flexion of the left foot.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the spine was performed. The MR images demonstrated thoracolumbar levoscoliosis. Additionally, syringomyelia was present throughout the T5–T7 vertebral levels, and the spinal cord was tethered with a low-lying conus medullaris at the L4 level. Congenital fusion of the T7–T8 and T12–L1 vertebral bodies was observed. Vertebral anomalies, including hemivertebrae and butterfly vertebrae at the T9–T11 levels, were also identified. The spinal canal was ectatic at the lower thoracic and lumbar levels.

Diastematomyelia with a bony spur extending from the T11 to L2 levels was detected, producing the characteristic “owl sign.” At these levels, the bony spur within the spinal canal divided the spinal cord into two hemicords, consistent with a Type I split cord malformation with two dural sacs.

A T1–T12 laminectomy with removal of the bony spur was performed to repair the diastematomyelia. The dural tubes were subsequently repaired, and a common thecal sac was formed. During surgery, a coccygeal dermal sinus tract not extending into the spinal canal was observed and excised. The patient was discharged uneventfully following the surgery.
