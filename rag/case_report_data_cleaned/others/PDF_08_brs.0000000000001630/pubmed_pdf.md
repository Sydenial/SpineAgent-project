# Fontan Fenestration Closure Prior to Posterior Spinal Fusion in Patients with Single Ventricle Heart Disease

## Abstract

**Study Design:** Case series.  
**Objective:** To describe transcatheter closure of the Fontan fenestration prior to posterior spinal fusion in two children to prevent paradoxical venous air embolism during the operation.  
**Summary of Background Data:** Scoliosis is common among patients with single ventricle congenital heart disease who have undergone the Fontan operation, and spinal surgery can offer physiologic benefit. Venous air embolism is a rare but important complication reported during spinal surgery performed in the prone position. Patients with Fontan circulation can have significant right-to-left shunting via a patent Fontan fenestration, which can increase the risk of paradoxical systemic embolization of entrained venous air.  
**Methods:** We retrospectively reviewed the charts of two patients with single ventricle congenital heart disease who had undergone a fenestrated Fontan operation and subsequently underwent transcatheter fenestration closure prior to spinal fusion.  
**Results:** Two patients with Fontan circulation underwent successful transcatheter fenestration closure using Amplatzer Ductal Occluder II devices. Five to six months after closure, both patients underwent uncomplicated posterior spinal fusion.  
**Conclusions:** Transcatheter closure of the Fontan fenestration prior to spinal fusion in patients with Fontan circulation and scoliosis is a rare but important indication for fenestration closure that warrants emphasis.

**Key Words:** Fontan, scoliosis, fenestration, venous air embolism  
**Level of Evidence:** N/A

## Introduction

Scoliosis is common among patients with congenital heart disease who have undergone the Fontan operation [1]. Venous air embolism (VAE) is a rare complication of spinal surgery performed in the prone position [2]. Patients with Fontan circulation can have significant right-to-left shunting via a Fontan fenestration, which increases the risk of paradoxical systemic embolization of venous air. We report transcatheter fenestration closure prior to posterior spinal fusion in two children with significant scoliosis to prevent paradoxical VAE during surgery.

## Case Report

Two adolescents with single ventricle heart disease who had undergone a fenestrated Fontan operation also developed significant thoracolumbar scoliosis requiring posterior spinal fusion. Both patients underwent cardiac catheterization prior to spinal fusion with closure of the Fontan fenestration. In each case, mixed venous saturation improved and Fontan conduit pressure remained unchanged during temporary fenestration occlusion. Five to six months after catheterization, both patients demonstrated complete fenestration occlusion based on aortic saturation and echocardiographic evaluation. Both subsequently underwent uncomplicated posterior spinal fusion without evidence of venous air embolism.

## Discussion

In these two adolescents with Fontan circulation, closure of the fenestration prior to posterior spinal fusion was performed to eliminate right-to-left shunting and decrease the risk of paradoxical venous air embolism during surgery. This represents an important but uncommon indication for fenestration closure.

Venous air embolism has been reported in more than 20 cases of spine surgery performed in the prone position in patients with structurally normal hearts, many involving pediatric patients [2–12]. Some cases resulted in catastrophic embolic events [2,3,5,7–10,12]. VAE occurs when air from the operative field enters the venous vasculature. During spine surgery, decorticated bone is directly exposed, allowing air to enter venous channels. For air entrainment to occur, a negative pressure gradient must exist between the exposed venous channels and central venous pressure [2,3]. In the prone position, the operative field is elevated above the heart, creating a gravitational gradient that favors air entrainment. Central venous pressure may further decrease during inspiration, significant blood loss, or vasodilation [24].

Patients with single ventricle heart disease undergo staged surgical palliation resulting in Fontan circulation, in which both the superior and inferior vena cavae are directly connected to the pulmonary arteries, typically using an extracardiac conduit or an intra-atrial baffle. A communication between the conduit and the atrium is often created and termed the Fontan fenestration. This fenestration is associated with decreased postoperative morbidity [13] but represents a right-to-left shunt that can allow paradoxical embolization of air in the setting of VAE.

Scoliosis is common among patients with Fontan circulation and may negatively affect Fontan physiology [1,16–18]. Significant complications during scoliosis surgery have been reported in this population, including operative mortality associated with massive bleeding [1,16–18]. Venous air embolism has not been specifically reported in these series.

In patients with Fontan circulation, it is important to eliminate significant right-to-left shunting prior to spine surgery. A patent Fontan fenestration is the most common source of such shunting. Other potential sources include venovenous collaterals or pulmonary arteriovenous malformations. In both of our patients, venovenous collaterals were not observed, and fenestration closure was undertaken. Transcatheter fenestration closure is commonly performed to eliminate symptomatic hypoxemia and to reduce the risk of paradoxical embolization [19–21]. Both patients demonstrated favorable oximetric and hemodynamic responses during temporary occlusion. The Amplatzer Duct Occluder II device was used, consistent with prior reports [20].

## Conclusion

Scoliosis is common in patients with Fontan circulation. Venous air embolism is a rare but potentially catastrophic complication of scoliosis surgery. Right-to-left shunting via a Fontan fenestration may increase the risk of paradoxical embolization of air during spinal surgery in this population. We report successful transcatheter closure of the Fontan fenestration in two patients prior to posterior spinal fusion.
