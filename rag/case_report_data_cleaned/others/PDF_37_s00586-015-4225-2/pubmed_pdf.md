# Lumbar artery injury from which the Adamkiewicz artery originated associated with lumbar spine injury: successfully treated by transcatheter arterial embolization

## Abstract

**Purpose**: Lumbar artery injury associated with lumbar spine injury can lead to shock and life-threatening conditions. The Adamkiewicz artery often bifurcates from a lumbar spine or intercostal artery at the thoracolumbar junction, where spine injury commonly occurs. However, in emergency transcatheter arterial embolization for lumbar artery injury, hemostasis takes priority, and blood supply to the Adamkiewicz artery is not frequently confirmed. The aim of this report is to present a case of lumbar spine injury with lumbar artery injury from which the Adamkiewicz artery bifurcated.

**Methods**: Retrospective description of a case.

**Results**: A 58-year-old man was pinned under a 300-kg steel container that fell on his back. He was transported to our hospital, presenting with hemorrhagic shock. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated L1-2 flexion-distraction injury and left psoas major muscle swelling with extravasation of contrast medium, which suggested lumbar artery injury. Emergency angiography demonstrated bilateral 2nd lumbar artery injury. The Adamkiewicz artery originated from the distal part of the left 2nd lumbar artery. Fortunately, selective angiography of the left 1st lumbar artery showed collateral circulation to the Adamkiewicz artery. Embolization of the bilateral 2nd lumbar artery was performed, and massive hemorrhage was controlled successfully without spinal cord ischemia.

**Conclusions**: Close attention must be paid to lumbar artery injury in the management of patients with lumbar spine injury. Once lumbar artery injury is identified, transcatheter arterial embolization can be the treatment of choice with careful attention to the Adamkiewicz artery.

**Keywords**: Artery, Embolization, Lumbar vertebrae, Retroperitoneal space, Spinal cord

## Introduction

Massive hemorrhage is the most common cause of shock in trauma patients. The retroperitoneal space is one of the major bleeding cavities, where hemorrhage is most frequently associated with pelvic fracture. Lumbar artery injury (LAI) can also lead to upper retroperitoneal hemorrhage, which can be life-threatening. 

Lumbar artery injury associated with lumbar spinal fracture/dislocation is relatively rare. Once retroperitoneal hemorrhage is caused by lumbar artery injury, surgery often fails to identify bleeding sources and can cause further bleeding. Transcatheter arterial embolization (TAE) has been demonstrated as a safe and effective method for immediate control of active extravasation, as well as to prevent future hemorrhage from an injured lumbar artery.

The Adamkiewicz artery (AKA) supplies blood to the anterior spinal artery and often bifurcates from a lumbar or an intercostal artery at the thoracolumbar junction, where spinal injury most commonly occurs. Thus, embolization or ligation of the lumbar artery carries a risk of spinal cord ischemia. However, in emergency TAE for retroperitoneal hemorrhagic patients, hemostasis takes priority, and blood supply to the AKA is not frequently confirmed, which may result in paralysis after treatment.

We describe here a case of lumbar spine injury with lumbar artery injury from which the AKA bifurcated. In this case, the collateral circulation to the AKA was carefully confirmed by selective angiography, and emergency TAE was successfully conducted not only to obtain hemostasis but also to prevent spinal cord ischemia.

## Case Report

A 58-year-old man was pinned under a 300-kg steel container that fell on his back at a parcel delivery service distribution center. He was transported to our hospital, complaining of back pain 40 minutes after the accident. On arrival, he presented with hemorrhagic shock, and a subcutaneous hematoma was noted in the thoracolumbar junction. Neurological findings were normal.

Computed tomography (CT) demonstrated L1-2 type C injury according to the Magerl classification. The left psoas major muscle swelling with extravasation of contrast medium was suggestive of lumbar artery injury. No other lesions related to hemodynamic instability were detected. Red blood cell concentrate (4 units) was transfused, and emergency angiography was performed.

A large amount of contrast medium extravasation was demonstrated from the proximal part of the left 2nd lumbar artery. The AKA originated from the distal part of this artery. Intra-arterial infusion of 1% lidocaine into the proximal part of the left 2nd lumbar artery induced paralysis of both legs, suggesting that embolization of this artery might cause spinal cord ischemia. Accordingly, the proximal part of this artery could not be selected for embolization.

Subsequent selective angiography of the left 1st lumbar artery depicted collateral circulation to the left 2nd lumbar artery without arterial damage. Therefore, embolization of the proximal part of the left 2nd lumbar artery was performed. After embolization, the left 1st lumbar artery was confirmed to maintain patency of the AKA. Selective angiography of the right 2nd lumbar artery showed multiple extravasations. After embolization, circulation stabilized without the need for additional blood transfusions, and no paralysis occurred.

On the 7th day after injury, when the general condition had stabilized, L2 spine-shortening osteotomy was conducted using the T12–L3 pedicle screw and rod system. The patient had a favorable postoperative course and was referred for rehabilitation 2 weeks after surgery. On a follow-up examination 2 years after surgery, there were no subjective symptoms of back pain, and no abnormal neurological findings. Plain X-rays revealed complete bone union.

## Discussion

Lumbar artery injury is rare and can be caused by penetrating, blunt trauma, or iatrogenic injury. Lumbar arteries are less mobile, making them vulnerable to laceration by the mobile aorta in high-energy trauma. LAI associated with spinal fracture/dislocation can be life-threatening when it causes massive hemorrhage. Since the retroperitoneal space is abundant in blood vessels, surgical hemostasis is very invasive and difficult, especially in cases of severe hemorrhage with coagulopathy. Therefore, TAE is the treatment of choice for lumbar artery injury.

The AKA typically bifurcates from one of the T8–L2 intercostal or lumbar arteries on the left side and nourishes the lower part of the spinal cord. Since spinal injuries frequently occur at the thoracolumbar junction, the intercostal or lumbar arteries from which the AKA bifurcates can be injured. Once the artery from which the AKA originates is embolized at the proximal part of the bifurcation, severe paraplegia may result. Therefore, before embolization of these vessels, the relationship between the intercostal or lumbar arteries and the AKA should be carefully confirmed.

In the present case, the main source of hemorrhage was the retroperitoneal space, as indicated by contrast-enhanced CT, and angiography confirmed that the bilateral 2nd lumbar arteries were the source of bleeding. Selective angiography revealed collateral circulation to the AKA from the left 1st lumbar artery, allowing the embolization of the left 2nd lumbar artery without affecting the AKA. Massive hemorrhage was successfully controlled.

## Conclusions

Close attention must be paid to LAI in the management of patients with lumbar spine injury. Once LAI is detected, TAE can be the treatment of choice with careful attention to the AKA. If the AKA bifurcates from the injured lumbar artery, selective angiography of the adjoining lumbar arteries to evaluate collateral circulation before performing TAE should be carried out to maintain blood supply to the AKA.
