# Posterior Arch Defect of the Atlas in a Patient with Acute Trauma

## Case Report

A 30-year-old man was admitted to the emergency department with head and neck trauma after a low-velocity road traffic accident. The patient complained of neck pain. Physical and neurological examinations, as well as plain radiographs of the head and cervical spine, revealed no abnormalities. Computed tomography demonstrated a posterior arch defect of the atlas, a condition encountered in approximately 3–5% of the population [1]. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed no evidence of soft tissue injury. The patient was treated with conservative medical management, and the neck pain resolved within three days.
