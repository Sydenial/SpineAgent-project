## Introduction

Desmoid-type fibromatosis is a rare, benign fibroblastic tumor that can occur in various anatomical locations. It is characterized by infiltrative growth, a tendency for local recurrence, and a lack of metastatic potential. This case discusses thoracic paraspinal desmoid-type fibromatosis presenting with low back pain, and its imaging characteristics, diagnostic process, and treatment options.

## Methods

A 53-year-old female presented to our hospital with complaints of lower back pain and a nonmobile, palpable mass in the middle of the posterior low thoracic region. Her history revealed a 12-month history of a slowly growing mass. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the thoracic spine demonstrated a well-defined mass between the T9 and L1 vertebrae levels, originating from the trapezius muscle in proximity to the thoracic spinous processes. The lesion appeared heterogeneous, hyperintense on T2-weighted images, and hypointense on T1-weighted images. On fat-suppressed T2-weighted images, the lesion showed a heterogeneous hyperintense signal. After gadolinium administration, a heterogeneous strong enhancement of the mass was observed. A fine-needle aspiration biopsy was performed, and the tumor was pathologically diagnosed as desmoid-type fibromatosis.

## Results

After surgical treatment, the patient reported relief from her lower back pain. Desmoid-type fibromatosis is a benign tumor, and its primary treatment consists of wide resection with negative resection margins. Imaging plays a critical role in diagnosing desmoid-type fibromatosis and in pre-surgical planning.

## Discussion

Desmoid-type fibromatosis, while benign, can cause significant symptoms due to its location and mass effect. In this case, the patient’s imaging findings and biopsy confirmed the diagnosis. The role of MRI in identifying the lesion's characteristics and aiding in surgical planning is essential. Complete surgical resection remains the primary treatment approach for desmoid-type fibromatosis, though recurrence can occur, requiring long-term follow-up.

## Conclusion

Thoracic paraspinal desmoid-type fibromatosis should be considered in patients presenting with lower back pain and a palpable mass. MRI is a valuable diagnostic tool that aids in the assessment of the lesion’s characteristics, which is essential for surgical planning. Early diagnosis and wide surgical resection with negative margins lead to favorable outcomes.
