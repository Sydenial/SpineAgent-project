# Invasive Intramedullary Melanotic Schwannoma: Case Report and Review of the Literature

## Abstract

**Purpose**: Melanotic schwannoma (MS) is rare, accounting for less than 1% of primary peripheral nerve sheath tumors, and most often occurs in the paraspinal nerve roots. Intramedullary MS is exceedingly rare, with only nine cases reported in the literature.  
**Methods and Results**: We present a 47-year-old male, who underwent excision of a thoracic intraspinal space-occupying lesion 6 years ago, as the 10th known case of intradural intramedullary MS to date. This case displayed a more invasive growth pattern compared to previously reported cases. The diagnosis, clinicopathologic features, treatment, and prognosis of intramedullary MS are reviewed.  
**Conclusions**: The behavior of intramedullary MS is unpredictable and can lead to an aggressive clinical course, including recurrence and metastasis.

**Keywords**: Intramedullary, Melanotic, Invasive, Schwannoma

## Introduction

Melanotic schwannoma (MS) is a rare tumor, accounting for less than 1% of primary peripheral nerve sheath tumors. It was first identified in 1932 by Millar, who described it as a "malignant melanotic tumor of the ganglion cells arising from the thoracic sympathetic ganglion." Since then, literature on this topic has been mainly restricted to single case reports or small case series, with fewer than 200 cases, most commonly occurring in the paraspinal nerve roots. Intramedullary MS is even rarer, with only nine cases reported. This report presents the 10th known case of intradural intramedullary MS, and to the best of our knowledge, this is the second reported case of Asian ethnicity.

## Case Report

### Presentation and History

The patient, a 47-year-old male, presented with back pain, zonesthesia below the papillary level, and left-leg weakness, which progressively worsened over a period of 14 months. The pain was particularly severe when lying down and turning over. The patient did not report any other neurological symptoms such as lower limb numbness, hemianesthesia, or bladder or anal sphincter dysfunction.

### Physical Examination

The physical examination showed mild (grade 3 strength) weakness and hypermyotonia in the left leg, along with hyperactivity of tendon reflexes, ankle clonus, and a positive Babinski sign. Temperature and pain sensation were reduced below the T4 level bilaterally. Neurological examination of the right leg, upper limbs, and cranial nerves was unremarkable, and no pigmentation of the skin was noted.

### Radiography and Initial Diagnosis

A magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan of the thoracic spine revealed an enhancing, oval-shaped, space-occupying mass measuring $1.8 \times 2.1 \times 4.4$ cm at the T4-T5 level. Spinal cord edema and syringomyelia above and below the mass were evident, with significant T2 hyperintensity. Initially, schwannoma or meningioma was considered as the likely diagnosis.

### Surgery

The patient underwent T4–T6 laminectomies for better exposure and protection of the spinal cord. The dural sac was swollen, and the dura mater was thinned posteriorly, where the lesion was intradural. Upon opening the dura, a large, dark brown intramedullary lesion reminiscent of melanoma was found. The lesion had extensively invaded the spinal cord and was associated with intense adhesion and indistinct borders. Microsurgical resection was performed, though some small tumor spots were left behind due to their inability to be separated from the spinal cord.

### Pathology

Histopathological examination revealed heavily pigmented spindle cells arranged in fascicular or swirling patterns with notable vascular tissue, consistent with melanotic schwannoma. Immunohistochemistry was positive for Human Melanoma Black-45 (HMB-45), melan-A, Ki-67 (approximately 10%), and S-100. Electron microscopy revealed melanosomes in the cytoplasm of tumor cells and continuous basal lamina. The final diagnosis was intramedullary melanotic schwannoma.

### Post-operative Course and Follow-up

Immediately after surgery, the patient had worsened muscle strength in the lower limbs, with bilateral dysesthesias. Physical examination showed grade 1 strength on the left and grade 0 on the right. There were no issues with urination or defecation. Over the following weeks, muscle strength and sensation improved. Three months later, the patient exhibited partial recovery, though he remained unable to walk. One year after surgery, the patient could walk slowly with a walker.

Six years post-operatively, the patient experienced a recurrence with a new, larger mass at the previous surgical site, which was confirmed by MRI. The patient and his family refused reoperation, and the patient currently lives with paraplegia and fecal and urine incontinence.

## Discussion

MS is a rare pathologic variant of schwannoma, comprising melanin-producing cells and ultrastructural features of schwann cells. It accounts for less than 1% of primary peripheral nerve sheath tumors. MS can be sporadic or associated with the Carney complex, which includes myxomas, skin pigmentation, and endocrine overactivity. However, MS has not been linked to neurofibromatosis type 1 or 2. This patient presented with a sporadic case, as there was no evidence of Carney complex or a family history.

MS typically presents at a younger age than conventional schwannoma and lacks gender predilection. While MS is usually benign, approximately 10-15% of cases show malignant behavior. Although MS can occur in various organs, it most commonly occurs in the paraspinal nerve roots and gastrointestinal tract. Intramedullary MS is extremely rare, with only nine cases previously reported. 

Various hypotheses have been proposed to explain the occurrence of MS in the intramedullary space. Some suggest that it results from disordered migration of pluripotent neural crest cells during neural tube closure.

MRI is important for diagnosis, with melanotic lesions appearing hyperintense on T1-weighted images and hypointense on T2-weighted images due to the paramagnetic effect of melanin. However, MRI appearance can vary depending on melanin content.

## Conclusions

Intramedullary melanotic schwannomas are exceedingly rare. This report presents the 10th known case, which demonstrates a more invasive growth pattern than previously reported cases. These tumors have unpredictable behavior and can lead to recurrence and metastasis, despite being malignant in only 10-15% of cases. Microsurgical gross total resection is the treatment of choice, and patients may benefit from this approach.
