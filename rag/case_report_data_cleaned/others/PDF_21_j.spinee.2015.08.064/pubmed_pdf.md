# Osteoid Osteoma Presenting as Thoracic Scoliosis

## Abstract

**Background Context:** Osteoid osteoma of the thoracic spine is relatively uncommon and is often difficult to diagnose, especially when patients do not complain of pain.  
**Purpose:** This study aims to describe an unusual case of scoliosis caused by osteoid osteoma of the thoracic spine that was challenging to diagnose.  
**Study Design/Setting:** A case report of a 12-year-old girl who presented with scoliosis caused by osteoid osteoma of the thoracic spine without apparent pain was carried out.  
**Methods:** Diagnosis of the lesion was made using computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging, as well as the Scoliosis Research Society-22 (SRS-22) patient-based questionnaire.  
**Results:** A preoperative CT myelogram revealed a mass lesion in the lamina of the 10th thoracic vertebra that was considered to be osteoid osteoma. This diagnosis was histologically confirmed following tumor excision. The patient’s spinal deformity and SRS-22 scores were both improved at 5 months postoperatively.  
**Conclusions:** Osteoid osteoma of the thoracic spine may present as non-painful scoliosis. Tumor resection is effective. Clinicians should bear this uncommon lesion in mind during recommended CT examination before scoliosis surgery.

**Keywords:** Osteoid osteoma; Scoliosis; Spinal deformity; Tumor excision; Vertebral rotation; CT myelogram

## Introduction

Osteoid osteoma was first described by Jaffe in 1935 [1]. It is a benign reactive bone lesion that most frequently occurs in male adolescents between 10 and 20 years of age [2,3]. The predominant cause of painful scoliosis in adolescents is osteoid osteoma of the spine [4]. Approximately 10%–25% of osteoid osteoma cases occur in the spine [5–9]: 60% of tumors are found in the lumbar spine, 27% in the cervical spine, 12% in the thoracic spine, and 2% in the sacrum. There is a very strong correlation between the neoplasm and scoliosis because two-thirds of cases manifest as painful scoliosis [10]. However, osteoid osteoma is commonly missed in a large, albeit undetermined, number of patients [11] because the lesion is often recognized only after months or years of ineffective bracing [12].

Osteoid osteoma of the thoracic spine is relatively uncommon and is often difficult to diagnose, especially when the patient does not complain of pain. We herein describe the clinical outcome of a girl who presented with scoliosis caused by osteoid osteoma of the thoracic spine that was detected by preoperative computed tomography (CT).

## Case Report

A 12-year-old girl was suspected to have scoliosis in a routine school health screening in May 2013. Because her condition worsened despite the use of a brace, she visited our institution for scoliosis correction surgery in April 2014. Her height was 154 cm and her weight was 40 kg at presentation. She had pervasive developmental disorders along with an expressive language impediment, although her intelligence quotient score was average. She had no family history of scoliosis and complained of no additional symptoms, including back pain.

Physical examination revealed right shoulder elevation. Apical trunk rotation, as determined by a scoliometer, was 19°. No neurologic deficits were observed. Whole-spine radiographs revealed a Lenke type 1AN curve with a Cobb angle of 45° from T5 to L2 (main thoracic) and 28° from L2 to L5 (lumbar). Risser grade was 2.

Magnetic resonance imaging of the whole spine did not reveal any evidence of Arnold–Chiari malformation, syringomyelia, or dural ectasia. As posterior correction surgery for scoliosis was planned, a CT examination was performed after myelography. This demonstrated a mass lesion in the lamina of the 10th thoracic vertebra with a visible nidus. Re-examination of the MRI results verified this finding.

The patient’s preoperative SRS-22 scores for function, pain, self-image, mental health, and subtotal were 4.5, 5.0, 2.4, 4.0, and 3.9, respectively. Based on these findings, a revised diagnosis of osteoid osteoma was made, and the lesion was surgically excised. Histologic examination confirmed the diagnosis.

Postoperative brace therapy improved the patient’s spinal deformity. Shoulder imbalance was diminished, and apical trunk rotation improved to 9° at 5 months after surgery. At 5 months postoperatively, the Cobb angle from T5 to L2 improved to 30°. SRS-22 scores for function, pain, self-image, mental health, subtotal, satisfaction, and total improved to 4.8, 5.0, 3.4, 4.6, 4.5, 4.0, and 4.4, respectively.

The patient and her family provided consent for the data in this case to be published.

## Discussion

Osteoid osteoma is a benign osteoblastic neoplasm characterized by a well-demarcated core of less than 1 cm and a distinctive surrounding zone of reactive bone formation [13]. Although the lesions may occur anywhere in the cortex or medulla of the skeleton, they usually affect the lower extremities. Approximately 10%–25% of cases manifest in the spine [5–9], with a strong tendency to involve the posterior elements, reported in 70%–100% of cases [14–16]. Osteoid osteoma of the thoracic spine is relatively rare, accounting for only 12% of spinal cases [10].

Pain is the primary symptom of both initial and recurrent disease [17], although painless osteoid osteoma has been reported [18]. In the present case, the preoperative SRS-22 questionnaire revealed a pain score of 5.0, highlighting the difficulty of diagnosing osteoid osteoma in the absence of pain.

The lesion in this patient was first identified on CT imaging. Preoperative CT is indispensable for avoiding missed diagnoses of osteoid osteoma. Furthermore, three-dimensional CT revealed that the spinal curvature exhibited less rotation than that seen in typical adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, underscoring the utility of CT before potentially unnecessary scoliosis surgery. Surgical removal of the lesion improved both spinal deformity and patient-reported outcomes. Retrospective examination of preoperative radiographs showed a widened pedicle on the convex side at the apex. Because pedicle width on the concave side is typically narrow in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis due to vertebral rotation, pain-related scoliosis should be considered when vertebral rotation is minimal.

Certain curve patterns are associated with specific underlying conditions. Early-onset scoliosis with a long C curve or left thoracic curve is often associated with Chiari malformation or syringomyelia [19], whereas short, sharp, angular curves are characteristic of neurofibromatosis type 1 [20]. In contrast, scoliosis in Marfan syndrome often shows long kyphosis extending through the thoracolumbar junction [21]. Careful evaluation of curve characteristics is therefore essential for accurate diagnosis. A limitation of this case report is the short follow-up period.

## Conclusions

We encountered an unusual case of osteoid osteoma of the thoracic spine presenting as scoliosis that was difficult to diagnose due to the absence of pain. Tumor excision successfully improved spinal deformity and clinical outcomes. Continued follow-up is needed. Clinicians should consider the possibility of osteoid osteoma before scoliosis surgery, even in patients without apparent pain, and should carefully evaluate CT findings for evidence of tumor involvement.
