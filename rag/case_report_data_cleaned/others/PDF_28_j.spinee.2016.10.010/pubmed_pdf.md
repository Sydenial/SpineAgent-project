# A multicentre cohort study of spinal osteoid osteomas: results of surgical treatment and analysis of local recurrence

## Abstract

### Background Context

Spinal osteoid osteomas are benign primary tumours arising predominantly from the posterior column of the spine. These 'osteoblastic' lesions have traditionally been treated with intralesional excision.

### Purpose

The purpose of this study was to review the treatment and local recurrence rates for symptomatic spinal osteoid osteomas.

### Study Design/Setting

Multicentre ambispective cross-sectional observational cohort study.

### Patient Sample

During the study period, a total of 84 patients (65 male, 19 female) were diagnosed with a spinal osteoid osteoma and received surgical treatment. The mean age at surgery was 21.8 ± 9.0 years (range: 6.7-52.4 years) and the mean follow-up was 62.7 years (13 days-14.5 years).

### Outcome Measures

Local recurrence, perioperative morbidity, and cross-sectional survival.

## Introduction

Osteoid osteomas are rare benign bone tumors representing only 3% of all primary bone tumors. In 10% of the cases, they occur in the spine, with 70-100% located in the posterior elements. The majority of osteoid osteomas occur in the 2nd and 3rd decades of life, with a male predilection of 2–3:1. These tumors are characterized by a nidus of osteoid tissue or even mineralized immature bone, with a highly vascularized stroma, often surrounded by sclerotic reactive bone. The nidus is less than 1.5 cm in diameter, with larger lesions classified as osteoblastomas.

Bone scintigraphy is almost invariably positive and has been advocated for localizing the vertebral level in patients with clinically suspected osteoid osteoma. Subsequent targeted CT is generally regarded as the preferred cross-sectional technique for the demonstration and precise localization of the nidus. Osteoid osteoma characteristically manifests as a low-attenuation nidus with central mineralization and varying degrees of perinidal sclerosis.

Patients typically present with pain, usually at night, which is relieved by salicylates. There may be stiffness in 89% of cases and scoliosis in 60-70% of adolescents, which may become structural. Neurological impairment is rare. Often, these tumors go into remission over 2 to 8 years. Surgical intervention is usually indicated in adults with pain not controlled with medication and in adolescents with a risk of developing a structural scoliotic curve. Surgical options include intralesional curettage, marginal, or en-bloc excision. Minimally invasive options such as radiofrequency thermo-ablation or CT-guided core excision have also been reported.

## Material and Methods

This study was developed and funded under the umbrella of the AOSpine International Knowledge Forum Tumour. AOSpine is a non-profit, international organization of spine care professionals dedicated to delivering knowledge, experience, and evidence to improve patient care and outcomes.

### Database Development

AOSKFT investigators identified centers with experience in the treatment of primary spinal tumors (PSTs) and an established research track record. A feasibility questionnaire was sent to potential sites to gather data on infrastructure, epidemiology, tumor pathology, treatment modalities, and outcomes. Centers treating ≥10 patients per year with multidisciplinary oncology care and prospectively collected data were identified. All centers obtained institutional review board or ethics committee approval prior to study commencement.

A PST database consisting of a secure, web-based application to support data capture (REDCap, Vanderbilt University, Nashville, TN, USA) was created.

### Data Collection

Between 1981 and 2012, data were collected on 1,495 surgically treated patients with specific primary benign and malignant spine tumors performed at spine oncology referral centers from three geographical regions. Cases were only included if they were admitted to one of the participating spine centers with a diagnosis of a PST and received surgical treatment and standardized follow-up. A study coordinator was employed to assist with data collection, data entry, and capture of cross-sectional survival data.

The treating surgeon used the Weinstein-Boriani-Biagini (WBB) classification system to classify the osteoid osteoma. This system incorporates the unique anatomical complexity of the spine, with the goal of identifying a safe surgical corridor for tumor excision that will ensure sparing of the spinal cord without compromising surgical margins.

## Results

### Patient Demographics

A total of 84 patients identified as having received surgical treatment between July 1990 and June 2012. The mean age at surgery was $21.8 \pm 9.0$ years (range: 6.7-52.4 years), with 36 children (0-18 years) and 48 adults (≥19 years). The mean follow-up was 2.7 years (range: 13 days-14.5 years). Most cases appeared in the mobile spine (N=81, 96%), with C2, T9, T11, L2, and L4 being the most affected vertebrae.

### Tumor Characteristics

The mean size of the osteoid osteoma in the anterior-posterior dimension was 1.40 ± 0.86 cm (range: 0.30-7.00 cm), 1.31 ± 0.60 cm (range: 0.20-4.00 cm) in the left-right dimension, and 1.27 ± 0.53 cm (range: 0.30-3.00 cm) in the cephalad-caudal dimension. Using the WBB classification system, 92% involved the pedicles, 23% involved the spinous process, and 25% involved the vertebral body.

### Treatment

Preoperative embolization was performed in 15 (19%) patients. The majority underwent a posterior surgical approach (N=69, 83%), and spinal fixation was not utilized in most patients (N=64, 76%). 

Postoperatively, 10 (12%) patients received an en bloc resection with marginal or wide margins, 2 (2%) patients received en bloc resections with intralesional margins, and 69 (82%) patients were treated by piecemeal resection with intralesional margins. The type of surgery was not assessed in three patients (4%).

### Complications

Five patients experienced complications, with three intra/postoperative complications observed, including one incidental durotomy, one minor issue with postoperative ventilation, and one superficial wound infection. Additionally, three delayed complications occurred: postoperative neuropathic pain, pneumonia, and wound dehiscence.

### Local Recurrence & Mortality

Six (7%) patients experienced a local recurrence, all of which occurred in patients who underwent piecemeal 'intralesional' resection. Four of the six recurrences occurred within two years of surgery. One patient died 2.85 years postoperatively due to an unrelated cause.

## Discussion

This study represents the largest multicenter series of primary symptomatic spinal osteoid osteomas undergoing surgical intervention. A total of 84 patients were surgically treated, with the majority of tumors located in the mobile spine (81/84, 96%). The study demonstrates that piecemeal intralesional resection is effective, with a low recurrence rate. However, en bloc excision may provide a better chance of complete removal of the nidus.

Although osteoid osteomas may be difficult to detect radiologically, bone scans, CT, and MRI can aid in diagnosis and localization. Surgical treatment is preferred when conservative management with anti-inflammatory medications fails.

## Conclusion

Most patients in this series underwent piecemeal intralesional resection for symptomatic osteoid osteomas, with low complication and recurrence rates. While en bloc excision may be considered, complete excision of the nidus is the primary goal.
