# Omovertebral Bone Associated with Sprengel Deformity Presented with Chronic Cervical Pain

## Case Report
A 63-year-old woman presented with chronic cervical pain. Computed tomography showed elevation and rotation of the left scapula, partial fusion of the C–C7 vertebrae, and cervical scoliosis. An abnormal osseous connection between the superomedial border of the scapula and the left lamina of the C6 vertebra was also seen. Imaging findings were compatible with an omovertebral bone associated with Sprengel deformity. Sprengel deformity is the gestational failure of caudal migration of the scapula to its normal position.

## Discussion
The omovertebral bone is an abnormal bony or fibrous connection between the spinous processes, lamina, or transverse processes of the C4–C7 vertebrae and the superomedial border of the scapula. It could be seen in approximately one-third of patients with Sprengel deformity [1]. It might cause local pain and restricted shoulder motion.
