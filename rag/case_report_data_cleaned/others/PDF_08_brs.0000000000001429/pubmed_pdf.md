# Case Presentation of Sagittal Balance

## Abstract

Sagittal balance is an important concept in spinal coronal and kyphotic deformity correction. Sagittal alignment and lumbar lordosis are increasingly recognized and discussed in terms of surgical outcomes. Positive sagittal balance can lead to the development of iatrogenic kyphosis, flatback syndrome, adjacent level degeneration, and junctional kyphosis. This concept is no longer associated only with deformity. Spine surgeons have learned that sagittal alignment and lumbar lordosis are very important even in one-level or two-level interbody fusion procedures, and that they can be attained, maintained, or lost during any procedure, resulting in complications.

## Introduction

Global spinal alignment can be divided into thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis. The sagittal vertical axis ignores cervical alignment and underappreciates the role of pelvic compensation. An acceptable sagittal vertical axis measures less than 5 cm. All components of spinopelvic alignment—pelvic incidence, pelvic tilt, and sacral slope—contribute in some way to overall alignment. Clinical experience and imaging have revealed that an increase in pelvic incidence leads to a compensatory change, specifically an increase in lumbar lordosis, to maintain neutral sagittal alignment and a mechanically efficient posture. The adaptability of the lumbar spine contributes to strong alignment, which must be preserved by the surgeon if possible.

To successfully maintain lumbar lordosis during spine surgery, the surgeon applies the following equation as a goal:

$$
\text{Pelvic incidence} - \text{Lumbar lordosis} < 10
$$

The value attained by this equation is one of the more relevant measures of lumbosacral sagittal balance. Traditionally, complex deformity procedures such as osteotomy and subtraction have been used to correct deformity and restore balance. Once attained, the challenge has been to maintain lordosis over time. For interbody fusion procedures, a focus on the interbody space and the implementation of new technologies to recreate disc space more safely and restore disc height are helping to reduce failure and loss of correction.

## Discussion

Revision surgery remains an ongoing need because too often the patient is left in suboptimal sagittal balance. New technologies that permit implant placement with expansion to create lordosis help to minimize stresses on adjacent levels. Surgeons are learning to avoid past mistakes by applying these technologies while paying attention to upper-level stresses that were previously underappreciated. Biomechanically, implant loading and expansion assist surgeons in maintaining correction and achieving improved fusion quality. Consistent correction is now a viable goal with optimal implant placement and the use of expandable technologies.

How current technologies that have worked well can be modified to further improve lordosis remains an important question. Achieving optimal implant positioning and effectively using new technologies to restore disc space height and attain lordosis are key challenges. The pursuit of these goals in transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion technology represents a new frontier for spine surgeons and researchers.
