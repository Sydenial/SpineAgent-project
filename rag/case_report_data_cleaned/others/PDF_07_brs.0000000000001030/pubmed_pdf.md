# Resection of a Hemivertebra Within the Craniocervical Junction

## Abstract
Hemivertebrae of the cervical spine have been described as a part of more complex congenital malformations, such as Goldenhar syndrome or Klippel-Feil syndrome. This case report discusses the resection of a hemivertebra at the craniocervical junction (axis) in a 42-year-old patient. The procedure involved a combined transoral and posterior approach, leading to a successful outcome with a perfect clinical result and complete correction of the deformity.

## Introduction
Hemivertebrae are frequently seen in individuals experiencing Goldenhar or Klippel-Feil syndrome. The presence of a hemivertebra in the craniocervical junction can lead to significant imbalance, especially in the coronal and sagittal planes, and may result in malposture, local, or radicular pain. This case report details the approach to resecting a hemivertebra at C2, presenting a novel combined anterior and posterior surgical technique.

## Materials and Methods
A 42-year-old female Caucasian patient presented with intractable pain along the right-sided root of C2. Imaging revealed a complex bony malformation, including a bilateral assimilation of the atlas to the occiput and a right-sided hemivertebra at the axis. After confirmation of pain origin, a decompression was performed, followed by C1-C2 fixation/fusion. Despite initial relief, the patient returned with local pain and dissatisfaction with head positioning. A transoral approach was planned for the resection of the hemivertebra due to proximity of the vertebral arteries to the apex of the odontoid process. A second, posterior approach was used for further resection and fixation.

## Results
Postoperative recovery was uneventful. Radiographic imaging showed complete correction of the deformity with perfect alignment in both the coronal and sagittal planes. The patient achieved a satisfactory clinical outcome, with resolution of the trunk shift and head tilt. No complications were observed following the procedure.

## Discussion
Cervical hemivertebrae are rare and often associated with congenital conditions like Goldenhar syndrome and Klippel-Feil syndrome. Traditional resection techniques for hemivertebrae, especially in the thoracic or lumbar spine, may involve an isolated posterior approach. However, in the cervical spine, due to the risk of damaging the vertebral arteries, a combined anterior and posterior approach is recommended. This case highlights the importance of preoperative imaging to assess the vertebral artery course and the benefits of a two-stage surgical approach for safe resection.

## Key Points
- A hemivertebra at the craniocervical junction was safely resected using a combined anterior and posterior approach, resulting in a favorable clinical and radiographic outcome.
- Preoperative computed tomographic scan angiography is crucial to evaluate the course of the vertebral arteries and to facilitate intraoperative decision-making.

## Conclusion
Hemivertebra resection at the craniocervical junction is a viable surgical option for correcting deformities caused by congenital malformations. The combined transoral and posterior approach provides a safe and effective means of achieving deformity correction with excellent clinical outcomes.
