# Intraosseous Hibernoma
A Case Report and Review of the Literature

## Abstract
Hibernomas are lesions of brown fat. Brown fat is typically found in newborn mammals and is rich in mitochondria, thus enabling its role in thermoregulation. It represents a small proportion of adult fat and is distinct from the more common "white fat." Rarely does a hibernoma occur within bone. To the authors' knowledge, five cases in all have been reported in the literature. This case report describes the first intraosseous hibernoma within the lumbar spine and reviews the characteristic imaging and pathology findings.

## Introduction
Hibernomas are rare benign tumors consisting of brown fat, the type of adipose tissue seen in hibernating animals. Although present in greatest abundance in human newborns, its presence steadily decreases thereafter and accounts for less than 1% of total body mass in adult humans. Hibernomas in humans are usually observed in the soft tissue of the neck, axillae, mediastinum, and periaortic perirenal zones. Intraosseous hibernomas are extremely rare.

## Methods
We report the first case to our knowledge of an intraosseous hibernoma occurring within the lumbar spine, along with a review of the literature.

## Results
Characteristic findings from magnetic resonance studies include variable T1W signal relative to skeletal muscle and hyperintense signal on fluid-sensitive imaging. Computed tomography has consistently demonstrated a sclerotic lesion with variable definition. Pathologic findings include sheets of multivacuolated cells with centrally placed nuclei and numerous tiny surrounding cytoplasmic vacuoles overlying bony trabeculae.

## Case Report
A 67-year-old female at a long-term nursing facility with a long-standing history of diabetes and immobilization secondary to a complex psychiatric history presented with a pressure sore. Physical examination revealed a soft tissue sacral decubitus ulcer with overlying warmth and redness and a clinical concern for underlying osteomyelitis. The patient did not report any pain, fever, chills, night sweats, or weight loss. Her medical history was otherwise noncontributory, and she had no history of malignancy.

### Radiological Findings
Magnetic resonance image of the lumbar spine, initially obtained to rule out osteomyelitis, demonstrates diffusely hypointense signal of the entire L3 vertebral body with T1WI and diffusely hyperintense signal with T2WI. Computed tomographic scans obtained just prior to biopsy reveal speckled sclerosis with intervening lucency at the L3 vertebral body, most suggestive of hemangioma. Bone scintigraphy and positron emission tomography were not performed.

### Pathologic Findings
The computed tomography-guided core biopsy of the L3 vertebral body yielded tan-red/tan-brown soft and bony tissue. The biopsy revealed diffusely thickened sclerotic bone with irregular contours. The bone had features of both lamellar and woven bone. Although foci of normal hematopoietic marrow were present within the lesional tissue, the bulk of the marrow was replaced by cells of various appearances. Some foci showed polygonal cells with a finely granular brown cytoplasm, while other foci showed cells with abundant cytoplasm filled with a multivacuolated appearance mimicking lipoblasts. There were no identifiable mitoses and no significant cytologic atypia. Immunohistochemical stains were positive for S-100 and CD68 and negative for epithelial membrane antigen and AE1/AE3, confirming the diagnosis of hibernoma.

## Discussion
Intraosseous hibernoma is an exceedingly rare entity. The limited number of cases in the literature have demonstrated characteristic clinical, radiological, and pathologic findings. Intraosseous hibernomas are most commonly diagnosed in females (6:1) in the fifth and sixth decades of life. The presence of clusters of macrophage-like cells can mimic a storage disease such as Gaucher disease, and vacuolated cells can mimic a range of fatty tumors including lipoma and liposarcoma. The multivacuolated "physaliferous"-like cells can mimic a chordoma or notochordal rest.

## Conclusion
Our review of the literature demonstrates that intraosseous hibernoma is most likely an incidental finding with a predilection for the lower extremities in middle-aged females. The significance of this observation is not yet understood.

## Key Points
- Intraosseous hibernoma comprises brown fat, which plays a role in thermoregulation in mammalian newborns, and represents a small proportion of fat in adults.
- The lesion is benign and without malignant potential.
- It has characteristic clinical, radiological, and pathologic features as described in this report and literature review.
- The lesion has a predilection for the lower extremities in middle-aged females.
