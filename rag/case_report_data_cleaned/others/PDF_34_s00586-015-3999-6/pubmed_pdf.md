# Identification of a novel mutation of the COL2A1 gene in a Chinese family with spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita

## Abstract

**Purpose**: To identify potential disease-causing mutation in the COL2A1 gene in a Chinese family with autosomal dominant spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita (SEDC) and to analyze the phenotype-genotype correlation.  
**Methods**: Complete physical and radiographic examinations of four affected individuals from a SEDC family were conducted. Genomic DNA was isolated from peripheral blood leukocytes. All 54 exons and exon-intron boundaries of the COL2A1 gene were amplified by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and bidirectionally sequenced.  
**Results**: All four affected individuals were found to carry a novel missense mutation of c.2224G>A (p.Gly687Ser) in COL2A1, while normal members of the family and 50 healthy controls did not have this mutation. Protein prediction of the missense mutation by PolyPhen-2, SIFT software, and MutationTaster indicated severe damage to the function.  
**Conclusions**: c.2224G>A (p.Gly687Ser) is a novel mutation of COL2A1 associated with spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita. There is heterogeneity of phenotype for the mutation in members of the pedigree analyzed. Onset becomes earlier and more severe with each successive generation.

**Keywords**: Spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita, COL2A1, Mutation, Phenotype

## Introduction

Spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita (SEDC; OMIM 183900) is an autosomal dominant inherited chondrodysplasia that mainly affects vertebral bodies and proximal epiphyses of the long bones. Patients diagnosed with SEDC can present with various clinical features involving short stature and bone malformation. Spinal deformities like scoliosis and flattened vertebral bodies are common. Hip deformities including coxavara and avascular necrosis-like changes in bilateral femoral epiphyses may be present, sometimes accompanied by hip pain or decreased walking tolerance. In addition, patients can have hearing loss, cleft palate, and ocular anomalies including myopia with retinal detachment.

According to earlier genetic investigations, most cases of SEDC result from mutations sparsely distributed in the 54 exons of the COL2A1 gene. COL2A1, located on chromosome 12q13.11–q13.2, provides key elements for producing the α1 chain of procollagen type II, which is the main structural protein of cartilage and bone. Exons 6–48 encode the core area of type II collagen, composed of a triple-helical domain. To date, 27 different mutations related to SEDC have been identified worldwide, including several novel mutations in Chinese patients.

In the present study, we report our findings of phenotype and mutation in COL2A1 from a three-generation family affected with SEDC.

## Methods

### Subjects

The three-generation family enrolled in this study, of Han nationality, was found in Peace Hospital Attached to Changzhi Medical College, Changzhi, China. Detailed clinical records, including medical history, physical examinations, and laboratory studies, were obtained.

Ethical approval was granted by the Changzhi Medical College Ethics Committee, and the study conformed to the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from the subjects after explaining the nature and possible consequences of the study.

### Genomic DNA Preparation

Genomic DNA was extracted and purified from whole blood using the Maxwell® 16 System (Pomega Corporation, USA). High-concentration genomic DNA (>100 ng/µL) was used directly for PCR after a short centrifugation.

### Mutation Analysis of COL2A1

Genetic analysis was performed by sequencing the COL2A1 gene, as it was the candidate gene linked to inheritable epiphyseal dysplasia. The 54 exons of COL2A1 (Gene registration number in NCBI: MIM#120140) were amplified using primers designed by Primer 5.0 software. PCR was conducted with a 20 µL reaction volume containing 100 ng of DNA template, 2.0 µL of 10× PCR buffer, 1.5 mmol MgCl₂, 1 U Taq DNA polymerase (TaKaRa, Dalian, China), 200 µmol dNTPs, and 200 nmol of each primer. PCR cycling conditions were: 5 minutes at 95°C, followed by 35 cycles of 1 minute at 95°C, 45 seconds at 57°C, 1 minute at 72°C, and a final extension at 72°C for 10 minutes. A 5 µL PCR product was separated on a 2% agarose gel stained with ethidium bromide and sequenced using ABI 3730XL (Applied Biosystems, USA). BioEdit Software was used to compare the sequences with the reference sequence of COL2A1.

## Results

### Case Presentation

The investigated pedigree of the family is shown in Figure 1. The proband was a 16-year-old boy with a shortened trunk, flat face, short neck, and moderate thoracic hyperkyphosis (160 cm height). He presented with waddling gait, but his hearing, vision, and intellectual development were normal, and he did not have a cleft palate. The laboratory examination results were normal, including growth hormone and serum 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3. The MRI of the spine showed epiphyseal dysplasia, ovoid vertebral bodies, and mild scoliosis. X-rays of the pelvis revealed abnormalities of the capital femoral epiphyses, including flattening of the acetabular roof.  

The proband's father had moderate scoliosis, and os odontoideum was found in the cervical spine by X-ray. The proband's uncle did not have scoliosis but showed flattened vertebral bodies. The family members exhibited similar abnormalities, including femoral head epiphyses, flattening of the acetabular roof, and shortening of the femoral neck. The grandmother displayed degenerative changes in the hip joints, such as flattened acetabular roofs, dysplasia of the femoral heads, marginal osteophytes, cystic changes, and joint space narrowing. The spine showed thoracic hyperkyphosis and platyspondyly.

### Mutation Analysis

Sequencing of the COL2A1 gene showed a heterozygous G>A change in exon 32 at nucleotide position c.2224. This nucleotide change resulted in the substitution of serine for a highly conserved glycine at codon 687 (p.Gly687Ser). This mutation was not detected in unaffected family members or 100 healthy control subjects.

The mutation was predicted by SIFT, PolyPhen-2, and MutationTaster to significantly affect protein function, with scores of 0.01 (SIFT), 0.998 (PolyPhen-2), and disease-causing (MutationTaster). The mutation was not found in ESP6500, 1000 Genomes, or in-house exome databases, suggesting it is a rare variant co-segregating with the phenotype in the family.

## Discussion

Mutations in the type II collagen gene are associated with a variety of human disorders, collectively known as type II collagenopathies. These include conditions such as achondrogenesis type II, Kniest dysplasia, Stickler syndrome type II, and spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita (SEDC). Over 200 different mutations in the COL2A1 gene have been described, with mutations typically located within the triple-helical glycine residue of the α1 chains.

In the present study, we identified a novel mutation (c.2224G>A, p.Gly687Ser) in COL2A1, which was not present in unaffected family members or healthy controls. The phenotype in affected individuals varied, with earlier onset and more severe symptoms in successive generations. These findings suggest that the mutation plays a significant role in the progression of SEDC.
