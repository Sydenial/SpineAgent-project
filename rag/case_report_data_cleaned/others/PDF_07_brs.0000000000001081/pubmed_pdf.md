# An Isolated Posterior Spinal Aneurysm Resection in Which Intraoperative Electrophysiological Monitoring Was Successfully Used to Locate the Lesion and to Detect the Possibility of Ischemic Complications

## Abstract

Electrophysiological monitoring was used successfully in the surgical resection of a ruptured spinal artery (SA) aneurysm to locate the lesion and predict ischemic complications. This case report discusses the use of motor evoked potentials (MEPs) during surgery to identify the posterior SA and prevent ischemia.

## Introduction

Aneurysms of spinal arteries (SAs) are rare, with isolated aneurysms found in less than 1/3000 spinal angiograms. The posterior SA is less frequently affected, with only 13 reported cases in the literature. The use of electrophysiological monitoring during surgery for posterior SA aneurysms is scarce.

## Methods

We performed the surgical resection of a dissected posterior SA aneurysm in an elderly patient with spinal subarachnoid hemorrhage, utilizing intraoperative electrophysiological monitoring to assist in lesion localization and predict ischemic complications.

## Results

Intraoperatively, motor evoked potentials (MEPs) decreased by more than 50% when a distal site of the lesion was clipped, confirming it as the posterior SA. The vascular anatomy around the lesion revealed the posterior SA buried in a thick thrombus. Clipping and resection were successfully performed without ischemic complications, and the patient's postoperative recovery was uneventful.

## Conclusion

Intraoperative electrophysiological monitoring can be invaluable when anatomical findings are insufficient to determine the hemodynamic structures. In this case, MEP monitoring helped identify ischemic risk by detecting changes in evoked potentials, preventing ischemic damage to the posterior spinal artery.

## Case Report

A 72-year-old female with no significant medical or family history was admitted with acute back pain. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed T4-T10 spinal subarachnoid hemorrhage. Preoperative spinal angiography suggested an aneurysm or varix at the connection of the T9 radiculopial artery. Due to the inability to differentiate between the posterior spinal artery and vein, we utilized intraoperative electrophysiological monitoring.

Intraoperatively, after laminectomy and facetectomy, the dilated vessel was identified and clipped. The amplitude of MEPs decreased by approximately 50% after clipping the distal site of the lesion, suggesting that the posterior SA was involved. Further investigation of the vascular anatomy confirmed this, and the clip was repositioned to avoid ischemia.

Postoperatively, the patient experienced no neurological deficits, except for a transient urinary disturbance. A follow-up angiogram at three weeks showed no abnormalities, and at six months, all symptoms had resolved.

## Discussion

Posterior spinal arteries have an extensive pial network, and occlusion of these arteries can potentially lead to ischemic complications. However, the risk of ischemia after occluding a posterior SA has never been widely reported. In our case, MEP monitoring suggested that occluding the posterior SA could induce ischemia, which prompted further surgical adjustments to preserve spinal cord function.

Intraoperative electrophysiological monitoring, particularly MEPs, is a sensitive and specific method for detecting potential ischemia during spinal surgeries, including the treatment of spinal vascular disorders such as arteriovenous malformations and aneurysms.

## Key Points

- We encountered an isolated posterior spinal artery aneurysm, an extremely rare condition.
- Electrophysiological monitoring is useful when anatomical findings are inadequate to assess hemodynamic structures.
- Ischemia may be induced by occluding one of the posterior SAs, although the posterior circulation of the spinal cord is typically thought to have ischemic tolerance. MEPs are effective in detecting this ischemic risk.
