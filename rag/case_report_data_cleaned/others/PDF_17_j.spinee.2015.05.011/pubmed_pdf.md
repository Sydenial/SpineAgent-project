## Abstract

A 19-year-old male patient was admitted to our Neurosurgery Department with cervical pain for one year. Neurological examination was normal. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a Chiari 1 malformation, including tonsillar herniation into the foramen magnum, syringomyelia, and hydrocephalus. Additionally, the patient had cervical kyphosis, a rare occurrence with Chiari 1 malformations.

---

## Introduction

Chiari type 1 malformation (CMI) is a condition where the cerebellar tonsils herniate into the foramen magnum. It is associated with a variety of neurological symptoms and is often linked with conditions like syringomyelia and hydrocephalus. In this report, we highlight a rare complication of CMI—cervical kyphosis, observed in a young male patient.

---

## Case Report

### Patient Presentation

A 19-year-old male presented with a one-year history of cervical pain. Upon clinical examination, no significant neurological deficits were noted. Imaging studies, including MRI, were conducted.

### Imaging Findings

The MRI revealed the following:
- Tonsillar herniation into the foramen magnum
- Syringomyelia within the cervical spinal cord
- Hydrocephalus
- Cervical kyphosis, a rare condition associated with Chiari I malformation

## Discussion

Chiari 1 malformation is often associated with a variety of complications, but cervical kyphosis is a rare occurrence. This case highlights the importance of recognizing this rare complication in patients presenting with cervical pain and Chiari I malformation. The presence of syringomyelia and hydrocephalus in this case further complicates the clinical picture.

---

## Conclusion

This case report underscores the need for careful evaluation of cervical spine alignment in patients diagnosed with Chiari type 1 malformation. Although cervical kyphosis is rare in such cases, its presence can influence the management and treatment strategy for these patients.
