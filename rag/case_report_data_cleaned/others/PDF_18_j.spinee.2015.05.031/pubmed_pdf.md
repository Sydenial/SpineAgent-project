# Minimally Invasive Combined Direct Lateral and Posterior Transpedicular Approach for 360° Resection of a Lumbar Aneurysmal Bone Cyst with Spinal Stabilization

## Abstract

A 23-year-old female presented with intractable and progressive low back pain for 6 months despite conservative management. Her physical examination was unremarkable. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of lumbar spine revealed a large expansile lytic lesion with fluid-fluid levels in the L4 vertebral body consistent with an aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC). Computed tomography (CT) demonstrated an expansile osteolytic mass with a thin rim of sclerosis. CT-guided biopsy confirmed the diagnosis of ABC. Given her persistent symptoms and failed course of conservative management, a minimally invasive combined direct lateral and posterior transpedicular approach was used to perform a 360° resection. The patient experienced immediate and dramatic improvement in her back pain post-operatively. She demonstrated no deficits in left thigh sensory or motor function. Post-operative x-rays showed proper placement of hardware. At 2-year follow-up, she had minimal back pain (1/10) and had returned to full-time school and work.

## Introduction

Aneurysmal bone cysts (ABC) are benign, expansile lesions that often involve the spine. The treatment for ABCs is typically surgical, aiming for complete resection while preserving spinal stability. The traditional approach to spinal ABC resection involves a posterior or anterior approach, but these may not always offer the best outcomes, especially in terms of preserving spinal function and minimizing complications.

## Methods

The patient underwent a minimally invasive combined direct lateral and posterior transpedicular approach. This approach allows for 360° resection of the lesion while maintaining spinal stability. The surgery was performed under general anesthesia with the patient in a prone position.

## Results

Post-operatively, the patient showed significant improvement in her symptoms. Her back pain, previously unrelieved by conservative measures, was reduced to minimal levels. The procedure resulted in no motor or sensory deficits, and the hardware placement was confirmed to be correct through post-operative x-rays.

## Discussion

The minimally invasive combined approach for ABC resection offers significant advantages, including reduced tissue disruption, quicker recovery, and less postoperative pain compared to traditional methods. Additionally, this approach allows for a more comprehensive resection of the lesion while maintaining spinal integrity.

## Conclusion

Minimally invasive surgery for the resection of spinal aneurysmal bone cysts is a safe and effective option. This combined approach offers significant benefits, including faster recovery, reduced pain, and a lower risk of complications, while ensuring complete lesion removal and spinal stabilization.
