# Surgical Management of Recurrent Spontaneous Spinal Epidural Hematoma

## Abstract

Spontaneous spinal epidural hematoma (SSEH) is a rare condition that causes spinal cord compression and neurological deficits. The cause of bleeding remains unclear, and recurrent bleeding is rarely reported. We present a case of recurrent SSEH in a patient who had three bleeding episodes and discuss the surgical management of recurrent spontaneous spinal epidural hematoma.

## Case Report

A 39-year-old female presented with sudden back and right upper extremity pain and was referred to our hospital. She had been treated conservatively at the local hospital for similar episodes 19 and 4 months previously. Magnetic resonance (MR) images revealed a spinal epidural hematoma at the C6-T1 level. During the first two episodes, MR images revealed spinal epidural hematomas at the same level. In the third episode, the patient's neurological condition was stable, and we initially managed her conservatively. To identify the cause of the hematoma, surgery was performed 15 days after the third onset. Microscopic examination revealed the development of a venous plexus around the old hematoma in the dorsal epidural space. The patient was discharged without any further neurological deficits, and recurrent bleeding has not occurred for 6 months after surgery.

## Discussion

SSEH typically presents with acute onset pain in the back, occasionally followed by compressive myelopathy. The cervicothoracic region is the most common location for these hematomas, most of which arise dorsal to the spine because the dorsal epidural space is occupied by epidural fat. Previous studies suggest that the source of epidural hematoma is the fragile valveless vertebral venous plexus located within the epidural space, which is unprotected from fluctuations in intrathoracic and intra-abdominal pressure. In our case, the posterior venous plexus was considered the cause of recurrent hematomas based on operative findings. 

Surgery can be performed to relieve compressive myelopathy. Decompressive surgery is most effective when performed within a narrow time window, such as within 8 hours of the onset of weakness. Conservative therapy can be considered in patients with mild neurological deficits or rapid symptom improvement. In our case, the symptoms were mild in the first two episodes, so conservative management was applied. However, we performed surgery in the third episode to identify the source of the hematoma. 

## Key Points

- Recurrent spontaneous spinal epidural hematoma is extremely rare. This is the first report of operative and histological observation of recurrent spontaneous spinal epidural hematoma caused by a posterior venous plexus.
- Recurrent bleeding could occur within a relatively short span.
- In cases of recurrent spontaneous spinal epidural hematoma, surgery might be required to prevent further hematomas.
