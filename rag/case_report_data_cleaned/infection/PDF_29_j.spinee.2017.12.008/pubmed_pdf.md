# Odontoid-sparing transnasal approach for drainage of cranio-cervical epidural abscess; a novel technique and review of the literature

## Abstract

### Background
Surgical approaches to the cranio-vertebral junction (CVJ) are challenging. Available approaches include posterior, trans-oral, endonasal, and anterior extended retropharyngeal approach. Resection of the odontoid process is necessary to gain access to the pathology posterior to it. The resultant cranio-atlanto-axial instability usually necessitates subsequent posterior stabilization.

### Purpose
To describe a new odontoid-sparing approach to the spinal canal at the CVJ. This dens-sparing approach preserves occipito-atlanto-axial stability and avoids the need for occipito-cervical stabilization that adds to the extent of surgery and its associated morbidity and mortality.

### Study Design
Describing a novel technique and reporting two cases.

### Patient Sample
Two patients that presented with infection at the CVJ with a retro-odontoid epidural abscess were operated upon.

### Outcome Measures
- **Self-reported measures**: Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) for neck pain.
- **Physiologic measures**: Plain x-rays (antero-posterior and lateral views), MRI with contrast, CT scan, C Reactive Protein (CRP) and leukocytic count.
- **Functional measures**: Dynamic flexion-extension views of the cervical spine.

## Methods
Two patients were operated upon using a combined endoscopic transnasal-transoral approach for follow-up over a period of 12 months.

## Results
Both patients improved clinically, and a follow-up MRI showed complete resolution of the abscess after three weeks. Culture-sensitivity tests were positive for *Staphylococcus aureus* in one patient. Antibiotic therapy with Clindamycin and flucloxacillin was continued for 12 weeks postoperatively. There were no intraoperative or postoperative complications. There was no need for posterior occipito-cervical stabilization in both cases.

## Conclusion
The odontoid-sparing approach, enhanced by the addition of an endoscopic-assisted supra-dental approach, improved visibility and allowed access to the cranial part of the spinal canal without compromising stability. This novel odontoid-sparing approach showed favorable outcomes in our first two cases with retro-odontoid abscess, although it would likely pose a higher risk in other pathologies, including tumors.

## Introduction
The craniovertebral junction (CVJ) allows large degrees of motion while maintaining stability. An understanding of the complex anatomy and biomechanics of the CVJ is necessary to evaluate and effectively treat various pathological processes that affect this region. The stability of the CVJ is determined by ligaments that constrain movement at the bony articulations involved. The most critical ligaments contributing to CVJ stability are the transverse ligament of the cruciform complex, the alar ligaments, and the tectorial membrane.

Surgical approaches to the CVJ are challenging, with available options including posterior, anterior trans-oral, anterior trans-nasal, and extended retropharyngeal approaches. Resection of the odontoid process is commonly performed to access posterior pathologies. The extent of the surgery and its goals depend on the pathology, anatomy, and surgeon's experience, tailored for each patient.

This report describes a combined endoscopic trans-nasal and trans-oral approach to the spinal canal cranial to the dens, preserving CVJ stability and reporting outcomes in two patients.

## Methods

### Surgical technique
Both operations were performed using a combined endoscopic trans-nasal and trans-oral approach. After preparing and draping the patient, a self-retaining mouth retractor was applied. A 4 mm endoscope (185 cm length with a 30-degree lens) was placed through one nostril, with the other nostril used as a working channel. Mucosal dissection was performed just cranial to the guide wire, and haemostasis was maintained using bipolar diathermy. Following dissection of soft tissue and ligaments, the anterior tubercle and tip of the dens were exposed. The middle part of the anterior atlanto-occipital membrane and the apical ligament were resected. Deeper layers were severely affected by the inflammatory process.

### Postoperative care
Stability of the CVJ was not assessed intra-operatively; however, no complications or deterioration of stability were observed postoperatively. Postoperative flexion-extension views showed no instability. A nasogastric tube was placed for feeding, and the patient was extubated and transferred to the ICU.

## Case 1
An 87-year-old female presented to the emergency room (ER) with fever and progressive weakness of both upper limbs. MRI confirmed spondylodiscitis with a large anterior epidural abscess. An anterior cervical decompression, drainage, debridement, and fusion of C4-6 were performed. Despite initial improvement, she developed sepsis and was transferred to the ICU. Two days later, MRI and CT revealed a retro-odontoid abscess. Drainage through a combined endonasal and trans-oral approach was performed, and there were no postoperative complications. Culture confirmed *Staphylococcus aureus*, and treatment was continued for 12 weeks.

## Case 2
A male with multiple comorbidities presented with meningismus-like symptoms and worsening cervical infection. MRI confirmed a retro-odontoid epidural abscess extending from the clivus to C2. A combined endonasal and trans-oral debridement and drainage were performed. Postoperative recovery was uneventful, and follow-up MRI showed significant improvement.

## Discussion
Pathologies of the CVJ can result in anterior bulbomedullary compression, which requires decompression. The endonasal approach provides direct access to anterior and anterolateral CVJ lesions without the need for mobilizing or retracting critical structures like cranial nerves or the brainstem. Combining trans-oral and endonasal approaches has shown advantages in visualization and reducing morbidity.

Previous studies suggest that the endonasal approach can minimize trauma to retropharyngeal tissues, improving recovery time and reducing complications compared to traditional trans-oral approaches. In this report, both cases demonstrated successful outcomes with minimal morbidity.

## Conclusion
This combined trans-oral and endonasal approach provides a novel technique for accessing the spinal canal without requiring odontoid or anterior arch resection, preserving CVJ stability. It offers a favorable outcome for retro-odontoid abscesses but may pose a higher risk in patients with other pathologies, such as tumors.
