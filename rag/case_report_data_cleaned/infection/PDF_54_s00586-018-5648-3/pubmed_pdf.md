# Staged surgical treatment for infection of total disc arthroplasty: three cases and a narrative review of the literature

## Abstract

Although total disc arthroplasty (TDA) is a common procedure for selected cases of degenerative disc disease, infection of TDA remains a serious and rare complication. We report three cases of TDA infection, with one patient developing cutaneous fistulas. To eradicate the infection, we propose a staged removal of the device, debridement, and stabilization plus fusion. Surgeons face challenges due to major retroperitoneal vessels adherent to the device, surrounding tissue, and complications when removing and replacing the implant. A staged multidisciplinary approach, involving vascular and plastic surgery alongside spine specialists, is crucial for successful outcomes.

**Keywords**: Lumbar, Total disc arthroplasty, Disc prosthesis, Infection, Surgical treatment

## Introduction

Lumbar total disc arthroplasty (TDA) is used for motion-preserving replacement in degenerative disc disease (DDD). Infection of TDA, though rare, is a serious complication, and its management is based on expert opinion. In this paper, we present three cases of TDA infection and discuss treatment recommendations.

## Presentation of cases

Common to all three cases is a latency between the index surgery and the onset of infection-related symptoms. Table 1 summarizes the clinical data. All patients had their initial TDA procedures at other institutions via anterior retroperitoneal approaches (one single-level and two two-level TDAs, all keel-type devices M6-L, Spinal Kinetics). Two patients developed cutaneous abdominal fistulas, which were revised surgically 6 and 8 times, respectively. Preoperative diagnostic imaging included X-rays, CT, MRI scans, and CT angiograms for all patients. Loosening of the TDA device was observed on X-rays and dynamic studies in all three cases. MRI scans revealed paraspinal abscesses, and epidural empyema was absent. CT scans showed bony erosion in one patient. The fistula tract was visualized using contrast media in two cases, while an additional PET-CT scan was used in one case to identify the infected areas.

All patients were treated surgically and with antibiotics after the collection of infectious material for microbiological analysis. Intraoperatively, a double-J stent was placed preoperatively in one case to better identify the ureter.

The procedures were divided into several steps aimed at eliminating the infection, stabilizing the spine, and achieving fusion.

## Rationale for treatment and evidence-based literature

Although TDA is a common treatment for DDD, few reports describe the management of postoperative TDA infection. Two case reports are noteworthy. Spivak et al. (2010) describe a case of a 35-year-old male with a retroperitoneal abscess after two-level TDA. After unsuccessful percutaneous drainage, the patient underwent revision surgery to remove the devices. Similarly, Flouzat-Lachianette et al. (2013) describe a 38-year-old woman who developed a retroperitoneal abscess after TDA, but the infection was managed with surgical debridement and antibiotic therapy.

The scarcity of published management reports led us to review the results of our own series of TDA infections, which comprises three cases. Common to all cases is the late manifestation of infection (9, 21, and 72 months after surgery). Two cases developed cutaneous fistulas, which were strongly indicative of chronic bacterial infection.

Surgical eradication of the infection, including aggressive debridement and implant removal, is crucial for successful healing. Meticulous planning and a multidisciplinary approach involving vascular and plastic surgeons are essential for managing complicated revisions.
