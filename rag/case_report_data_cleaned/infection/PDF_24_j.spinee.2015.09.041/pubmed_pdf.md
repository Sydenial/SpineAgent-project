# Extrinsic Thoracic Spinal Cord Compression Related to Supine Position: From Diagnosis to the Creation of a Spinal Protection Shield

## Abstract

**Background:** Rapidly progressing extrinsic spinal cord compression syndromes are rare, especially when the compression is associated with the supine position.  
**Purpose:** This work presents a case of extrinsic thoracic spinal cord compression related to the supine position and describes the creation of a spinal protection shield.  
**Study Design:** Case report.  
**Patient Sample:** A patient with sequelae of Pott disease who underwent several decompressive and stabilizing surgeries for major kyphoscoliosis subsequently developed extrinsic thoracic spinal cord compression caused by the supine position.  
**Outcome Measures:** Progressive severe paraparesis occurred when the patient was supine. Imaging assessment confirmed dynamic and intermittent compressions triggered by the supine position, facilitated by exposure and vulnerability of the thoracic spinal cord.  
**Methods:** A tailored titanium mesh spinal protection shield and a trapezius flap for spine coverage were implanted. Diagnostic aspects and surgical technique options are presented.  
**Results:** At 6-year follow-up, the patient’s neurologic condition was significantly improved, with no sphincter disorder, persistent spasticity, and lower-limb weakness not affecting full ambulation.  
**Conclusions:** To our knowledge, no other cases of spinal protection shields used for compression caused by the supine position have been reported. Surgical and technical management therefore remains innovative.

## Introduction

Acute or rapidly progressing spinal cord compression syndromes related to posture are rare. They can be difficult to diagnose, especially when neurologic deficits only appear while the patient is in the supine position. The case discussed here was observed in a patient presenting major thoracic kyphoscoliosis sequelae following Pott disease.

The patient had biologically confirmed pulmonary tuberculosis, revealed by extrapulmonary symptoms and complications caused by thoracic Pott disease. Iterative surgery on a vulnerable and deformed spine led to extrinsic spinal cord compression at the T5–T7 apex of a kyphoscoliosis. The diagnosis was challenging, as was the uncommon treatment, which consisted of implantation of a spinal cord protection shield associated with a musculocutaneous flap.

To our knowledge, no similar cases have been reported, particularly involving the addition of a custom-made spinal cord protection shield.

## Case Report

A 27-year-old Coptic priest consulted for a spinal disorder caused by T5–T7 thoracic deformation following Pott disease diagnosed at the age of 13. Progressive paraparesis and neurologic deterioration were observed over several months. Computed tomography scans and plain radiographs demonstrated a 95° kyphotic deformity with an apex at T5–T7.

Corrective surgery was performed through a wide posterolateral T5–T8 laminoarthrectomy. The posterior wedge of the deformity was resected, allowing anterior spinal translocation. An additional posterolateral fusion with T2–T10 transpedicular plate instrumentation was performed to stabilize, but not correct, the global kyphotic deformity. Postoperative evolution was uneventful, and the patient recovered full neurologic function.

Eight years later, instrumentation was removed due to pain. Fusion was confirmed intraoperatively. Six months postoperatively, intermittent neurologic deterioration occurred, identified as position-related paraparesis triggered by the supine and standing positions. Dynamic imaging showed a small residual range of motion at T5–T7, while supine computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging revealed a spontaneous reduction of the apex deformity and severe anterior compression of the exposed spinal cord.

Revision surgery with anterior strut grafts and posterior reinstrumentation was performed to stabilize the kyphosis. Additional cross-links were added to avoid dorsal compression. After rehabilitation, the patient regained full ambulation but retained lower-limb weakness and persistent spasticity. He resumed professional activities and remained stable until 2005.

In 2005, the patient developed a local infection with fistula formation. After treatment and staged removal of posterior instrumentation over three years, he developed a new progressive spinal cord compression syndrome. Neurologic deficits were triggered by scar palpation or the supine position and relieved in lateral decubitus. Imaging confirmed thoracic spinal cord compression at the site of the large laminoarthrectomy, where thin scar tissue directly covered the exposed cord. By this time, the kyphoscoliosis apex had completely fused.

In February 2008, a spinal cord protection shield was implanted via a posterior approach following wide excision of the scar. A custom-shaped cylindrical titanium mesh device was fixed to the posterolateral fusion mass, fully overhanging and protecting the exposed cord from external compression. Soft tissue coverage was achieved using a trapezius flap.

Within one year, neurologic condition significantly improved, with no sphincter disorder and residual spasticity and weakness not affecting ambulation. No delayed healing or infection occurred. At last follow-up in 2014, the spinal shield remained intact with satisfactory functional and aesthetic outcomes.

## Discussion

This report describes a rare case of dynamic extrinsic spinal cord compression triggered by the supine position in a patient with severe thoracic deformity due to Pott disease sequelae. The clinical presentation, diagnostic approach, and surgical management were unusual. Although spinal cord compression syndromes are known in Pott disease, they are typically progressive and related to abscess formation or fixed kyphotic deformity rather than intermittent, posture-dependent mechanisms.

In this case, intermittent neurologic deficits were multifactorial, involving kyphoscoliosis, residual local instability, and significant spinal cord exposure. Supine positioning resulted in compression of the cord between the kyphotic apex and dorsal soft tissues. Extensive posterolateral bony resection had removed protective posterior elements, leaving the cord vulnerable.

Standard imaging was insufficient, and only supine magnetic resonance imaging and myelography revealed the compression mechanism. Dynamic or alternative imaging modalities may be helpful in similar atypical cases. Conventional surgical strategies were inadequate due to the specific mechanism of compression. Posterior spinal cord shielding with a custom-made titanium mesh device provided effective protection and symptom resolution.

Alternative strategies, such as posterior cross-links, were considered but deemed insufficient or potentially harmful in this context. Experimental and limited clinical data suggest that spinal cord shields may reduce extrinsic compression and preserve neurologic integrity.

Soft tissue coverage posed additional challenges due to poor nutritional status, previous infection, and compromised skin. The trapezius musculocutaneous flap provided reliable coverage and healing. Although experience with spinal cord shields remains limited, this case supports their potential usefulness in selected situations.

## Conclusions

This case confirms that spinal cord compression related to the supine position can occur in patients with severe thoracic deformity and spinal cord exposure. Imaging investigations performed in the provoking position are essential to identify the compression mechanism. In selected cases, spinal cord shielding combined with appropriate stabilization and soft tissue coverage may provide effective neurologic protection and favorable long-term outcomes.
