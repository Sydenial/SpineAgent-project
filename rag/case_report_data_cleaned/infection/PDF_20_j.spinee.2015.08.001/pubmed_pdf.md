# Endometrial cancer metastasis mimicking spondylodiscitis and psoas abscess

## Case Report

A 51-year-old female patient was admitted to the hospital with lumbago for 3 weeks. Lumbar vertebral magnetic resonance imaging was performed. Magnetic resonance imaging showed a heterogeneous lesion with peripheral enhancement in the right psoas muscle associated with bone involvement of the lumbar vertebrae. It was interpreted as a psoas abscess associated with spondylodiscitis.

At this time, the patient underwent endometrial biopsy because of vaginal discharge. Histopathologic diagnosis revealed endometrial cancer. Percutaneous drainage of the presumed psoas abscess was performed, and histopathologic examination demonstrated squamous cell cancer metastasis. Consultations with radiation therapy and medical oncology suggested no additional therapy.

## Discussion

Endometrial cancer is the most common gynecologic malignancy in the United States and primarily affects postmenopausal women who are 40 years or older. When metastases develop, they most frequently involve the lungs and liver but very rarely affect skeletal muscle. Radiologically and clinically, these lesions can be mistaken for abscesses, especially in the absence of a known primary tumor.
