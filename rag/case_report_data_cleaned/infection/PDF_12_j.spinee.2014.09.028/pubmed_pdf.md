# Epidural Chronic Inflammatory Mass of the Lumbar Spine in a Human Immunodeficiency Virus Positive Patient With a History of Repeated Epidural Injections

## Case Report

This study reports a rare case of an epidural chronic inflammatory mass of the lumbar spine in a human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)–positive patient with a history of repeated epidural injections. The patient was a 61-year-old man with a 10-year history of low back pain (LBP) and buttock pain, and a 4-year history of HIV infection.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the lumbar spine initially demonstrated degenerative disc bulging, mild spinal stenosis, and hypertrophied ligamentum flavum at multiple lumbar levels. The patient was treated for chronic LBP with epidural injections of triamcinolone and ropivacaine, receiving a total of 12 injections over a 3-year period at a pain clinic.

Three years later, the patient experienced aggravation of back pain and bilateral buttock pain, prompting follow-up lumbar MRI. Imaging revealed compression of the thecal sac caused by an oval-shaped posterior epidural mass measuring 17 × 12 × 9 mm at the L4–L5 level, originating from the ligamentum flavum. The lesion demonstrated heterogeneous hypointense signal intensity on T1-weighted images, heterogeneous hyperintense signal intensity on T2-weighted images, and peripheral rim enhancement. No associated bony abnormalities were identified.

The patient underwent L4 laminectomy with total excision of the epidural mass, which involved the ligamentum flavum and adjacent epidural adipose tissue. Immediately after surgical removal, the patient’s symptoms resolved. Postoperative follow-up MRI confirmed complete removal of the epidural mass.

Histopathological examination revealed chronic inflammation with hemorrhage and fibrous necrosis throughout the specimen, without evidence of neoplastic lesions. There was no histological evidence of mycobacterial or fungal infection. Previous studies have suggested that such pathology may be related to prior surgery, trauma, immune disturbances, or infection [1,2]. To date, however, no reported cases have described an epidural chronic inflammatory mass of the lumbar spine associated with repeated epidural injections in an HIV-infected patient.
