# Successful Non-Surgical Treatment for Highly Unstable Fracture-Subluxation of the Spine Secondary to Myeloma

## Abstract

### Background Context
In multiple myeloma (MM), patients may develop rapidly progressive lytic spinal lesions with poor bone quality. In addition, patients are immunocompromised and therefore at increased risk of deep infection.

### Purpose
The aim was to describe a patient presenting with an unstable fracture-subluxation of the thoracic spine secondary to myeloma, successfully treated with non-surgical management.

### Study Design
Case report.

### Methods
A 74-year-old Caucasian female presented with destructive myelomatous lesions of T9 and T10. Greater than 50% of the T9 vertebral body was involved, with subluxation and translation of T9 on T10 (SINS score 14). There was a single episode of transient paraesthesia of both lower limbs. The patient was in considerable pain, requiring large quantities of opioid analgesia. She was treated non-surgically in a thoracolumbar sacral orthosis for a period of 3 months, with strict bed rest for the first 3 weeks.

### Results
A CT scan at 3 months demonstrated bony fusion and the brace was removed. The patient returned to her normal activities 5 months post-treatment. Her pain and patient-reported outcome scores were significantly improved.

### Conclusions
We present the successful non-surgical management of an unstable myelomatous vertebral fracture without neurological deficit. However, surgical stabilization remains the treatment of choice in unstable vertebral fractures, and spinal surgical opinion should be sought in all cases.

## Keywords
Myeloma; vertebral fractures; non-surgical treatment; unstable fracture

## Objective
Patients with multiple myeloma often develop numerous lytic vertebral lesions. These lesions may rapidly increase in size, eventually resulting in vertebral fractures [1]. Vertebral compression fractures in patients with myeloma are often associated with poor outcomes due to a combination of bone resorption and steroid-induced osteoporosis [4]. Surgical fixation of vertebral fractures in this setting may therefore carry an increased risk of failure [5]. A recent case report suggested vertebral fractures in MM can be successfully managed non-surgically if the myeloma is adequately controlled [6].

We present the case of a patient with an unstable thoracic fracture-subluxation secondary to myeloma, in whom we were concerned that surgical stabilization would fail. This patient was successfully managed non-surgically and returned to her pre-fracture level of function. We discuss our management and the rationale behind our choice of treatment.

## Clinical Presentation
A 74-year-old Caucasian female was referred to our specialist spinal myeloma unit in May 2015 with an unstable fracture-subluxation of T9/T10. She had been diagnosed with multiple myeloma (IgG lambda subtype) 15 months prior to presentation and had commenced chemotherapy.

The patient initially presented to her local hospital in February 2015 with severe thoracic back pain. Magnetic resonance imaging of her spine demonstrated myelomatous infiltration of the vertebra with extension of soft tissue tumor into the spinal canal, with associated spinal cord impingement but no neurological deficit. She was treated with high-dose steroids, bortezomib, and melphalan, but did not receive radiotherapy.

Over the next two months, the patient’s paraprotein levels improved but her back pain significantly deteriorated. By April 2015, she required 60 mg of slow-release morphine twice daily. Repeat MRI demonstrated a fracture-subluxation of T9/T10 with no residual soft tissue in the spinal canal. The patient experienced a single transient episode of numbness and paraesthesia in both lower limbs, but no persistent neurological deficit.

Computed tomography demonstrated a fracture-subluxation of T9/T10. Her spinal instability neoplastic score was 14, indicating an unstable fracture requiring stabilization. At presentation, her EuroQol-5 Dimension score was 0.56, Oswestry Disability Index was 32, and Roland Morris Disability score was 12. Prior to her diagnosis of myeloma, she was independently mobile.

## Treatment
The patient had no persistent neurological deficit and had recently completed high-dose chemotherapy and a prolonged course of steroids. Given concerns regarding fixation failure and poor wound healing, non-surgical management was selected.

She was admitted to a spinal ward and immobilized in a clam-shell thoracolumbar sacral orthosis. Strict bed rest with log-rolling precautions was instituted. Prophylactic low-molecular-weight heparin and bisphosphonates were commenced. A repeat CT scan after 3 weeks of immobilization showed no further displacement, and the patient was allowed to mobilize while wearing the orthosis.

## Outcome
A repeat CT scan at 3 months demonstrated satisfactory evidence of bony fusion, and the orthosis was removed. At 5 months follow-up, the patient had returned to her normal activities with minimal pain and reduced analgesic requirements. Imaging demonstrated fusion between T9 and T10 with extra-osseous bone formation contributing to stability.

## Discussion
The spinal instability neoplastic score has been shown to reliably assess stability of pathological vertebral fractures. In this case, the score of 14 indicated an unstable fracture, for which surgical stabilization is often recommended [7]. However, fixation in lytic and osteoporotic bone is challenging, and this patient was immunocompromised following chemotherapy and steroid treatment, increasing the risk of wound complications and infection.

Minimally invasive spinal stabilization was considered, but concerns regarding screw purchase in poor quality bone led to the decision for non-operative management. Recent guidelines suggest that unstable pathological fractures in myeloma may be considered for treatment with bracing if the myeloma is well controlled [2]. In this case, cement augmentation was not suitable due to fracture configuration.

Close liaison with hematology ensured adequate disease control throughout treatment. By 3 months, sufficient bony fusion had occurred to allow removal of the orthosis, and by 5 months the patient had returned to full function.

## Conclusions
We present a case of spinal myeloma with an unstable thoracic fracture-subluxation successfully managed non-surgically. Further studies are required to determine the role of non-operative management in unstable vertebral fractures in multiple myeloma.
