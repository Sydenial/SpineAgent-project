# Complete Fracture of the Lamina of the Sixth Cervical Vertebra with Hemiplegia: A Case Report

## Abstract

### Introduction
We report the rare case of a 27-year-old man who presented with a right-sided complete hemiplegia after a neck trauma due to a road traffic accident.

### Materials and Methods
Computed tomography revealed a complete fracture of the C6 lamina, including a partial fracture of the right articular process, with complete rotation of the fragment into the spinal canal and major compression of the right side of the spinal cord. The patient underwent urgent surgery with a posterior approach for C6 arch removal, followed by a C6–C7 anterior fusion. T2-weighted magnetic resonance imaging revealed a completely torn C6–C7 disc with a hematoma under the posterior longitudinal ligament associated with increased spinal cord signal.

### Results
The patient was able to walk normally 6 weeks after the accident. At 2-year follow-up, he recovered full sensation on the right side of his body but had a residual intrinsic muscle weakness in his right hand.

### Conclusion
This is the first paper in the literature to describe a complete laminar fracture at the cervical spine level associated with hemiplegia. Early surgical intervention probably provides a better neurological outcome.

## Case Report

A 27-year-old man presented with neck trauma after a road traffic accident occurring around midnight. He was involved in a head-on collision while driving his pickup truck, was not wearing a seat belt, and there was no alcohol involvement. Initial medical assessment by the emergency team found a fully conscious patient with severe neck pain and complete right-sided hemiplegia. No other injuries were noted. The patient was intubated and immediately transported to our hospital for further evaluation.

A full-body computed tomography scan ruled out fractures of the limbs and head injury. Cervical spine imaging showed a complete fracture of the C6 lamina, including a partial fracture of the right articular process, with complete sliding and rotation of the fragment into the spinal canal. The fragment was in contact with the posterior wall of the C6 vertebral body, causing major compression of the right side of the spinal cord, explaining the patient’s right-sided neurological symptoms. No other bony lesions of the cervical spine were identified.

The patient underwent emergency surgery approximately 3 hours after the accident. He was positioned prone, and a posterior approach was performed. The posterior arch of C6 was found to be completely mobile, with its right side twisted into the spinal canal. The fragment was removed en bloc, revealing an intact dura without tear or cerebrospinal fluid leakage. A small medial fracture of the right superior articular process of C7 was observed, but no fixation was performed at that stage.

Postoperatively, the patient was extubated. Neurological examination revealed paresthesia of the right forearm and right leg, with altered temperature sensation in the left leg, consistent with an initial Brown-Sequard syndrome. Motor examination showed improvement, with residual partial deficits of the intrinsic muscles and finger flexors of the right hand (3/5 strength), as well as the right quadriceps and ankle extensors (3/5 strength).

A postoperative MRI was performed to assess spinal cord, disc, and ligament damage. It revealed a completely torn C6–C7 disc with a ruptured annulus and a hematoma beneath the posterior longitudinal ligament, associated with increased spinal cord signal on T2-weighted images. The C5–C6 disc appeared intact.

A second surgery was therefore performed 3 days after the accident to stabilize the C6–C7 level. The patient was positioned supine, and an anterior prevascular approach was used. Intraoperatively, the C6–C7 disc was found to be completely torn with high instability. The C5–C6 disc was tested by discography and showed no leakage of contrast medium. The C6–C7 level was stabilized with a cage filled with autologous iliac crest bone graft and an anterior plate.

The patient wore a rigid cervical collar for 2 months. Two weeks after the accident, he was transferred to a rehabilitation center to continue physiotherapy. At 6 weeks, he was able to stand and walk normally, with only a minor residual deficit of the intrinsic muscles of the right hand. No bladder or bowel disturbances were noted, and sensory function fully recovered.

At 2-year follow-up, recovery was satisfactory, with a normal gait pattern and almost normal motor function of the right upper extremity. A residual intrinsic muscle weakness of the right hand persisted. Early surgical intervention probably contributed to the favorable neurological outcome.

## Discussion

This report describes a rare case of hemiplegia caused by cervical trauma. A complete fracture of the C6 lamina with intrusion of the fragment into the spinal canal on the right side resulted in a complete neurological deficit of the right upper and lower limbs.

Isolated laminar fractures are rare [1–7]. Similar injuries have been described only three times in the literature, but in all reported cases the laminar fracture was incomplete, typically representing an invagination due to a greenstick fracture.

Makan [6] described a case of incomplete C5 laminar fracture caused by direct trauma, with spinal canal encroachment leading to right hemiparesis. The patient underwent laminectomy without fusion and recovered with residual intrinsic muscle weakness of the right hand. De Barros Filho [4] reported quadriplegia following an incomplete laminar fracture of C6, treated with posterior laminectomy and fusion, resulting in partial recovery. Hähnle [5] described traumatic invagination of the C4 and C5 laminae with acute hemiparesis; open reduction and internal fixation with wires led to full neurological recovery at 6 weeks.

The originality of the present case lies in the completeness of the laminar fracture, involving both sides of the lamina, with maximal rotation of the fragment into the spinal canal, compressing the spinal cord against the posterior wall of the vertebral body on the right side.

The patient reported that during the accident he experienced hyper-rotation of his head to the left and attempted to contract his muscles to protect himself. This rotation mechanism explains the rotated laminar fragment and the associated C6–C7 disc rupture with a small unilateral fracture of the right articular process, as uniarticular fractures are a known consequence of rotational trauma [8]. The laminar fracture itself was likely caused by hyperextension with axial compression [1], with head rotation contributing to this rare injury pattern.

In spinal cord injury, prognosis is closely related to the timing of surgical decompression. Fehlings [9] recommends urgent decompression for bilateral cervical dislocation in patients with neurological deterioration, noting that surgery within 24 hours may reduce intensive care unit stay and post-injury complications. Other studies have shown that early intervention is associated with shorter hospital stays and fewer pulmonary complications [10], and helps minimize secondary spinal cord damage due to prolonged compression [11]. Experimental studies have demonstrated that the degree of reperfusion hyperemia after decompression is inversely related to the duration of spinal cord compression and directly related to electrophysiological recovery [12].

In the present case, urgent decompression was performed approximately 3 hours after the accident, likely playing a crucial role in the rapid neurological recovery and favorable overall outcome.

This is the first report in the literature describing a complete cervical laminar fracture associated with hemiplegia. A combination of hyper-rotation and hyperextension mechanisms caused a complete fracture of the C6 lamina with intrusion of the fragment into the spinal canal, resulting in complete neurological deficit of the right upper and lower limbs.
