# Multiple noncontiguous spinal fractures and occipitocervical dislocation in a patient with ankylosing spondylitis treated with a hybrid open and percutaneous spinal fixation technique: a case report

## Abstract

**Keywords**: Ankylosing spondylitis, Spinal fractures, Percutaneous fixation, Minimally invasive techniques, Occipital-cervical dislocation, Hybrid technique

**BACKGROUND CONTEXT**: Spinal fractures occur with greater frequency in ankylosing spondylitis (AS) patients. Treatment is complicated due to a high incidence of medical comorbidities.

**PURPOSE**: To report a case involving a novel, combined open and percutaneous surgical technique for treating multiple noncontiguous spinal fractures in an AS patient.

**STUDY DESIGN/SETTING**: We describe the surgical treatment and the outcome of an AS patient who sustained an occipitocervical dislocation and two noncontiguous three-column extension injuries. A hybrid technique combining open occipital to T3 fusion and percutaneous T5–L1 instrumentation was used at a tertiary care facility.

**PATIENT SAMPLE**: A 77-year-old man with multiple comorbidities and newly diagnosed AS.

**OUTCOME MEASURES**: Two-year clinical and radiographic outcomes after surgical treatment for multiple spine injuries in an ankylosed spine.

**METHODS**: The patient was treated with a hybrid approach, combining both open fusion and percutaneous instrumentation techniques.

**RESULTS**: At 2 years postoperatively, the patient had regained ambulatory ability and had a favorable clinical outcome.

**CONCLUSIONS**: We report a unique case of noncontiguous spinal trauma in a medically complex AS patient, treated with a hybrid open and percutaneous technique. This approach minimized surgical insult and blood loss, yielding a positive clinical and radiographic outcome 2 years later.

## Introduction

Spinal disease in ankylosing spondylitis (AS) predisposes individuals with AS to spinal fractures, which can occur with very minor trauma. Treating these injuries is challenging due to multiple comorbidities and the morbidity associated with open surgery. Here, we present a unique case of a patient with AS who sustained three simultaneous spinal fractures and an occipitocervical dislocation after a fall from standing height. This patient was treated with a combined open cervical fusion and percutaneous thoracolumbar fixation to minimize morbidity.

## Case Report

A 77-year-old man with multiple medical comorbidities, including morbid obesity, congestive heart failure, coronary artery disease, diabetes mellitus, and hypertension, sustained a fall from standing height. A computed tomography (CT) scan revealed a C1 ring fracture, a three-column extension fracture at C7–T1, and an occipitocervical ligamentous injury with joint incongruity. Due to his comorbidities and obesity, he was a poor candidate for traditional open stabilization. He was transferred in halo vest immobilization for surgical management. On admission, an additional T9–T10 three-column extension fracture was identified, which had initially been missed in the patient’s evaluation.

Given the imaging characteristics, including spinal ligamentous ossification and marginal syndesmophytes, the patient was diagnosed with AS. A magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the cervical spine also showed ligamentous edema at the occipitocervical junction, consistent with a traumatic occipitocervical injury.

The patient was optimized medically and underwent an occipital to T3 posterior fusion via open technique and T5–L1 percutaneous instrumentation. The procedure involved positioning the patient supine on a radiolucent table, flipping the patient into the prone position to avoid displacing the fractures. Fluoroscopy was used to check the occipitocervical alignment. Through an open approach, occipital plating, C2 pars screws, bicortical cervical lateral mass screws, and thoracic pedicle screws (T1–T3) were placed. A contoured rod was placed from the occipital plate down to T3 and secured with end cap blockers. A crosslink was placed at the C5 level, followed by decortication and the insertion of bone morphogenetic protein.

Biplanar fluoroscopy was used to place percutaneous pedicle screws from T5 to L1. The rods were contoured to address the patient’s hyperkyphotic deformity, passing subfascially from proximal to distal. The percutaneous and open instrumentation systems were connected using rod connectors.

The operation took 5 hours with an estimated blood loss of 1,000 cc. The patient did well postoperatively. After an 18-day hospital stay, he was transferred to a rehabilitation facility and eventually discharged home. Follow-up CT at 2 years showed complete fusion of the C7–T1 and T9–T10 three-column fractures and a pseudoarthrosis at the occipitocervical junction, with no signs of lucency around the occipitocervical instrumentation. At the most recent follow-up, the patient was ambulatory and had no pain, though he had lost some occipitocervical motion.

## Discussion

Noncontiguous spinal fractures have been reported to occur in up to 19% of patients with AS, often involving the cervical and thoracic spine. A case of an AS patient with three spinal fractures has been reported, but these were not simultaneous or as extensive as those in our patient. Typically, spinal fractures in AS occur at transitional zones or at the apex of kyphotic segments. Occipitoatlantal injuries in AS patients have been described in only two other cases, but this is the first case of simultaneous occipitocervical dislocation with multiple noncontiguous spinal fractures.

Spinal fractures occur 3.5 times more frequently in AS patients than in the general population and are often unstable. Multiple fractures can be easily missed, leading to devastating consequences, including neurologic compromise in 81% of cases with delayed diagnosis. Occult noncontiguous fractures, such as the T9–T10 fracture in our patient, can frequently occur. This highlights the importance of evaluating the entire spine in AS patients with trauma, preferably using advanced imaging like CT or MRI.

Although our patient had several comorbidities, surgical stabilization was preferred due to the poor outcomes of nonoperative treatment in AS patients. Surgical stabilization has been shown to yield better results than nonoperative treatment. While percutaneous techniques in trauma patients with ankylosed spines are not well-documented, they are gaining popularity due to their lower blood loss and the tendency for AS patients to heal across their injuries once stabilized. Our patient underwent a combined procedure with open occipitocervical and cervicothoracic fusion, as well as percutaneous thoracolumbar stabilization, minimizing surgical morbidity and intraoperative blood loss.

## Conclusion

We report a unique case of three simultaneous noncontiguous spinal fractures and an occipitocervical dislocation in an AS patient. This injury was successfully treated using a combined open and percutaneous technique to minimize surgical morbidity and intraoperative blood loss. This hybrid approach may be a viable option for patients with significant medical comorbidities who may not tolerate more extensive open procedures.
