# Management of a Posterior Gunshot Injury with a Floating Pedicle and Cerebrospinal Fluid Leak

## Abstract

We present the case of a 30-year-old male, who received a bullet in his back after an exchange of fire with the police. Initial assessment revealed paraparesis with cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leak from the bullet entry hole. CT scan showed metal debris with two bullet fragments in the L5/S1 right foramen and lateral recess, and another fragment in the central canal posteriorly. Surgery was performed with L5/S1 posterior approach, right L5 pedicle stabilization, removal of the bullet fragments, dural repair with a patch, and insertion of a lumbar CSF drain. The patient showed satisfactory radiological results at one year with a remaining partial motor deficit of both ankles, mainly on the right side. Literature contains controversy regarding the management of spinal gunshot injuries. The current case shows that early surgical management when partial neurological deficit with a CSF leak is noted could improve the clinical outcome and prevent related complications.

**Keywords**: Gunshot wound, Floating pedicle, Cerebrospinal fluid leak, Spine injury

## Case Presentation

A 30-year-old male, in the context of drug dealing, was trying to escape the police in a high-speed car chase, with exchange of fire. He suddenly felt a sharp pain in his low back area and the vehicle stopped. The patient was found with back bleeding and inability to stand or walk. Initial medical assessment found a conscious man with severe lumbar pain and paraparesis mainly concerning both ankle movements. He was transported to the hospital for further evaluation. Upon arrival, the patient was alert and oriented. Exposure of the back revealed a left paravertebral wound at the L5 level corresponding to the entry hole of a bullet, with minimal bleeding, but with a leak of transparent fluid corresponding to CSF. Sensory and motor exams revealed saddle anesthesia, weak anal tone, urine retention, and partial deficit of ankle plantar flexors on both sides (2/5), with complete right ankle dorsal flexor deficit (0/5), but sparing the tibialis anterior muscle. Lumbar X-ray and CT scan confirmed the gunshot injury with multiple bullet fragments seen at the L5/S1 level posteriorly, along with two fracture lines around the right L5 pedicle. The patient underwent surgery for exploration, bullet fragment removal, dural repair, and L5/S1 posterior instrumented fusion.

## Diagnostic Imaging

Lumbar X-ray showed multiple bullet fragments at the L5/S1 level posteriorly with metal debris. CT scan revealed three main fragments: one in the right L5/S1 foramen, one in the right lateral recess, and the third in the central canal posteriorly. No vertebral body fracture was noted, but a fracture of the right L5 isthmus and another fracture line at the junction of the right pedicle and vertebral body were seen, resulting in a floating right L5 pedicle.

## Historical Review, Epidemiology, Diagnosis, Pathology, and Differential Diagnosis

Gunshot wound to the spine is the third most common cause of spine injuries after falls from height and road traffic accidents. It accounts for 13–17% of all gunshot injuries and occurs predominantly in the thoracic region in civilian practice, with a rate of incomplete injury in the lumbosacral area about 70%. The damage caused by a bullet depends on its kinetic energy (KE) related to its velocity. Low-velocity weapons cause injuries from bullet fragments or fractured vertebral segments, while high-velocity weapons cause spinal cord injury due to shock waves and cavitation generated by high-speed bullets.

A detailed neurological examination, including motor, sensory, reflexes, and anal sphincter tone, is necessary. Plain radiographs help locate bullet fragments and associated fractures, while CT scans more accurately localize foreign bodies in the spinal canal. MRI is reported to be safe in patients with retained bullet fragments, but caution is advised regarding ferromagnetic metals.

Acute infections, such as empyema, intra-abdominal sepsis, and spinal abscess, can occur, especially with visceral perforation. The lumbar spine is the most common site of infection due to bullet passage through the gastrointestinal tract.

## Rationale for Treatment

The management of gunshot wounds to the spine is controversial. Conservative management aims to avoid further harm, while others recommend surgery to remove foreign bodies and improve outcomes by facilitating nerve root regeneration. Surgical intervention depends on several factors: neurological status, spinal stability, bullet location, injury level, and the presence of a CSF leak. If a patient is neurologically intact, surgery is rarely indicated, except in cases of acute lead intoxication, intracanal copper bullets, or new neurological deficits. Surgical decompression may help improve motor and sensory function in cases of nerve root compression due to bone, metal fragments, or hematoma. Surgery may also be indicated for complications such as infection or persistent CSF fistulae.

In the case of our patient, given the CSF leak, unstable right L5 pedicle, and partial neurological deficit, a posterior instrumented L5/S1 fusion with laminectomy was performed for exploration, bullet removal, dural repair, and stabilization.

## Operative Procedure

Under general anesthesia, the patient was positioned prone. The spine was exposed subperiosteally from L4 to S1. Pedicle screws were placed at L5 and S1 on both sides using free-hand technique. For the right L5 pedicle, a reduction forceps was used to stabilize the pedicle, allowing pedicle screw insertion. The right foramen was opened to remove bullet fragments. A wide L5 laminectomy revealed one bullet fragment in contact with neurological elements, causing significant dura laceration. A dural patch was placed, and the dura was sealed. A lumbar CSF drain was inserted at L3/L4. Autologous graft was used for posterior fusion, and vancomycin powder was applied intraoperatively. The three bullet fragments were returned to the police as evidence.

## Clinical Outcome

The patient showed significant neurological improvement, especially in ankle plantar flexion, and was able to walk independently with a walking frame by the end of his 2-week hospital stay. His anal tone improved, and the Foley catheter was removed at 4 weeks. Postoperative CT confirmed the removal of all bullet fragments and the correct position of the implants. At 1-year follow-up, the patient had stable spinal alignment and walked with a cane, but weakness in right ankle dorsal extension persisted due to right L5 root damage.
