# Magnetic Resonance Imaging of the Codman Microsensor Transducer Used for Intraspinal Pressure Monitoring

## Abstract
Objective: To test the Codman Microsensor Transducer (CMT) in a cervical gel phantom and intraspinal pressure monitoring in a patient with spinal cord injury.  
Summary of Background Data: The technique of intraspinal pressure monitoring using the CMT has been introduced to guide management of traumatic spinal cord injury, analogous to intracranial pressure monitoring for traumatic brain injury. The safety of MRI in patients with a CMT in situ is unclear.

## Introduction
We recently introduced intraspinal pressure monitoring using the Codman Microsensor Transducer (CMT) for guiding the management of traumatic spinal cord injury. This technique is similar to intracranial pressure monitoring used in traumatic brain injury. The safety of performing magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with the CMT in place for monitoring intraspinal pressure in patients with spinal cord injuries is still uncertain.

## Methods
We tested the CMT in a cervical gel phantom using a 3-T MRI system. The CMT was then inserted subdurally at the injury site in a patient with traumatic spinal cord injury, and MRI was performed using a 1.5-T system. We measured heating produced by the CMT in different configurations.

## Results
In the gel phantom, heating of up to 5°C occurred when the CMT wire was placed straight through the magnet bore. Heating was abolished when the wire was coiled. MRI of the spinal cord was performed on a patient with American Spinal Injuries Association grade C cervical cord injury, and no neurological deterioration occurred after MRI.

## Conclusion
Our data suggest that the CMT is MR-conditional when used in the spinal configuration in humans. Further studies with larger patient groups are required to confirm these findings.

## Materials and Methods

### CMT/MRI Setup
The CMT system (Depuy Synthes, Leeds, UK) consists of a pressure probe tip, CMT wire, connector, and cable connected to the pressure monitor. The system was isolated from the magnetic and RF fields during imaging in a 3-T Siemens Magnetom Verio system for the gel phantom and in a 1.5-T Philips Intera system for the TSCI patient.

### CMT Setup in Gel Phantom
A spinal cord phantom was created using a 15-mL Falcon centrifuge tube filled with agar gel. Temperature was measured using a Luxtron 3100 Fluroptic thermometer with four Fiberoptic Thermocouples (FT1-FT4) secured along the CMT wire. The CMT wire was inserted into the gel phantom.

### CMT Testing in Gel Phantom
Temperature measurements were taken during an FLAIR imaging sequence at 2-second intervals. Temperature readings were recorded before, during, and after imaging, with a focus on the heating effects caused by RF energy deposition.

## Results

### CMT Testing in Gel Phantom
Ambient temperature was maintained at approximately 20.2°C. Heating of the CMT probe tip was measured during MRI, with temperature fluctuations corresponding to RF pulses. In all test configurations, heating was localized to the tip of the transducer.

### Postoperative TSCI Patient Imaging
Postoperative MRI was performed on a TSCI patient using a 1.5-T system. The CMT transducer was coiled and positioned away from the magnet bore, and no change in the patient's neurological condition was observed after MRI.

## Discussion
Our main finding is that MRI of the cervical spinal cord with a CMT in situ for intraspinal pressure monitoring is safe, as long as the transducer wire is coiled and positioned away from the magnet bore. This setup prevents excessive heating and preserves transducer functionality. MRI in the presence of metallic constructs, such as titanium for cervical fixation, did not significantly degrade spinal cord imaging.

## Conclusion
The Codman Microsensor transducer is MR-conditional in the cervical spine configuration for MRI at 1.5-T and 3-T systems. We observed good-quality images of the spinal cord in a patient with a CMT in situ, with no neurological deterioration.

## Key Points
- The Codman Microsensor Transducer (CMT) is MR-conditional in a cervical configuration.
- MRI of the cervical spinal cord with the CMT in situ is safe and provides high-quality images without affecting neurological function.
- Coiling the CMT wire and positioning it away from the magnet bore prevents excessive heating and maintains its functionality during MRI.
