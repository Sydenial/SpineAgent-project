# Effective Treatment of Delayed Union of a Lumbar Vertebral Fracture With Daily Administration of Teriparatide in a Patient With Diffuse Idiopathic Skeletal Hyperostosis

## Abstract

### Introduction
We herein describe a case of delayed union of a lumbar spine fracture in a 70-year-old patient with diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH).

### Clinical Course and Result
Because the patient decided not to undergo surgical treatment, we provided conservative treatment with teriparatide (TPTD). Union was obtained in 2 months, and no adverse events were observed during treatment. Six months after starting TPTD, further bone formation was observed and the lumbar instability had resolved.

### Conclusion
This is the first report of successful use of TPTD to treat delayed union of a spine fracture in a patient with DISH without surgical intervention.

## Case Report

A 70-year-old man arrived at the emergency room by ambulance with a 1-day history of lower back pain and fever. He had fallen several times during the past 3 weeks. His medical history included a complete thoracic spinal cord injury with associated complete paraplegia and a left femoral neck fracture at the age of 50 years, as well as myocardial infarction at the age of 68 years.

On physical examination, his blood pressure was 136/80 mmHg, pulse was regular at 76/min, respiratory rate was 20 breaths/min, and temperature was 38.5 °C. The patient exhibited lower back pain. Spinal percussion pain was noted at the L2–3 level, and tenderness of the paravertebral muscles was present. Laboratory data indicated the presence of an inflammatory reaction (C-reactive protein level, 35.4 mg/dL; white blood cell count, 16,100/μL) and acute renal failure (blood urea nitrogen level, 51.6 mg/dL; creatinine level, 1.59 mg/dL).

Plain radiographs and computed tomography images suggested a bone defect of the L2 vertebral body and diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis from the thoracic to lumbar spine. Magnetic resonance imaging showed high-intensity T1- and T2-weighted changes in the L2 vertebral body. Based on these findings, pyogenic spondylitis, metastatic spinal tumor, or delayed union of a spinal fracture was suspected.

A biopsy of the L2 vertebral body was performed on the second day of hospitalization, and neither infection nor neoplastic disease was detected. The patient was therefore diagnosed with delayed union of an L2 fracture associated with DISH. Bacteria were detected by urinalysis, and the elevated body temperature indicated a urinary tract infection. Antibiotic therapy was initiated.

Although operative treatment was considered because of severe lower back pain, the patient had complete paraplegia, diabetes mellitus, and a history of myocardial infarction. Consequently, the patient and his family decided against surgical intervention. Conservative treatment using teriparatide was therefore selected. The patient was fitted with a frame corset, and daily administration of TPTD was initiated 2 weeks after hospitalization.

Callus formation was observed 3 weeks after starting TPTD, and the patient’s lower back pain gradually subsided. After 2 months, hyperplastic bone formation was observed around the circumference of the L2 vertebral body, and the patient regained a physical activity level close to that before hospitalization. Six months after initiation of TPTD treatment, further bone formation was observed and lumbar instability had resolved, allowing the patient to return to his home life.

Pain and functional outcomes were assessed using the visual analog pain scale [1] and the Barthel Index [2] at admission and after 3 weeks, 2 months, and 6 months of daily TPTD administration. Pain decreased from 86 mm on admission to 30 mm after 3 weeks, 20 mm after 2 months, and 11 mm after 6 months. The Barthel Index increased from 10 on admission to 15 after 3 weeks and to 20 after both 2 and 6 months. After 6 months of treatment, an 87% reduction in pain score and a 100% improvement in functional score were observed. These findings indicate the efficacy of daily TPTD administration for conservative treatment of delayed union of a vertebral fracture in a patient with DISH.

## Considerations

Resnick and Niwayama [3] coined the term diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH), which is now widely used. DISH is a systemic, noninflammatory disease characterized by ossification of entheses, the bony attachments of tendons, ligaments, and joint capsules. It is a systemic condition involving ossification and calcification of ligaments and entheses and is observed worldwide, most commonly in men over 50 years of age [4].

Although DISH is asymptomatic in most individuals, it often reflects underlying metabolic disease. Spinal or extraspinal ossification may lead to pain, stiffness, reduced range of motion, dysphagia, and an increased risk of unstable spinal fractures. Early internal fixation of spinal fractures in patients with DISH is generally recommended, and surgical treatment is favored over conservative management for thoracolumbar fractures due to the high incidence of complications and poor prognosis associated with nonsurgical treatment [5].

Teriparatide has been shown to reduce fracture risk in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis [6–12], and its efficacy in human osteoporosis is well established. Several reports have demonstrated its preventive and analgesic effects in osteoporosis-related spinal compression fractures [13–15]. Teriparatide is currently indicated for patients with osteoporosis who are at high risk of fracture or who have failed or are intolerant to other osteoporosis therapies.

Increasing attention has been given to the use of teriparatide for fracture repair, particularly in cases of delayed union and nonunion. Its efficacy has been reported in delayed union of odontoid fractures [16], periprosthetic fractures [17], distal femoral metaphyseal fractures [18], atypical subtrochanteric femoral fractures [19], olecranon fractures [20], sternal fractures [21], and femoral neck fractures [22].

Although teriparatide has been used to treat fresh spinal fractures in patients with DISH [23], its application to delayed union has not been previously reported. While surgical intervention is generally recommended for vertebral fractures in patients with DISH, the present case demonstrates successful conservative treatment of delayed union of a lumbar spine fracture using daily teriparatide administration.

## Conclusions

This is the first report describing successful treatment of delayed union of a spinal fracture in a patient with DISH using teriparatide without surgical intervention. Although based on a single case, these findings suggest a potential advantage of teriparatide administration in the treatment of delayed union of spinal fractures in patients with DISH.
