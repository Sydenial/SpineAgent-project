# The hyperextension triad of type II odontoid fracture, posterior atlantoaxial dislocation (reducible in flexion), and C1 posterior arch fracture: implications on emergency management and anterior versus posterior surgical approach

A 45-year-old man presented with quadriparesis following a motor vehicular accident. Computed tomography of the craniovertebral junction showed type II odontoid fracture, posterior atlantoaxial dislocation (post-AAD), and fracture of the posterior arch of the atlas. On immobilization in extension, there was worsening of posterior AAD. Thus, traction was applied in flexion, which reduced the dislocation. In the operating room, the feasibility of anterior odontoid screw was assessed under an image intensifier; however, the necessary space for anterior approach could not be obtained as the reduction was achieved only in the flexed position. Therefore, the patient underwent a C1 lateral mass and C2 pars screw and rod fixation through a posterior approach. Postoperative radiographs were satisfactory, and the patient showed gradual improvement in neurologic status.

The above-mentioned triad, associated mechanism, and management issues in odontoid fracture have never been addressed. It is possible that hyperextension forces on the craniovertebral junction caused type II odontoid fracture, with avulsion of the anterior longitudinal ligament resulting in posterior displacement of the distal fragment of the dens. The C1 posterior arch fracture may be caused by impact of the suboccipital portion of the skull during hyperextension. Although AAD and other cervical spine injuries are usually stabilized in extension in emergency, this rare triad requires a flexion position for stabilization. On flexion, the intact transverse ligament probably brings the distal dens fragment into alignment. The implication on surgical decision lies in the inadequate corridor available for anterior odontoid screw fixation. Hence, the posterior approach with the C1-C2 fixation would be the best approach for this rare triad.
