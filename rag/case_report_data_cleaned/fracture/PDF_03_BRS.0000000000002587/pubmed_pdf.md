# Delayed Fatal Lumbar Artery Bleeding Following Less Invasive Posterolateral Decompression and Fusion

## Abstract

### Study Design
A case report.

### Objective
To highlight a rare but potentially life-threatening vascular complication following less invasive dorsolateral approaches to the thoracolumbar spine.

### Summary of Background Data
Although less invasive dorsolateral approaches are associated with a low risk of major intraoperative bleeding, traditional vessels may rarely be damaged. Spine surgeons involved with these approaches should be aware of these rare but potentially dangerous vascular complications, particularly in elderly patients with increased morbidity and/or metastatic disease to the spine.

### Methods
A 76-year-old Caucasian woman with a comminuted osteoporotic fracture of the L1 vertebra with spinal canal encroachment underwent a corpectomy and 360° fusion using a titanium mesh cage through a less invasive dorsolateral approach.

### Results
Despite the absence of visible intraoperative bleeding and an initially uneventful postoperative period, the patient died on the seventh postoperative day due to massive bleeding from a segmental artery at the level of the L3 vertebral body, two levels below the intervention site.

### Conclusions
This paper describes a rare complication of delayed and fatal bleeding from the left L3 segmental artery following less invasive L1 corpectomy, mesh cage insertion, and pedicle screw fixation in an elderly female patient with a history of two malignancies. Although injury to large vessels must always be prevented during these procedures, injury to segmental vessels occurs more frequently. The dorsolateral approach and other retroperitoneal approaches to the thoracolumbar region are established methods for surgical stabilization of comminuted vertebral body fractures, especially in the hands of experienced spine surgeons. Great care should be taken postoperatively to identify signs of bleeding and hematoma, and surgeons should be aware of these life-threatening complications.

### Key Words
Fatal bleeding; segmental artery; dorsolateral approach; delayed complication; osteoporotic fracture; corpectomy; spinal fusion; death; thoracolumbar spine.

### Level of Evidence
5

## Introduction

Life-threatening bleeding following less invasive dorsolateral approaches in the thoracolumbar junction is exceptional. There are only a few reports of intraoperative injuries to great vessels and segmental arteries in lateral approaches to the thoracolumbar spine. To the authors’ knowledge, this is the first report of postoperative massive bleeding from the L3 segmental artery following less invasive dorsolateral L1 corpectomy in a 76-year-old woman with a fatal outcome.

## Materials and Methods

A 76-year-old woman presented with a six-month history of progressive thoracolumbar spine pain without neurological deficits. Imaging revealed a pathological fracture of the L1 vertebra with spinal canal encroachment. Her medical history included a right colectomy for colon malignancy and a total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy for ovarian malignancy fifteen and ten years prior to admission, respectively.

Due to the high perioperative morbidity associated with a traditional anterior approach in an elderly patient with previous abdominal surgeries, a less invasive left dorsolateral retroperitoneal approach was selected. A preoperative transpedicular biopsy of L1 showed no malignancy, and CT angiography did not reveal vascular malformations or increased blood supply to the anterior thoracolumbar spine.

Surgery was performed with the patient in the prone position. A skin incision was made 1 cm left of the midline. The left-sided dissection included unilateral detachment of paravertebral muscles and removal of the transverse processes of T12, L1, and L2, as well as the T12 rib head, via a retroperitoneal approach to the lateral vertebral walls of T12–L2. Pedicle screws were inserted on the left side at T11, T12, L2, and L3. The left L1 nerve root was identified, ligated, and sectioned. Meticulous intraoperative control of bleeding was performed following ligation of the T12–L2 segmental vessels. A partial corpectomy was followed by insertion of a titanium mesh cage filled with allograft. Four pedicle screws were then inserted transfascially on the right side at T11, T12, L2, and L3. Bilateral longitudinal rods were applied. Intraoperative neuromonitoring was used.

## Results

The postoperative course was initially uneventful, with no clinical or laboratory signs of significant bleeding. The patient was mobilized on the second postoperative day with a custom-made orthosis under low-molecular-weight heparin anticoagulation. She was discharged on postoperative day seven. During hospitalization, she received a total of three units of blood. Laboratory values at discharge were within normal limits (hematocrit 36.3%, hemoglobin 12 g/dL, platelets 131,000/μL).

On the afternoon of the discharge day, the patient developed dizziness at home and was transferred unconscious and hemodynamically unstable to a regional hospital. Laboratory evaluation revealed a hematocrit of 18.2% and hemoglobin of 6.1 g/dL. After initial stabilization, CT angiography demonstrated a large retroperitoneal hematoma with active bleeding, likely originating from the left L3 segmental artery. Due to her critical condition, embolization was recommended; however, the family declined further intervention. The patient was transferred to the intensive care unit and died eight hours later from disseminated intravascular coagulation.

## Discussion

This report describes an extremely rare and fatal complication of delayed, initially asymptomatic retroperitoneal hemorrhage caused by bleeding from the left L3 segmental vertebral artery following semi-open left-sided posterolateral decompression and L1 corpectomy in an elderly female patient. Notably, the bleeding vessel was located two levels below the corpectomy site.

The exact cause of this complication could not be determined, as an autopsy was declined. The authors speculate that the bleeding was related to previous malignancies and abdominal surgeries, which may have caused vasculopathy, arteriovenous shunts, and vessel fragility. Surgical manipulation during cage insertion near the upper border of L2 may have resulted in a minor indirect injury to the L3 segmental artery. A postoperative hypertensive episode, combined with thromboprophylaxis, may have precipitated the fatal bleeding. Alternative explanations include an unrecognized vascular puncture during spinal biopsy or pedicle screw insertion.

Less invasive lateral thoracolumbar approaches for vertebrectomy are technically demanding due to complex anterior anatomy and limited exposure. Although reported rates of retroperitoneal or psoas hematoma are low, such complications may be missed for weeks after surgery. Delayed arterial injuries have also been reported after percutaneous vertebral procedures. In the present case, despite thorough preoperative evaluation including CT angiography, this rare complication resulted in delayed but catastrophic bleeding and death seven days postoperatively.

Less invasive approaches to the thoracolumbar region remain effective methods for stabilizing comminuted vertebral body fractures, particularly when performed by experienced surgeons. However, stricter patient selection criteria should be applied. Elderly patients with a history of malignancy, prior abdominal surgery, or radiotherapy may be at increased risk for vascular complications. When such approaches are chosen, vigilant intraoperative technique and close postoperative monitoring for early or delayed signs of bleeding are essential. Early recognition and intervention, such as embolization of injured segmental arteries, may be life-saving.
