# Incidence and Risk Factors of Anterior Arch Fracture of the Atlas Following C1 Laminectomy Without Fusion

## Abstract

**Study Design.** A retrospective multi-center study.

**Objective.** To determine the incidence of anterior arch fracture (AAF) of the atlas following C1 laminectomy without fusion, and its risk factors.

**Summary of Background Data.** C1 laminectomy without fusion is a widely accepted surgical procedure for conditions involving the C1/2 region when overt instability is absent. Several case series have reported spontaneous AAF following this procedure. However, the incidence of post-laminectomy AAF and its risk factors have not been studied.

**Methods.** This retrospective study included patients who underwent C1 laminectomy without fusion in any of the four participating institutions between April 2002 and March 2016. The incidence of AAF following C1 laminectomy was determined, and patients were grouped into those who developed AAF (AAF group) and those who did not (non-AAF group). Patient demographics and radiographic parameters, including subaxial cervical balance on X-ray (C2–7 sagittal vertical axis, C2–7 lordosis, C2–7 coronal Cobb angle, and T1 slope), and morphology of the atlas on computed tomography (CT), were compared between the groups.

**Results.** Seventy patients who underwent C1 laminectomy without fusion were included. The incidence of AAF was 14.2% (10/70). Multivariate analysis revealed that a large inferior facet angle (IFA, defined as the coronal inclination angle of the C1/2 facet measured on CT) and the presence of subaxial ankylosis were independent risk factors for AAF. There were no significant differences in subaxial cervical balance on X-ray between the AAF and non-AAF groups $(p>0.05)$.

**Conclusions.** The incidence of AAF after C1 laminectomy without fusion is not uncommon. Preoperative assessment using CT may identify patients at high risk of AAF.

**Key Words.** Anterior arch fracture; atlas; C1 laminectomy without fusion; inferior facet angle; subaxial ankylosis.

## Introduction

C1 laminectomy without fusion is accepted for selected indications, and complications have generally been considered rare, although supporting data are limited. O’Shaughnessy et al. first reported cases of anterior arch fracture (AAF) of the atlas following this procedure. Although AAF is typically described in the setting of trauma (e.g., Jefferson fracture), spontaneous AAF after C1 laminectomy without fusion has also been reported. Despite recognition of this possibility, no published reports have described its incidence and risk factors. This information may support appropriate case selection and reduce complications.

Thus, the aim of the present study was to determine the incidence and risk factors of AAF after C1 laminectomy without fusion.

## Materials and Methods

### Patient population and incidence of anterior arch fracture

This study was approved by the institutional ethics board. A retrospective review of medical records between April 2002 and March 2016 was performed. Patients who underwent C1 laminectomy without fusion during this period were included if they met the following criteria: (1) absence of congenital nonunion of the anterior arch, and (2) follow-up period greater than eight months.

The diagnosis of AAF was made based on CT scans that were usually performed when patients experienced new-onset neck pain following surgery. Patients who did not develop new-onset neck pain were included in the non-AAF group based on CT findings if available, or cervical X-ray findings if a routine CT scan was not performed.

### Surgical procedure

The surgical procedure (C1 laminectomy) performed by surgeons across the four institutions was similar, with the exception of whether to dissect muscles inserting into the C2 spinous process. Following a longitudinal skin incision, the nuchal ligament was divided in the midline to identify the C2 spinous process. Some surgeons split the C2 spinous process longitudinally using a bone saw, preserved the muscles inserting into the C2 spinous process (rectus capitis posterior major and obliquus capitis inferior), and reconstructed the spinous process after resection of the C1 posterior arch. Other surgeons dissected these muscles totally or partially.

The C1 posterior arch resection included an approximately 20 mm segment after dissection of the muscles inserting into its surface (rectus capitis posterior minor). Decompression of additional stenotic levels (laminectomy or laminoplasty) was also performed in cases with co-existing subaxial canal narrowing.

### Patient data

Baseline demographics (age, sex, preoperative diagnosis, body mass index [BMI], and comorbidities) were recorded. Radiographic parameters that may affect cervical kinematics were evaluated on preoperative and postoperative images (postoperative data were collected at the time of fracture detection in the AAF group). X-ray measurements included C2–7 sagittal vertical axis (SVA), C2–7 lordosis (CL), C2–7 coronal Cobb angle, T1 slope (TS), and TS–CL.

CT-based measurements included anterior arch length, laminectomy length (distance between edges of the excised posterior arch), superior and inferior facet angles of C1 (SFA/IFA; defined relative to the inferior endplate of C2 on coronal images; averaged across consecutive slices), facet tropism (positive if absolute right–left difference of SFA or IFA was > 7), and the presence of subaxial ankylosis (positive if any bony bridge formation was observed between C2 and C3 on sagittal or coronal views). Radiographic and baseline demographic parameters were compared between AAF and non-AAF groups.

### Statistical analyses

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation unless specified otherwise. Chi-square test and Student’s t-test were used as appropriate. Risk factors were evaluated using univariate and multivariate logistic regression; variables with $p<0.10$ on univariate analysis were entered into multivariate analysis. A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was created to determine cutoff values of IFA as a predictor of AAF. Diagnostic accuracy was evaluated by the area under the curve (AUC). Radiographic parameters were measured by two board-certified orthopedic surgeons; inter-observer reliability was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). JMP version 11 (SAS, Cary, NC) was used for all analyses. Statistical significance was set at $p<0.05$.

## Results

Of the 73 patients who underwent C1 laminectomy without fusion in the study period, three were excluded owing to congenital nonunion of the anterior arch or a short follow-up period (< 8 months). A total of 70 patients were included for analyses.

### Incidence of anterior arch fracture

The incidence of spontaneous AAF following C1 laminectomy without fusion was 14.2% (10/70) over a mean follow-up period of $8.1{\pm}8.0$ months. Nine of the 10 patients were diagnosed on CT scans obtained for sudden-onset neck pain, while one was incidentally diagnosed on a routine follow-up CT scan. Salvage fusion surgery was required in two patients with intractable neck pain. All other patients reported symptomatic improvement with conservative treatment using a neck collar, although nonunion of the fracture was noted.

### Baseline demographics

The AAF and non-AAF groups were similar in baseline demographics except for statistically significant differences in sex $(p=0.01)$ and follow-up period $(p=0.03)$. Preoperative diagnoses included ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL), retro-odontoid pseudotumor (RST), cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM), atlantoaxial subluxation (AAS), and spinal tumor. Comorbidities potentially affecting bone quality or cervical kinematics were recorded, with no major intergroup differences.

### Radiographic parameters

Inter-observer agreement was excellent $(\mathrm{ICC}=0.78)$. Twenty-five of 60 non-AAF patients were excluded from radiographic analyses due to lack of adequate CT data. Subaxial balance on X-ray (CL, C2–7 SVA, C2–7 coronal Cobb angle, TS, and TS–CL) was not significantly different between groups in preoperative or postoperative periods. On CT, IFA, facet tropism of IFA, and presence of subaxial ankylosis were associated with AAF on univariate analysis $(p<0.10)$. On multivariate logistic regression, IFA (odds ratio per unit increase: 2.5; $p<0.01$) and presence of subaxial ankylosis $(\mathrm{OR}=26.3;\ p=0.02)$ were significant risk factors.

The ROC curve for IFA as a predictor of AAF showed AUC = 0.931, indicating high accuracy. A cutoff value of IFA = 23.0° demonstrated sensitivity of 90% and specificity of 86.2%.

### Representative cases

An 86-year-old male with retro-odontoid pseudotumor developed AAF after C1 laminectomy without fusion. Preoperative CT showed subaxial ankylosis (C2–4) and an IFA below the cutoff value. He complained of sudden-onset neck pain six months postoperatively and was found to have AAF on CT.

A 58-year-old male with CSM showed no subaxial ankylosis on preoperative CT, but had a large IFA. He presented with severe neck pain 15 months postoperatively, and AAF was confirmed on CT.

## Discussion

This study represents the largest case series of spontaneous AAF following C1 laminectomy without fusion and identifies its incidence and radiographic risk factors. Prior reports described AAF after C1 laminectomy in limited numbers of cases; in contrast, the observed incidence of 14.2% in this cohort suggests AAF may be more common than previously expected. This finding supports careful preoperative assessment of AAF risk among patients being considered for C1 laminectomy without fusion.

A biomechanical explanation may relate to force transmission through the C1/2 facets. Greater coronal inclination of the inferior facet may increase laterally directed forces and contribute to stress injury of the anterior arch, particularly when the posterior arch has been resected. In this cohort, IFA (rather than SFA) was identified as an independent risk factor, and an IFA > 23.0° was strongly predictive.

The association between subaxial ankylosis and increased risk of AAF may be explained by adjacent-segment mechanics: reduced motion in subaxial segments may shift stress cranially, potentially increasing load transmission through the C1/2 joint to the anterior arch. The lack of significant differences in global subaxial balance parameters suggests focal kinematics may be more relevant than overall alignment.

Although most patients improved with bracing, fracture nonunion was common without salvage fusion, and persistent incongruity at the C1/2 facet could contribute to secondary osteoarthritis over time. Therefore, mandatory preoperative CT assessment and avoidance of C1 laminectomy without fusion in high-risk cases may be the best approach to prevent this complication.

This retrospective study has limitations, including variable follow-up, lack of a control group, and incomplete evaluation of factors such as bone mineral density and postoperative bracing protocols. Further prospective studies are needed to confirm these findings.

## Conclusions

The incidence of anterior arch fracture following C1 laminectomy without fusion is relatively higher than expected and warrants careful preoperative assessment. CT findings of a large inferior facet angle and the presence of subaxial ankylosis are predictive of a high risk of AAF.
