# "Sternum-Into-Abdomen" Deformity With Vertebral Compression Fractures Managed by Abdominal Compression Following Osteoporotic 2-Level Vertebral Column Resection and Reconstruction

## Abstract

We describe a unique case of abdominal compression causing gastric disturbance after treatment of an osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture and its ultimate management by vertebral column resection (VCR). We propose a new terminology, "sternum-into-abdomen deformity," to describe this condition.

### Background

Management of osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures primarily focuses on pain control and deformity reduction. VCR, to alleviate abdominal compression due to severe kyphosis after osteoporotic compression fractures, has never been reported in the literature to our knowledge.

## Case Report

A 73-year-old cachectic patient underwent vertebroplasty for a midthoracic compression fracture with progressive, severe kyphosis. His condition worsened, and spinal reconstruction with a 2-level VCR restored more normal sagittal alignment and decreased his gastric compression. His back pain decreased, and his ability to tolerate oral intake returned.

## Methods

This case report focuses on a single patient. The hospital and office charts were reviewed. Reports of prior treatment of his compression fracture were analyzed.

## Results

The patient, who had undergone vertebroplasty for a midthoracic compression fracture, developed severe kyphosis. His sternum and lower rib cage impinged on his gastrointestinal tract, leading to early satiety and weight loss. The patient was successfully treated with a 2-level vertebral column resection (VCR) and spinal fusion. His back pain decreased, and his ability to tolerate food improved.

## Discussion

Osteoporotic compression fractures in elderly patients cause pain, diminished mobility, depression, decreased quality of life, and difficulties with activities of daily living. Multiple fractures can lead to progressive kyphosis, resulting in pulmonary and gastrointestinal dysfunction due to reduced thoracic/abdominal cavity volumes. Gastrointestinal issues may include gastroesophageal reflux, hiatal hernia, and dysphagia. The patient presented here had severe thoracolumbar kyphosis, resulting in gastric compression by the sternum and lower rib cage, leading to early satiety despite a good appetite. This patient became cachectic due to inadequate food intake, losing 25 lbs. 

We propose the term "sternum-into-abdomen deformity" for patients with osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures that result in this condition. The term describes severe kyphosis with prominent abdominal wall pressure from the sternum and lower rib cage, which causes significant gastrointestinal dysfunction.

### Management

Pain management is the primary focus after compression fractures, but nutritional status and digestive function should also be evaluated. Patients should be assessed for underlying causes of osteoporosis and treated accordingly. VCR, for improving sagittal alignment and reducing gastrointestinal compression in severe osteoporotic kyphotic deformities, is a viable option in these cases, especially when performed through an all-posterior approach.

### Surgical Treatment

Patients with pain and a sternum-into-abdomen deformity, as well as respiratory and digestive dysfunction, may benefit from corrective surgical treatment over vertebroplasty or kyphoplasty, provided they can tolerate posterior spinal reconstruction. However, those with severe malnutrition or respiratory compromise may not be suitable candidates for surgery until their conditions improve.

## Conclusion

This case report illustrates the treatment of a patient with severe kyphosis resulting in abdominal compression. Surgical realignment through VCR and fusion improved the patient's postural alignment and alleviated his gastric compression. The proposed terminology "sternum-into-abdomen deformity" can be used to describe this condition.

## Key Points

- A new term, "sternum-into-abdomen deformity," is proposed to describe severe kyphosis with gastric compression caused by vertebral compression fractures.
- VCR and spinal fusion can improve sagittal alignment and reduce gastric compression, leading to better quality of life for patients with this condition.
- Osteoporotic compression fractures in elderly patients often lead to progressive kyphosis, causing gastrointestinal dysfunction and weight loss.
