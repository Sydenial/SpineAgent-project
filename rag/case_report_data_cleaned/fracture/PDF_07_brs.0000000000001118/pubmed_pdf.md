# Treating Huge Tear-Drop Fracture of Axis With Trapezoidal Bone  
A Case Report and Literature Review

## Abstract  
Teardrop fracture of axis is rarely seen, especially the huge type. The surgical technique is demanding due to the special anatomical structure and difficulty with bone grafting. Moreover, the surgical approach is controversial in the literature.

## Introduction  
Schneider and Kahn first described teardrop fracture of the cervical spine in 1956, which was defined as the fracture of the anterior-inferior corner of the vertebra. But teardrop fracture of the axis is rarely seen, only accounting for 1% to 3% of cervical fractures. The mechanism of the injury is hyperextension or hyperflexion. Small teardrop fractures can be conservatively managed, but huge types still require surgery due to intervertebral disc injury and instability. Because of the special anatomical structure of the axis, removal of the fractured bone leads to local instability. Therefore, the surgical strategy is crucial to restore stability and cervical alignment.

## Methods  
A 51-year-old male patient suffered from neck pain after falling from a bicycle. Neck movement was limited with no neurological compromise. X-ray showed a huge teardrop fracture of the anterior-inferior corner of the axis, with narrowing of the C2/3 intervertebral disc. Fusion with a self-designed tricortical trapezoidal iliac bone was performed.

## Results  
Treating the huge teardrop fracture of the axis by anterior bone grafting with self-designed tricortical trapezoidal iliac bone is effective and stable. A 3-month follow-up showed that fusion was achieved, the upper cervical curvature was restored, and neck pain disappeared.

## Discussion  
Most researchers consider the mechanism of injury for axial teardrop fractures to be hyperextension, but others support a combination of hyperflexion and hyperextension. The treatment of axial teardrop fractures remains controversial, with different surgical approaches based on the size and location of the fractured bone, extent of intervertebral disc injury, and local instability. Anterior bone grafting and fusion are favored for reconstructing the anterior and middle columns and restoring intervertebral height.

## Case Report  
A 51-year-old male patient experienced neck pain after falling from a bicycle, with limited neck movement and no neurological compromise. X-ray showed a huge teardrop fracture of the anterior-inferior corner of the axis, with narrowing of the C2/3 intervertebral disc. MRI revealed discontinuity of the anterior-inferior cortex of the axis, with hyperintensity in the C2/3 intervertebral disc and precervical soft tissue. A Philadelphia collar was used to immobilize the neck, and two days later, anterior discectomy and self-designed tricortical trapezoidal iliac bone grafting with instrumentation was performed. The patient’s neck pain disappeared one week after surgery, and the trapezoidal bone showed no signs of dislocation and achieved fusion at the 3-month follow-up.

## Conclusion  
Due to the special anatomical structure and difficulty with bone grafting in huge teardrop fractures of the axis, surgical management is quite demanding. Self-designed tricortical trapezoidal iliac bone provided an adequate fusion area for bone grafting, restoring normal intervertebral height and cervical alignment. The midterm outcome is satisfactory.

## Key Points  
- Huge teardrop fractures destruct the anterior longitudinal ligament and intervertebral disc, leading to local instability.  
- Anterior grafting and fusion reconstruct the anterior and middle columns, stabilizing C2 and C3.  
- Self-designed trapezoidal bone provides a large fusion area and restores normal intervertebral height and upper cervical alignment.
