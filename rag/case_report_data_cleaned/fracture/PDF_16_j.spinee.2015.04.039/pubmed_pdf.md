# Traumatic high-grade L5-S1 spondylolisthesis with vertebral physeal injury

## Case Report
An 11-year-old girl was struck by a collapsed beam and injured her back during the 2008 Wenchuan earthquake. Her presentation was severe lumbosacral pain and numbness in both lower limbs. Neurologic examination showed diminished sensation below L5 and Grade 4 power in both tibialis anterior and posterior muscles. Lumbar plain radiographs showed unilateral multiple transverse process fractures (L1-L5 on the right) and almost total anterolisthesis of L5 over S1. Computed tomography demonstrated severe (Grade 4) anterior slippage of L5 with an intact L5-S1 intervertebral disc and physeal cartilage of the S1 superior endplate (Fig. 1).

The patient underwent L5-S1 laminectomy, reduction, and posterolateral fusion 3 days after injury. The L5-S1 intervertebral disc was confirmed intact intraoperatively, and the torn S1 physeal cartilage was reduced in conjunction with the reduction of the slippage. Postoperatively, lower limb power improved to Grade 5, and sensation returned to normal. She underwent another surgery to remove the internal fixation 3 years later. At 6.5 years after the primary surgery, radiographs showed that the heights of L5 and S1 were slightly increased (2 mm) compared with those at the initial injury. Lumbar magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a repaired L5-S1 intervertebral disc. Surprisingly, a mild primary lumbar scoliosis appeared 2 months after surgery and remained stable at 27 degrees from 3 years after operation (Fig. 2).

## Discussion
Traumatic lumbosacral spondylolisthesis in children is extremely rare [1,2]. In the developing spine, a zone of relative weakness occurs at the junction of physeal cartilage and vertebral body ossification [3,4]. In this patient, a drastic tangential force likely led to a Salter-Harris Type 1 physeal injury. By posterior reduction, we reset the avulsed physeal cartilage. For reconstruction, we chose posterolateral fusion rather than the usually recommended interbody fusion because of its damage to the growth plate of the anterior column. Ultimately, the intervertebral disc was repaired, and longitudinal growth of the vertebral body was well preserved (Fig. 2).
