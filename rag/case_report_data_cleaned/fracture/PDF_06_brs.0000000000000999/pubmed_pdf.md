# Complete Remodeling After Conservative Treatment of a Severely Angulated Odontoid E  
A Case Report

## Abstract
Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) is a rare genetic disorder resulting in low bone mass and bone fragility, predisposing patients to fractures that often occur at a young age. Although odontoid fractures are uncommon in this population, conservative management is generally accepted as a safe and efficient treatment for fractures in children. This case report presents a 5-year-old female with OI who experienced a type III odontoid fracture, which was initially treated conservatively. Despite severe angulation observed during follow-up, the fracture remodeled successfully, demonstrating the potential for conservative treatment in similar cases.

## Introduction
Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) is a genetic disorder characterized by low bone mass and increased bone fragility. Although odontoid fractures are uncommon in OI, they pose significant challenges due to the fragile bone structure. Conservative management has been widely accepted in pediatric odontoid fractures, though surgical intervention is often recommended when conservative treatment fails, particularly in the presence of severe angulation or displacement.

## Methods
A 5-year-old female diagnosed with OI type I presented with neck pain after falling backward from a rocking chair. A CT scan confirmed a type III odontoid fracture. The patient was initially treated with a rigid cervical orthosis. Follow-up imaging at 1 and 2 months showed progressive angulation, but conservative management continued as the space for the spinal cord was adequate, and the patient's history of OI was considered.

## Results
Eight months post-injury, the patient had no clinical symptoms, and CT scans revealed osseous healing of the fracture with remodeling of the odontoid to normal morphology. 

## Discussion
This is the first reported case of a successful conservative treatment for an isolated type III odontoid fracture in a patient with OI. Conservative management is typically recommended for type III fractures without instability or severe neurological symptoms. Although OI patients often have poor bone quality, this case demonstrates that conservative treatment can lead to healing and remodeling, even in the presence of severe angulation.

## Case Report

### History and Examination
The patient, a 5-year-old female diagnosed with OI type I, presented to the emergency department after falling backward from a rocking chair. She complained of neck pain but had no neurological deficits. Initial imaging suggested a fracture at C2, which was confirmed by CT as a type III odontoid fracture without angulation or displacement. The patient was treated with a rigid Philadelphia collar for stabilization.

### Imaging and Treatment
At 1 month, follow-up CT scans showed kyphotic angulation and anterior displacement of the odontoid. The decision was made to continue conservative treatment. By 3 months, the fracture was healing without instability. After 8 months, the patient had no complaints, and follow-up CT revealed complete healing and remodeling of the odontoid.

## Conclusion
This case report illustrates that even in patients with OI, severely angulated odontoid fractures may heal and remodel successfully under conservative management. This treatment strategy is effective for selected cases, but careful patient evaluation is necessary.

## Key Points
- Osteogenesis imperfecta predisposes patients to fractures; however, odontoid fractures are very rare in this population.
- This is the first case report of a type III odontoid fracture in a young child with OI, who developed progressive angulation and displacement over the course of conservative treatment.
- Despite the angulation and displacement, treatment with external immobilization and close follow-up led to complete healing and remodeling of the odontoid.
