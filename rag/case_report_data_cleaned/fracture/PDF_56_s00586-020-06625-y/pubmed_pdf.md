# Emergency surgical management of cervical spine fracture-dislocation with acute paraplegia in COVID-19 (Coronavirus disease 2019)-suspected patient: first experience from a German spine centre

## Abstract

**Purpose**: We present an organized hospital plan for the management of Coronavirus disease (COVID-19) patients requiring emergency surgical interventions. To introduce a multidisciplinary approach for the management of COVID-19-infected patients and to report the first operated patient in the Corona unit.  
**Methods**: A detailed presentation of the hospital plan for a separate Corona unit with its intensive care unit and operating rooms. Description of the management of the first spine surgery case treated in this unit.  
**Results**: The Corona unit showed a practical approach for the management of an emergency cervical spine fracture-dislocation with acute paralysis. The patient is a 92-year-old female. The mechanism of injury was a simple fall during her stay in the internal medicine department. The patient had no other injuries and was awake and oriented. The patient did not have the clinical symptom of COVID-19, and the COVID-19 test result from the referring hospital was not available on admission in our emergency room. Education of the medical staff and organization of the operating theatre facilitated the management of the patient without an increased risk of spreading the infection.  
**Conclusions**: The current COVID-19 pandemic requires extraordinary organization of medical and surgical care. It is possible to manage an infected or potentially infected patient surgically, but a multidisciplinary plan is necessary to protect other patients and the medical staff.

**Keywords**: COVID-19, Emergency, Cervical fracture, Corona ward, Surgical planning

## Introduction

### Coronavirus disease 2019

In late 2019, a series of severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS) cases were reported in China. The causative organism was detected, and the term Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) was introduced. A few weeks later, the number of cases increased dramatically, and cases outside China were detected. Confirmation of human-to-human transmission led to the application of specific measures to reduce the rate of infection and allow medical systems to manage the exponential demand on intensive care beds for such patients. By 11 March 2020, as the number of countries involved reached 114 with more than 118,000 cases and over 4,000 deaths, the WHO declared the pandemic status.

### Start of national measures in Germany

On 27 January 2020, a Chinese woman from Shanghai informed the company she was working for in Bavaria about her Coronavirus infection. Her German colleague was then tested positive. He was the first confirmed case in Germany. Starting from this day, the national authorities in Germany began a general plan to prepare the medical system for a dangerous situation. Hospitals were prepared to accommodate many seriously ill Corona patients under high pressure, and the government’s activities aimed to ensure the best possible care.

Efforts were made to expand intensive care capacity, with the number of beds increasing from 28,000 to 40,000 and ventilation places increasing to 30,000. The rescheduling of elective surgical procedures was implemented to allow hospitals to manage the expected increase in the number of COVID-19 patients and reduce the risk of postoperative infections.

## Methods

### Changes in spine surgery practice

In our hospital, the spine surgery department rescheduled all elective operations. The surgical interventions were limited to four categories of spine disorders: unstable spine fractures, acute paralysis due to spinal canal stenosis or disc prolapse, spinal oncology, and spondylodiscitis patients with sepsis or neurological deficits. Outpatient visits were limited to patients with a planned first postoperative examination or those presenting with acute spine disorders.

### Corona department

An emergency plan for COVID-19 patients was developed, which included a separate emergency room with a separate entrance, an isolation station for positively tested patients, and two operating theatres for emergency surgical interventions. A clear strategy for patient referral was established to ensure proper placement in the hospital.

### Staff protecting measures

Protecting the staff is a priority to maintain continuous medical service. Staff who were on vacation or visited a risk zone were instructed to stay in home isolation for 14 days. The medical staff was trained to deal with infected or potentially infected patients, and protective measures such as masks and eye protection were mandatory.

## Results

### Case presentation

On 22 April 2020, a 92-year-old female patient was referred to our emergency department with acute paraplegia after cervical spine trauma. The trauma occurred during her hospital stay in the internal medicine department. The patient was considered a suspected COVID-19 case and was immediately admitted to the Corona emergency room. The neurological examination showed incomplete paraplegia below the neurological level of C6 (ASIA B). A traumatic fracture-dislocation C5-6 with spinal cord oedema was diagnosed. Due to the patient's unstable fracture with acute paralysis, an emergency surgical intervention was planned, involving posterior open reduction and stabilization combined with anterior decompression and fusion.

### Modifications in the Corona operating rooms

Before surgery, the Corona unit had two equipped operating rooms—one for suspected COVID-19 patients and the other for confirmed cases. Protective measures were taken to prevent unnecessary movement of staff, and the medical staff was trained to handle infected materials during surgery. During the operation, the anesthesia staff used the same protective measures as the surgical team.

## Discussion

### Triage during the pandemic

The COVID-19 outbreak has induced significant changes in spine surgery practices. Elective spine surgeries were postponed to save hospital capacities for COVID-19 patients and to prevent nosocomial infections. Only urgent surgeries, such as those for neurological deficits, spondylodiscitis, tumours, and fractures, were performed.

### Operating room considerations

Dedicated operating theatres for COVID-19 patients were established, equipped with operating microscopes and C-arms. The operating room measures were implemented at the very beginning of the pandemic to ensure the safety of both patients and staff. These steps were essential to manage emergencies like the cervical spine fracture-dislocation presented in this case.

## Conclusion

The current COVID-19 pandemic has required extraordinary organization in the medical and surgical care of patients. With a multidisciplinary approach and well-defined protocols, it is possible to manage infected or potentially infected patients surgically while minimizing the risk of infection to other patients and medical staff.
