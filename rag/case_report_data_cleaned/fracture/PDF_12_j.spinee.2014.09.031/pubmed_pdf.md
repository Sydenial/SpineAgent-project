# Hyperextension Injury of the C1–C2 Cervical Spine With Neurologic Deficits: Horizontal Splitting Fracture of the C1 Arch

## Case Report

A 45-year-old man was admitted to the emergency department of another medical center after a fall from a height of 7 m accompanied by neck hyperextension. He was transferred to our hospital 2 days after the injury. On admission, he was in a quadriplegic state, with Grade 0 motor power of the upper extremities and Frankel classification Grade B on physical examination.

A lateral cervical computed tomography (CT) image revealed a Type II odontoid fracture and a splitting fracture of the C1 anterior and posterior arches. The atlantodens interval was normal. A Jefferson fracture was observed on the axial CT view. On coronal CT imaging, an oblique occipital condyle fracture accompanied by an odontoid fracture was identified. Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a signal change in the spinal cord at the C2 level, and no rupture of the transverse ligament was observed.

Based on the diagnosis of central cord syndrome with associated occipital condyle, Jefferson, and odontoid fractures, the patient underwent surgical intervention 3 days after the injury. Postoperative plain radiographs confirmed excellent reduction and fixation of the dens. Magnetic resonance imaging performed 5 days after surgery demonstrated expansion and increased signal intensity compared with the preoperative findings. Neurologic symptoms in the upper extremities showed gradual recovery of sensory deficits over time.
