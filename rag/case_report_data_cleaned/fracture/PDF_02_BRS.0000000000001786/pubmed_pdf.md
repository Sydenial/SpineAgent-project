# Temporary Iliac Fixation to Salvage an Acute L4 Chance Fracture Following Pedicle Screw Fixation for Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis

## Abstract

The aim of this study was to describe a technique of temporary distal fixation to the ilium to salvage an acute L4 fracture following a T3-L4 kyphoscoliosis correction. Pedicle fracture is a possible complication in pedicle screw fixation for scoliosis, which may lead to postoperative instability, resulting in loss of fixation and pseudoarthrosis. This report highlights the salvage treatment of a Chance fracture that occurred in the lower instrumented vertebra following deformity correction for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) without sacrificing further distal motion segments.

## Introduction

Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) is commonly treated with pedicle screw fixation. A potential complication of this treatment is pedicle fracture, which can lead to significant issues such as loss of fixation and pseudoarthrosis. This case study demonstrates a technique to address such complications effectively, focusing on a salvage treatment for an acute L4 Chance fracture that occurred after a T3-L4 correction for kyphoscoliosis.

## Methods

A retrospective chart and radiograph review of a 13-year-old female who underwent surgical treatment for correction of AIS was performed. The initial surgery involved a T3-L4 pedicle screw construct, including Smith-Petersen osteotomies at T12-L1 and L1-2. The patient had an initial good position of the implants and balanced correction, as confirmed by posteroanterior radiographs.

## Results

After the T3-L4 correction, the patient developed an L4 Chance fracture on the third postoperative day. Attempts to salvage the fracture with an infralaminar hook were unsuccessful. A second revision was performed, which involved replacing the hook and adding bilateral temporary fixation to the ilium through side-to-side connectors. The temporary fixation was removed after six months, with successful fracture healing and maintenance of the scoliosis correction at a two-year follow-up.

## Case Report

### Surgical Treatment

The patient, a 13-year-old female, underwent posterior spinal fusion for her kyphoscoliosis, which included a T3-L4 pedicle screw construct. Postoperative radiographs confirmed a successful deformity correction. However, three days after surgery, the patient developed new left radicular leg pain, and a subsequent CT scan revealed a Chance fracture at L4, along with a left pedicle fracture and focal kyphosis at L3-4.

The patient was returned to the operating room one week later, where the left L4 pedicle screw was replaced with an L4 upgoing infralaminar hook. Compression was applied to reduce the deformity, and a longer pedicle screw was inserted on the right side. Two weeks later, the L4 hook dislodged, prompting a second revision. The revision included securing the hook and adding bilateral temporary rods extending from the iliac screws placed in each hemipelvis through side-to-side connectors.

### Postoperative Course

The patient's symptoms resolved, and neurological function normalized. Six months after the revision, the bilateral iliac screws, connectors, and rod extensions were removed. At the two-year follow-up, the patient was pain-free, and radiographs demonstrated sustained correction of her deformity.

## Discussion

This report describes a technique to salvage an acute L4 Chance fracture of the lower instrumented vertebra following pedicle screw fixation for AIS. By temporarily extending the construct to the ilium, the fracture healed successfully, and the deformity correction was maintained without the need for further distal fusion. The technique employed in this case offers a potential solution for similar complications in scoliosis surgery, preserving important lumbar motion segments and avoiding the need for additional distal fusions.

## Conclusion

Temporary extension of the construct to the ilium successfully salvaged an acute L4 Chance fracture following a T3-L4 construct for kyphoscoliosis. This technique allowed for successful fracture healing and maintenance of deformity correction without the need to fuse further distal segments.

## Key Points

- Acute pedicle fractures leading to kyphotic deformity and loss of fixation can occur at the distal end of a pedicle screw construct for scoliosis.
- Temporary extension of the construct to the ilium can salvage an acute L4 Chance fracture without the need for fusion of further distal lumbar segments.
