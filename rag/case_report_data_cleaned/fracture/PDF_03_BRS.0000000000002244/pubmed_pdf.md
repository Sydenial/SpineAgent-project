# Acute and Hyper-Acute Thoracolumbar Corpectomy for Traumatic Burst Fractures Using a Mini-Open Lateral Approach

## Study Design

Retrospective chart review.

## Objective

The study aimed to examine the feasibility of acute (<24 hours) and hyper-acute (<8 hours) treatment of traumatic burst fractures.

## Summary of Background Data

Historically, traumatic thoracolumbar fractures were considered "acute" within a timeframe of 24 hours. Patient polytrauma and coordination between multiple surgical specialties and hospital resources affect the time to treatment. The mini-open lateral approach for thoracolumbar corpectomy eliminates the need for an access surgeon, which may allow for earlier surgical intervention.

## Methods

Sixteen patients treated within 24 hours with a mini-open lateral corpectomy for traumatic spinal pathology were reviewed for preoperative, perioperative, and postoperative data. Neurologic status was assessed using ASIA scores. Fractures occurred primarily from L1 to L3. Wide-footprint expandable titanium devices were used in 75% of patients. All patients received supplemental fixation.

## Results

- Average time from injury to admission to the hospital was 1.8 hours.
- Average time from the ER to the operating room was 8.2 hours.
- Average operating room time was 2.7 hours.
- Eight patients required ≤8 hours from injury event to surgical initiation, while seven were treated between 8 and 24 hours.
- Blood loss averaged 646 mL with no intraoperative complications.
- One perioperative complication occurred, and one patient developed an asymptomatic inferior vertebral body fracture.
- Length of hospital stay averaged 6 days.
- At last follow-up, nearly all patients experienced full or near complete neurologic recovery, with at least one ASIA grade improvement seen in 73% of patients, and 20% improving two grades or more.

## Conclusions

These results suggest that the mini-open lateral approach allows for immediate decompression for hyper-acute (<8 hours) treatment of spinal burst fractures in eligible patients. Additionally, this approach allows for rapid recovery.

## Introduction

The treatment of acute fractures of the thoracolumbar spinal column requires careful consideration of both the injury and the patient’s overall health status. Recent advances in mini-open approaches, including the lateral approach, have allowed for more efficient surgical interventions. The timing of surgical intervention remains a critical factor for improving neurological outcomes following traumatic injuries. Early surgical intervention can facilitate recovery of neural anatomy and reduce the impact of injury. Studies have demonstrated that surgical decompression within a defined time window, particularly within 72 hours, improves neurological function. The goal of this study was to evaluate the feasibility of acute and hyper-acute (<8 hours) treatment of thoracolumbar burst fractures.

## Methods

An institutional review board (IRB) approved retrospective chart review was conducted for patients treated with mini-open lateral corpectomy for thoracolumbar burst fractures. Preoperative, perioperative, and postoperative data were reviewed, including time to surgery and neurological status using ASIA scores at pre- and post-op time points. The patients included in the review ranged from 19 to 69 years old, with the majority being male. Patients’ ASIA scores at admission included grades A, C, D, and E. The fractures were primarily located at L1, L2, and L3, and the devices used were expandable titanium corpectomy cages.

## Surgical Technique

The mini-open lateral approach for thoracolumbar corpectomy was performed with the patient in a lateral decubitus position using fluoroscopic guidance to locate the appropriate levels. This technique is similar to the extreme lateral interbody fusion (XLIF) technique, with adjustments based on whether the spine is thoracic or lumbar. Once the initial access is performed, sequential dilators are placed, and a table-mounted split-blade retractor is used for direct visualization of the fracture. Standard surgical techniques for corpectomy and decompression are applied. The vertebral body replacement (VBR) is performed using an expandable titanium device, with supplemental fixation provided as deemed appropriate by the surgeon.

## Case Reports

### Case 1:
A 21-year-old Airman (United States Air Force) with an ASIA grade of C was treated with a mini-open lateral corpectomy using an expandable titanium cage with anterior fixation. The patient was upgraded to an ASIA grade D at two months postoperatively, and kyphosis was corrected from 20° to 1°.

### Case 2:
A 19-year-old male with a traumatic thoracolumbar burst fracture was treated with a similar approach. Postoperatively, the patient was upgraded to an ASIA grade E and returned to normal activities within 12 months.

## Discussion

Traumatic fractures of the thoracolumbar spinal column require prompt evaluation of fracture instability and potential spinal cord injury. Early intervention can provide better outcomes, including improved neurological recovery. Several studies have highlighted the benefits of early decompression, especially within 72 hours, which has been shown to improve neurological function and reduce hospital stay and complications. The mini-open lateral approach presents a less invasive alternative to traditional approaches, facilitating earlier intervention and recovery. Our study supports the use of this technique for acute and hyper-acute management of thoracolumbar burst fractures.
