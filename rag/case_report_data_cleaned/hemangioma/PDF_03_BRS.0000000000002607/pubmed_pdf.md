# Anterior to Dorsal Root Entry Zone Myelotomy (ADREZotomy): A New Surgical Approach for the Treatment of Ventrolateral Deep Intramedullary Spinal Cord Cavernous Malformations

## Abstract

**Study Design:** A retrospective review of prospectively collected data.  
**Objective:** To confirm the feasibility of using anterior to dorsal root entry zone myelotomy (ADREZotomy) for the removal of ventrolateral deep intramedullary spinal cord cavernous malformations (ISCCMs).  
**Summary of Background Data:** Surgical removal of ventrolateral deep intramedullary spinal cord cavernous malformations (ISCCMs) is highly risky and remains problematic.  
**Methods:** A total of 10 patients underwent ADREZotomy for the removal of ventrolateral intrinsic CCMs. The Frakl grading system was used to evaluate preoperative and postoperative stages. American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) Scale scores were also obtained.  
**Results:** Gross total resections were performed in all 10 patients. Immediately after surgery, 8 (80.0%) patients had stable neurological function, 1 patient improved, and 1 worsened. Follow-up showed that 2 (20%) patients improved, and 8 (80%) remained stable with no recurrences or additional neurological deterioration.  
**Conclusion:** ADREZotomy is a minimally invasive technique for removing cervical and thoracic ventrolateral deep ISCCMs, without disrupting critical spinal cord tracts or requiring broad bone exposure.

## Keywords

Cavernous malformations, dorsal root entry zone, intramedullary tumor, myelotomy, outcome, resection, surgical approach, spinal cord, surgical technique, vascular malformations.

## Level of Evidence: 4

## Introduction

Intramedullary spinal cord cavernous malformations (ISCCMs) constitute 5% to 12% of all spinal vascular diseases. These lesions can cause neurological deficits due to acute macrohemorrhage or repetitive intralesional microhemorrhages. Surgical resection is often the only treatment for symptomatic ISCCMs. However, for ventrolateral deep ISCCMs, traditional approaches can be problematic. We introduce a new myelotomy technique, anterior to dorsal root entry zone myelotomy (ADREZotomy), which avoids disrupting vital spinal cord tracts while offering a minimally invasive surgical approach.

## Methods

We retrospectively analyzed 10 patients treated between July 2010 and March 2017 for ventrolateral deep ISCCMs. The patients were selected from a spinal vascular disease database, and all procedures were approved by the local ethics board. MRI scans were used for preoperative diagnosis, and follow-up MRIs were performed to ensure complete resection.

## Patients

The 10 patients (5 males, 5 females) had a mean age of 39.9 years (range: 22–63 years). The ISCCMs were located in 8 cervical and 2 thoracic regions. Symptoms included neck pain, upper and lower limb weakness, and sensory disturbances. The duration of symptoms ranged from several months to years. The Frankel grading system and ASIA Scale scores were used to evaluate neurological function before and after surgery.

## Surgical Technique

All patients underwent ADREZotomy using a three-point Mayfield head holder for cervical lesions. Intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring (IONM) was employed during the procedure. The spinal cord was carefully exposed, and the ADREZotomy was performed to remove the lesion without disturbing surrounding spinal tracts.

## Follow-Up Data

All 10 patients were followed up with clinical assessments and spinal MRIs. The follow-up period ranged from 3 months to 6.8 years (mean, 1.4 years). No recurrences or significant neurological deterioration were observed during follow-up.

## Results

In total, 9 (90%) patients presented with mild symptoms before surgery. ADREZotomy was successfully performed in all cases, with gross total resections achieved. Immediate postoperative neurological function remained stable in 8 patients, improved in 1, and worsened in 1. The follow-up period showed favorable outcomes in 9 patients, with no neurological deterioration or recurrence of ISCCMs.

## Illustrative Case

### Case 5

A 30-year-old woman presented with neck and right shoulder pain. A spinal MRI indicated a ventrolateral cervical ISCCM at the C2-C3 level. Neurological examination showed normal motor strength but sensory deficits. ADREZotomy was performed, resulting in complete resection of the lesion. Postoperative follow-up showed improvement in motor function and no residual lesions.

## Discussion

### Treatment Strategies for Ventrolateral Deep ISCCMs

Ventrolateral deep ISCCMs are associated with worse functional outcomes compared to superficially located lesions. Our findings support the use of ADREZotomy for resection of deep lesions, even in patients with mild symptoms, due to its minimally invasive nature and ability to preserve important spinal cord tracts.

### Surgical Outcomes of ADREZotomy

In our series, 9 (90%) patients experienced favorable outcomes after surgery. This technique shows promise in treating ventrolateral deep ISCCMs, with no significant long-term neurological deterioration or recurrence.

### Anatomical Basis of ADREZotomy

The ADREZotomy provides a surgical corridor that allows for the resection of ventrolateral ISCCMs without disrupting critical spinal tracts such as the lateral corticospinal tract. The technique minimizes the risk of postoperative complications compared to traditional approaches.

## Conclusions

ADREZotomy is a safe and effective surgical technique for the removal of ventrolateral deep ISCCMs. It offers a minimally invasive approach, preserves spinal cord tracts, and provides favorable outcomes with minimal risk of neurological deterioration.
