# Intradural Extramedullary Spinal Hydatid Cyst Causing Bone Destruction

## Case Report

A 66-year-old man presented with complaints of back pain, paresthesia, and weakness in the right lower limb for the last 3 months. Magnetic resonance imaging examination showed a few lobulated cystic lesions with a regular contour, measuring 3 × 1.5 cm in the largest dimension, located in the intradural extramedullary region at the level of T11–T12. There was also a cystic lesion in the extradural region. The lesions were hypointense on T1-weighted images and hyperintense on T2-weighted images with a hypointense rim. After intravenous gadolinium injection, there was no contrast enhancement on T1-weighted images. The medulla spinalis was compressed, especially on the left side (Fig. 1). Additionally, bone destruction was detected in the adjacent vertebral pedicle (Fig. 2). The radiologic diagnosis was consistent with a hydatid cyst. The diagnosis was confirmed histopathologically after surgery.

## Discussion

Spinal hydatid disease constitutes only 1% of all hydatid cysts. In 90% of spinal cases, the disease occurs in the bone and the epidural space [1]. Intradural extramedullary spinal hydatid cyst is extremely rare [1,2]. Therefore, it should be considered in the differential diagnosis of intradural extramedullary spinal masses.
