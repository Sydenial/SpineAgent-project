# Aggressive thoracic vertebral lesion with intraosseous and epidural components

## Case Report

A 51-year-old woman presented with a history of low backache and progressive spastic paraparesis of 1 year's duration. Magnetic resonance imaging of the thoracic spine demonstrated an enhancing lesion in the T8 vertebral body that was hyperintense on both T1- and T2-weighted images and showed prominent secondary trabeculations. These signal changes extended into the lamina, pedicles, and articular processes. An associated soft tissue component was seen in the paravertebral and epidural regions, causing compression of the spinal cord at this level.

Computed tomography revealed a decreased height of the T8 vertebral body with multiple lucencies, coarse trabeculations, and a characteristic “polka-dot” appearance. A breach in the posterior cortex of the vertebral body was also noted. Spinal angiography demonstrated a tumor blush arising from bilateral T8 segmental artery feeders.

Following endovascular embolization of the lesion, the patient underwent T8 corpectomy with excision of the epidural component and stabilization using a cage and pedicle screws. Histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of a vertebral hemangioma.
