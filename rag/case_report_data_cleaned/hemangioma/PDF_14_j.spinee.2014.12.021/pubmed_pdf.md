# Isolated spinal sarcoidosis

## Case Report

A 15-year-old man was admitted with complaints of loss of strength below both knees and inability to walk for the past 2 weeks. Physical examination revealed hyperactive deep tendon reflexes of the lower extremities, clonus, and decreased muscle strength (3/5). The patient was referred for brain and spinal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Brain MRI was normal, whereas spinal MRI revealed an intraspinal extradural solid mass lesion at the level of the T6–T8 vertebrae, measuring $13\times26\times42$ mm. The lesion was hyperintense on T2-weighted images and showed vivid enhancement except for the central portion after contrast medium injection. The spinal cord was displaced anteriorly and compressed by the lesion, and the neural foramina were obliterated at the T6–T7 and T7–T8 levels.

A T6–T8 laminectomy and excision of the mass were performed. Histopathologic examination demonstrated noncaseating granulomas, and a diagnosis of sarcoidosis was established. There was no evidence of systemic involvement. Following 3 months of postoperative corticosteroid therapy, the patient showed rapid recovery.
