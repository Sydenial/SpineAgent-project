# Long-term survival case of malignant glomus tumor mimicking “dumbbell-shaped” neurogenic tumor

## Abstract

**Purpose:** We report a very rare case of long-term survival of a malignant glomus tumor (MGT) without widespread metastasis.  
**Study participants:** Sixty-three-year-old woman who experienced multiple relapses over a 40-year period beginning with a scapular lesion at age 21.  
**Results:** After four local recurrences, the patient underwent wide resection of the scapula at age 36. Thirteen years later, the patient had a neck tumor on the same side as the scapulectomy. The tumor originated from the C6 spinal nerve and was resected. Twelve years after the surgery, the cervical tumor relapsed with myelopathy at age 61. The tumor infiltrated from the extraspinal canal to the intraspinal canal at the C5/C6 foramen and appeared as a dumbbell-like tumor. Furthermore, local recurrence occurred two years after the operation at age 63. The tumors detected at ages 36 and 63 were confirmed to be histologically identical. They were more than 2 cm in size and arose from a deep location. The basement membrane stained positively for collagen type IV and α-smooth muscle actin on immunohistochemistry. Based on these aspects, the tumor was diagnosed as an MGT.  
**Conclusions:** This is the first case report of MGT where lesions at two time points were confirmed to have common histological features and where long-term survival was observed over a 40-year period.

**Keywords:** Malignant glomus tumor (MGT) · Long-term follow-up · Histology

## Introduction

Glomus tumors are uncommon and account for approximately 1.6% of the 500 consecutive soft tissue tumors reported by the Mayo Clinic. Although glomus tumors are considered benign, some may present unusual clinical features such as large size, deep soft tissue or visceral location, infiltrative growth pattern, or multicentricity. Malignancy is a very rare feature of glomus tumors. To our knowledge, this report is the first to describe the long-term follow-up of a malignant glomus tumor (MGT) based on histological findings over 40 years. No reports have described cases of MGT wherein the lesions were confirmed to have common histological features at two time points, with patients exhibiting long-term survival.

## Case Report

A 49-year-old woman presented with a cervical tumor on the right side. At 21 years of age, she developed right scapular hemangioma, which was finally treated with scapulectomy at age 36 after four recurrences. The cervical tumor was found to have originated from the C6 spinal nerve and was resected.

At 12 years after the resection, the patient developed cervical myelopathy. Magnetic resonance imaging indicated that the spinal cord was compressed by a tumor, which had relapsed at the C6 spinal nerve root and entered the spinal canal via the C5/6 intervertebral foramen.

Moreover, another tumor had arisen from the C5 spinal nerve. The tumors were marginally resected via a two-step operation.

After 2 years (at age 63), local recurrence was observed at the residual C6 spinal nerves. The tumor was simultaneously resected via an anterior-posterior spinal procedure. First, the anterior procedure, which involved wide resection of the tumor and ligation of the vertebral artery, was performed. Thereafter, anterior spinal C5-7 fusion was performed for stabilization.

Second, a dorsal procedure was performed to resect the residual tumor at the intraspinal canal. On histological examination, the tumor exhibited polygonal or dense spindle cells, and hematoxylin-eosin staining indicated hyperplasia around the blood vessels.

Immunohistochemical analysis indicated positive staining of the basement membrane for collagen type IV and α-smooth muscle actin.

Thus, the specimens obtained during the last two operations were histologically diagnosed as glomus tumors.

We obtained specimens from the scapular region from the oncology center where the patient was treated 27 years previously. The tumors resected at ages 36 and 63 were confirmed to be histologically identical.

We have followed up the patient for 3 years since the previous operation, and no recurrence has been observed.

## Discussion

Glomus tumors are generally benign, solitary tumors that can be treated via simple excision. Although a few MGT cases have been reported thus far, they have primarily involved locally invasive tumors, and the occurrence of metastases is rare. Through a retrospective analysis of 52 cases of atypical glomus tumor of the peripheral soft tissues, Folpe et al. observed that malignancy developed in benign glomus tumors. The authors proposed that a deep location and a size greater than 2 cm, atypical mitotic figures, or moderate-to-high-grade nuclear atypia with at least 5 mitoses per 50 high-powered fields are criteria for malignancy. Moreover, cytoplasmic staining for actin and at least focal staining for collagen type IV are highly suggestive of MGT. The features of the present case are consistent with these criteria for malignancy. MGTs are known to exhibit an aggressive clinical course and are believed to be low-grade sarcomas. In the present report, we describe a rare case of scapular MGT that exhibited repeated local relapse without any distant widespread metastasis to other organs for many years. Moreover, infiltration from the scapular region into the intraspinal canal led to the formation of a dumbbell-shaped tumor, which is also a rare phenomenon. The probable underlying mechanism may become evident when considering the course of the suprascapular nerve. The tumor was derived from the C5 and C6 nerves, which are associated with the supra- and infraspinatus muscles. We speculate that the tumor arose from the suprascapular nerve and infiltrated through the nerve root into the intraspinal canal after many years.

In conclusion, we report a rare case in which long-term survival was observed despite the development of MGT. Physicians should carefully consider the treatment of large glomus tumors that arise in non-acral sites. A long-term follow-up of at least 10 years may be required after resection. Nevertheless, further studies are warranted to determine the appropriate treatment for MGTs, given its significant potential for aggressive behavior.
