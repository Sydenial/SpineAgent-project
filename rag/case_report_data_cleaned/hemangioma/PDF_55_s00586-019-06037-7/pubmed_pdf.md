# A Comprehensive Review of Adult-Onset Spinal Teratomas: Analysis of Factors Related to Outcomes and Recurrences

## Abstract

A comprehensive review along with an illustrative case managed at the authors’ institute is presented. Materials and methods: An online database search was performed for literature review. Results: A 26-year-old male presented with acute-onset neurological deficits, and imaging revealed features of a conus teratoma. Subtotal resection was performed, and the patient achieved fair recovery. Including the present case, a total of 146 cases have been reported and analyzed. The mean age was 39.6 years (range 18–85 years), and males predominated. Limb weakness and backache were the most common symptoms. The majority of lesions were intramedullary and located in the thoracolumbar region. Complete resection was achieved in 45% of cases. All except one were mature teratomas. Recurrences were noted in nine cases (6.1%). Outcomes were good/excellent in 60% and fair/poor in 18% of cases. The presence of pain, absence of limb weakness, and complete resection were significantly associated with good outcomes. Subtotal resection had significantly higher recurrence rates than complete resection. Four deaths (2.7%) were reported. Conclusions: Total surgical resection is the standard treatment and appears beneficial for outcomes and recurrence prevention. Recurrences are rare and may be managed with re-surgery. The present report represents the eighth case of adult spinal teratoma with acute-onset presentation.

## Introduction

Spinal teratomas are rare tumors, constituting less than 1% of central nervous system tumors. They are more common in the pediatric population, particularly in the sacrococcygeal region. In adults, the thoracolumbar region is the most frequent location, and adult-onset spinal teratomas remain rare, with fewer than 150 cases reported to date. Common symptoms include pain, progressive limb weakness, and bowel or bladder disturbances. Acute-onset presentations are rare. This report describes a case of mature cystic teratoma of the conus–cauda region in a 26-year-old male with acute-onset paraplegia and bowel/bladder dysfunction, along with a comprehensive literature review.

## Materials and Methods

All published reports of adult-onset spinal teratomas were retrieved and analyzed without language restriction. Adult onset was defined as age $\geq 18$ years. Cases reported from 1888 to the present were included. Sacrococcygeal and pediatric-onset teratomas were excluded. Databases searched included PubMed, Scopus, EMBASE, and Google Scholar using relevant keywords. Demographics, clinical features, lesion location, associated anomalies, imaging findings, histopathology, surgical approach, extent of resection, recurrence, time to recurrence, and outcomes were analyzed. Acute progression was defined as symptom development within two weeks. Outcomes were classified as good/excellent or fair/poor. Statistical analysis was performed using chi-square tests, with significance set at $p < 0.05$.

## Results

### Case Illustration

A 26-year-old male presented with acute-onset weakness of both lower limbs over 48 hours, accompanied by bladder and bowel dysfunction. Neurological examination revealed complete paraplegia. MRI demonstrated a heterogeneous lesion with cranial intramedullary and caudal extramedullary components at the conus–cauda region. Subtotal resection was performed due to difficulty in differentiating tumor from neural tissue. Histopathology confirmed a mature teratoma. At 15-month follow-up, the patient showed modest motor improvement but persistent urinary dysfunction.

### Summary of Reported Cases

A total of 146 adult spinal teratoma cases were analyzed. The mean age was 39.6 years, with a male predominance. Limb weakness and pain were the most frequent symptoms. The thoracolumbar region was most commonly involved, and intramedullary location predominated. Complete resection was achieved in 45% of cases. Recurrence occurred in 6.1% of cases, more commonly after subtotal resection. Good or excellent outcomes were achieved in 60% of patients. Mortality was 2.7%.

## Discussion

### Epidemiology and Clinical Features

Spinal teratomas are distinct from intracranial and sacrococcygeal teratomas. Adult-onset cases are rare and typically present in the thoracolumbar region. Symptoms usually progress slowly, although acute presentations can occur. Lesions may be intramedullary, extramedullary, or extradural, with intramedullary tumors being less common.

### Imaging

MRI is the most sensitive imaging modality. Typical findings include heterogeneous lesions with fat, cystic components, and occasional calcification. Contrast enhancement is variable. Associated congenital anomalies may be present but are less common in adults.

### Embryology and Pathogenesis

Several theories have been proposed, including dysembryogenic and misplaced germ cell theories. The exact pathogenesis remains unclear.

### Histopathology

Teratomas consist of tissues derived from all three germ layers. Most adult spinal teratomas are mature and benign. Immature and malignant forms are rare and associated with poorer prognosis.

### Treatment and Outcome

Surgical resection is the treatment of choice. Complete resection is preferred but may not be feasible for intramedullary lesions, where subtotal resection may be necessary to preserve neurological function. Recurrences are uncommon and typically occur after long latency periods, particularly following subtotal resection. Long-term follow-up is recommended.

## Limitations

Despite being the most comprehensive review to date, some cases may have been missed due to language barriers and indexing limitations.

## Conclusions

Adult-onset spinal teratomas are rare and most commonly involve the thoracolumbar region. Surgical resection remains the mainstay of treatment. Complete resection is associated with better outcomes and lower recurrence rates. Subtotal resection may be appropriate in selected intramedullary cases. Overall outcomes are favorable in the majority of patients, and recurrences are rare.
