# Extradural Spinal Cavernous Malformation Presenting With Radiculopathy

## Case Report
A 10-year-old boy presented with pain radiating down the left lower extremity and gait changes noted by his parents. Neurologic examination revealed left foot plantar-flexion weakness and decreased muscle bulk in the left calf. Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a cystic lesion in the epidural space with mass effect on the left S1 nerve root. MRI signal features included intrinsic T1- and T2-hyperintensity, suggesting blood products or proteinaceous cyst contents. Intraoperatively, a cystic lesion with a greenish-brown wall was found; puncture resulted in leakage of contents consistent with liquefied chronic hematoma. Pathologic evaluation demonstrated fibrovascular tissue with ectatic blood vessels and patchy hemosiderin deposition indicative of a cavernous malformation. Near-total resection of the lesion was achieved without complication.

## Discussion
Cavernous malformations (cavernomas) are low-flow vascular malformations consisting of clusters of dilated blood vessels. These lesions are almost always intra-axial in the brain or spinal cord and have a tendency to hemorrhage repeatedly, producing variable neurologic deficits. Extra-axial cavernous malformations are rare but have been reported [1,2]. Given their atypical location, preoperative recognition may be difficult. However, if a cystic lesion containing complex fluid or blood products is seen on MRI, this entity should be considered in the differential diagnosis. Nerve sheath tumor with cystic necrosis and ganglion cyst of the posterior longitudinal ligament are other cystic lesions that can occur at this location.
