# Exclusively Epidural Spinal Metameric Arteriovenous Shunts: Case Report and Literature Review

Alaa Elkordy, MD, Toshiki Endo, MD, PhD, Knichi Sato, MD, PhD

## Abstract

**Background Context**: Spinal arteriovenous metameric syndrome (SAMS) is a subgroup of spinal arteriovenous malformations (AVMs). Most SAMS cases have intra- and extradural AVMs and suffer from hematomyelia, subarachnoid hemorrhage, or venous congestive myelopathy.  
**Purpose**: To present a rare case of SAMS in which spinal AVMs were exclusively epidural. We reviewed previous literature and evaluated the feasibility of a treatment strategy using endovascular interventions, followed by surgical obliteration.  
**Study Design**: A case report and literature review of SAMS.  
**Methods**: We report a case of a 15-year-old boy suffering from SAMS in which epidural venous ectasia due to extradural AVMs caused spinal cord compression.  
**Results**: The patient was successfully treated with multiple sessions of transarterial embolization followed by open surgery. After the treatment, his neurologic deficits resolved. Postoperative angiography confirmed complete obliteration of the extradural AVM.  
**Conclusions**: Although exclusively epidural spinal AVM is an uncommon type of SAMS, combined endovascular and surgical interventions can be an effective treatment for AVMs, achieving better radiologic outcomes and complete resolution of patient symptoms.

Keywords: Arteriovenous malformation, Cobb syndrome, Epidural arteriovenous shunt, Endovascular treatment, Metameric, Myelopathy, Spinal cord.

## Introduction

Spinal arteriovenous metameric syndrome (SAMS) is a rare clinical form of spinal arteriovenous malformation (AVM), characterized by spinal vascular malformations and skin nevi in the same dermatome. SAMS is also known as Cobb syndrome, named after a case described by Stanley Cobb in 1915. Most patients with SAMS suffer from intradural spinal vascular pathology, which is consistent with its classification as "extradural-intradural arteriovenous malformations" in one of the widely used classification schemes.

This report presents an unusual case of SAMS involving exclusively epidural spinal AVM. The patient suffered myelopathy due to midthoracic spinal cord compression. Multiple sessions of endovascular interventions followed by surgical obliteration of extradural arteriovenous shunts (AVSs) led to complete regression of the epidural venous engorgement and a good clinical outcome.

## Case Report

### Presentation

A 15-year-old boy with a history of acute thoracic (T4–T7) epidural hematoma at 1 year of age was admitted and treated in our department. At the age of 1, he developed acute flaccid paraparesis, and the hematoma was surgically resected. No vascular malformation was confirmed during the surgery, and angiography was not performed postoperatively. Over the next 5 years, no spinal or paraspinal vascular anomaly was detected.

At 15 years of age, he presented with complaints of paresthesia in the left back and arm, along with numbness and muscle weakness on the ulnar side of the left hand. There was no family history of such symptoms.

### Examination

On admission, the neurologic examination revealed reduced superficial sensation in areas over the left C8 to T3 and bilateral T4 to T7 dermatomes. Grasping power was diminished in his left hand, but there was no weakness in the other extremities. There was no Romberg sign or pathologic reflexes. Patellar and Achilles tendon reflexes were normal, and he did not have difficulty walking or urinating. Pigmentation along the previous incision site was noted.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the thoracic spine revealed an enhanced area in the epidural space, most prominent from C7 to T3 vertebral levels. T2-weighted MRIs showed no abnormal intensity in the spinal cord. No intradural signal flow void indicating intradural vascular pathology was observed. The MRI findings prompted spinal angiography.

Spinal angiography revealed multiple epidural and paraspinal AVSs in the left T4-T7 vertebral segments. Feeding arteries included bilateral costocervical, bilateral intercostal (T5–T8), and the left descending scapular artery. The draining routes were through the azygos and hemiazygos veins.

## Clinical Course

The patient initially showed no signs of myelopathy, and his symptoms were attributed to left C8 radiculopathy. Over time, however, his symptoms improved, and conservative treatments were preferred by the patient and his family.

Four months later, the patient developed difficulty walking and urinating. Although his left arm symptoms improved, he had recently developed motor weakness in his bilateral iliopsoas muscles. The MRI now showed enlargement of the epidural venous structure at T4 and T5, leading to spinal cord compression. T2-hyperintensity areas were visible in the spinal cord.

## Endovascular Interventions

Given the patient's progressive myelopathy, endovascular treatments were initiated to reduce the shunting flow through AVS and alleviate the mass effect of the engorged epidural venous plexus. Transarterial embolizations using n-butyl cyanoacrylate (NBCA) were performed over three sessions. After selective catheterization, NBCA (30%) was delivered through segmental arteries of bilateral T4, T5, T6, the left T7, and bilateral costocervical and left deep scapular arteries.

After endovascular treatment, the enlarged epidural vascular structure at T5 disappeared. The patient regained motor strength in his lower extremities and could walk independently, although with hyperreflexia. Angiograms showed significantly reduced blood flow through the AVSs.

## Surgery

Open surgery was performed to expose the venous pouch and obliterate the shunting points around its wall. During the procedure, indocyanine green videoangiography demonstrated early filling of the pouch and epidural venous plexus. The wall of the venous pouch was circumferentially coagulated and detached from the feeder arteries. Indocyanine green videoangiography confirmed the obliteration of the extradural AVS in the surgical field.

## Postoperative Course

The patient did not develop new neurologic deficits after surgery. Postoperative angiograms demonstrated complete remission of the epidural shunts. Six months after the intervention, the patient's symptoms and signs of myelopathy completely subsided. MRI revealed no recurrence of the spinal AVM.

## Discussion

Spinal arteriovenous metameric syndrome (SAMS), also termed Cobb syndrome, is characterized by multiple vascular malformations derived from the same spinal metameric segments. The malformations can involve the bone, epidural space, spinal cord, paraspinal soft tissues, muscles, subcutaneous tissues, and skin. SAMS is classified as "genetic nonhereditary" due to its association with mutations in the neural crest before migration.

SAMS is estimated to account for 6% to 19% of all spinal cord AVM cases. Common clinical manifestations include subarachnoid hemorrhage, hematomyelia, or myelopathy due to venous hypertension of the spinal cord. When extradural pathology is present, as in this case, venous engorgement compresses the spinal cord or nerve roots, leading to myelopathy or radiculopathy.

## Treatment: Endovascular

Due to its rarity and poorly understood pathophysiology, the optimal management of SAMS remains to be established. Endovascular interventions are often the first treatment of choice, as surgical resection carries high morbidity. Embolization with NBCA has proven to be a safe and feasible option in treating SAMS.

## Treatment: Surgery

In several cases, including the current one, surgical excision combined with endovascular interventions was used to treat intra- and extradural pathologies. Post-surgical outcomes showed a high success rate, with complete obliteration of AVMs and good clinical outcomes.

## Conclusions

The subgroup of SAMS with exclusively extradural AVMs can achieve better treatment outcomes compared to patients with both intra- and extradural AVMs. Therefore, it is crucial to accurately diagnose whether AVMs are exclusively extradural. If so, a combination of multiple sessions of transarterial embolizations followed by surgical resection is recommended.
