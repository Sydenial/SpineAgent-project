# Thoracic Hemangioma From Rib Presenting as Compressive Paraparesis in a Young Adult: A Treatment Dilemma

## Abstract
Hemangiomas are rare bone tumors, and those arising from the rib are even rarer. Only about 50 such cases have been reported in the literature. This case report describes a 21-year-old male student who presented with a 6-week history of progressive weakness in both lower limbs and loss of bowel and bladder control. He had previously undergone surgery for a left periscapular tumor, diagnosed as hemangioendothelioma, a benign but locally aggressive variant of hemangioma. Imaging revealed an expansile lesion of the left fifth rib and a tumor on the left dorsal thoracic wall causing compressive myelopathy. The patient underwent embolization of the tumor followed by physical therapy, resulting in complete recovery within two years.

## Introduction
Hemangiomas are uncommon tumors, with those originating from the ribs being especially rare. These tumors can be challenging to diagnose and treat, as their clinical presentation may mimic other more common pathologies, such as metastatic or primary malignant bone tumors. This case study highlights the challenges associated with managing thoracic rib hemangiomas, particularly when they present with compressive myelopathy.

## Case Report
A 21-year-old male student presented with a 6-week history of progressive weakness in both lower limbs and loss of bowel and bladder control. On neurological examination, power in both lower limbs was graded at 2/5 (MRC grading), and the patient had lost all sensory modalities below T5. His plantar reflex was upgoing, and he exhibited brisk knee and ankle reflexes with clonus at the ankle. The patient had a history of surgery in July 2011 for a left periscapular thoracic wall tumor, diagnosed histopathologically as hemangioendothelioma.

Radiological imaging, including a chest radiograph and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), showed an expansile lesion of the left fifth rib and a tumor on the left dorsal thoracic wall, associated with arteriovenous (AV) malformation causing compressive thoracic myelopathy at the T5 vertebral level. The tumor was found to be highly vascular, with a significant risk of intraoperative bleeding. As a result, a plan for embolization followed by decompression surgery was adopted.

### Treatment
The patient underwent three embolization procedures using embolic glue (embucrylat glue), performed on August 14, 21, and 28, 2012. Each embolization targeted the feeding artery near the malformation. The goal was to occlude the arteries feeding the tumor while preserving the proximal arteries for future intervention if necessary. The patient also received intensive physiotherapy during the recovery process.

### Outcome
The patient showed significant neurological improvement within three weeks of the first embolization. His motor power improved from grade 2 to grade 4/5 in the lower limbs. Sensory modalities also improved, and he became able to stand with support. After two years, the patient had fully recovered and could walk independently without assistance. Follow-up MRI showed a reduction in cord compression caused by the AV malformation.

## Discussion
This case underscores the dilemma faced by spine surgeons when a patient presents with symptoms requiring immediate intervention. While the initial clinical findings suggested the need for decompressive surgery, digital subtraction angiography revealed that the tumor was highly vascular, with significant risks associated with surgical resection. It was determined that the primary cause of the patient's paraparesis was the arteriovenous malformation, not the hemangioma itself. Consequently, a conservative approach involving embolization was pursued, leading to excellent results.

This case highlights the importance of evaluating the vascular status of such tumors with angiography before deciding on the surgical approach. Embolization, as a less invasive procedure, was the definitive treatment in this instance, yielding significant neurological recovery.

## Conclusion
This case presents a unique challenge for spine surgeons, as the need for immediate surgical intervention was replaced by a less invasive treatment approach—embolization. The results demonstrate the efficacy of this method in treating AV malformations causing thoracic myelopathy. This case also emphasizes the importance of careful preoperative evaluation and consideration of non-surgical treatment options.

## Key Points
- Acute paraparesis due to costal/rib hemangiomas close to the spinal canal is a rare entity.
- Large intraspinal arteriovenous malformations should be evaluated with digital subtraction angiography to assess the vascular status of the tumor.
- Embolization is a less invasive and effective treatment option for these tumors.
