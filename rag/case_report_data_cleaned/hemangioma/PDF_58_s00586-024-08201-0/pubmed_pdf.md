# Preoperative elastoplasty of aggressive vertebral hemangiomas in elderly patients: a new strategy for reducing intraoperative bleeding and complications  

## Abstract 

Purpose: Preoperative elastoplasty could be an alternative strategy for treating aggressive vertebral hemangiomas (VHs) in frail patients needing spinal cord decompression, combining the advantages of embolization and vertebroplasty.  
Methods: Three elderly patients with spinal cord compression from thoracic aggressive VHs underwent XperCT-guided percutaneous elastoplasty, followed by spinal decompression. At 12-month follow-up, no recurrences, vertebral collapse, or segmental kyphosis were noted in the CT scans, with patients reporting an improvement in preoperative neurological deficits, VAS, and Smiley-Webster pain scale (SWPS) parameters.  
Results: With its elastic modulus, non-exothermic hardening, and lower viscosity than PMMA, VK100 allowed preoperative augmentation of the affected vertebral body, pedicles, and laminae without complications, with a controlled silicone delivery even in part of VH's epidural components thanks to XperCT guidance.  
Conclusion: VK100 combines the advantages of embolization and vertebroplasty, especially in elderly patients, by permeating the whole VH's angioarchitecture, significantly reducing tumor hypervascularity.

Keywords: Vertebral hemangioma, Elastoplasty, Silicone, Augmentation, Complication, Elderly  

## Introduction 

Vertebral hemangiomas (VHs) are benign vascular tumors with a prevalence of 41% in the adult population. Although they are usually asymptomatic, in 0.9–1.2% of cases these lesions may enlarge aggressively and cause pain or neurological deficit due to vertebral body or posterior arch bony expansion, VH's epidural extension compressing the spinal cord/nerve roots, or a pathological vertebral fracture. Surgical decompression is considered when neurological symptoms are present, although it can be treacherous due to profuse intraoperative bleeding sustained by VH's extreme vascularity. Therefore, strategies exploiting preoperative injection of embolizing agents or various cement-like materials are advised. Interventional treatments for increasing vertebral stiffness and reducing blood supply to the lesion may serve as preoperative treatments for both reducing intraoperative blood loss and preventing postoperative fractures.  

We report our experience with thoracic aggressive VHs in elderly patients using a percutaneous injection of a silicone elastomer bicomponent (VK100), also known as elastoplasty due to the elasticity of the injected material, followed by spinal cord decompression.

## Methods 

We retrospectively analyzed data from three elderly patients admitted to our department from June 2016 to June 2020 with thoracic aggressive VHs classified as Type IV according to the Weinstein–Boriani–Biagini (WBB) classification for spinal tumors. All patients underwent thoracic spine CT scanning and gadolinium-enhanced MRI, and the images were reviewed and discussed by a multidisciplinary group. The group approved a double-staged combined treatment consisting of percutaneous elastoplasty of the affected vertebra and subsequent spinal cord decompression, performed by an interventional neuroradiologist and a neurosurgeon (GL).

## VK100 

VK100 is a vertebral augmentation system consisting of a dispenser handle and a cartridge of 25 ml prefilled with a silicone elastomer. It was originally designed for treating osteoporotic fractures. The material is biocompatible, and each dose (VK100 material cartridge) consists of reinforced dimethyl methyl vinyl siloxanes, methyl hydrogen siloxane crosslinker, platinum catalyst (<0.002%), and barium sulfate powder. The barium sulfate powder makes VK100 visible under intraoperative fluoroscopy. The silicone cross-links at room temperature, absorbing heat (endothermic), and restarting each time material is added, without activating chemicals. At the usual operating room temperature, VK100 hardens in 12 minutes with a final elastic modulus of $233 \pm 14$ N/mm, similar to trabecular bone ($120–180$ N/mm), far lower than the elastic modulus of PMMA ($1730–1770$ N/mm). These characteristics allow atypical intravertebral distribution of the silicone elastomer through a pressure gradient, enhancing trabecular bone interdigitation.

## Percutaneous elastoplasty 

All procedures were performed with patients under general anesthesia, in prone position, integrating cone beam CT and biplanar angiography. Under high-resolution fluoroscopy and cone beam CT guidance, bilateral transpedicular needles of 11 gages were introduced into the affected vertebral bodies through a percutaneous approach. After biopsy samples were collected from the pedicles and vertebral body, the tips of the needles were inserted at the ventral edge of the lesion to fill the VH with as much silicone as possible. The injected material starts hardening after its two substances are mixed, producing the final product. As silicone augmentation reached the posterior wall of the vertebral body, partially permeating the ventral epidural component, both needles were gradually retracted while continuing the silicone injection until fulfilling the pedicles and spreading VK100 into the laminae and lateral intracanal component of the tumor.

## Surgical technique 

After percutaneous elastoplasty, the patients were transferred to the contiguous operating room, preserving sedation and positioned prone. The vertebral level above and below the VH were exposed through a midline longitudinal incision. After uncovering the facet joints, fluoroscopy was used to check the vertebral level, and the adjacent caudal and cranial laminae were partially removed to identify a few millimeters of the normal dural sac, minimizing the posterior decompression. Laminectomy of the affected vertebra was performed, and the medial wall of the pedicles was removed using a diamond burr. A satisfactory decompression was achieved by shrinking the epidural component of the tumor through bipolar coagulation and removing it piecemeal until a complete release of the dural sac and nerve roots was attained. Intraoperative blood loss was calculated based on the weight of the soaked surgical sponges and volume in suction canisters, subtracting the irrigation fluid sprayed on the surgical field. A drainage was positioned in all patients and left in place for two days, with its volume included in the blood loss data collection.

## Results 

### Case 1

An 84-year-old woman presented with difficulty walking due to lower limb weakness for two months and increasing dorsal pain (VAS 7/10). Neurological examination revealed spastic paraparesis, power of 3/5 in the right lower limb and 4/5 in the left, and sensory loss below the D9 dermatome. CT and MRI scans showed classical findings of a D5 aggressive VH (Type IV—WBB classification) with eccentric epidural compression on the right side. The patient underwent D5 laminectomy and decompression of the epidural component, preceded by percutaneous D5 elastoplasty, fulfilling the vertebral body and pedicles. Intraoperative blood loss was 830 ml, and postoperative drainage was 230 ml. No blood transfusions were needed. The postoperative course was uneventful, with physiotherapy starting on the third day after surgery, and the patient was transferred to a neurorehabilitation center four days later.

### Case 2

A 71-year-old man was admitted for progressively worsening spastic paraparesis, sphincter disturbances with urinary retention, and dorsal throbbing pain (VAS 7/10). Neurological examination revealed lower limb power of 4/5 and sensory loss below the D5 dermatome. CT and MRI revealed a D3 aggressive VH (Type IV—WBB classification) with posterior spinal cord compression. The patient underwent percutaneous elastoplasty of D3, fulfilling the vertebral body, pedicles, facet joints, and the soft component of the hemangioma, followed by decompressive laminectomy. Intraoperative blood loss was 860 ml, with postoperative drainage of 210 ml. No blood transfusions were needed, and improvement of the hyposthenia was observed immediately after surgery. The patient was discharged on the ninth postoperative day with an outpatient rehabilitation program.

### Case 3

A 74-year-old woman with chronic renal failure developed progressive hyposthenia in the lower limbs and intense back pain (VAS 8/10). Neurological examination revealed spastic paraparesis, residual strength of 3/5 in the lower limbs, and distal dysesthesia below the D12 dermatome. MRI and CT showed a D11 VH (Type II—WBB classification) and a myelopathy sustained by a D12 aggressive VH (Type IV—WBB classification) causing circumferential spinal cord compression. After undergoing percutaneous elastoplasty of D11 and D12, with VK100 fulfilling the vertebral body and pedicles and partially permeating the epidural component, the patient underwent D12 laminectomy and decompression of the intracanal tumor. Surgery was influenced by a blood loss of 1020 ml, requiring transfusion of three units of red blood cells. The patient had an uneventful recovery, walking with support by the third postoperative day, and was discharged on the eighth day.

## Clinical and radiological outcome 

At 12-month follow-up, all patients reported significant improvement of their preoperative neurological deficit, with cases 1 and 2 walking autonomously but with residual episodic paresthesia. All patients were referred to a rehabilitation program for six months, with case 3 still under outpatient follow-up and walker-dependent for daily activities. No radiological recurrences, vertebral collapse, or postoperative segmental kyphosis were documented at 12-month CT scans.

## Discussion 

When facing aggressive VHs causing spinal cord compression, pathologic vertebral fracture, or spinal instability, active treatment is usually required. Many options have been proposed, including endovascular embolization, alcohol ablation, and vertebroplasty with bone cement, surgery, radiotherapy, or combinations of these techniques. Vertebral augmentation with bone cement consolidates trabecular microfractures and shrinks the epidural component due to decreased blood supply. However, complications such as heat-related damage from PMMA polymerization and mass effect on spinal cord due to cement leakage have been reported. VK100, with its non-exothermic properties, allows safer distribution without thermal damage, filling vertebral trabeculae and reducing bleeding risk, while minimizing the risk of adjacent vertebral fractures.

## Conclusion 

Preoperative elastoplasty with VK100 could represent a valid alternative to traditional embolization and vertebroplasty, especially in patients with extensive bony erosion and spinal cord compression. It combines the benefits of embolization and vertebroplasty, ensuring controlled distribution and reduced complications, particularly in elderly or osteoporotic patients.
