# Dumbbell-Shaped Epidural Capillary Hemangioma

## Abstract

**Study Design.** A case report and literature review.  
**Objective.** We present the fourth case of a spinal epidural capillary hemangioma with a dumbbell-shaped appearance on magnetic resonance imaging reported in the literature and the second presented as a lung mass.  
**Summary of Background Data.** Hemangiomas are congenital vascular malformations that pathologists frequently consider to be hamartomatous malformations. Hemangiomas of the spine are usually lesions of the vertebral bodies, but they can occur in other locations such as the intramedullary or epidural space. Purely epidural hemangiomas are rare and most of them are of cavernous type.  
**Methods.** We present a 67-year-old female with a thoracic dumbbell-shaped capillary hemangioma with both foraminal and intrathoracic extensions, whose presentation was pleural effusion associated with a mediastinal mass suggestive of pulmonary neoplasia. Surgical treatment consisted of total removal en bloc of the lesion.  
**Results.** Microscopic evaluation showed fibrofatty tissue with a proliferation of vascular structures that were generally small in size, with areas of myxoid appearance. To date, there have been eight epidural capillary hemangiomas of the thoracic and lumbar spine reported in the literature, and only three of them were dumbbell-shaped with extraforaminal extension.  
**Conclusion.** It is important to consider the diagnosis of hemangiomas in the differential diagnosis of epidural lesions with a dumbbell-shaped appearance on magnetic resonance imaging, especially at the thoracic level. It is a benign and potentially curable disease, and the most appropriate surgical treatment is en bloc resection of the entire lesion. These lesions are usually presented as a progressive myelopathy, so early treatment may prevent permanent neurological deficits.

## Introduction

Purely epidural hemangiomas of the spine are extremely rare. Most of them are of the cavernous type and, to date, there have been only eight reported cases of epidural capillary hemangiomas. We present a female patient with a thoracic dumbbell-shaped capillary hemangioma with both foraminal and intrathoracic extensions, whose presentation was pleural effusion associated with a mediastinal mass suggestive of pulmonary neoplasia.

## Case Report

A 67-year-old female, without a significant medical history, presented with a 20-day history of sudden dyspnea. On chest radiograph, an increased density in the right hemithorax with an ipsilateral pleural effusion was observed. The patient was admitted to the hospital with a suspicion of lung cancer.

A chest computed tomographic scan demonstrated an extensive right pleural effusion with mediastinal shift to the left and involvement of the T4 spinal segment with a predominantly lytic component, affecting the right posterior arch and the adjacent vertebral body.

Magnetic resonance imaging showed a right paravertebral heterogeneous rounded mass extending from T4 to T5 with intrathoracic extension. It was isointense to the spinal cord on T1-weighted images, strongly hyperintense on T2-weighted images, and enhanced homogeneously after intravenous gadolinium administration. The mass extended through the ipsilateral intervertebral foramen, widening and obliterating the epidural fat and displacing and compressing the contralateral spinal cord. No medullary hyperintensity indicating compressive myelopathy was observed.

Under neuronavigation guidance, a posterolateral approach was performed with a T4–T5 hemilaminectomy and removal of the right T4–T5 facet joint and the corresponding costotransverse joint to expose the lesion. A red hemorrhagic epidural mass was observed and removed en bloc after circumferential dissection.

Microscopic evaluation showed fibrofatty tissue with a proliferation of vascular structures, generally of small size, with areas of myxoid appearance. No cellular atypia was seen. The diagnosis was capillary hemangioma.

The patient had a good postoperative course, with full expansion of the right lung after an evacuative thoracentesis. A postoperative MRI showed the usual postoperative changes and complete resection of the lesion.

## Discussion

Hemangiomas are congenital vascular malformations that pathologists frequently consider to be hamartomatous malformations [1]. Hemangiomas of the spine are usually lesions of the vertebral bodies, but they can occur in other locations [2]. The most frequent site of involvement in the spinal canal is the intradural extramedullary compartment [3].

Purely epidural hemangiomas are rare; only eight cases in the English literature have been described. Seven of these cases involved thoracic spinal levels and one case involved a lumbar level. Three cases presented with an extraforaminal extension causing a dumbbell-shaped appearance on spinal MRI studies. Our patient is the fourth case with a dumbbell-shaped appearance reported in the literature and the second presented as a lung mass.

Radiologically, these tumors may be iso- or hypointense on T1-weighted images, hyperintense on T2-weighted images, and show relatively homogeneous enhancement on T1-weighted images after gadolinium administration. These radiological aspects are nonspecific, and well-circumscribed tumors with dumbbell shapes on magnetic resonance imaging are also commonly found in other tumors such as schwannomas, neurofibromas, or meningiomas.

Histologically, hemangiomas are divided into capillary and cavernous types depending on the dominant vessel size. Cavernous hemangiomas present a large number of sinusoidal channels in collagenous tissue, whereas capillary hemangiomas are characterized by encapsulated lesions composed of lobules of thin, irregular capillary-sized vessels lined by endothelial cells and separated by fibrous septa.

Capillary hemangiomas usually present in a chronic progressive manner because of mass effect and nerve root irritation. Cavernous hemangiomas, in contrast, often present with acute symptomatology, mainly related to intratumoral bleeding.

Complete surgical removal is the treatment of choice for epidural capillary hemangiomas. En bloc removal of the tumor is preferred when feasible. In our case, a wide exposure of the lesion, including the intrathoracic extension, was planned through a posterolateral approach with T4–T5 hemilaminectomy and removal of the right T4–T5 costotransverse joint, allowing true en bloc resection.

Recurrences have not been described in the literature, and in most cases, as in our patient, pain improved considerably and sensory and motor deficits were reversed.

## Conclusion

Hemangiomas should be considered in the differential diagnosis of epidural lesions with a dumbbell-shaped appearance on magnetic resonance imaging, especially at the thoracic level. This is a benign and potentially curable disease, and the most appropriate surgical treatment is en bloc resection of the entire lesion. Because these lesions usually present as a progressive myelopathy, early treatment may prevent permanent neurological deficits.

## Key Points

- Purely epidural hemangiomas of the spine are rare.  
- Well-circumscribed tumors with a dumbbell shape on magnetic resonance imaging are also commonly found in other neurogenic tumors.  
- Capillary hemangiomas usually present in a chronic progressive manner, whereas cavernous hemangiomas often present with acute symptomatology.  
- This is a benign and potentially curable disease, and en bloc surgical resection is the treatment of choice.
