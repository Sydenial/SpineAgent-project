# Multifocal Hemangioendothelioma of the Lumbar Spine and Response to Surgical Resection and Radiation

## Abstract

**Keywords**: Epithelioid; Hemangioendothelioma; Spine; Vascular; Surgery; Radiation; Vascular tumor; Imaging; Bone neoplasm

**BACKGROUND CONTEXT**: Epithelioid hemangioendothelioma rarely occurs in the lumbosacral spine, with very few case reports of spinal hemangioendothelioma in the literature. There is variability in the aggressiveness of these lesions without established treatment guidelines.  
**PURPOSE**: The aim was to present a case of epithelioid hemangioendothelioma in the lumbar spine, including magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) findings, which rapidly progressed over a 2-month period as regional multifocal lumbosacral spinal lesions with epidural extension causing severe spinal canal stenosis.  
**STUDY DESIGN/SETTING**: This was a case report in a university hospital setting.  
**PATIENT SAMPLE**: The sample included an otherwise healthy adult male with low back pain.  
**METHODS**: Multimodality imaging was performed to help with diagnosis and management including computed tomography, MRI, and positron emission tomography (PET). The patient was treated by embolization, L5 corpectomy, L4–S1 spinal fusion, and radiation therapy. The diagnosis was confirmed by tissue biopsy.  
**RESULTS**: The patient initially presented with severe back and leg pain after a vertebroplasty for an L5 compression fracture at an outside hospital where biopsy was negative for malignancy. MRI showed diffuse abnormality of L5 with several smaller lesions in the sacrum. Due to progressive pain two weeks after the vertebroplasty, the patient underwent an L5 laminectomy, L4–S1 instrumented posterior fusion, and attempted partial corpectomy for stenosis. At this surgery, the L5 corpectomy was aborted due to profound bleeding. Pathology was again negative for malignancy. Presumed to be an atypical hemangioma, the lesion was embolized before repeat surgery where the thecal sac was decompressed by partial L5 corpectomy. Biopsy at this time revealed a vascular neoplasm, with hemangioendothelioma not excluded. Approximately two months after the stabilization procedure, the patient had increasing pain and bilateral lower extremity weakness. MRI was performed and demonstrated marked local progression of disease with new multifocal lesions involving L4 through S2 vertebrae and new severe spinal canal stenosis. These lesions were subsequently treated with localized radiation therapy. MRI two months after radiation therapy showed significant regression of the epidural tumor, although a new metastatic lesion was discovered at the T6 vertebra.  
**CONCLUSIONS**: Spinal hemangioendothelioma is a rare disease and can present in variable forms, including as a multifocal regional process—which may be mistaken for infection. Additionally, there are no standard treatment protocols for this entity. This case adds to the few case reports describing success with preoperative embolization, spinal stabilization, and radiation therapy.

## Introduction

Hemangioendotheliomas in the spine are a rare occurrence, reported in the literature only as case reports and small case series. The forms of spinal hemangioendotheliomas that have been described include kaposiform, epithelioid, spindle cell, and recently, composite forms. Hemangioendotheliomas generally occur between 10 and 30 years of age. When epithelioid hemangioendothelioma occurs in the bone, it behaves less aggressively than when occurring in soft tissue and skin.

Epithelioid hemangioendothelioma of the bone is generally considered an intermediary between epithelioid hemangioma (considered benign) and angiosarcoma. In the spectrum of vascular lesions, epithelioid hemangiomas have low malignant potential. Although these lesions can be asymptomatic and incidentally discovered on imaging, a common presenting symptom of skeletal epithelioid hemangioma and epithelioid hemangioendothelioma is pain. A case series reported that back pain and point tenderness over the lesion were common symptoms in spinal hemangioendothelioma patients, with some presenting with movement restrictions or paraparesis.

Multilevel vertebral body involvement has been noted in thoracic hemangioendotheliomas. To our knowledge, no cases of lumbosacral multifocal hemangioendothelioma have been documented. This case presents a rare instance of epithelioid hemangioendothelioma with multifocal lumbosacral spine involvement, demonstrating rapid progression.

## Case Report

A 67-year-old patient without significant medical history first presented to an outside hospital with back pain and was found to have a compression fracture at L5. The patient was treated with vertebroplasty and a core needle biopsy that was negative for malignancy. Two weeks later, the patient presented with progressive back pain and severe pain radiating down both legs. Noncontrast MRI revealed severe vertebral body height loss at L5 with post-vertebroplasty changes and abnormal signal on short tau inversion recovery (STIR) and T1 images, with extension into the bilateral pedicles. Severe spinal canal stenosis at L5 and S1 and foraminal stenosis were also observed. CT confirmed the L5 fracture and severe canal stenosis, along with lytic lesions of S1 and S2.

The patient underwent L5 laminectomy and L4–S1 stabilization. L5 corpectomy was attempted but aborted due to excessive bleeding. Pathology was negative for malignancy, revealing fibroadipose tissue with large-walled vessels. Spinal angiography demonstrated vascular lesions at L5 and S1, which were embolized with Onyx. FDG-PET and contrast-enhanced MRI showed hypermetabolic activity and enhancement of the lesions. A second surgery was performed for partial posterior L5 corpectomy, with pathology showing a vascular tumor positive for CD31, confirming hemangioendothelioma.

Two months later, the patient presented with worsening pain and bilateral lower extremity weakness. MRI showed marked progression of disease, now involving L4 to S2 vertebrae and the right iliac bone. Radiation therapy was initiated, and follow-up imaging showed improvement of the epidural mass, although a new lesion was discovered at T6.

## Discussion

### Imaging Findings

Radiographic features of epithelioid hemangioendothelioma usually include well-circumscribed lytic lesions, sometimes with sclerosis or expansion, which may resemble hemangiomas. MRI characteristics include hypointense signal on T1-weighted images and slightly hyperintense signal on T2-weighted images. On T2, a "target" appearance may be seen, with a necrotic center and hyperintense peripheral zone.

In this case, the MRI revealed an aggressive process with peripheral enhancement and mass effect on the spinal cord. The lesions showed T1 hypointensity, mild T2 hyperintensity, and a peripheral rim of edema and neoplastic infiltration. CT revealed lytic lesions without significant cortical destruction, and FDG-PET imaging demonstrated hypermetabolism in the lesions.

### Pathology

Epithelioid hemangioendothelioma can be distinguished from epithelioid hemangioma by immature vascular features and hyalinized matrix. Immunohistochemical analysis typically shows positive staining for CD31 and CD34 in hemangioendotheliomas. Our case demonstrated CD31 positivity, confirming the diagnosis. The lesion was classified as hemangioendothelioma by both our institution and an outside consult.

### Treatment

The first vertebral case of epithelioid hemangioendothelioma was reported in a 31-year-old woman who was treated with vertebroplasty and corpectomy. A case series of eight patients with spinal hemangioendothelioma reported varying treatment strategies, including external beam irradiation, surgical resection, and embolization. Preoperative angiography and embolization help minimize intraoperative bleeding and identify feeder arteries.

In our case, the patient's disease progressed rapidly after initial surgical intervention, with local metastasis occurring within two months. Radiation therapy improved the epidural component of the lesion, and the patient's prognosis improved with ongoing symptom management.

## Treatment

The treatment for spinal hemangioendothelioma remains complex due to the vascular nature of the tumors and the potential for significant intraoperative bleeding. Preoperative embolization is often utilized, followed by surgical resection or stabilization, and radiation therapy when necessary.

In our case, the patient received radiation therapy, which resulted in a marked improvement in the epidural mass. Given the aggressive nature of this rare tumor, further studies and case reports are needed to develop a standardized treatment protocol.
