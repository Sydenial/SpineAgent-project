# Acute and Hyper-Acute Thoracolumbar Corpectomy for Traumatic Burst Fractures Using a Mini-Open Lateral Approach

## Abstract

The study investigates the feasibility and outcomes of performing thoracolumbar corpectomy using a mini-open lateral approach for traumatic burst fractures in acute and hyper-acute settings. The results suggest that the mini-open lateral approach allows for immediate decompression and has favorable outcomes in terms of neurological recovery and complication rates.

## Introduction

The treatment of acute thoracolumbar spinal fractures requires careful consideration of fracture severity, potential spinal cord injury, and surgical timing. Surgical intervention within 72 hours of injury has been shown to improve neurological outcomes. This study focuses on the mini-open lateral approach for thoracolumbar corpectomy, which minimizes procedural morbidity compared to conventional methods and allows for early intervention.

## Study Design

Retrospective chart review.

## Objective

The study aims to examine the feasibility of treating thoracolumbar burst fractures within 24 hours (acute) and 8 hours (hyper-acute) using a mini-open lateral corpectomy approach.

## Summary of Background Data

Historically, traumatic spinal fractures required long surgical delays due to complex polytrauma and hospital resource coordination. The mini-open lateral approach eliminates the need for an access surgeon, potentially allowing for quicker interventions and improved patient outcomes.

## Methods

Sixteen patients were treated within 24 hours with a mini-open lateral corpectomy for traumatic spinal pathology. Preoperative, perioperative, and postoperative data were reviewed, including ASIA scores for neurological status. Fractures primarily occurred from L1 to L3. Titanium devices were used in 75% of patients, and all patients received supplemental fixation.

## Results

The average time from injury to admission was 1.8 hours, with an average operating room time of 8.2 hours and surgery duration of 2.7 hours. Eight patients underwent surgery within 8 hours of injury, while seven were treated between 8 and 24 hours. Blood loss averaged 646 mL, and no intraoperative complications occurred. At follow-up, 73% of patients showed an improvement in ASIA scores, with 20% improving by two grades.

## Conclusions

The results suggest that the mini-open lateral approach allows for effective decompression in patients with thoracolumbar burst fractures, even within hyper-acute (<8 hours) and acute (<24 hours) time frames. This approach is associated with favorable neurological recovery and fewer complications compared to traditional methods.

## Methods (Expanded)

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB). The data collected included preoperative assessment, operative details, and post-operative recovery, particularly ASIA scores. The timing of the intervention was categorized into injury to ER admission, ER admission to surgery start, and time in the operating room. The mean age of patients was 32 years (range: 19–69 years), with 81% being male. Four patients were smokers, and three had a history of drug use. The most common fracture levels treated were L1 (7 levels), L2 (3 levels), and L3 (4 levels).

## Surgical Technique

The mini-open lateral approach for thoracolumbar corpectomy is performed with the patient in a lateral decubitus position. Fluoroscopy is used to guide the entry point and verify the appropriate levels for surgery. The approach is similar to the extreme lateral interbody fusion (XLIF) technique. Once the initial access is established, sequential dilators are placed, followed by a table-mounted split-blade retractor. Decompression and corpectomy are performed, and vertebral body replacement (VBR) is completed using expandable titanium devices with supplemental internal fixation.

## Results (Expanded)

The total time from injury to surgery averaged 12.7 hours, with significant variability in surgical timing. Eight patients were treated within 8 hours, while the remainder had surgery between 8 and 24 hours. Postoperative hospital stay averaged 6 days. Follow-up at an average of 8.6 months showed that 73% of patients improved by at least one ASIA grade, with 73% achieving full or near-full neurological recovery.

## Case Reports

### Case 1
A 21-year-old Airman with an ASIA grade of C was treated within 8 hours of injury. The patient underwent a mini-open lateral corpectomy with an expandable cage and anterior fixation. At 2 months post-op, the ASIA grade improved to D, and preoperative kyphosis of 20° was corrected to 1°.

### Case 2
A 19-year-old male with a thoracolumbar burst fracture was treated with a mini-open lateral corpectomy and expandable cage. The patient achieved ASIA grade E by 12 months post-op and resumed normal activities. Preoperative kyphosis of 21° was corrected to 5°.

## Discussion

Early surgical intervention for thoracolumbar burst fractures has been shown to improve outcomes. This study supports the use of the mini-open lateral approach for early intervention, showing significant neurological improvements and minimal complications. The approach is less invasive than traditional methods and offers an alternative to more complex surgical techniques.
