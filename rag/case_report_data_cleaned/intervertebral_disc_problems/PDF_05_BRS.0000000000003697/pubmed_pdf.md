# A Novel Technique for Spondylolysis Repair With Piece

## Abstract

Our study aims to present a new technique using three components to repair the pars defect: pedicle screws, rod, and polyester band. We also perform a systematic literature review of the previously described techniques. Spondylolysis is a common condition in children and adolescents, and surgery is required if nonoperative treatment fails to give sustained pain relief. Several surgical techniques have been described.

## Introduction

Spondylolysis is a defect or fracture of the pars interarticularis, a region in the lumbar spine. It was first described in the mid-1800s, and its incidence varies based on factors like ethnicity, age, sex, and occupation. It is often seen in athletes who participate in sports involving hyperextension and rotation of the lumbar spine. 

## Methods

### Systematic Literature Review

A literature review was conducted to investigate studies related to surgical techniques for spondylolysis. We searched databases including MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane, and ScienceDirect up to May 2019. The Screw-Rod-Band (SRB) technique, which combines pedicle screws, a rod, and a polyester band, was identified as an effective surgical procedure for spondylolysis.

## Case Report: Screw-Rod-Band (SRB) Technique

In July 2016, a 14-year-old male with L5 spondylosis and persistent low back pain underwent a L5 isthmic reconstruction using the SRB technique. The procedure involved the use of pedicle screws, a rod, and a polyester band to repair the spondylolysis. The patient had no complications at the 4-year follow-up and showed good clinical outcomes.

## Results

### Study Design

We identified 982 studies, of which 21 met the inclusion criteria. The studies presented a variety of surgical techniques, including Isthmic Screw, Wiring, Butterfly-Plate, and Hook-Screw Constructs.

## Discussion

The SRB technique offers several advantages, including minimal soft tissue dissection, reduced blood loss, and the application of strong compression to ensure fusion. It is a simple and safe procedure that avoids the complications seen in earlier methods using metal wires. Further studies are required to validate the procedure.

## Conclusion

The SRB technique for repairing spondylolysis is a promising new approach that provides good clinical outcomes and reduces the invasiveness of surgery. The technique appears to be comparable to existing methods and offers the added benefit of reducing the risk of neurological injury.

## Key Points

- The SRB technique is a novel method for spondylolysis repair using pedicle screws, a transverse rod, and a polyester band.
- It requires minimal soft tissue dissection and reduces blood loss.
- The procedure applies compressive forces to promote fusion and is associated with low complication rates.
