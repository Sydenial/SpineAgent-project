# Prognostic Indicators of Surgical Outcome in Painful Foot Drop: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

## Abstract

**Purpose**: Foot drop is a relatively uncommon presentation of lumbar degenerative diseases, and there is currently a paucity of evidence on management and outcomes, reflective of the lack of standardized treatment provided to patients. The purpose of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to determine the effectiveness of surgical management and the factors that predict surgical outcome.  

**Methods**: A systematic database search of Cochrane Library, Ovid Medline, PubMed, Embase, and Google Scholar was undertaken from inception through August 2018. Only studies reporting on surgical outcomes in adult patients who had a painful foot drop and underwent decompression were included. Case reports and studies with surgical fixation were excluded. Study quality was assessed using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale. Data were pooled using a random-effects model.

**Results**: 797 studies were screened, and 9 observational studies met the inclusion criteria, resulting in a total of 431 patients who underwent decompression for foot drop. The pooled rate of improvement in foot drop MRC grade was 84.5% (range 67.9–96%). Sub-group meta-analyses revealed a statistically significant association between duration of foot drop, severity of preoperative weakness, and age on postoperative outcome.

**Conclusion**: This is the first systematic review and meta-analysis to explore the outcome and prognostic indicators of lumbar decompression for foot drop. Findings indicate that age, duration of foot drop, weakness, and MRC grade prior to intervention are strong predictors of surgical outcome.

**Keywords**: Foot drop, Spinal surgery, Lumbar degenerative disease, Systematic review, Meta-analysis, Surgical decompression

## Background

Foot drop is characterized by weakness of the tibialis anterior muscle, the principal dorsiflexor of the foot. The underlying etiology can be classified into three broad categories: neurological, muscular, or anatomical. It is a relatively uncommon presentation of lumbar degenerative disease (LDD) and is secondary to nerve root compression due to disc herniation, facet joint cysts, hypertrophy of the ligaments, osteophytes, or a combination of these. The tibialis anterior muscle predominantly receives its supply from the L5 nerve root, but L4 and S1 can also contribute to this supply. The impact of this condition can be life-long and debilitating. The prevalence and incidence of foot drop from lumbosacral radiculopathy are estimated to be between 0.6% and 7%.

There is currently no consensus on the management of foot drop, and controversy exists regarding whether surgical management confers any beneficial outcome. Some studies suggest that the natural history of radicular weakness improves with time when managed non-operatively. However, many of these studies are outdated, and surgical practices have advanced.

## Objectives

The primary objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to clarify the evidence surrounding painful foot drop secondary to LDD and determine the efficacy of surgical management. Secondary objectives include identifying factors that influence the recovery of motor weakness.

## Methods

A systematic search of the literature surrounding painful foot drop was conducted, followed by a meta-analysis of the pooled data from eligible studies. The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) and Meta-Analysis of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (MOOSE) guidelines were adhered to.

## Study Selection and Inclusion Criteria

The Population, Intervention, Criteria, Outcome, and Study design (PICOS) framework was used to specify eligibility criteria. Only studies where foot drop was diagnosed based on the Medical Research Council (MRC) power grade of foot dorsiflexion and lumbar decompression surgery was the intervention were included. The primary outcome was the extent of recovery of motor weakness in foot dorsiflexion as measured by the MRC scale.

## Search Strategy

A comprehensive search was performed across the following databases: Cochrane Library, Ovid Medline, PubMed, Embase, and Google Scholar. The last search date was August 2018.

## Data Extraction

Two authors independently screened article abstracts for inclusion. Full-text articles were reviewed, and disagreements were resolved through consultation with a senior investigator.

## Data Synthesis and Statistical Analysis

A favorable outcome was defined as an improvement in foot dorsiflexion strength by at least one grade on the MRC scale. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) were generated to determine the effect size of each variable considered. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I² statistic.

## Risk of Bias

The quality of each study was classified as A, B, or C based on the American Heart Association/American Stroke Association methods. The Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) and GATE checklists were used to minimize bias.

## Results

### Study Selection

The literature search identified 797 articles, of which 64 were selected for abstract screening. Nine studies met the inclusion criteria, with a total of 431 patients who underwent surgical lumbar decompression for painful foot drop.

### Study Characteristics

The nine included studies were published in English. The median sample size was 43, and the mean age of patients was 48 years, with a predominance of males (59%).

### Pooled Outcomes Following Surgical Intervention

The pooled portion of studies reporting improvement in foot drop following surgery ranged from 67.9% to 96%, with an overall mean improvement of 84.5%.

### Meta-analysis

A meta-analysis of 301 patients found no significant difference between studies with sufficient data and those without. The pooled improvement rate was 83.6% for the former and 87.5% for the latter.

### Preoperative Muscle Strength

Operating on patients with moderate preoperative weakness (MMT grade 2–3) was associated with lower odds of an unfavorable outcome compared to severe weakness (MMT grade 0–1).

### Preoperative Duration of Foot Drop

Surgery performed more than 6 weeks after the onset of foot drop weakness was associated with higher odds of an unfavorable outcome.

### Herniated vs. Non-Herniated Disc Pathology

Surgical outcomes did not show a statistically significant reduction in unfavorable outcomes for patients with disc herniation compared to those without.

### Age

Patients under 50 years old had a more favorable outcome compared to those over 50 years old.

## Discussion

The primary outcome of this study was the improvement in foot drop weakness following surgical decompression. The pooled data analysis showed a favorable outcome with surgery, particularly for early surgery, moderate preoperative weakness, and younger age. The presence of disc herniation did not demonstrate a significant improvement in outcomes, although patients without disc herniation showed a tendency towards improved odds of favorable outcomes.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations, including the small sample size and the retrospective observational design of the included studies. Furthermore, the lack of a conservatively managed group limits the applicability of the findings.

## Conclusion

Foot drop is a serious condition with significant long-term effects. This meta-analysis suggests that earlier surgical intervention, moderate preoperative weakness, and age under 50 years are strong predictors of improved outcomes following lumbar decompression for foot drop. The lack of randomized controlled trials in this field highlights the need for further research to improve clinical decision-making.
