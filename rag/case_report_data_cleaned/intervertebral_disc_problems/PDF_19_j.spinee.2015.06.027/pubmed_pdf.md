# Posterior epidural lumbar disc fragment mimicking epidural mass

## Abstract

This 65-year-old man presented with a one-year history of low back pain and a new-onset radicular right leg pain. Neurological examination revealed no abnormalities except a positive straight leg raise test at 45 degrees. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a hypointense mass lesion at the L3–L4 level indenting the thecal sac circumferentially from the right posterolateral side on T2-weighted images. Gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted images showed rim-like peripheral contrast enhancement. The lesion was considered to be an epidural mass and surgery was planned. Through an L4 total laminectomy, the mass lesion was exposed and the surgical view indicated that the 2 cm mass lesion was a posteriorly migrated disc fragment. The sequestered disc fragment was followed downwards, and it was clearly seen that the disc fragment was laterally compressing the L5 nerve root at the axilla.

## Case Report

To our knowledge, there have only been 55 reported cases of posterior epidural migration of lumbar disc fragments [1–3]. The L3–L4 disc level is the most commonly affected [3]. Rim-like peripheral contrast enhancement, due to highly vascularized epidural fatty tissue and inflammatory vasogenic features of the sequestered disc fragment, creates difficulties in achieving an accurate diagnosis. A minimal hemilaminotomy has been reported to be sufficient for the removal of the fragment; however, a larger surgical exposure may be required depending on the extent and location of migration.
