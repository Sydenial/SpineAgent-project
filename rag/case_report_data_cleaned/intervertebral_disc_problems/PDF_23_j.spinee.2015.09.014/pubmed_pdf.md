# The Hyperextension Triad of Type II Odontoid Fracture, Posterior Atlantoaxial Dislocation (Reducible in Flexion), and C1 Posterior Arch Fracture: Implications on Emergency Management and Anterior Versus Posterior Surgical Approach

## Case Report

A 45-year-old man presented with quadriparesis following a motor vehicular accident. Computed tomography of the craniovertebral junction showed a type II odontoid fracture, posterior atlantoaxial dislocation (post-AAD), and fracture of the posterior arch of the atlas. On immobilization in extension, there was worsening of posterior AAD. Thus, traction was applied in flexion, which reduced the dislocation.  

In the operating room, the feasibility of anterior odontoid screw fixation was assessed under an image intensifier; however, the necessary space for an anterior approach could not be obtained, as the reduction was achieved only in a flexed position. Therefore, the patient underwent C1 lateral mass and C2 pars screw and rod fixation through a posterior approach. Postoperative radiographs were satisfactory, and the patient showed gradual improvement in neurologic status.

## Discussion

The above-mentioned triad, its associated mechanism, and the management issues in odontoid fractures have not been previously addressed [1–3]. It is possible that hyperextension forces acting on the craniovertebral junction caused the type II odontoid fracture, with avulsion of the anterior longitudinal ligament resulting in posterior displacement of the distal fragment of the dens. The C1 posterior arch fracture may be caused by impact of the suboccipital portion of the skull during hyperextension.  

Although atlantoaxial dislocation and other cervical spine injuries are usually stabilized in extension in emergency settings, this rare triad requires stabilization in a flexed position [4]. On flexion, the intact transverse ligament probably brings the distal dens fragment into alignment. The key implication for surgical decision-making lies in the inadequate corridor available for anterior odontoid screw fixation. Hence, a posterior approach with C1–C2 fixation appears to be the most appropriate surgical strategy for this rare triad [5,6].
