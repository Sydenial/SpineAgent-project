# Superior Mesenteric Artery Syndrome Caused E  
A Case Report

## Abstract  
Superior mesenteric artery (SMA) syndrome is a rare disorder, often secondary to functional obstruction. We present a case of SMA syndrome caused by massive lumbar osteophytes, a previously unreported cause. An 82-year-old man with a history of polysurgery was diagnosed with SMA syndrome after several radiological tests. He was treated successfully by resecting the osteophytes.

## Introduction  
SMA syndrome, first described by Rokitansky in 1842, results from the compression of the third segment of the duodenum by the SMA and aorta, leading to obstruction, nausea, vomiting, and weight loss. While various predisposing conditions for SMA syndrome have been documented, lumbar osteophytes have not been previously associated with this syndrome.

## Methods  
An 82-year-old male with a history of multiple surgeries presented with frequent vomiting and weight loss. He underwent total gastrectomy, posterior decompression for lumbar spinal stenosis, and other procedures in his medical history. Conservative treatment with total parenteral nutrition for two months showed no improvement. Imaging studies, including abdominal radiography and CT scans, revealed large lumbar osteophytes that displaced the aorta anteriorly, causing SMA syndrome. The patient underwent resection of the osteophytes.

## Case Report  
The patient presented with a weight loss of approximately 5 kg over two months, resulting in a body weight of 37.5 kg. Radiological investigations revealed lumbar osteophytes at L3/4 and L4/5, which displaced the aorta and caused a narrowing of the duodenum. After confirming the diagnosis of SMA syndrome secondary to lumbar osteophytes, the patient underwent surgical resection of the osteophytes, which resulted in a significant improvement in symptoms.

## Results  
The patient underwent osteophyte resection, which improved the aortomesenteric distance and resolved the symptoms of SMA syndrome. Postoperative imaging confirmed no residual osteophytes and normal aorta positioning. Over the course of six months, the patient’s weight increased from 37.5 kg to 48.4 kg.

## Discussion  
SMA syndrome typically presents with a decreased aortomesenteric angle and reduced distance, often leading to duodenal obstruction. In this case, the large lumbar osteophytes displaced the aorta, exacerbating the condition. While SMA syndrome is commonly associated with surgical interventions like gastrojejunostomy, the resection of osteophytes offers a viable and less invasive alternative, particularly in patients with a complex surgical history.

## Conclusion  
This case highlights a rare cause of SMA syndrome, secondary to massive lumbar osteophytes. Resection of these osteophytes proved effective in relieving symptoms and restoring the patient’s health. Clinicians should consider this etiology when the cause of SMA syndrome is unclear.

## Key Words  
Superior mesenteric artery syndrome, osteophytes, lumbar spine, surgical resection, aortomesenteric distance.

## Discussion on Surgical Approaches  
SMA syndrome is often treated conservatively, but surgical options like gastrojejunostomy or duodenojejunostomy may be considered when conservative measures fail. In this case, resecting the osteophytes offered a less invasive option and resolved the obstruction effectively.
