# Hypervascular cervical spine metastases: embolization by direct injection of Onyx-18

## Abstract

**Purpose**: Spinal metastases are common in patients with cancer. Following lung and liver, the spine is the most common site for cancers to metastasize. Many of these metastases are hypervascularized, posing a significant challenge for surgeons due to the risk of massive blood loss during surgery. Hypervascularized metastases in the cervical spine also carry the risk of postoperative bleeding with severe neurological impairment.

**Case Summary**: We report a case of a 67-year-old woman with breast cancer (BC) metastasis within the vertebral bodies of C3 and C4. The metastasis caused near complete bony destruction of the ventral column and intraspinal tumor masses compressing the spinal cord at levels C3 and C4. The hypervascularized tumor was supplied by multiple minor vessels from both vertebral arteries, too small to be coiled individually. Due to an allergy to aspirin, intravascular stenting of the vertebral arteries was not an option. We decided to perform a preoperative direct injection of Onyx-18 for embolization of the tumor.

**Conclusion**: Presurgical direct injection of Onyx-18 for treating hypervascular spinal metastases of breast cancer appears to be an effective and safe technique that reduces intraoperative bleeding to a minimum.

**Keywords**: Onyx, Direct puncture, Spinal metastases, Breast cancer, Embolization

## Introduction

Vertebral lesions can severely reduce quality of life, causing excessive pain, pathologic fractures, vertebral instability, and the risk of severe neurological impairment. Metastases from well-vascularized primary tumors, such as breast carcinoma, are frequently encountered in the spine. The majority of these metastases are hypervascularized. The cervical spine is a less common site for metastatic disease (10%) compared to the thoracic or lumbar spine (70% or 20%). Around 50% of metastases arise from one of three primary cancer types: breast, lung, or prostate. Wong et al. found spinal metastases in 36% of cancer-deceased patients by autopsy. Surgical stabilization and neural decompression are part of the standard oncologic management. Due to hypervascularization, there is a high risk of massive blood loss during surgery. Preoperative embolization should be considered for patients undergoing open stabilization of spinal neoplasms.

Onyx is a nonpolymerizing liquid agent composed of ethylene-vinyl alcohol copolymer dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide. It creates a spongy cast that facilitates intravascular occlusion. Some studies describe it as a safe and efficient technique for embolizing head and neck tumors. In a retrospective study, Ghobrial et al. presented data on patients undergoing preoperative transarterial embolization of spinal tumors, concluding that transarterial embolization is a safe and feasible option.

This study reports, to our best knowledge, the first case of cervical metastasis of breast carcinoma embolized by direct injection of Onyx-18.

## Case Presentation

A 67-year-old woman was referred to our department with progressive pain in the cervical spine and weakness in both hands. Upon admission, her Oswestry Disability Index was 98%, indicating serious restriction. The visual analog scale for pain in the spine and leg was 10.0. X-ray, CT, and MRI scans revealed an osteolytic mass within the vertebral bodies of C3 and C4, causing nearly complete bony destruction of the ventral column and intraspinal tumor masses compressing the spinal cord at levels C3 and C4. The tumor masses infiltrated the posterior arch of both vertebrae and showed intraspinal growth from the dorsolateral left side. Due to segmental instability, she had to wear a stiff neck collar for stabilization.

The patient had been diagnosed with left-sided breast carcinoma 10 years earlier, for which she underwent a subcutaneous mastectomy and received adjuvant chemotherapy. Additionally, she had undergone a hysterectomy 9 years ago when diagnosed with uterine carcinoma.

The indication for operative decompression and internal stabilization of the cervical spine was made. Permission for a case report was granted, and informed consent about potential complications was obtained from the patient. The preoperative embolization was performed by the Department of Radiology. Due to the diffuse blood supply of the tumor, preoperative coiling was not possible. Because of the patient's aspirin allergy, the implantation of a drug-eluting stent was also not an option. We decided to use Onyx-18. Through a superselective introduced catheter, a direct injection of Onyx-18 into the supplying branch of the tumor was performed. The success of the injection was verified by angiography.

Surgery was performed the day after the direct injection of Onyx-18, in a supine position under general anesthesia, using a ventral approach. After microsurgical exposure, the tumor was found to be completely extradural. Following the resection of the tumor and corporectomy, the vertebral bodies C3 and C4 were replaced with a Pyramesh-Cage (Medtronic) and ventral plate-stabilization (CSLP, Synthes) from C2 to C5.

Due to the preoperative embolization, intraoperative blood loss was minimized to 750 ml, and the surgeon had an optimal view of the operational site. A blood transfusion was not necessary, either intraoperatively or postoperatively.

Macroscopic pathological examination revealed a compact, white-brown colored tumor. The histological examination revealed tumor cells with eosinophilic staining stoma and basophilic nuclei. Following immunohistochemical staining, the tumor cells were positive for cytokeratin (CK) 7, CK20, P63, TTF-1, and negative for Chromogranin A. 95% were positive for estrogen receptor (ER), negative for HER2/neu and progesterone receptor (PR). The Ki67 proliferation index was 7%. The final histopathology report confirmed the tumor as a metastatic lesion originating from the previously diagnosed breast adenocarcinoma.

Postoperatively, the patient reported a significant reduction in pain (VAS postoperative spine 3, leg 0) and a substantial increase in the Oswestry Disability Index to 78%. She was able to walk down the hallway without the support of crutches and was transferred to the medical oncologic service to receive combined radiochemotherapy.

## Discussion

The spinal column is the most common location among osseous sites for metastatic deposits, and spinal involvement may occur in up to 40% of patients with cancer. Most of these metastases are hypervascularized. The presurgical embolization of hypervascular bone metastases has been shown to be effective in reducing blood loss during surgery and providing an excellent view of the site during the operation.

Aggressive spinal hemangiomas have been successfully treated with Onyx. Additionally, the effectiveness and safety of Onyx injection have been demonstrated in some head and neck tumors, such as paragangliomas. The use of Onyx for cervical arteriovenous fistulas has also been described.

Clarençon et al. reported two cases of presurgical devascularization by direct puncture and injection of Onyx-18 to treat hypervascular spinal metastases close to the anterior spinal artery. The primary tumors in these cases were renal adenocarcinoma and thyroid cancer.

To the best of our knowledge, this case represents the first description of the use of Onyx-18 in a patient with spinal metastases from breast cancer who also had an allergy to aspirin.

## Conclusion

Presurgical direct injection of Onyx-18 for treating hypervascular spinal metastases of breast cancer appears to be an effective and safe technique that minimizes intraoperative bleeding.
