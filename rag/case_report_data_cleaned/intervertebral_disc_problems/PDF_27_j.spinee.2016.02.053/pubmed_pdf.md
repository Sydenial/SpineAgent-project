# Circumferential Intradural Meningioma of the Thoracic Spinal Cord

## Abstract

**Background and Context:** There are very few reported cases of a meningioma circumferentially surrounding the spinal cord. To date, this entity has only been described at the conus medullaris and in the cervical region, with authors basing diagnosis on unusual extramedullary meningioma that completely encircled the spinal cord.

**Case Report:** A 40-year-old woman with progressive numbness of the lower limbs and spasticity of gait following a fall presented to our hospital. Magnetic resonance imaging of the spine demonstrated an abnormality at T6–T7 completely encircling the spinal cord. The patient underwent subtotal resection of the anterolateral portion as total excision was not feasible. Histology revealed a psammomatous meningioma (WHO Grade I). The patient had a good clinical outcome with improvement in numbness of her feet and right hemithorax.

**Conclusion:** This is the first reported case of an intradural extramedullary meningioma completely encircling the thoracic spinal cord. Achieving complete resection of this circumferential meningioma was not possible via a posterior approach. The optimum management of this condition is unknown; clearly, achieving symptomatic relief with adequate cord decompression is paramount. However, the long-term outcome and risk of recurrence in these cases, given their rarity and the difficulties in achieving complete resection, remain unknown.

## Introduction

Meningioma is a common form of spinal neoplasia, accounting for 25%–46% of cases [1]. The majority tend to be intradural, extramedullary, and located within the thoracic region [1]. Typically well-circumscribed, discrete lesions, it is rare to encounter a meningioma that completely encircles the spinal cord.

There are only three cases of fully circumferential spinal meningioma reported in the literature: two in the cervical region [2,3] and one at the conus medullaris [4]. Here, we present the first reported case of a fully circumferential intradural, extramedullary meningioma of the thoracic spinal cord.

## Case Report

A 40-year-old woman with a known history of systemic lupus erythematosus presented following recurrent falls. She had experienced progressive numbness in her lower limbs for approximately 18 months, and pelvic numbness for approximately 4 months. The onset of pelvic symptoms coincided with episodes of urinary and fecal urgency. She had also experienced significant weight loss of approximately 13 kg six months prior to presentation.

On examination, she had a spastic gait with normal, symmetrical power in both lower limbs. Tone was increased, with bilaterally brisk deep tendon reflexes, extensor plantar responses, and bilateral ankle clonus. A sensory level was noted at approximately T6.

Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated an enhancing lesion within the subdural space circumferentially encasing the spinal cord at the level of T6 and T7, without involvement of the surrounding bone. Differential diagnoses included lymphoma or other primary or secondary spinal tumors, with tuberculosis or inflammatory processes considered less likely.

Computed tomography of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis revealed extensive calcification within the subdural space at T6–T7, without epidural infiltration or destruction of the adjacent vertebral structures. No distant abnormalities were detected.

Given the unusual radiological appearance and progressive neurological symptoms, urgent decompression with biopsy and debulking was undertaken. A midline durotomy was performed under microscopic vision. The dura was dissected free from a calcified layer beneath, which was divided longitudinally to reveal an associated extramedullary soft tissue lesion. This was carefully dissected away from the spinal cord.

Frozen section histology supported the intraoperative suspicion of meningioma. Posterolateral resection was achieved with piecemeal removal of heavily calcified tissue, decompressing the spinal cord. The remaining anterolateral portion of the tumor was left in situ due to dense adherence to the cord and unacceptable risk of neurological injury. The dura was left open and repaired using dural substitute and fibrin sealant.

The patient recovered well postoperatively, received a short course of dexamethasone, and was discharged home after physiotherapy.

Histopathology revealed a meningothelial tumor with dense psammomatous microcalcifications. No atypical or malignant features were present, and Ki-67 labeling was very low. A diagnosis of psammomatous meningioma (WHO Grade I) was made.

At six-month follow-up, the patient’s gait had markedly improved, though numbness of the right hemithorax persisted. She had returned to work and resumed full exercise. Postoperative MRI confirmed satisfactory posterior decompression with residual anterior and lateral tumor as expected. Annual MRI surveillance was planned, with further intervention to be considered if tumor progression occurs.

## Discussion

A review of the literature identified three cases of fully circumferential spinal meningioma [2–4] and two cases of partially circumferential lesions [4,5]. Only three reported cases were fully circumferential: two cervical and one involving the conus medullaris and cauda equina. To our knowledge, this is the first reported case involving the thoracic spinal cord.

The unusual imaging features and rarity of the condition initially made meningioma an unlikely diagnosis. Infection and inflammatory etiologies were considered due to the patient’s medical and travel history, and lymphoma was initially favored based on imaging characteristics. The presence of calcification on CT and intraoperative findings ultimately supported the diagnosis of meningioma.

The natural history of circumferential spinal meningiomas is unknown. Complete resection is typically achievable in standard spinal meningiomas and is associated with low recurrence rates [1]. However, complete resection is significantly more challenging in fully circumferential lesions, and incomplete resection carries a higher risk of recurrence [7].

Achieving complete resection in this case would require anterior thoracic cord access, typically via a transthoracic approach. Given the rarity of the condition, the risks of such invasive surgery may outweigh the uncertain benefits. Extensive calcification, as seen in this patient, has been associated with increased surgical morbidity [10].

Symptomatic relief through adequate decompression remains the primary goal when complete resection is not feasible. Leaving the dura open has been suggested to reduce the risk of arachnoiditis and postoperative cord compression [4], a strategy adopted in this case.

None of the reported cases, including ours, received radiotherapy. While adjunctive radiotherapy may be considered in cases of recurrence [1], we elected to defer radiotherapy given the patient’s young age, good neurological recovery, and heavy tumor calcification, reserving it for future progression if necessary.

## Conclusion

This case represents the first reported instance of a meningioma completely encircling the thoracic spinal cord. Optimal management of this rare entity remains undefined. Symptomatic relief via spinal cord decompression is paramount. Complete resection, while ideal, is often not achievable in fully circumferential lesions. Consequently, long-term surveillance with serial imaging and extended follow-up is required.
