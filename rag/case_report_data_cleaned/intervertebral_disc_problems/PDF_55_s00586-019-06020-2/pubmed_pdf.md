# Management of a Pseudarthrosis with Sagittal Malalignment in a Patient with Ochronotic Spondyloarthropathy

## Abstract

The case of a 56-year-old female who underwent L4L5 laminectomy for central canal stenosis and later complained of progressively severe low back pain with significant forward imbalance while walking is presented. X-rays showed non-compensated sagittal malalignment due to thoracolumbar kyphosis, CT scans revealed multilevel central intradiscal calcifications, and MRI showed hypointensity at L4L5 with bone marrow edema. Alkaptonuric ochronosis was suspected and confirmed by the presence of homogentisic acid in the urine, leading to a diagnosis of L4L5 pseudarthrosis with associated sagittal malalignment in the context of ochronotic spondyloarthropathy. The patient underwent surgery with a posterior-only approach and showed satisfactory clinical and radiological results at 2 years.

## Case Presentation

A 56-year-old female complained of progressive forward imbalance for the past 4 years. Her past history revealed generalized joint pain since early adulthood, chronic low back pain, and restricted thoracic and lumbar spine movement in the last 10 years. Six years ago, she underwent L4L5 laminectomy for neurogenic claudication. Two years later, she experienced progressively disabling low back pain with a significant forward imbalance, requiring a cane for support. Physical examination revealed a forward bending posture, painful supine position, and normal neurological findings.

X-rays showed a widely opened L4L5 intervertebral disc space with major vacuum phenomena and sclerotic margins. CT confirmed the radiological findings with multiple levels of disc calcification. Sagittal MRI showed hypointensity at L4L5 with bone marrow edema. Alkaptonuric ochronosis was suspected and confirmed by the presence of homogentisic acid in the urine.

The diagnosis of pseudarthrosis of L4L5 with associated sagittal malalignment in the context of ochronotic spondyloarthropathy was established. Surgical intervention was proposed as the patient had exhausted all non-operative therapies, including medications, physical therapy, and injections. A single-stage posterior correction with 360 fusion and a long construct from T10 to the pelvis was planned.

## Diagnostic Imaging Section

Due to severe forward imbalance, standing full spine X-rays could not be obtained. The lateral view was divided into two images, showing significant sagittal malalignment with positive sagittal vertical axis and thoracolumbar kyphosis. Supine X-rays showed partial reduction of the kyphosis and calcification within the discs. CT confirmed the intervertebral disc calcification, vacuum disc at L4L5, and sclerotic endplates. MRI showed loss of intervertebral disc height with multiple levels of low signal on T1 and T2, consistent with disc calcification, and bone marrow edema at L4L5.

## Historical Review, Epidemiology, Diagnosis, Pathology, and Differential Diagnosis

Alkaptonuria is a rare autosomal recessive disorder with an incidence of 1 in 250,000 to 1 in 1,000,000 live births. It is caused by an enzyme deficiency leading to high levels of homogentisic acid in blood and urine. Homogentisic acid oxidizes and is deposited in articular cartilage, leading to calcification and cartilage breakdown. The disease progresses through four stages: inflammatory, early disc calcification, fibrous ankylosis, and bony ankylosis. Ochronotic spondyloarthropathy is often confused with ankylosing spondylitis due to similar features, including intervertebral disc calcification, disc protrusion, and thoracic myelopathy.

## Rationale for Treatment

There is no specific treatment for ochronosis, and symptomatic therapies are used to prevent further disability. Surgical intervention is considered after non-operative treatments fail. Surgical approaches include anterior, posterior, or combined methods. A combined approach involves anterior debridement with cage placement followed by posterior instrumentation. The posterior-only approach is increasingly popular as it allows for 360° fusion, correction of sagittal malalignment, and shorter operative time. Long-segment instrumentation is recommended for ankylosing spinal disorders to ensure alignment and stability.

For this patient, a posterior-only approach was used with a long-segment pedicle screw construct from T10 to the pelvis, incorporating a high cage at L4L5.

## Operative Procedure

Under general anesthesia, the patient was placed in a prone position. Spinal cord monitoring was used, including transcranial motor evoked potentials and electromyography (EMG) of the lower extremities. A midline incision was made, and the spine was exposed subperiosteally from T10 to the sacrum. Facetectomies were performed to increase flexibility, and pedicle screws were placed under image guidance. The interspinous ligament was removed and found to have black pigmentation, supporting the diagnosis of ochronosis.

Ponte osteotomies were performed at L1L2, L2L3, and L4L5. The disc at L4L5 was removed, revealing dark pigmentation, and a high cage was inserted. Autologous bone graft was used, and cobalt chrome rods were placed to correct the kyphosis. The posterior elements were decorticated, and bone graft was applied.

## Clinical Outcome

The patient began walking on day 2 with assistance and was discharged on day 6. At 2 years, clinical and radiological results were satisfactory, with good alignment and a stable construct. Pathological examination confirmed ochronosis, and postoperative X-rays and CT scans showed successful fusion at L4L5 and correction of the kyphosis.
