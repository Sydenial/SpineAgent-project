# Anterior Submandibular Retropharyngeal Odontoid Osteotomy and Posterior Atlantoaxial Fusion for Irreducible Atlantoaxial Dislocation Associated with Odontoid Fracture Malunion

## Abstract

**Purpose**: To report a case of complex odontoid fracture malunion accompanied by atlantoaxial dislocation, which was treated with a new surgical approach.  
**Methods**: A 53-year-old female was admitted due to progressive symptoms with a stiff limp and unsteady gait. Preoperative examination, diagnostic radiography, computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) were performed following admission. The examinations showed odontoid fracture malunion, an old right axial zygapophyseal fracture, atlantoaxial dislocation, and spinal cord injury. Anterior submandibular retropharyngeal odontoid osteotomy and posterior atlantoaxial fusion were then performed.  
**Results**: Good reduction of the atlantoaxial dislocation was achieved. The cervical spinal cord compression was significantly relieved, and neurological function improved.  
**Conclusion**: Anterior submandibular retropharyngeal odontoid osteotomy and posterior atlantoaxial fusion fixation is an effective method for treating irreducible atlantoaxial dislocation (IAAD) associated with odontoid fracture malunion. It avoids the adverse effects of anterior transoral odontoid osteotomy and provides a new option for the treatment of odontoid fracture malunion associated with atlantoaxial dislocation.

**Keywords**: Anterior, Odontoid osteotomy, Atlantoaxial dislocation, Odontoid fracture, Malunion

## Introduction

Odontoid fracture malunion is intractable and can lead to irreducible atlantoaxial dislocation, causing spinal cord irritation. Anterior surgery is typically the primary treatment or is combined with adjuvant therapy for complete reduction of atlantoaxial dislocation. Anterior transoral odontoid partial resection or atlas anterior arch resection is routinely used for anterior reduction; however, the disadvantages include tedious perioperative preparation, narrow operative field, and a high incidence of complications. A patient with complex odontoid fracture malunion accompanied by atlantoaxial dislocation was treated with a new surgical approach. Anterior submandibular retropharyngeal odontoid osteotomy was used for anterior reduction, avoiding the adverse effects of anterior transoral odontoid osteotomy and providing a new option for treating odontoid fracture malunion associated with atlantoaxial dislocation.

## Case Report

A 53-year-old female farmer in previous good health fell from a farm vehicle three months prior to evaluation. She had neck pain but did not seek medical attention at that time. One month prior to evaluation, she developed progressive symptoms with a stiff limp and unsteady gait. One week prior to admission, an odontoid fracture malunion associated with atlantoaxial dislocation was diagnosed using CT. Preoperative examination, diagnostic radiography, CT, and MRI were performed following admission. The examinations showed odontoid fracture malunion, an old right axial zygapophyseal fracture, atlantoaxial dislocation, and spinal cord injury. Physical examination revealed increased limb muscle tone, decreased strength in the right upper extremity, left upper extremity, and bilateral lower extremities. The preoperative Japanese Orthopaedic Association (JOA) score was 8.

After general anesthesia, skeletal traction was performed, and traction weight was gradually increased to a maximum of 5 kg, with no change in dislocation. Anterior submandibular retropharyngeal odontoid osteotomy and posterior atlantoaxial fusion were then performed. The patient was placed in a supine position with sustained traction. A 4-cm oblique incision was made 2 cm inferior to the mandible, extending along the anterior border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle. The superficial fascia and platysma were divided in line with the incision. The dissection exposed the sternocleidomastoid muscle and superior belly of the omohyoid muscle, allowing for exposure of the superior thyroid artery and the superior laryngeal nerve. Care was taken not to injure or stretch the superior laryngeal nerve.

Blunt dissection was then performed in the retropharyngeal space. The mandible was retracted upward, and the fascia was transected to expose the C1-C2 lateral facet. Odontoid osteotomy was performed using a high-speed burr and ultrasonic bone curettage, with continuous monitoring of atlantoepistrophic joint reduction. After the anterior incision was closed, cranial traction was removed, a neck collar was applied, and the patient was placed in a prone position. X-ray showed good reduction of the atlantoaxial dislocation. Cancellous bone was harvested from the posterior superior iliac spine for subsequent graft fusion. The operative time was 245 minutes with approximately 200 ml of blood loss. CT reexamination showed complete decompression of the ventral spinal cord. The atlantoepistrophic slope angle increased from 126° to 141°. One month after surgery, the JOA score improved to 12, showing a 50% improvement.

## Discussion

The etiology of atlantoaxial dislocation includes trauma, inflammation, tumors, or congenital malformation. Decompression and internal fixation are typically performed. Yin et al. classified reducible atlantoaxial dislocation (RAAD), irreducible atlantoaxial dislocation (IAAD), and fixed atlantoaxial dislocation (FAAD). Patients with IAAD can be repositioned via anterior transoral decompression, while FAAD patients typically require osseous atlantoaxial fusion. The atlantoaxial vertebra has a unique structure, and its stability depends on the transverse and alar ligaments. When the odontoid process is fractured, these ligaments become ineffective, leading to atlantoaxial instability and dislocation. Although odontoid fractures are not generally associated with spinal cord injury, they can lead to neurological impairment if misdiagnosed.

Hao et al. reported that anterior atlantoaxial release through an anterior retropharyngeal approach following traction reduction with C-arm fluoroscopy monitoring achieved satisfactory reduction in most cases. Xu et al. performed transoral anterior release, odontoid partial resection, and posterior fusion for the treatment of irreducible atlantoaxial dislocation caused by odontoid fracture malunion, with an improvement rate of 87.4%. Vender et al. described seven patients requiring C-2 corpectomy who underwent retropharyngeal C1–3 fusion, achieving good therapeutic effects but requiring tracheal cannula support for 3–5 days.

Anterior transoral odontoid partial resection is increasingly used due to the complex anatomy of the cervical region. However, this approach is associated with a high risk of complications, including cerebrospinal fluid leakage, wound infections, and meningitis. Anterior submandibular retropharyngeal odontoid osteotomy reduces the risk of such complications and also simplifies perioperative preparation. The use of piezosurgery and high-speed burrs reduces the risk of injury to vascular, neurological, and dural tissues.

In our patient, spinal cord compression was mainly caused by fractures of the proximal odontoid and posterior arch of the atlas. The preservation of the anterior arch and release of surrounding soft tissue avoided posterior occipitocervical fusion, relieved compression on the ventral spinal cord, and maintained atlanto-occipital stability. The use of pedicle screws and lateral mass screw fixation provided satisfactory results in posterior surgery.
