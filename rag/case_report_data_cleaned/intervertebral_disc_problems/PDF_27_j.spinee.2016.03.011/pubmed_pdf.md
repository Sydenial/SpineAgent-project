# Multiple Myeloma Presenting With Acute Bony Spinal Cord Compression and Mechanical Instability Successfully Managed Nonoperatively

## Abstract

**Background Context:** Multiple myeloma (MM) with spinal involvement may present with spinal cord compression due to soft tissue or bony disease. Patients without neurological compromise are commonly managed with chemotherapy or radiotherapy alone, whereas those with neurological deficit traditionally require surgical decompression with or without internal fixation. Infrequently, patients present with acute bony spinal cord compression associated with mechanical instability, for which surgery is generally considered mandatory.

**Purpose:** To report a high-risk myeloma patient with a mechanically unstable spine, acute spinal cord compression, and neurologic deficit who was treated successfully using nonoperative means.

**Study Design:** Case report.

**Methods:** A 37-year-old male with MM was referred to a tertiary spinal unit with acute bony spinal cord compression and neurological impairment. Computed tomography revealed lytic lesions at T2 and T3 with anterolisthesis of T1 on T2 causing mechanical instability. Magnetic resonance imaging confirmed epidural disease extension and spinal cord compression. The patient was managed nonoperatively using a brace.

**Results:** Management with a brace restored clinical and radiological stability and normal neurological function.

**Conclusion:** Selected high-risk myeloma patients with a mechanically unstable spine, acute spinal cord compression, and neurologic deficit may be treated effectively in an appropriate brace when managed within a tertiary referral spinal unit.

## Introduction

A 37-year-old male with undiagnosed multiple myeloma (MM) presented with a 2-month history of upper thoracic back pain. He was subsequently diagnosed with light chain MM and commenced on chemotherapy and corticosteroids for disease control, along with radiotherapy to the spine for pain relief. Following an acute deterioration in lower limb motor and sensory function, he was referred to a tertiary spinal myeloma service.

Neurological examination revealed a sensory deficit in the right T7–T11 dermatomes and motor weakness graded as 4/5 on the Medical Research Council scale in both lower limbs, consistent with ASIA grade D. There was no bowel or bladder dysfunction, and his Visual Analogue Scale score for back pain was 7 out of 10.

## Case Report

Computed tomography of the thoracic spine demonstrated multiple lytic lesions, most severe at T2 and T3, resulting in significant vertebral body height loss and regional kyphosis. There was marked anterolisthesis of T1 on T2, producing substantial narrowing of the spinal canal. Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated involvement of the posterior vertebral walls at T2 and T3, with extension of disease into the epidural space and associated spinal cord compression. The patient’s Spinal Instability Neoplastic Score was 18, indicating gross instability.

The patient was managed with a custom-made cervicothoracolumbosacral orthosis for a planned period of 3 months, in conjunction with ongoing chemotherapy and radiotherapy. He was closely monitored with regular clinical and radiographic assessments.

After 10 weeks of bracing, interval computed tomography demonstrated significant anterior and posterior extraosseous bone formation, with reconstitution of the T2 and T3 vertebral bodies and posterior elements. Bridging bone between the affected vertebral levels conferred mechanical stability to the upper thoracic spine. At 12-week follow-up, brace treatment was discontinued. Back pain had resolved completely, neurological function had returned to normal (ASIA grade E), and there was no clinically apparent sagittal deformity. The patient demonstrated a pain-free functional range of spinal movement.

## Discussion

Patients with MM commonly present with back pain secondary to pathological vertebral compression fractures, and cement augmentation techniques can provide effective analgesia in these cases [3,4]. A smaller cohort presents with soft tissue disease extending into the spinal canal, resulting in spinal cord compression and neurological deficit [1]. In the absence of neurological impairment, such lesions are typically managed with chemotherapy and radiotherapy, often avoiding the need for surgical decompression [5–8]. Surgical intervention is generally reserved for cases with acute neurological deficit due to soft tissue compression of the spinal cord or cauda equina [2,9].

Patients with MM presenting with bony spinal cord compression and neurological impairment have traditionally been treated with decompression and instrumented fixation to relieve neural compression and stabilise the spine [9]. This presentation is often associated with spinal instability due to vertebral body collapse, involvement of facet joints and pedicles, and vertebral translation. In our institution, a more conservative approach has been adopted for selected patients with MM and spinal involvement. Radiotherapy and chemotherapy can effectively decompress the spinal canal, and there is significant potential for extraosseous bone healing that may restore spinal stability [4].

Operative management carries risks including anaesthetic complications, wound sepsis, wound dehiscence, iatrogenic neurological injury, and metalwork failure due to poor bone quality. Avoidance of instrumentation is particularly relevant in patients receiving chemotherapy who are pancytopenic or planned for stem cell transplantation, as they are at increased risk of infection [4,9].

This case describes a patient with maximal spinal instability according to the Spinal Instability Neoplastic Score, including lytic disease at a junctional level, greater than 50% vertebral body collapse, bilateral posterolateral involvement, and spinal malalignment due to translation and kyphosis [10,11]. The patient was treated as having an unstable spinal injury and managed with a cervicothoracolumbosacral orthosis to provide relative stability and allow mobilisation. Following treatment, spinal stability was restored clinically and radiographically, with new bone formation in both the anterior and posterior columns and complete neurological recovery.

## Conclusion

This case demonstrates successful nonoperative management of a high-risk myeloma patient with a mechanically unstable spine, acute spinal cord compression, and neurologic deficit. The treatment strategy was safe and effective, avoiding surgical risks while allowing sufficient extraosseous bone formation to restore mechanical stability. Bracing may have a role as part of a comprehensive nonoperative management strategy for selected patients with mechanically and neurologically unstable vertebral fractures due to MM, and should be considered only within a tertiary referral spinal unit with appropriate expertise.
