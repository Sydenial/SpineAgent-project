# "Sternum-Into-Abdomen" Deformity With Vertebral Compression Fractures Managed By Abdominal Compression Following Osteoporotic 2-Level Vertebral Column Resection and Reconstruction  

## Abstract
Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures cause pain and progressive kyphosis. This report discusses a patient with severe thoracic kyphosis after treatment of an osteoporotic compression fracture, whose sternum and lower rib cage were impinging on his gastrointestinal tract, causing early satiety and weight loss. He was successfully managed with a 2-level vertebral column resection (VCR) and spinal fusion. We propose the term "sternum-into-abdomen deformity" for this condition, where VCR was used to alleviate gastrointestinal compression, a technique not previously reported.

## Introduction
Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures primarily aim for pain control and deformity reduction. Vertebral column resection (VCR) for decreasing abdominal compression caused by severe kyphosis after treatment of these fractures has not been reported in the literature. We introduce the term "sternum-into-abdomen deformity" to describe the condition.

## Case Report
A 73-year-old cachectic male patient sustained a T10 vertebral compression fracture after a fall. He was treated with vertebroplasty, but his condition worsened, resulting in a severely stooped posture. Nine months later, he presented with severe back pain, difficulty walking, and gastric fullness. His weight decreased by 25 lbs. Examination revealed severe kyphosis with a prominent skin crease in the anterior abdominal wall. His sternum and lower rib cage were compressing his abdomen, resulting in the "sternum-into-abdomen deformity." Radiographs showed a T9-T10 collapse with vertebroplasty cement. 

The patient underwent a 2-level vertebral column resection (VCR) and spinal fusion. Postoperatively, his gastric compression decreased, back pain reduced, and he regained the ability to tolerate oral intake. His pulmonary function improved, and he regained weight.

## Methods
This case report is based on the review of the hospital and office charts, as well as the analysis of prior treatment reports for the patient's compression fracture.

## Results
The patient had a significant improvement following the 2-level VCR, which restored more normal sagittal alignment and reduced gastric compression. He was able to tolerate food and his weight returned to 142 lbs. Postoperative follow-up radiographs showed stable kyphosis correction at 5 years, and the patient was very satisfied with the improvement in posture.

## Discussion
Osteoporotic compression fractures in elderly patients lead to pain, decreased mobility, and decreased quality of life. Severe kyphosis from multiple fractures can cause pulmonary and gastrointestinal dysfunction due to reduced thoracic/abdominal cavity volume. The term "sternum-into-abdomen deformity" describes the compression of the abdomen by the sternum and rib cage, causing early satiety and weight loss. The VCR procedure for improving sagittal alignment and reducing gastrointestinal compression is a viable option in severe osteoporotic kyphotic deformities.

## Conclusion
We propose the term "sternum-into-abdomen deformity" for the condition where severe kyphosis results in gastric compression. VCR, combined with spinal fusion, can improve quality of life by reducing gastric compression and improving postural alignment.

## Key Points
- "Sternum-into-abdomen deformity" is a new term for severe kyphosis with gastric compression.
- A 2-level vertebral column resection (VCR) can improve alignment and decrease gastric compression, improving the patient's quality of life.
