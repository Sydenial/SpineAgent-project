# Esophageal Perforation in a Cervical Fracture Patient

## Abstract

Esophageal perforation in cervical fracture patients is extremely rare. This report describes a rare complication of esophageal perforation in a patient with progressive ankylosing spondylitis (AS) and cervical fracture. A 61-year-old male with AS sustained a C6 fracture and dislocation after a vehicle accident. Initial treatment involved combined anterior-posterior fixation and fusion. Esophageal perforation was detected during anterior surgery and was treated with surgical debridement, drainage, and resorbable sutures. The patient had a satisfactory recovery with improved neurological function and fracture union at one-year follow-up.

## Introduction

Esophageal perforation in cervical fracture patients is an uncommon and potentially life-threatening complication. This case report highlights the occurrence of this complication in a patient with advanced ankylosing spondylitis (AS), a condition rarely associated with esophageal perforation. The report details the diagnosis, surgical treatment, and outcomes of this case.

## Methods

A 61-year-old male patient with a 25-year history of AS was admitted to the emergency room 12 hours after a vehicle accident. The patient presented with neck pain, left upper limb pain, and numbness. Preoperative radiographic examinations revealed a typical "bamboo spine" and C6 fracture and dislocation. A CT scan confirmed a transverse fracture through the C6 vertebral body with laminar and spinous process fractures. MR imaging showed prevertebral edema, rupture of the anterior longitudinal ligament, and spinal cord compression.

The patient was initially treated with skull traction. However, three days post-admission, the patient developed a fever and throat pain, which worsened with swallowing. Laboratory tests indicated leukocytosis with neutrophilia. The patient was treated with moxifloxacin, and his temperature returned to normal.

An anterior-posterior combined fixation and fusion was planned. During the anterior surgery, yellowish viscous pus was found in the prevertebral space, and a 0.5 cm esophageal perforation caused by a sharp fracture fragment was identified. After thorough debridement, the esophageal lesion was closed with resorbable interrupted sutures. The incision was washed with saline and hydrogen peroxide but was not primarily closed. Posterior surgery was then performed, and fracture reduction was achieved.

## Results

Postoperative management included total parenteral nutrition (TPN) and broad-spectrum antibiotics. The surgical incision was irrigated with saline twice daily, and it was closed after three weeks. The patient’s neurological function improved significantly, with a JOA score increase from 11 points preoperatively to 15 points at the final follow-up. Postoperative radiographs confirmed satisfactory fracture reduction and fixation. Radiological evidence of fracture union and cervical spine stability was obtained at the one-year follow-up.

## Discussion

Esophageal perforation in cervical fracture patients is exceedingly rare, with only 10 reported cases in the literature from 1960 to 2013. This report is the first to describe this complication in a patient with AS. The diagnosis can be challenging due to the atypical signs and symptoms associated with cervical injury. Clinical signs, such as anterior cervical pain, tenderness, and dysphagia, should raise suspicion of esophageal perforation, particularly in the presence of prevertebral edema or emphysema seen on CT or MR imaging.

Barium esophagogram and esophageal endoscopy are useful for confirming the diagnosis. The management of esophageal perforation typically involves surgical debridement, drainage, and intensive care. Continuous irrigation has also been suggested as an alternative. In our case, the outcome was favorable due to the prompt diagnosis and appropriate management, highlighting the importance of timely intervention in such severe complications.

## Key Points

- Esophageal perforation complicating cervical fractures in patients with advanced ankylosing spondylitis is extremely rare and potentially life-threatening.
- Timely diagnosis is critical, with attention to clinical signs that may indicate esophageal perforation.
- Surgical debridement, drainage, and intensive care are essential for a positive outcome in these patients.
