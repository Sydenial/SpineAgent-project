# Pure Distraction Injury of T1-2 with Quad Fever

## Abstract

We report a pure distraction injury of the upper thoracic spine and uncontrolled hyperthermia without an infectious cause. Quad fever appears in the first several weeks to months after a cervical or upper thoracic SCI and is characterized by an extreme elevation in body core temperature beyond 40°C without an infectious cause. Discriminating between infectious and noninfectious causes is important, and a thorough clinical assessment is required.

## Introduction

A 52-year-old male visited the emergency room complaining of back pain with complete paralysis (American Spinal Injury Association [ASIA] A) of both lower extremities after a pedestrian-motor vehicle accident. He had trouble breathing due to a hemothorax and flail chest caused by fractures of the right second to eleventh and left fourth to seventh ribs. A computed tomography scan revealed severe distraction of the T1–2 intervertebral space. A magnetic resonance image showed signal changes in the spinal cord and a clean-cut margin between the T1–2 disc and T2 body. The neurological level of injury was C8 upon the initial neurological assessment. Emergency surgery was performed, involving C6-T3 posterior instrumentation and an autologous iliac bone graft.

## Materials and Methods

A 52-year-old male was involved in a pedestrian-motor vehicle accident, resulting in back pain, complete paralysis of both lower extremities, and difficulty breathing. He was admitted with a blood pressure of 61/40 mmHg, a pulse rate of 60 beats/min, and a body temperature of 36.6°C. CT scans revealed severe distraction of the T1–2 intervertebral space, and MRI showed signal changes in the spinal cord. The patient underwent emergency surgery with posterior instrumentation from C6 to T3 and autologous iliac bone graft.

## Results

After surgery, the core temperature increased gradually to above 38.0°C on post-trauma day 4 and increased to 40.8°C on post-trauma day 7. None of the repeated aerobic, anaerobic, or fungal cultures of the blood, tracheal aspirate, line tips, urine, or stool were positive until post-trauma day 21, when *Candida tropicalis* was identified in the urine culture. On post-trauma day 63, the blood pressure, pulse, and body temperature stabilized, and the patient was transferred to the general ward. At post-trauma year 6, the injury state was still complete, and the neurological level of injury had changed to C4.

## Discussion

This case highlights the rare occurrence of pure distraction injury of the T1-2 spine accompanied by quad fever. Quad fever is characterized by an unexplained fever in patients with cervical or upper thoracic spinal cord injuries (SCI). The etiology remains unclear, but it is thought to result from autonomic dysfunction associated with SCI. The clinical challenge lies in differentiating it from infectious causes of fever. Early recognition and management are critical in preventing secondary complications.

## Case Report

A 52-year-old male was admitted following a pedestrian-motor vehicle accident, presenting with paraplegia (ASIA Impairment Scale A), respiratory distress from multiple rib fractures, and hemothorax. His neurological level of injury was identified as C8. CT scans revealed severe T1-2 distraction, while MRI confirmed spinal cord changes. The patient underwent emergency posterior instrumentation and fusion surgery. During recovery, his core temperature increased, leading to persistent fever without an infectious source, later identified as quad fever.

## Diagnostic Imaging

Thoracic spine CT scans revealed severe distraction of the T1–2 intervertebral space. Chest CT showed multiple rib fractures and hemothorax. MRI revealed signal changes in the spinal cord, and a clean-cut margin between the T1–2 disc and T2 body was observed.

## Historical Review of the Condition

Quad fever was first described in 1885, with thermoregulatory dysfunction in upper thoracic SCI patients. The condition is characterized by elevated body temperature beyond 40°C, typically without an infectious cause. Recognizing quad fever and differentiating it from infections is crucial for appropriate management.

## Rationale for Treatment and Evidence-Based Literature

Body temperature regulation is controlled by the thermoregulatory center in the anterior hypothalamus. After SCI, hypothalamic control of temperature is impaired, leading to thermoregulatory dysfunction. Treatment strategies include maintaining hydration, using antipyretics, and cooling measures. In some cases, invasive temperature modulation may be required to manage quad fever effectively.

## Procedure (Surgery, Intervention)

The posterior ligamentous complex was completely disrupted during surgery. The torn ligamentum flavum at T1–2 was resected, and posterior instrumentation from C6 to T3 with fusion using an autologous iliac bone graft was performed.

## Outcome, Follow-up

At post-trauma year 6, the patient's injury state remained complete, with the neurological level of injury changed to C4. Shoulder elevation motor power was reduced to grade 3, and fine hand movements were impossible. Spontaneous T1–2 interbody fusion was observed on follow-up cervical-thoracic CT scans.
