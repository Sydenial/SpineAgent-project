# Delayed Fatal Lumbar Artery Bleeding Following Less Invasive Posterolateral Decompression and Fusion

## Abstract
### Study Design
A case report.

### Objective
Familiarize spine surgeons with less invasive dorsolateral approaches to the thoracolumbar spine.

### Summary of Background Data
Less invasive approaches are associated with a low risk of major intraoperative bleeding; however, segmental and other vessels may rarely be damaged. Surgeons should be aware of these rare but potentially dangerous vascular complications because they can be life-threatening, particularly in elderly patients with increased morbidity and/or metastatic disease to the spine.

### Methods
A 76-year-old Caucasian woman with a comminuted osteoporotic fracture of the L1 vertebra with spinal canal encroachment underwent corpectomy and 360-degree fusion using a titanium mesh cage through a less invasive dorsolateral approach.

### Results
Despite the lack of visible intraoperative bleeding and an uneventful postoperative period, the patient died on the seventh postoperative day due to massive bleeding from a segmental artery at the level of the L3 vertebral body, two levels below the intervention area.

### Conclusions
This is a rare complication of delayed and fatal bleeding of the left L3 segmental vertebral artery following less invasive L1 corpectomy, mesh cage insertion, and pedicle screw fixation in an elderly female patient with a history of two malignancies. Although injury to large vessels must always be prevented during these procedures, injury to segmental vessels occurs more frequently. The dorsolateral and other retroperitoneal approaches to the thoracolumbar region are established methods for surgical stabilization of comminuted vertebral body fractures, especially in experienced hands. Great care should be taken postoperatively for signs of bleeding and hematoma given the potential for life-threatening complications.

### Keywords
Fatal bleeding; segmental artery; dorsolateral approach; delayed complication; corpectomy; spinal fusion; death; thoracolumbar spine; fracture.

### Level of Evidence
5

## Introduction
Life-threatening bleeding following less invasive dorsolateral approaches in the thoracolumbar junction is exceptional. There are only a few reports on intraoperative injuries to great vessels and segmental arteries in approaches to the thoracolumbar spine. To the authors’ knowledge, this is the first report of postoperative massive bleeding of the L3 segmental artery following less invasive dorsolateral L1 corpectomy in a 76-year-old woman with a fatal outcome.

## Materials and Methods
A 76-year-old woman presented with a 6-month history of progressive thoracolumbar pain without neurologic deficits. Plain radiographs and CT revealed a pathological fracture of the L1 vertebra (Figures 1a, b). Her past history included right colectomy for malignancy and total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy for ovarian malignancy fifteen and ten years, respectively, before this admission.

Because of high perioperative morbidity (elderly, previous abdominal operations) associated with a traditional anterior approach, a less invasive left dorsolateral retroperitoneal approach was selected. Preoperative transpedicular biopsy of L1 disclosed no malignancy, while CT angiography did not reveal vascular malformation or pathologically increased blood supply to the anterior thoracolumbar spine.

Surgery was performed in the prone position. A skin incision was made 1 cm left of the midline. Left-sided dissection included unilateral detachment of paravertebral muscles and removal of the transverse processes of T12, L1, and L2, and the T12 rib head, proceeding retroperitoneally to the lateral vertebral wall of T12–L2. Pedicle screws were inserted on the left at T11, T12, L2, and L3. The left L1 nerve root was prepared, ligated, and cut. Meticulous intraoperative control of bleeding followed ligation of the segmental vessels T12–L2. Following partial left corpectomy, a titanium mesh cage filled with allograft was inserted into the vertebrectomy defect. Four pedicle screws were then transfascially inserted on the right at T11, T12, L2, and L3, and both sides were secured with longitudinal rods. Neuromonitoring was used.

## Results
The postoperative course was uneventful without clinical or laboratory signs of substantial bleeding. The patient was ambulatory by postoperative day 2 with a custom-made orthosis under low molecular weight heparin anticoagulation and was discharged on postoperative day 7. During the perioperative period, the patient received a total of 3 units of blood. On the day of discharge, laboratory values were: Hct 36.3%, Hb 12 g/dL, PLTs 131,000, with other values within normal limits.

On the afternoon of discharge, at home, the patient felt dizzy, became unconscious, and was transferred hemodynamically unstable to a regional hospital. Admission labs showed Hct 18.2% and Hb 6.1 g/dL. After initial hemodynamic stabilization, CT angiography revealed a large retroperitoneal hematoma with active bleeding, possibly from the left L3 segmental artery. Due to her critical condition, embolization was suggested by interventional radiologists; however, the family declined further intervention. She was transferred to the ICU, where she died 8 hours later from disseminated intravascular coagulation.

## Discussion
This report describes an extremely rare and fatal complication: delayed, initially asymptomatic postoperative hematoma due to bleeding from the left L3 segmental vertebral artery following semi-open left-sided posterolateral decompression and L1 corpectomy in an elderly female patient. Notably, the bleeding vessel was located two levels below the corpectomy level. The exact cause could not be established because an autopsy was declined. The authors speculate that prior malignancies and abdominal surgeries (colon cancer, ovarian malignancy) may have induced vasculopathy, arteriovenous shunts, and vessel fragility. Surgical manipulation for cage insertion at the upper border of L2, close to L3, may have provoked an accidental, minor, indirect laceration of the left L3 segmental artery, and a potential postoperative hypertension crisis could have triggered bleeding. In combination with thromboprophylaxis, the result was fatal. Another theoretical explanation is an unrecognized puncture during spinal biopsy or pedicle screw insertion [4].

Less invasive lateral thoracolumbar approaches for vertebrectomy are technically demanding due to complex anterior anatomy and narrow exposure of vital structures at this junction. The reported rate of psoas hematoma in retroperitoneal transpsoas approaches is relatively low and can be missed even months after initial surgery [5]. Injury of the L4 vertebral artery with a trocar during percutaneous vertebroplasty for an L5 fracture, with bleeding 10 days later, has also been reported [6]. In our case, despite appropriate preoperative evaluation including CT angiography for suspected malignancy of L1 [7], this rare complication led to delayed but significant bleeding and, ultimately, death 7 days after surgery.

Less invasive approaches to the thoracolumbar region are established methods for stabilizing comminuted vertebral body fractures, particularly in experienced hands, but stricter selection criteria may be warranted. Elderly patients and those with a history of malignancy or prior radiotherapy to the abdomen or spine may carry increased risk for complications. If a less invasive approach is chosen, great care should be taken intraoperatively and postoperatively to detect signs of acute or delayed bleeding and prevent life-threatening events. An embolization of the L3 segmental artery might have saved the patient’s life.
