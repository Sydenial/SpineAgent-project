# Multiple noncontiguous spinal fractures and occipitocervical dislocation in a patient with ankylosing spondylitis treated with a hybrid open and percutaneous spinal fixation technique: a case report

## Abstract

**Keywords**: Ankylosing spondylitis; Spinal fractures; Percutaneous fixation; Minimally invasive techniques; Occipital-cervical dislocation; Hybrid technique.

Spinal fractures occur with greater frequency in ankylosing spondylitis (AS) patients. Treatment is complicated due to a higher incidence of medical comorbidities. We report a case of a novel combined open and percutaneous surgical technique used for the treatment of multiple noncontiguous spinal fractures in a patient with AS. A 77-year-old man with multiple comorbidities was treated for occipitocervical dislocation and two noncontiguous three-column extension injuries using a hybrid approach involving open occipital to T3 fusion and percutaneous T5–L1 instrumentation at a tertiary care facility. At two years postoperatively, the patient had recovered ambulatory ability and had a good clinical outcome.

## Introduction

Spinal disease in ankylosing spondylitis (AS) predisposes individuals to spinal fractures, which can occur even with minimal trauma. These fractures are challenging to treat due to the multiple comorbidities common in these patients and the morbidity associated with open surgeries. Here we present a unique case of a patient with AS who sustained three simultaneous spinal fractures and an occipitocervical dislocation after a fall from standing height. This patient was treated with a combined open cervical fusion and percutaneous thoracolumbar fixation to minimize morbidity.

## Case Report

A 77-year-old man with multiple medical comorbidities, including morbid obesity, congestive heart failure, coronary artery disease, diabetes mellitus, and hypertension, sustained a fall from standing height. Computed tomography (CT) revealed a C1 ring fracture, a three-column extension fracture at C7–T1, and an occipitocervical ligamentous injury with incongruity of the joints. Given the patient's comorbidities, he was a poor candidate for traditional open stabilization and was transferred to our institution for surgical management. On admission, an additional T9–T10 three-column extension fracture was identified that had been missed in the initial evaluation. The patient was diagnosed with AS due to spinal ligamentous ossification and marginal syndesmophytes.

The patient underwent an occipital to T3 posterior fusion via open technique and T5–L1 percutaneous instrumentation. The procedure was performed under fluoroscopic guidance. The operation lasted 5 hours with an estimated blood loss of 1,000 cc. Postoperatively, the patient did well and was transferred to a rehab facility after an 18-day hospital admission. Follow-up CT at two years showed complete fusion of the C7–T1 and T9–T10 three-column injuries and a pseudoarthrosis at the occipitocervical junction. The patient was ambulatory and had no pain, except for loss of motion at the occipitocervical junction.

## Discussion

The incidence of noncontiguous spine fractures in AS patients has been reported as high as 19%. Typically, these fractures involve the cervical and thoracic spine. Our case is unique in that the patient sustained three simultaneous spinal fractures along with occipitocervical dislocation. Spinal fractures in AS are often unstable and may be easily missed, leading to severe complications. Neurological compromise is common in cases of delayed diagnosis, emphasizing the importance of thorough imaging, such as CT or MRI, in AS patients with spinal trauma.

Although surgery is preferred over nonoperative management due to the risks of neurologic decline and nonunion, the use of percutaneous stabilization techniques in AS patients is growing. These techniques are less invasive, resulting in decreased blood loss and potentially better outcomes for patients who may not tolerate more extensive open procedures. The hybrid approach used in this case—combining open occipitocervical and cervicothoracic fusion with percutaneous thoracolumbar stabilization—minimized surgical dissection and blood loss, while providing effective stabilization for multiple spinal injuries.

## Conclusion

We report a unique case of three simultaneous noncontiguous spinal fractures and an occipitocervical dislocation in a patient with AS. This injury was successfully treated with a hybrid technique combining open and percutaneous procedures. This approach may offer an effective treatment option for patients with significant medical comorbidities who are at high risk for complications from more extensive open surgeries.
