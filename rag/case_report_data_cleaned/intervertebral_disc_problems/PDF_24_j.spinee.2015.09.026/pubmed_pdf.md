# Posterior Epidural Migration of Lumbar Disc Fragment as an Unusual Ring-Enhancing Mass

## Case Report

A 60-year-old man was admitted with a 2-month history of low back pain and difficulty walking. Bladder and bowel functions were normal, and the clinical history was unremarkable. On examination, both lower limbs showed muscular weakness with increased muscle tone, especially on the right side. Sensation was intact in all dermatomes, and reflexes were normal. Laboratory tests were within normal limits.

Magnetic resonance imaging showed a well-circumscribed, smoothly marginated solid mass within the posterior epidural space at L2–L3, compressing the dura. Signal intensity of the mass was similar to that of the intervertebral disc on all sequences (Fig. 1). The lesion revealed intense rim enhancement on fat-saturated, contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance images (Fig. 1). Computed tomography demonstrated no calcification within the epidural mass (Fig. 2).

Decompressive L2 laminectomy was performed. A large, solid, sequestered disc fragment in the posterior epidural space was found and removed. Microscopic examination showed fragments of disc tissue with histopathologic features of degenerated fibrocartilage and clusters of chondrocytes, findings suggestive of repair. The postoperative course was uneventful.

## Discussion

Posterior epidural migration of a lumbar disc herniation is very rare and may present with low back pain, radicular compression, or cauda equina syndrome [1]. Preoperative diagnosis is challenging because the rim enhancement can mimic an intraspinal extradural tumor [2,3]. The peripheral enhancement of the disc fragment on contrast-enhanced imaging is attributed to an inflammatory response with vascular granulation tissue around the disc mass. Recognition of the mass having signal intensity similar to the intervertebral disc on magnetic resonance images, together with peripheral rim enhancement, is key to correctly diagnosing posterior epidural migration of a disc fragment.
