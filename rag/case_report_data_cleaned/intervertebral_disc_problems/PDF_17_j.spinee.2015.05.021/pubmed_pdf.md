# Redundant Nerve Root Syndrome of the Cauda Equina: The Benefits of 3D CISS MRI Sequence

## Abstract

A 58-year-old woman presented with progressive lumbalgia. Conventional lumbar vertebra magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed tortuous structures like vascular malformations located in cauda equina fibers with a narrow lumbar spinal canal. Following this, a three-dimensional (3D) constructive interference in steady state (CISS) sequence was obtained. Thanks to its high spatial resolution (SR), 3D CISS sequence images showed the continuity of cauda equina nerve roots in this region. These findings are consistent with redundant nerve root syndrome of the cauda equina. SR constitutes one of the major problems and objectives in spinal imaging. The high SR can be obtained by this sequence, and 3D CISS provides detailed information about anatomic structures. Confusing and subtle abnormalities of the spine are more fully clarified by using 3D CISS.

## Introduction

Redundant nerve root syndrome of the cauda equina is a condition characterized by an unusual anatomical presentation in which multiple nerve roots appear redundant or abnormal. Conventional imaging techniques like MRI can struggle to differentiate such abnormalities due to the low spatial resolution. However, advanced MRI sequences, such as the 3D CISS sequence, offer significantly improved resolution, making them invaluable for better visualization of complex spinal anatomy.

## Methods

### Patient Presentation

The patient, a 58-year-old female, had been experiencing progressive lumbalgia. Traditional lumbar MRI revealed tortuous structures resembling vascular malformations and narrow spinal canal findings. However, these images lacked clarity in showing the continuity of cauda equina nerve roots.

### Imaging Technique

A 3D CISS MRI sequence was utilized, which provides superior spatial resolution compared to traditional imaging methods. This sequence allowed for high-definition images that revealed the continuity of the cauda equina nerve roots, a key characteristic of redundant nerve root syndrome.

## Results

The application of 3D CISS imaging was critical in identifying the redundant nerve root syndrome. The high spatial resolution of this imaging technique allowed for the clear depiction of nerve root continuity, which was not visible in conventional MRI scans. The images revealed the anatomical details needed to diagnose the syndrome accurately, providing clarity on the structural abnormalities that were previously difficult to identify.

## Discussion

The 3D CISS sequence plays a crucial role in enhancing the clarity of spinal imaging, particularly for complex conditions such as redundant nerve root syndrome. The improved resolution enables clinicians to visualize subtle abnormalities that may be missed with traditional imaging techniques. This case highlights the importance of advanced MRI techniques in diagnosing and understanding spinal disorders, especially those affecting the cauda equina.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, the use of 3D CISS MRI sequence significantly enhances the ability to diagnose redundant nerve root syndrome of the cauda equina. This technique's high spatial resolution provides detailed images that are critical for identifying complex anatomical abnormalities, ultimately improving diagnostic accuracy and patient care.
