# Risk factors and prognosis for acute progression of myelopathic symptoms in patients with ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament after minor trauma

## Abstract

**Study design:** Retrospective Cohort study.

**Objective:** To identify the risk factors for acute progression of myelopathic symptoms (MS) associated with ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL) after minor trauma and to compare the prognosis between an acute PMS group and a chronic PMS group.

**Summary of Background Data:** Although the prevalence of OPLL among patients with cervical myelopathy is high, few studies have been published regarding the risk factors for acute PMS associated with OPLL after minor trauma.

## Methods

Patients with OPLL who had histories of minor trauma and had undergone surgery were divided according to clinical course into an acute (within 48 hours, n=38) and a chronic PMS group (n=32). The type of trauma and the clinical and radiologic characteristics were compared. The clinical outcomes were also compared at admission and at 1 and 2 years post-operatively.

## Results

The types of trauma were significantly different between the two groups (p<0.05). Univariate analysis revealed that older age, a narrower space available for the cord, and a higher rate of stenosis in the spinal canal were associated with acute PMS after minor trauma (p=0.014, 0.020, and 0.006, respectively). However, the rate of stenosis in the spinal canal was the only risk factor that was identified in a multivariate analysis (p=0.023; OR, 0.872; 95% CI, 0.774-0.982). The Japanese Orthopedic Association scores at the initial visit and at post-operative years 1 and 2 were significantly lower in the acute PMS group than in the chronic PMS group (p<0.001, <0.001, and <0.001, respectively).

## Conclusion

One risk factor for acute PMS in patients with OPLL after minor trauma is a higher rate of stenosis of the spinal canal. Patients with acute PMS exhibited unfavorable neurologic outcomes. Preventive surgical treatment may be recommended for patients with significant OPLL with mild cervical myelopathy.

Keywords: cervical spine, ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament, spinal canal stenosis, trauma, cervical myelopathy, spinal cord injury, open-door laminoplasty

Level of Evidence: 3

## Introduction

Ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL) is common in the elderly Asian population, and its association with the progression of myelopathic symptoms (PMS) after trauma is well documented. Recent research has reported an alarmingly high prevalence of OPLL in individuals with cervical myelopathy. Trauma is a significant risk factor for individuals with OPLL, but the management of asymptomatic or mildly myelopathic patients remains controversial. This study aims to determine the risk factors for acute PMS in patients with OPLL following minor trauma and explore the clinical courses and neurologic recovery in patients with acute and chronic PMS.

## Materials and Methods

Between January 2003 and October 2014, 321 patients with OPLL underwent surgical treatment in our department. Among them, 108 had a history of trauma, and 91 experienced motor and/or sensory changes or gait disturbances. Patients with cervical fractures, congenital deformities, or chronic systemic illnesses were excluded. A total of 70 patients, with a minimum follow-up period of 2 years, were included in this study, including 38 in the acute PMS group and 32 in the chronic PMS group. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (H-1612-003809).

## Clinical and Radiologic Evaluations

The medical records and radiologic images were reviewed at admission and at post-operative years 1 and 2. The type of trauma, gender, age, surgical approach, and duration of follow-up were assessed. The neurological status was evaluated using the Japanese Orthopedic Association (JOA) score. Radiographic parameters included the type of OPLL, ossified levels, ossification thickness, diameter of the spinal canal, narrowest space available for the cord (AC), and the rate of stenosis in the spinal canal.

## Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using Student's t-test for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. Logistic regression analysis was employed for multivariate analysis. A p-value of <0.05 was considered significant.

## Results

The type of trauma significantly differed between the acute and chronic PMS groups (p=0.006). Falling was the most common type of trauma in both groups, but falling and bike accidents were more frequent in the acute PMS group, while traffic accidents and massages were more common in the chronic PMS group.

The acute PMS group was significantly older and exhibited a narrower space available for the cord and a higher rate of stenosis in the spinal canal than the chronic PMS group (p=0.014, p=0.020, and p=0.006, respectively). Multivariate analysis identified the rate of stenosis in the spinal canal as the only significant risk factor for acute PMS after minor trauma (p=0.023; OR, 0.872; 95% CI, 0.774-0.982).

## Illustrative Case

An 87-year-old man fell at home and immediately experienced quadriplegia. MRI showed central canal stenosis and compressive myelopathy at the C3-4 level. A CT scan revealed large ossified processes consistent with OPLL. The patient underwent C3-6 plate-only, open-door laminoplasty. Despite surgery, he developed pneumonia and pleural effusion and ultimately passed away after 4 months.

## Discussion

OPLL is a significant risk factor for the development of acute cervical spinal cord injury (SCI). In patients with OPLL, the cervical spinal canal is often narrower, increasing the risk of trauma-induced SCI. This study demonstrates that minor trauma can lead to acute myelopathy, especially in patients with significant stenosis of the spinal canal. Early awareness and preventive measures, including potential surgical interventions, are critical for managing OPLL and preventing further complications.

## Limitations of the present study

The study has several limitations, including its retrospective design, the relatively small sample size, and the potential selection bias from including only surgical patients. Further large-scale, long-term studies are needed to confirm the findings and explore non-surgical management options for OPLL patients.

## Conclusions

Minor trauma can lead to PMS in patients with OPLL, especially those with significant spinal canal stenosis. Early surgical treatment might improve outcomes in patients with severe stenosis of the spinal canal.
