# Images of Spine Care

## Posterior Epidural Cyst Associated with Baastrup Disease

A 57-year-old man presented with a several-year history of midline lower back pain radiating down both legs. The pain worsened with extension. On physical examination, he had an antalgic gait of the left lower extremity from the hip to mid-leg, and brisk bilateral deep tendon reflexes. There was lateral lumbar spinal tenderness to palpation.

Lateral radiograph (Fig. 1) demonstrated apposition of the dorsal spinous processes that is characteristic of Baastrup disease. Magnetic resonance imaging (Fig. 2) demonstrated bursal fluid between the L4 and L5 spinous processes, as well as a posterior epidural cyst resulting in significant canal stenosis.

![Lateral radiograph demonstrates apposition of spinous processes at L4-L5 (arrow).](imgs/img_in_image_box_416_795_793_1492.jpg)

Fig. 1. Lateral radiograph demonstrates apposition of spinous processes at L4-L5 (arrow).

![MRI showing bursal fluid and a posterior epidural cyst.](imgs/img_in_image_box_9_43_1147_612.jpg)

Fig. 2. MRI showing bursal fluid and a posterior epidural cyst.

The patient underwent an L4–L5 laminectomy and excision of the epidural cyst. Intraoperatively, a cyst was encountered between the L4 and L5 interspace extending under the lamina of L4 with severe compression of the thecal sac. Postoperatively, the patient had resolution of his back and leg pain, and his left lower extremity strength improved to 5/5.
