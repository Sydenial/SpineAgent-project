# Recurrent dislocations of the atlantooccipital and atlantoaxial joints in a halo vest fixator are resolved by backrest elevation in an elevation angle-dependent manner

Go Kato, MD, PhD, Kenichi Kawaguchi, MD, PhD, Nobuaki Tsukamoto, MD, PhD, Keisuke Komiyama, MD, Kazutaka Mizuta, MD, Takayuki Onohara, MD, Hirofumi Okano, MD, Shunsuke Hotokezaka, MD, PhD, Takao Mae, MD

---

## Abstract

### Background Context:
Halo fixation is universally used in the initial reduction and fixation of unstable upper cervical spine injuries; however, persistent instability and recurrent dislocations of the atlantooccipital and atlantoaxial joints after fixation are not well recognized.

### Purpose:
The aim was to describe persistent instability of traumatic dislocations of the atlantooccipital and atlantoaxial joints after halo fixation and to propose a method for preventing instability.

### Study Design:
This is a case report of a patient who survived traumatic dislocations of the atlantooccipital and atlantoaxial joints.

### Patient Sample:
A 73-year-old woman diagnosed with dislocations of the atlantooccipital and atlantoaxial joints, along with multiple other injuries sustained in a traffic accident, was included.

### Methods:
After initial closed reduction and halo fixation, congruity of the atlantooccipital and atlantoaxial joints was evaluated using the condylar gap, atlantodental interval, and flexion angulation of C1-C2 after the initial examination and before surgery.

### Results:
Changes in parameters 12 hours after halo fixation revealed re-dislocations and instability of the joints. Backrest elevation with halo fixation tended to reduce re-dislocations. Therefore, the backrest angle was carefully increased, and parameters were measured at several angles of elevation within a range that did not affect vital signs. Elevation changed the parameters in an elevation angle-dependent manner, suggesting that elevation was effective in reducing re-dislocations. This method enabled maintenance of good congruity of the joints for approximately 2 weeks until posterior spinal fusion with internal fixation.

### Conclusions:
Backrest elevation with halo fixation appears to be safe, beneficial for preventing re-dislocations of both the atlantooccipital and atlantoaxial joints, and can be performed without additional devices.

## Introduction

Traumatic atlantooccipital dislocation (AOD) is a rare injury, comprising approximately 1% of cervical spine injuries. The injury is usually severe and often fatal, typically accompanied by atlantoaxial dislocation (AAD). AOD and AAD can cause translational movement of the upper cervical spine in both sagittal and vertical planes, potentially leading to severe neurologic deficits. In recent years, survival rates have increased due to advancements in emergency management techniques such as on-site resuscitation and immobilization.

---

## Case Report

A 73-year-old woman was involved in a head-on collision with a dump truck while driving. After being rescued, she immediately underwent intubation for respiratory distress. She was diagnosed with craniocervical dislocation and quadriplegia, along with other injuries including traumatic subarachnoid hemorrhage, hemopneumothorax, aortic dissection, and multiple fractures. After stabilization, she was transported to our hospital.

CT and MRI revealed AOD and AAD. Because of her poor general condition, she was placed in a halo fixator after reduction of AOD and AAD. Although mild dislocations remained, the incongruity of the atlantooccipital and atlantoaxial joints was initially resolved. However, 12 hours later, a follow-up CT showed recurrence of AOD and AAD.

We performed backrest elevation to increase compression force, resolving AOD and AAD in an elevation angle-dependent manner. Elevation angles greater than 40° were avoided due to concerns over low blood pressure. After improving her general condition, surgical reduction and posterior spinal fusion were performed 10 days after the injury. Despite no significant improvement in motor or sensory function, instability of the joints was resolved postoperatively.

---

## Discussion

This case describes traumatic simultaneous AOD and AAD, classified as Type I injury with Type II elements according to Traynelis et al. AOD occurs when ligaments such as the alar ligament or tectorial membrane are disrupted. AAD occurs due to rupture of the transverse ligament of the atlas. These injuries result in significant instability of the upper cervical spine, requiring prompt reduction and immobilization.

Halo fixation is commonly used for initial treatment; however, re-dislocations of AOD and AAD can occur, as demonstrated in our case. Backrest elevation with halo fixation offers a simple, effective alternative method for preventing re-dislocations. This method should be applied carefully to avoid adverse effects on vital signs.
