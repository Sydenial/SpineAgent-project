# Post Dengue Fever Epidural Abscess With or Without Spondylodiscitis  
Does the Dengue Have a Predilection for the Neural Tissue?

## Study Design
A retrospective case series.

## Summary of Background Data
This is the largest case series of epidural abscess with or without spondylodiscitis within 6 weeks following dengue virus infection.

## Methods
We performed a retrospective review of all the eight cases treated for epidural abscess with or without spondylodiscitis within 6 weeks following dengue virus infection in our center during the period of 3 months from June 2017 to August 2017.

## Results
Of the eight cases, three of them were infected with Methicillin Resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and all of them grew the organism in the blood culture. Three of them had Methicillin Sensitive Staphylococcus aureus (MSSA), one with MSSA septicemia, and one had associated Elizabethkingia meningoseptica infection. One patient had positive Acid Fast Bacilli (AFB) staining in smears. Two patients were infected with Pseudomonas.

Some patients had multiple site abscess and epidural abscess without spondylodiscitis. Five of them had neurological deficit, which recovered with abscess drainage and antibiotic treatment. The erythrocyte sedimentation rate, C-reactive protein, and serum ferritin were elevated in these patients.

## Conclusion
This is a large case series of epidural abscess with or without spondylodiscitis within 6 weeks following dengue virus infection. The predilection of dengue virus for the neural tissue should be further investigated. Post-dengue immunosuppression also needs further study.

## Introduction
Dengue fever is caused by dengue virus of Flaviviridae. It is a pandemic disease spread by Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. One of the dengue-related study points is that there occurs about 390 million dengue infections per year out of which 96 million manifest clinically. Dengue fever has a wide range of manifestations both typical and atypical. Some authors state that these unusual presentations of dengue may be due to concurrent infections caused by fungi, bacteria, or protozoa.

Recently, clinical reports have been emerging on the possible neurological complications associated with dengue. The unusual presentations of dengue fever include rhabdomyolysis, acute renal failure, encephalopathy, and Guillain-Barré syndrome. Associated bacterial infection may be considered if the fever goes beyond 7 days along with elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate and C-reactive protein.

Here, we are reporting spondylodiscitis with or without epidural abscess in patients with a recent history (within 6 weeks) of dengue fever. From June 2017 to August 2017, the total number of dengue cases reported or diagnosed in our hospital is 1407. Eight of them developed dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF). Nine cases presented in our department with recurrence of fever and were diagnosed as spondylodiscitis with or without epidural abscess. These patients presented with variable severity of fever and back pain.

## Immunosuppression Due to Dengue Virus
The neuropathogenesis in dengue infection is poorly understood. It is caused directly by the virus or postinfectious immune-mediated injury. A study suggests that the postinfectious onset of neurological symptoms and flaccid paraplegia can be a direct invasion of neural tissue by the virus, whereas late-onset neurological symptoms and spastic paraplegia are attributed to immunologically mediated neurological injury.

In a study, dengue virus is shown to reduce cell-mediated immunity in mice. The major factors are reduced lymphocyte proliferation and inhibition of leukocyte migration. In an in vitro study, splenic T lymphocytes from dengue virus–infected mice produce cytotoxic factor (CF), which kills a variety of cells mainly macrophages, helper T cells, and T effector lymphocytes, but not bacterial epithelial or fibroblastic tissue culture cells. Survived macrophages will produce another cytotoxic substance (CF2), which amplifies the cytotoxic effect. Induction of an antigen-specific suppression pathway in mice when infected with dengue virus is also demonstrated in vivo. When mice are infected, the virus induces activation of Ts1 in mouse spleen to produce a soluble factor that is transmitted through macrophages to recruit a second subpopulation, Ts2. Ts2 produces a prostaglandin-like substance SF2, which recruits a third subpopulation of Ts. This mediates antigen-specific suppression of IgM antibody-forming cells. Human leukocytes when treated in vitro with CF and CF2 show diminished phagocytic activity. The same effect is also observed when monkeys are inoculated with CF.

Thrombocytopenia and leucopenia are reported in dengue. Platelet count decreases from the 3rd day up to the 7th day. The actual mechanism of virus-induced bone marrow suppression during the acute phase remains unclear.

In the early phase of disease, bone marrow shows hypocellularity and arrest of megakaryocytic maturation. Factors responsible for this include: (1) direct lesion of progenitor cells by dengue virus; (2) infected stromal cells; and (3) changes in bone marrow regulation. Histopathological studies of bone marrow from patients with severe dengue virus infection suggest that marrow suppression is characterized by generalized hypoplasia by the 4th day and returns to normal by 7 to 10 days.

## Spine
No recorded data are available as of now on the incidence of epidural abscess in patients who have dengue fever.

## Case Report

### Case 1
A 63-year-old gentleman, a known case of diabetes mellitus, was diagnosed to have dengue infection with a positive NS1 antigen test and was symptomatically treated elsewhere (no blood or blood product transfusion in any of these patients). Six weeks later, he was admitted to our hospital with high-grade fever, swelling over the left sternoclavicular joint, swelling of the right knee, and low backache radiating to both lower limbs. There was an abscess of $2\times1$ cm over the left sternoclavicular joint and effusion in the right knee joint. Blood culture, knee aspirate, and pus from the sternoclavicular joint yielded MRSA sensitive to meropenem and teicoplanin.

MRI of the lumbosacral spine showed an epidural abscess at L4–5 extending up to S3. Abscess was seen in the paraspinal muscle from L4 to S2. He underwent hemilaminectomy at L4–5 and L5–S1 on the right side; epidural and paraspinal abscess was drained. MRSA was isolated in culture. In this case, there was no spondylodiscitis, and only epidural and paraspinal abscess were present.

He was treated with meropenem 1 g intravenous (IV) three times daily for 2 weeks and teicoplanin 600 mg IV for 6 weeks. At the time of discharge, he was put on oral rifampicin after discussing with the clinical microbiologist. He was reviewed after 2 weeks of first admission; the blood culture showed MRSA again, so the dose of teicoplanin was increased to 800 mg IV. At 6 weeks, repeat MRI lumbosacral spine showed no collection. The patient was asymptomatic and back to normal life at 6 weeks.

### Case 2
A 62-year-old lady was diagnosed with dengue fever (NS1 antigen positive) and was treated elsewhere symptomatically. Six weeks later, she had gradual onset neck pain and numbness of the right upper limb. She was afebrile. There was tenderness over the cervicothoracic junction. She had grip weakness on the right side (grade 3, MRC grading) and paresthesia over C7 and C8 dermatomes. Blood culture showed no growth. MRI cervical spine showed C7–T1 discitis with epidural and paravertebral abscess extending from C7 to T1.

She underwent abscess drainage, C7–T1 discectomy, and interbody fusion with iliac crest bone graft without instrumentation through an anterior approach. Culture yielded MSSA. AFB staining for the pus was positive. Biopsy specimen showed fibrocartilaginous tissue with focal infiltration of neutrophils and lymphocytes. AFB culture was sent, which was reported negative at 6 weeks of incubation.

She was treated with clindamycin 600 mg twice daily according to sensitivity reports. She was put on anti-tuberculous medicines as well (four-drug regime: INH, rifampicin, ethambutol, and pyrazinamide). At the time of discharge, she improved neurologically and her blood parameters also improved. Clindamycin injection was given for 6 weeks and anti-tuberculosis medicine is being continued for 1 year. At 6 weeks, she had completely recovered. Follow-up MRI showed no evidence of abscess.

### Case 3
A 75-year-old man was diagnosed to have dengue fever (positive NS1 antigen) and was treated symptomatically elsewhere. After 1 week of a normal period, he developed low backache and low-grade fever. Blood culture yielded no growth. MRI lumbosacral spine showed spondylodiscitis at L3–4 level with prevertebral, paravertebral, and epidural abscess extending from L3 to L4 level.

He underwent left hemilaminectomy, abscess drainage, and discectomy at L3–4 level. The specimen grew Pseudomonas species in culture. Histopathological examination showed fibrocollagenous tissue with dense infiltration of neutrophils and lymphocytes. He was treated with intravenous piperacillin-tazobactam combination 4.5 g three times daily for 6 weeks. Levofloxacin tablet was also added for 3 weeks in consultation with the microbiologist. At 6 weeks of follow-up, he was completely well radiologically and clinically.

### Case 4
A 71-year-old male was diagnosed to have dengue fever (positive NS1 antigen test) and was treated symptomatically elsewhere. Four weeks later, he developed acute onset neck pain and tingling sensation over both upper limbs. He had tenderness over the lower neck. He had weakness of hand grip bilaterally (grade 3) and sensory blunting over C5, C6, and C7 dermatomes of both upper limbs.

MRI showed C5–6 discitis with epidural abscess causing compression of the spinal cord at C5–6 and C6–7 levels. Prevertebral and paravertebral collection extended from C3 to T1. Blood culture grew MRSA. He was treated surgically with abscess drainage, discectomy at C5–6, and fusion with iliac crest graft. Culture grew MRSA. Histopathology showed sheets of neutrophils admixed with macrophages and lymphocytes with evidence of acute suppurative inflammation and degenerative changes.

The patient was neurologically normal before surgery, but 24 hours after surgery developed acute weakness of both upper and lower limbs (quadriplegia, Frankel grade B). MRI cervical spine was repeated and showed altered signal changes in the spinal cord from C3 to D2 level along with epidural collection from C4 to D1 level. Diffusion-weighted MRI showed cord ischemia probably due to anterior spinal artery thrombosis from septic emboli. It may be due to demyelination secondary to dengue viral infection. Neuromedicine consultation was sought and anti-dengue immunoglobulin was given.

The patient was taken back to theater again the next day. C6–7 discectomy, drainage of abscess, and anterior interbody fusion with iliac crest graft was done without instrumentation. Microbiological studies showed MRSA sensitive to teicoplanin. He was treated with teicoplanin 600 mg IV once daily along with rifampicin 450 mg once daily. Physiotherapy was continued. He remained in the hospital for 1 month and gradually improved. Teicoplanin was continued for 6 weeks and rifampicin for 4 weeks. At 3 months, he had recovered neurologically so that he could walk independently, but not completely. His blood parameters became normal.

### Case 5
A 65-year-old male was diagnosed with dengue fever (positive NS1 antigen test) and was treated symptomatically elsewhere. Two weeks later, he had acute onset tachypnea and desaturation. He was diagnosed to have secondary lung infection and right pleural effusion, intubated, and shifted to ICU. Blood cultures yielded MRSA and he was diagnosed to have MRSA septicemia.

He was treated with meropenem 1 g IV three times daily and teicoplanin 600 mg IV in consultation with the microbiologist. He developed acute kidney injury, compensated metabolic acidosis, and was hemodynamically unstable with uremic encephalopathy; he was started on hemodialysis. After starting teicoplanin, he improved but had poor neurological recovery.

MRI of the spine showed spondylodiscitis at L2–3 with prevertebral, paravertebral, epidural, and bilateral psoas abscess. A peripherally enhancing epidural lesion was noted from D12 to L2 level. He underwent hemilaminectomy at L2–3, epidural abscess drainage, and discectomy at L2–3. Culture of the specimen yielded MRSA. Histopathology was suggestive of acute spondylodiscitis. Repeat blood cultures also grew MRSA.

With antibiotics, the patient gradually improved and his GCS became normal. Repeated blood cultures were negative for MRSA. He had cellulitis of the right hand and forearm and Fournier gangrene, which was debrided. He was given linezolid 600 mg IV twice daily. He was in ICU for 26 days, then continued physiotherapy. At discharge, he was able to walk with support and blood cultures were negative. Teicoplanin 600 mg IV once daily and rifampicin 600 mg once daily was continued for 8 weeks; due to occasional fever spikes, antibiotics were continued for a total of 10 weeks. On follow-up, he was comfortable and walking normally.

### Case 6
A 49-year-old lady was diagnosed with dengue fever (positive NS1 antigen test) and was treated symptomatically elsewhere. Two weeks later, she had acute low backache with radiation to the lower limbs and fever. MRI showed spondylodiscitis at L5–S1 with epidural and paravertebral components.

She was treated surgically by left flavotomy at L5–S1 and drainage of abscess. Pus culture yielded MSSA and blood culture was negative. Histopathology showed acute suppurative inflammation. She was treated with piperacillin-tazobactam intravenously according to culture reports. Two days postoperatively, she developed fever and breathing difficulty. CT thorax showed empyema, which was aspirated under CT guidance. Culture yielded MSSA and blood culture also showed MSSA.

She was treated with cefoperazone-sulbactam, linezolid, and rifampicin according to sensitivity reports and in consultation with the microbiologist. At 6 weeks, she recovered completely clinically, radiologically, and hematologically.

### Case 7
A 54-year-old lady was diagnosed with dengue fever (positive NS1 antigen test) and was treated symptomatically elsewhere. One week later, she had acute low backache with radiation to the lower limbs and fever. She had extensor hallucis longus weakness on the left side. MRI showed L4–5 discitis. Blood culture was negative.

She was treated surgically by flavotomy and L4–5 discectomy. Culture of the specimen yielded Pseudomonas species. Histopathology showed acute discitis. Cefoperazone-sulbactam injection and ciprofloxacin were given according to culture reports for 4 weeks. At 4 weeks, she was completely asymptomatic. She was given oral ciprofloxacin and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole combination for another 3 weeks.

### Case 8
A 51-year-old male was diagnosed to have dengue fever (positive NS1 antigen test) and was treated symptomatically elsewhere. One month later, he presented with low backache and low-grade fever. MRI showed spondylodiscitis at L3–L4 with prevertebral, paravertebral, and epidural soft tissue components. Knee extension was grade 4 (right), and ankle dorsiflexion and toe extension were grade 4 (right).

He underwent L3 hemilaminotomy drainage of abscess and L3–L4 discectomy. Culture yielded Elizabethkingia meningoseptica and Staphylococcus aureus. AFB stain was negative. He was treated according to culture sensitivity reports with cefoperazone-sulbactam and ciprofloxacin. Histopathology showed acute discitis. He improved clinically and became normal at 6 weeks of therapy.

## Discussion
Recent upsurge of dengue fever is a major health concern. Complications and death associated with dengue fever are also increasing. Here, we report an unusual incidence of epidural abscess with or without spondylodiscitis in post-dengue patients within 6 weeks of the primary illness. During the period of 3 months from June 2017 to August 2017, we had eight patients who developed epidural abscess with or without spondylodiscitis, septicemia, and other foci of infection.

Out of this, seven patients were treated elsewhere for dengue fever symptomatically. One patient presented to our hospital with dengue fever. They presented with variable symptoms such as stiff back, low-grade fever, neurological deficits, and septicemia. All patients were evaluated hematologically, microbiologically, and radiologically. All patients underwent MRI to confirm epidural abscess and spondylodiscitis.

Out of eight patients, only one patient presented with septic shock and multiple abscesses. Among patients with other site involvement, one had empyema with pleural effusion and one had abscess in the left sternoclavicular joint and septic arthritis of the left knee. Preoperative neurological deficit was seen in five patients, out of whom two had positive blood culture and one had septic shock. Out of four positive blood cultures, three were MRSA and one was MSSA.

Most patients underwent hemilaminectomy or flavotomy, decompression, and abscess drainage. Cervical spine was addressed anteriorly with discectomy, abscess drainage, and iliac crest grafting. Culture and sensitivity of pus yielded MRSA in three patients. Pseudomonas was observed in two patients, MSSA in one patient, MSSA with AFB in one patient, and MSSA with Elizabethkingia meningoseptica in one patient. Biopsy showed suppurative discitis and infected tissues in all patients.

All patients except one improved immediately after surgery. Monitoring was done clinically and hematologically. One patient developed quadriplegia 24 hours post-surgery in spite of initial improvement. He had epidural abscess and discitis at C6–C7 along with cord signal changes at C3 to D2 level and increased collection with adjacent vertebral involvement. Imaging and neurological consultation suggested anterior spinal artery occlusion or postinfective (dengue) demyelination. Re-exploration with epidural and prevertebral abscess drainage and anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) of the adjacent level (C6–C7) was done. His neurological deficit improved progressively. On follow-up at 3 months, he was walking independently, though slowly; he had grade 4 power in the right hand grip and near normal power on the left.

All patients had very good neurological outcome. There was complete neurological recovery in all except one who was quadriplegic (Frankel grade B). He recovered to near normal stage at six months after treatment. All patients had negative blood cultures after the treatment. One patient was put on anti-tubercular medicines.

This case report includes eight cases of epidural abscess with or without spondylodiscitis associated with other focal abscesses. All patients were diagnosed after 1 to 6 weeks of an episode of dengue fever. No blood or blood products were given to any of the patients.

There is no reason reported why the dengue virus has predilection for the neural tissue. A possibility of emergence of a neurotropic serotype needs to be further investigated.

## Conclusion
This article reports high incidence of development of epidural abscess with or without spondylodiscitis in dengue-infected patients after a span of 1 to 6 weeks of asymptomatic period. Studies in mice show suppression of immunity through various mechanisms. Infection with dengue virus will inhibit leukocyte migration and lymphocyte proliferation. Splenic T lymphocytes of infected mice produce a CF that kills immune cells but is not effective against bacterial cells. Splenic T lymphocytes also mediate antigen-specific suppression of IgM antibody-forming cells.

Early diagnosis and treatment can prevent development of neurological deficits, septicemia, and death. Neurotropic nature of the dengue virus and its effect on immunity need further studies. Similar series of cases are not reported in the literature.

The predilection of dengue virus to the neural tissue and the disc space need further study. It may be attributable to the change in serotypes of the virus, which is evolving. The presence of multiple foci of infection in these cases points to the effect of the virus on immunity, which needs further studies. The rapid and excellent neurological recovery may be attributable to early diagnosis and immediate intervention. Whether it is related to some peculiarities of the dengue virus needs further studies.

From our study, there is evidence of manifestations of immunosuppression in other tissues or organs. One patient had septic shock, two patients had empyema with pleural effusion, one had left sternoclavicular joint abscess and septic arthritis of the knee, and one patient had concomitant tubercular infection.

## Key Points
This is a large case series of epidural abscess with or without spondylodiscitis within 6 weeks following dengue virus infection.

- The predilection of dengue virus for the neural tissue should be further investigated.  
- Post-dengue immunosuppression also needs further study.
