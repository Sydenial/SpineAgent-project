# Complete fracture of the lamina of the sixth cervical vertebra with hemiplegia: a case report

## Abstract
Introduction: We report a rare case of a 27-year-old man who presented with right-sided complete hemiplegia after a neck trauma due to a road traffic accident.

Materials and methods: Computed tomography revealed a complete fracture of the C6 lamina including a partial fracture of the right articular process with complete rotation of the fragment into the spinal canal, causing major compression of the right side of the cord. The patient underwent urgent posterior removal of the C6 arch, followed by a C6-7 anterior fusion because T2-weighted MRI showed a completely torn C6-7 disc with a hematoma under the posterior longitudinal ligament and an increased cord signal. The patient walked normally 6 weeks after the accident. At 2-year follow-up, he recovered full sensation on his right side but had residual intrinsic muscle weakness in his right hand.

Conclusion: This is the first report describing a complete laminar fracture at the cervical spine level with hemiplegia. Early surgical intervention likely provides better neurological outcomes.

Keywords: cervical trauma, complete laminar fracture, hemiplegia

## Case Report
A 27-year-old man sustained a neck trauma in a head-on motor vehicle collision around midnight while driving his pickup truck without a seat belt; there was no alcohol involvement. Prehospital assessment found normal consciousness, severe neck pain, and complete right-sided hemiplegia. No other injuries were noted. He was intubated and transported to our hospital.

A whole-body CT scan ruled out limb fractures and head injury. Cervical CT showed a complete fracture of the C6 lamina with a partial fracture of the right articular process and complete sliding and rotation of the fragment into the spinal canal, contacting the posterior wall of the C6 vertebral body and causing major compression of the right side of the cord (Fig. 1). No other cervical bony lesions were identified.

Emergency surgery was performed approximately 3 h after the accident. With the patient prone, a posterior approach was used. The posterior arch of C6 was completely mobile, with its right side twisted into the canal. The fragment was removed in an en bloc fashion, revealing an intact dura without cerebrospinal fluid leakage. A small medial fracture of the right superior articular process of C7 was noted, but no fixation was performed at that time.

Postoperatively, the patient was extubated. Examination showed paresthesia in the right forearm and right leg, with altered temperature sensation in the left leg, consistent with an initial Brown-Sequard syndrome at the time of injury. Motor examination showed improvement with residual weakness of the right hand intrinsic muscles and finger flexors (3/5), and of the right quadriceps and ankle extensors (3/5). MRI revealed a completely torn C6-7 disc with annular rupture and a hematoma under the posterior longitudinal ligament, with increased T2 cord signal (Fig. 2). The C5-6 disc appeared intact.

A second surgery to stabilize C6-7 was performed 3 days after the accident via an anterior prevascular approach. The C6-7 disc was completely torn and highly unstable. The C5-6 disc was intact and confirmed by discography with no contrast leakage. C6-7 was fused using a cage filled with autologous iliac crest bone graft and a plate (Fig. 3). A rigid collar was prescribed for 2 months.

Two weeks after the accident the patient began rehabilitation. He could stand and walk normally at 6 weeks, with minor residual weakness of the right hand intrinsic muscles, no bladder or bowel disturbance, and full sensory recovery. At 2-year follow-up, gait was normal and right upper extremity motor function was almost normal, with persistent mild intrinsic hand weakness. Early surgical intervention likely contributed to the favorable neurological outcome.

## Discussion
This case describes hemiplegia caused by a cervical trauma with a complete laminar fracture of C6 and intracanalar rotation of the fragment compressing the right side of the cord, resulting in complete right-sided motor deficit.

Isolated laminar fractures are rare [1-7]. Similar injuries have been reported three times, but in those cases the laminar fracture was incomplete, representing invagination due to a greenstick mechanism. Makan [6] reported an incomplete C5 laminar fracture with canal encroachment causing right hemiparesis; laminectomy without fusion led to recovery with residual hand intrinsic weakness. De Barros Filho [4] reported quadriplegia after an incomplete C6 laminar fracture; laminectomy with posterior fusion yielded partial recovery. Hahnle [5] described traumatic invagination of C4 and C5 laminae with acute hemiparesis; open reduction and wire fixation resulted in full recovery at 6 weeks. Table 1 summarizes these reports.

Compared to prior reports, the originality of this case lies in the complete fracture across both sides of the lamina with maximal rotation of the fragment inside the canal, contacting the posterior vertebral wall and compressing the cord entirely on the right side.

The patient, fully conscious at the time of trauma, recalled hyper-rotation of his head to the left while contracting his muscles, suggesting a rotational mechanism. This explains the rotated laminar fragment, torn C6-7 disc, and a small unilateral articular process fracture on the right; uniarticular fracture is a classical consequence of rotational injury [8]. The laminar fracture itself likely resulted from hyperextension with axial compression [1], with rotation producing this rare pattern.

Prognosis in such spinal cord injuries is strongly related to the timing of decompression. Urgent decompression within 24 h is recommended for bilateral cervical dislocations with neurological deterioration and is associated with shorter intensive care unit stay and fewer complications [9,10]. Early intervention also limits secondary cord damage from ongoing compression [11], and in animal models, the degree of reperfusion hyperemia after decompression is inversely proportional to the duration of compression and correlates with electrophysiologic recovery [12]. In this case, urgent decompression approximately 3 h post-injury likely contributed to the prompt neurological recovery and favorable overall outcome.

## Conclusion
This is the first reported case of a complete cervical laminar fracture causing hemiplegia. A combined mechanism of hyperextension with axial compression and superimposed rotation produced a complete C6 laminar fracture with right-sided canal intrusion and cord compression. Early surgical decompression and stabilization were associated with rapid and durable neurological recovery, with only mild residual intrinsic hand weakness.
