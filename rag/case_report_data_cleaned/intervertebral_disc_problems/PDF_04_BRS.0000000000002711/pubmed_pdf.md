# “Acquired” Type Castellvi-IIa Lumbarization Transformed From Castellvi-IIa Following Discectomy and Fusion at the Lumbosacral Level: A Case Report

## Abstract

**Study Design.** A retrospective case report.  

**Objective.** To report a case that transformed from type Castellvi-IIa sacralization to Castellvi-IIIa following decompression and fusion surgery at a transitional disc (TD).  

**Summary of Background Data.** Traditionally, lumbosacral transitional vertebra (LSTV) has been regarded as a congenital anomaly. No literature has previously reported transformation from one type of LSTV to another after birth.  

**Methods.** A 60-year-old man presented with low back pain and left sciatic pain and was diagnosed with lumbar disc herniation at the L4–5 and L5S1 levels. Lumbar digital radiography revealed an anomalous articulation between the sacrum and an enlarged right L5 transverse process. Dynamic lateral radiographs showed mobility at the L5S1 disc, which was the transitional disc. The patient underwent posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF) at L4–5 and L5S1. Postoperative CT examinations were performed to evaluate the progression of fusion.  

**Results.** After surgery, the patient’s symptoms were significantly relieved. As solid fusion developed at the intended interbody levels over time, bony bridging was observed gradually across the anomalous articulation region. One year after surgery, complete osseous fusion between the right L5 transverse process and the sacrum was identified.  

**Conclusion.** Following PLIF surgery at the transitional disc segment in a Castellvi-IIa sacralization case, an “acquired” transformation to Castellvi-IIIa may develop.  

**Key Words:** lumbosacral transitional vertebra; Castellvi classification; acquired type transformation; transitional disc herniation; fusion  

**Level of Evidence:** 5  

## Introduction

Lumbosacral transitional vertebra (LSTV) is defined as either lumbarization of the highest sacral segment or sacralization of the lowest lumbar vertebra. It is generally regarded as a congenital anomaly and is relatively common in the population [1–4]. To our knowledge, no case has been reported in which an LSTV transformed from one Castellvi type to another after birth. Here, we present a case that transformed from Castellvi-IIa sacralization to Castellvi-IIIa following posterior lumbar interbody fusion surgery.

## Case Report

A 60-year-old man presented to our department with low back pain and left sciatic pain on September 26, 2016. He was diagnosed with lumbar disc herniation at the L4–5 and L5S1 levels. Lumbar digital radiography revealed an anomalous articulation between the sacrum and an enlarged right L5 transverse process, consistent with typical Castellvi-IIa sacralization. Dynamic lateral radiographs demonstrated mobility at the L5S1 disc, which was identified as the transitional disc.

Lumbar CT scanning confirmed central disc herniation at the L4–5 level and left lateral herniation at the transitional disc level (L5S1). An anomalous articulation between the sacrum and a hypertrophic right L5 transverse process was also detected.

The patient underwent posterior lumbar interbody fusion at the L4–5 and L5S1 levels on September 28, 2016. During the operation, a midline posterior approach was used. Pedicle screws were placed in a standard fashion. Bilateral laminotomy and medial facetectomy from L4 to S1 were performed for decompression. After adequate decompression, bone obtained from the laminectomy was packed into cages and placed in the interbody spaces at L4–5 and L5S1. Pedicle screws were connected with rods and compressed to restore lumbar lordosis. The facet joints were decorticated and grafted with autologous bone for posterolateral fusion. The transverse processes were not exposed, and no intertransverse fusion was intended during the operation.

Postoperatively, the patient’s symptoms were markedly relieved. Follow-up examinations were scheduled at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after surgery. CT scans demonstrated solid fusion at the operated segments by 6 months. During follow-up, progressive bony bridging was observed across the anomalous articulation region. One year after surgery, complete osseous fusion between the right L5 transverse process and the sacrum had developed. According to the Castellvi classification, the patient had transformed from Castellvi-IIa to an “acquired” Castellvi-IIIa sacralization without new clinical complaints.

## Discussion

LSTV is generally regarded as a congenital anomaly of the lumbosacral spine [1–3,6]. In 1984, Castellvi classified LSTV into four types based on anteroposterior radiographs. Type I includes dysplastic transverse processes (unilateral or bilateral) measuring at least 19 mm in width. Type II involves an anomalous articulation between the transverse process and the sacrum (unilateral or bilateral). Type III is characterized by complete osseous fusion of the transverse process to the sacrum (unilateral or bilateral). Type IV represents a mixed type, with type II on one side and type III on the contralateral side. To our knowledge, no literature has reported postnatal transformation between these types.

The primary objective of the fusion operation in this case was to achieve solid fusion at the intervertebral and posterolateral regions of the last two mobile lumbar segments following decompression. Postoperative CT examinations were intended to evaluate fusion at the surgical levels. Unexpectedly, the pseudoarthrosis between the right L5 transverse process and the sacrum gradually disappeared, and solid fusion was achieved at this anomalous articulation one year after surgery. Consequently, the LSTV transformed from Castellvi-IIa to Castellvi-IIIa.

We hypothesize that immobilization of the transitional disc segment following PLIF played an important role in enabling osseous callus formation across the anomalous articulation, ultimately resulting in complete fusion. However, immobilization alone may not be sufficient to explain this phenomenon. If immobilization were the sole factor, similar transformations might be expected more frequently, particularly in Castellvi-IV cases. To date, however, such transformations have not been reported.

Recent studies have described spontaneous spinal arthrodesis in patients with lower lumbar spondylolisthesis. Whether spontaneous transformation from Castellvi-II to Castellvi-III LSTV can occur without surgical fusion of the transitional disc requires further observation. This case suggests that multiple factors may contribute to an acquired transformation of LSTV type and indicates that such transformation can occur after PLIF surgery at the transitional disc segment in Castellvi-IIa sacralization.
