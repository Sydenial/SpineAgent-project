## Abstract

A 56-year-old man presented to the neurosurgery clinic with back pain. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed an expansive mass in the T5 and T6 vertebrae that expanded vertebral bodies and destructed the T6 corpus. The mass was hypointense on T2-weighted (T2W) and T1-weighted (T1W) images, and it showed heterogeneous contrast enhancement. A pathological fracture was also noted in the T6 vertebra corpus. The mass was initially diagnosed as a metastasis by radiology, and subsequent endoscopic biopsy confirmed the presence of gastric carcinoma after a systemic investigation.

## Introduction

Gastric carcinoma often presents with non-specific symptoms, which may lead to delayed diagnosis. This case highlights an unusual presentation of gastric carcinoma manifesting as back pain, a symptom more typically associated with spinal pathologies. The initial radiological findings and clinical presentation made it challenging to distinguish the gastric origin of the tumor.

## Case Report

### Patient Presentation

The patient was a 56-year-old male with no significant past medical history who presented with persistent back pain. The pain was progressively worsening over the past few weeks. The patient did not report any other symptoms such as weight loss, dysphagia, or gastrointestinal distress that are commonly associated with gastric carcinoma.

### Imaging Findings

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the thoracic spine showed a mass in the T5 and T6 vertebrae. The mass was expansive and had caused significant destruction of the T6 vertebra. The lesion appeared hypointense on T1W and T2W images, with heterogeneous enhancement following contrast administration. A pathological fracture was noted at the T6 vertebra.

### Diagnosis

The lesion was initially suspected to be a metastatic tumor, given the pattern of destruction and imaging characteristics. However, further investigation, including endoscopic biopsy, confirmed the diagnosis of gastric carcinoma with metastasis to the spine.

## Discussion

### Gastric Carcinoma Metastasis

Gastric carcinoma metastasizing to the spine is relatively uncommon. The vertebrae are not typical sites for gastric cancer metastasis, which more commonly spreads to the liver, lungs, and lymph nodes. This case demonstrates that gastric carcinoma can present with unusual symptoms and affect atypical organs, such as the spine, particularly in cases of advanced disease.

### Imaging in Gastric Cancer

Imaging plays a crucial role in the diagnosis and management of gastric carcinoma and its metastases. MRI is particularly useful in assessing spinal involvement, as it provides detailed information about soft tissue masses and bone destruction. In this case, MRI findings guided the clinical team toward the correct diagnosis.

## Conclusion

This case underscores the importance of considering a broad differential diagnosis in patients with unusual presentations of common symptoms, such as back pain. Early detection and accurate diagnosis of gastric carcinoma, even when presenting with atypical symptoms, are critical for improving patient outcomes. Multidisciplinary collaboration, including radiological and endoscopic evaluation, is key to reaching the correct diagnosis.
