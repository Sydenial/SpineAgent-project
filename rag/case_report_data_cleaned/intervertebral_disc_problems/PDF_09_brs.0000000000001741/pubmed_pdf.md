# Surgical Management of the Pregnant Patient With Lumbar Disc Herniation in the Latter Stage of the Second Trimester

## Abstract

**Study Design.** Case report.  
**Objective.** To report on a pregnant woman successfully treated with microendoscopic discectomy in the left lateral position under general anesthesia at 24-week gestation.  
**Summary of Background Data.** Treatment for lumbar disc herniation in pregnant women poses a particular challenge due to the complexity of the clinical situation. Review of the literature emphasizes timely diagnosis with adequate management specific for each gestational period. A surgical approach mandates consideration of the physiologic parameters of pregnancy and the effects of these stressors on the fetus.  
**Methods.** A 38-year-old primigravid woman presented with persistent and incapacitating low back and left leg pain. Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a herniated disc at L4–5 with a severely compressed left L5 nerve root. Symptoms were resistant to conservative treatment (acetaminophen; 1200 mg/day), and nerve root block with corticosteroids (1 mg/0.5 mL of betamethasone plus 0.5 mL of 1% lidocaine) provided only transient pain relief. Operative management with surgical discectomy was discussed. Anesthesiologists, obstetricians, and neonatologists were consulted for preoperative planning, focusing on appropriate anesthesia, ideal positioning for surgical access, and provision for emergent fetal care. Surgery was ultimately performed in the left lateral position.  
**Results.** The patient experienced complete relief from pain after surgical intervention and delivered a healthy baby at 39-week gestation after normal labor.  
**Conclusion.** Although previously published cases noted the safety of operating in the prone position under epidural anesthesia, minimally invasive microendoscopic discectomy in the left lateral position under general anesthesia appears to be a safe and preferable alternative for pregnant patients in the latter stage of the second trimester.

## Introduction

Although symptomatic lumbar disc herniation (LDH) during pregnancy is rare [1], some patients experience incapacitating pain resistant to conservative management that requires surgical treatment [2]. Surgeons must carefully consider the risks and benefits of surgery, the effects of anesthesia and patient positioning on the mother and fetus, and provisions for urgent intervention in the event of preterm labor or fetal distress during surgical discectomy [3,4].  

We present the case of a pregnant woman who underwent microendoscopic discectomy (MED) in the left lateral position under general anesthesia at 24-week gestation. This case illustrates a safe approach to surgical management with respect to timing of surgery, anesthetic methods, and surgical positioning in the latter stage of the second trimester.

## Case Report

A 38-year-old primigravid woman presented with excruciating low back and left leg pain. Physical examination revealed muscle weakness in ankle dorsiflexion (4/5) and dysesthesia along the left L5 nerve root dermatome. Despite outpatient medical management with acetaminophen (1200 mg/day), her symptoms became progressively incapacitating, and she was hospitalized at 20-week gestation.

Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated LDH at L4–5 with a severely compressed left L5 nerve root. Nerve root block with corticosteroids (1 mg/0.5 mL of betamethasone plus 0.5 mL of 1% lidocaine) provided only transient pain relief. Surgical management was discussed with anesthesiologists, obstetricians, and neonatologists.

Preoperative planning was performed with the patient to optimize positioning, enabling intraoperative fetal monitoring and rapid conversion to alternative positioning in the event of fetal distress. Lateral positioning was deemed preferable to prone positioning. MED was therefore performed via an intralaminar approach in the left lateral position under general anesthesia at 24-week gestation. Fetal monitoring throughout surgery was uneventful.

The patient reported complete resolution of pain immediately after surgery (visual analog scale score: back pain 3 to 0, leg pain 10 to 0, preoperatively to 1 month postoperatively). She delivered a healthy boy at 39-week gestation after normal labor. At 2-year follow-up, she remained symptom free with normal motor and sensory function. The infant was developing appropriately.

## Discussion

Surgical treatment for pregnant patients with LDH is particularly challenging. Previously published cases have highlighted that surgical indications for lumbar discectomy during pregnancy are not different from those in nonpregnant patients [6,7]. However, many pregnant patients are reluctant to accept surgical treatment due to anxiety regarding potential fetal risk. In this case, consultation with multiple subspecialists and preoperative simulation contributed to patient reassurance and successful management.

Optimal surgical positioning should be selected according to gestational age. Although prone positioning has been reported as safe in some cases [3,7], its use in the latter stage of the second trimester is controversial. Prior studies have noted that prone positioning is not recommended beyond 12-week gestation because of the risk of aortocaval compression, whereas left lateral positioning is indicated during the second and third trimesters [6]. Left lateral positioning also allows easier abdominal access after sterile draping and rapid positional conversion in the event of fetal distress necessitating urgent delivery [9].

With respect to gestational age, neonatal survival rates by cesarean delivery in Japan are approximately 30% and 60% at 22 and 23 weeks, respectively, increasing to greater than 80% beyond 24 weeks [10,11]. When possible, surgical treatment should therefore be delayed beyond 24-week gestation unless severe neurological deficits are present.

Regarding anesthetic choice, epidural anesthesia has not been associated with adverse fetal effects [7], but it may cause sudden hypotension secondary to vasodilation, potentially leading to fetal distress and preterm labor [12]. General anesthesia offers rapid and reliable onset and may suppress preterm labor when volatile agents are used. To date, no anesthetic has been proven teratogenic in humans [3].

In view of these considerations, general anesthesia combined with minimally invasive MED was selected. MED provides sufficient surgical access to left- or right-sided herniated discs in the left lateral position. Percutaneous endoscopic discectomy under local anesthesia is an alternative, particularly for right-shifted herniated discs, but approach angulation may limit its applicability. Minimally invasive techniques are associated with high patient satisfaction, reduced blood loss, and shorter hospital stays compared with open discectomy [13,14].

## Conclusion

We report a case of a pregnant patient who underwent microendoscopic discectomy under general anesthesia at 24-week gestation in the left lateral position with complete symptom resolution. Surgical discectomy should be delayed beyond 24-week gestation when feasible in the absence of severe neurological deficits. Preoperative simulation, left lateral positioning, and general anesthesia represent a safe and effective alternative to continued medical management in selected pregnant patients.

## Key Points

- Appropriate management of lumbar disc herniation during pregnancy requires understanding gestational physiologic changes and their effects on the fetus.  
- Surgical management demands close collaboration among spine surgeons, obstetricians, anesthesiologists, and neonatologists.  
- Preoperative simulation with the patient is valuable in selecting optimal surgical and anesthetic strategies.  
- This case represents the first reported use of microendoscopic discectomy in the left lateral position under general anesthesia for lumbar disc herniation during pregnancy.  
- Endoscopic discectomy is a safe and effective option for managing lumbar disc herniation in pregnant patients.
