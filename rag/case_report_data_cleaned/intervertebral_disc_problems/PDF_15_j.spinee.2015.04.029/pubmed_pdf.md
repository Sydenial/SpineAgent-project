# Posterior Lumbar Subcutaneous Edema

## Case Report

A 64-year-old female patient weighing 75 kg complained of low back pain and right lower limb pain. She reported difficulty in walking, with a maximum walking distance not exceeding 300 m.

Physical examination revealed a positive Lasegue sign in the right lower limb, while Bragard and Babinski signs were negative.

Magnetic resonance imaging indicated lumbar spinal stenosis accompanied by subcutaneous edema in the low back. During surgery, the subcutaneous tissue of the low back was found to be brittle in texture and edematous. After skin incision, approximately 5–10 mL of water-like fluid was discharged, and the erector spinae muscle was observed to be infiltrated by fatty substance.

Although subcutaneous edema occurs in approximately 27.2% of patients with low back pain, it is often neglected by surgeons [1]. Possible causes include infection, inflammatory reactions, trauma, hydrostasis, and neoplastic changes. This finding has been shown to be significantly associated with increasing age and body weight among women.

This phenomenon may be related to pathophysiological changes occurring during low back pain and the potential disorganization of connective tissue layers [2]. However, subcutaneous edema should be differentiated from diffuse paraspinal muscular edema, which is often associated with polymyositis and must be carefully excluded prior to surgery [1].
