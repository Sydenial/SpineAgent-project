# Low-Energy Chronic Traumatic Spondylolisthesis of the Axis

## Abstract
Objective The aim of this study is to present a unique case of a patient who presented to our emergency department with evidence of a chronic traumatic spondylolisthesis of the axis with severe displacement treated with anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) of C2-3 as well as posterior cervical fusion (PCF) of C1-3.

Methods One patient with an untreated traumatic spondylolisthesis of the axis with Levine type II injury pattern and 1.2 cm of anterior subluxation underwent ACDF C2-3 and PCF C1-3.

Results The patient recovered well; radiographs demonstrated reduction of the anterior subluxation, and the patient reported a neck disability index (NDI) score of 20 at 6-month follow-up with full neurologic function intact. The patient was then lost to follow-up.

Conclusion We present an alcoholic patient with a history of many falls who presented with a Levine type II traumatic spondylolisthesis of the axis with signs of chronicity seen on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). We were able to partially reduce the anterior displacement with traction, but required both anterior and posterior cervical approaches to achieve adequate reduction and stabilization.

Keywords: Cervical fracture; Traumatic spondylolisthesis of the axis; ACDF; Hangman's fracture

## Introduction
Traumatic spondylolisthesis of the axis is a serious injury with potentially devastating neurologic sequelae. This fracture is the second most common fracture of the axis after the odontoid fracture and accounts for 5% of all cervical spine injuries [1, 2]. Hyperextension or hyperflexion can cause this fracture morphology, while distraction versus compression components are hypothesized to have the greatest effect on neurologic injury.

Classification systems, including the widely used Levine and Edwards system, attempt to assess stability to guide treatment [3]. To the authors' knowledge, no classification system decisively standardizes treatment with consistent and reliable results, creating variability in management and challenges in counseling patients.

In 2006, Li et al. conducted a systematic review of Hangman's fracture management that included 32 papers. The review summarized differing treatment options and indications. Healing rate was the primary reported outcome; no patient-reported outcomes such as the neck disability index (NDI) were consistently available. The literature is lacking in this regard, and future studies should include functional outcome scores to differentiate the most beneficial treatment for individual patients [4]. According to the review, 74% of Hangman's fractures are treated non-operatively with a halo fixator, while the remaining cases are usually Effendi type IIa and III injuries deemed unstable and appropriate for surgical intervention [5]. Anterior fixation via plating or fusion due to disk injury is common, as disk injury confers instability [6-8]. A 2015 narrative review similarly noted a paucity of data regarding return to work and patient-reported outcomes, though about 75% of patients across modalities were pain-free with self-reported full range of motion at 1 year [9].

## Case Report

### Presentation
A 47-year-old man with a history of alcohol abuse, mandibular osteomyelitis status post plate fixation, and chronic pancreatitis presented via ambulance with neck pain. He was heavily intoxicated and ambulating on arrival. Per family, he had fallen several times from standing height the previous evening while intoxicated, with no other reported trauma. He reported bilateral hand paresthesia but denied other sensory deficits, motor weakness, or bowel/bladder changes. He was minimally tender to cervical palpation, had 5/5 strength in all extremity muscle groups, and was distally neurovascularly intact.

### Diagnostic Imaging
Computed tomography demonstrated bilateral fractures of the C2 pars interarticularis with 1.2 cm anterior subluxation of C2 on C3 and locked C2-3 facet joints. MRI demonstrated granulation tissue and healing of surrounding soft tissue, severe central stenosis, and damage to the anterior longitudinal ligament (ALL), posterior longitudinal ligament (PLL), and intervertebral disk.

### Operative Management

#### Anterior Stage (ACDF C2-3)
Following successful closed reduction, the patient underwent ACDF of C2-3. Under fluoroscopic imaging, a 2-cm midline incision was made, and dissection revealed marked granulation tissue, edema, and hypertrophy of the ALL. After annulotomy, the nucleus pulposus was noted to be completely sheared off. Disk material was removed, and the endplates were prepared under microscopic visualization. Autograft and an allograft spacer device were placed. A 14-mm anterior cervical plate with appropriately sized screws was applied. Final radiographs were obtained, the wound was irrigated and closed, and a Hemovac drain was placed.

#### Posterior Stage (PCF C1-3)
On postoperative day five, the patient underwent posterior fusion. With the patient prone in a Mayfield head holder, reduction maneuvers under fluoroscopy achieved maximal extension to align the C2 body anatomically. A midline incision from the skull base to approximately C4 was made. The C1 posterior arch was subluxed anteriorly. The C2-3 facet joints were exposed laterally and the C3-4 joint exposed with capsule preservation. A high-speed drill was used to cannulate the C1 lateral mass. Twenty-six millimeter screws were placed bilaterally in the lateral masses of C1 and C3 with good purchase. Bony surfaces were decorticated. A rod was cut, contoured, and placed into the tulips of the lateral mass screws at C1 and C3. Final radiographs showed good implant placement with maintained reduction at C2-3. The wound was closed, sterile dressings applied, and a Hemovac placed.

### Outcome
The patient tolerated both procedures well and remained neurovascularly intact. He was discharged to a sub-acute rehabilitation facility in excellent condition. At 6 months postoperatively, he had no neurologic deficits and radiographs showed no new fractures or degenerative changes. His NDI raw score at 6 months was 20 (moderate impairment). The patient was lost to follow-up thereafter.

## Discussion
This case involved severe soft-tissue and disk disruption with locked C2-3 facets and evidence of chronicity on MRI. Indications for a posterior approach include atlas fractures, contraindications to anterior surgery, vertebral artery access needs, locked facet joints, or any fracture unable to be reduced closed [6, 8-10]. The presence of locked facets and a disrupted PLL supported the posterior stabilization. Given severe anterior soft-tissue instability at C2-3 and disk disruption, ACDF of C2-3 was also indicated. The patient’s history of alcoholism and frequent falls further supported selecting the most stable construct to reduce the risk of failure and re-injury.

## Conclusion
A patient with a Levine type II traumatic spondylolisthesis of the axis with chronic features achieved partial reduction with traction and ultimately required combined anterior and posterior cervical approaches for adequate reduction and stabilization. At 6 months, the patient had maintained reduction and a moderate NDI score, with intact neurologic function.
