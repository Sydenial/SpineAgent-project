# Posterior Atlantoaxial Dislocation without Fracture or Neurological Symptoms Treated by Transoral–Posterior Approach Surgery: A Case Report and Literature Review

## Abstract

### Background
Atlantoaxial dislocation usually results from hyperextension trauma and is almost always accompanied by odontoid fracture and neurological symptoms. In most cases, patients with atlantoaxial dislocation die instantly. This is a rare report of posterior atlantoaxial dislocation without fracture and neurological symptoms treated by a transoral–posterior approach surgery, and only eleven similar cases have been previously reported.

### Objective
To describe the very rare case of an adult patient with posterior atlantoaxial dislocation without fracture who was neurologically intact and treated using transoral–posterior approach surgery, and to review the relevant literature.

### Methods
A 52-year-old man riding a motorcycle was rear-ended by a car. Using X-ray, computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), he was diagnosed with posterior atlantoaxial dislocation without a related fracture or significant change in spinal cord signal. Transoral–posterior approach surgery with sustained skull traction was used after failed closed reduction.

### Results
During a 6-month follow-up, lateral cervical spine radiography and sagittal CT reconstructions demonstrated no instability of the atlantoaxial complex. Posterior atlantoaxial dislocation without fracture or spinal cord deficit is rare. For patients who experience hyperextension trauma, such as rear-end collisions, X-ray, CT, and MRI should be performed to ensure diagnosis. Surgical intervention is necessary to restore atlantoaxial stability even in the absence of fracture or neurological symptoms.

### Conclusion
Transoral–posterior approach surgery is a safe and effective method for managing irreducible posterior atlantoaxial dislocation.

## Introduction

Posterior atlantoaxial dislocation resulting from traffic accidents usually causes immediate death regardless of the presence of an odontoid fracture, and very few patients survive, with or without neurological symptoms. Reports describing treatment of posterior atlantoaxial dislocation without fracture or neurological deficit are extremely rare. In most cases, conservative traction can reduce the dislocation, but posterior or anterior internal fixation and fusion are often required to enhance atlantoaxial stability. Although several similar cases have been reported, the optimal treatment strategy remains controversial and prognosis unclear. Here, we present a new case successfully treated using a combined anterior–posterior surgical approach.

## Case Report

A 52-year-old man riding a motorcycle was struck by a car and transported to a local county hospital. He was diagnosed with atlantoaxial dislocation, multiple soft tissue lacerations, and dislocation of the left ankle joint. According to medical records, his consciousness and vital signs were normal, and he complained of neck and throat pain. After two days of treatment, including ankle reduction, soft tissue debridement, and cervical collar immobilization, he was transferred to our hospital.

On admission, physical examination showed markedly limited neck motion. Limb movements were normal with grade IV–V muscle strength. Neurological function was classified as ASIA grade E. Initial CT scans showed no severe brain or thoracoabdominal injury. Anteroposterior, lateral, and open-mouth cervical X-rays demonstrated posterior displacement of the atlas and complete atlantoaxial dislocation. CT and MRI confirmed these findings, with no evidence of spinal cord compression or abnormal cord signal.

Four kilograms of skull traction was applied. Intraoperative fluoroscopy showed widening of the atlantoaxial distance, but reduction was unsuccessful, and traction weight was not increased to avoid neurological injury. A transoral retropharyngeal approach was used to expose the atlas and axis. After anterior atlantoaxial release, intraoperative fluoroscopy confirmed reduction of the dislocation. The wound was closed without grafting. The patient was then repositioned for posterior surgery, and C1–C2 fixation was performed using a pedicle screw system with morselized autologous iliac crest bone graft.

Postoperatively, the patient had no complications, neurological deficits, or infection. Follow-up radiography and CT scans showed stable atlantoaxial alignment. Neck and throat symptoms gradually resolved. Three months after surgery, the cervical collar was removed, and the patient returned to normal daily activities.

## Discussion

The stability of the atlantoaxial complex is primarily provided by the odontoid process, transverse ligament, and interlocking articular surfaces. Most atlantoaxial dislocations involve odontoid fractures or ligamentous disruption. Posterior atlantoaxial dislocation without fracture or neurological deficit is exceedingly rare, with only 11 reported cases [1–11].

Hyperextension trauma is the primary mechanism, often causing fatal spinal cord injury. However, due to the relatively large spinal canal at the C1–C2 level, the spinal cord may escape compression in rare cases. Diagnosis can be challenging, as plain radiographs may be obscured by overlapping cranial structures. Therefore, CT and MRI are essential, especially in patients with hyperextension injuries such as rear-end collisions.

Treatment options include closed reduction with traction and open surgical reduction. Closed reduction is effective but carries significant risk of neurological injury or death. Open reduction allows direct decompression and stabilization. In our case, anterior release achieved reduction without the need for partial odontoidectomy, followed by posterior fixation to ensure stability. We recommend careful use of spinal cord monitoring when attempting closed reduction.

## Conclusion

Posterior atlantoaxial dislocation without fracture or neurological deficit is rare and easily missed. Comprehensive imaging is essential for diagnosis. For irreducible cases, combined transoral–posterior approach surgery provides a safe and effective means of achieving reduction and long-term stability.
