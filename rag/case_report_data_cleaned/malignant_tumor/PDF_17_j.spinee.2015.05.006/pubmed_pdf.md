# Spinal Metastasis of Medulloblastoma in Adults

## Case Report

A fifty-eight-year-old male patient who had undergone surgical treatment for medulloblastoma followed by radiation therapy 7 years earlier was admitted to our hospital with complaints of dorsal pain, upper extremity pain, and progressively worsening weakness in both upper extremities.

Physical examination revealed motor and sensory deficits in both upper extremities. Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated several ovoid-shaped solid mass lesions compressing and displacing the spinal cord at the C2–C5 levels. The largest lesion measured $23 \times 12$ mm in size. The lesions appeared hypointense on T1-weighted images, slightly hyperintense on T2-weighted images, and showed strong enhancement on postcontrast sagittal and axial T1-weighted sequences.

The patient underwent reoperation, and histopathological examination of the excised lesions confirmed the diagnosis of metastatic medulloblastoma.
