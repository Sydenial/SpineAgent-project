# Giant Cell Tumor of Cervical Spine Presenting as Acute Asphyxia  
Successful Surgical Resection After Down-Staging With Denosumab

## Abstract  
Giant cell tumor (GCT) of bone is a benign tumor that most often involves the long bones. Spinal disease is rare, and mostly involves the sacrum. We report the case of a young woman with a large GCT of C7 that caused marked tracheal narrowing with acute asphyxia. Therapy with denosumab resulted in dramatic decrease in tumor size, which relieved airway obstruction, and increased tumor mineralization, which facilitated resection.

## Introduction  
Giant cell tumor (GCT) of bone is a benign neoplasm that commonly affects the long bones, but its occurrence in the spine is rare. This case report discusses a unique presentation of a GCT in the cervical spine leading to acute airway obstruction and the successful management with preoperative denosumab followed by surgical resection.

## Methods  
We report a patient with a large C7 GCT that caused tracheal compression with almost complete airway obstruction, requiring emergency intubation. Treatment involved three cycles of subcutaneous denosumab to facilitate surgical resection.

## Case Report  
A 20-year-old woman presented to an outside emergency center with acute dyspnea requiring intubation. She had been treated for asthma due to progressive shortness of breath and wheezing over the past 4 months. CT imaging revealed a large expansile lytic lesion involving the C7 vertebra with a soft tissue component, leading to anterior tracheal displacement and marked tracheal narrowing. MRI showed a large, lobulated mass arising from a severely compressed C7, resulting in spinal canal stenosis due to C7 retropulsion.

The endotracheal tube was exchanged for a tracheostomy due to continued dyspnea, and the patient was transferred to our hospital. Review of outside biopsy material confirmed GCT. The tumor was initially deemed inoperable, so the patient received three cycles of subcutaneous denosumab. Follow-up CT showed significant reduction in tumor size and increased mineralization. MRI confirmed a reduction in tumor enhancement and resolution of tracheal compression.

A two-stage resection was performed, including anterior C7-T1 intralesional corpectomy with titanium cage reconstruction, followed by posterior element resection with anterior and posterior stabilization. Histological examination showed no residual GCT. The patient had good range of neck motion and no evidence of tumor recurrence two years after surgery.

## Results  
The tumor responded to subcutaneously administered denosumab, with a marked decrease in size and relief of symptoms. Increased tumor mineralization in response to therapy facilitated successful surgical resection. The patient remains symptom-free two years after surgery without tumor recurrence.

## Discussion  
Cervical GCTs are rare and often symptomatic. Small cervical GCTs can be treated with curettage and packing, but lesions confined to the anterior vertebral body have a high recurrence rate. Inoperable or large tumors may require preoperative therapy, such as denosumab, to reduce tumor size and facilitate surgical resection. 

Denosumab is a human monoclonal antibody that inhibits osteoclastic activity by targeting the receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-β ligand (RANKL). It has been shown to be effective in reducing the size of unresectable GCTs, thereby allowing for less invasive surgery.

## Conclusion  
We have described a unique case of a large C7 GCT with almost complete airway occlusion, requiring emergent intubation and tracheostomy. Denosumab resulted in tumor shrinkage, relieving tracheal compression, and increased mineralization that facilitated subsequent surgical resection.

## Key Points  
- GCT of the cervical spine presenting as acute asphyxia is rare.
- Therapy with denosumab can shrink the tumor and increase mineralization.
- Denosumab treatment can facilitate surgical resection.
