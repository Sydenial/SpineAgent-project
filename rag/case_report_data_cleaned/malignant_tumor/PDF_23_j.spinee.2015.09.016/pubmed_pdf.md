# Slowly Progressive Solitary Plasmacytoma of the Thoracic Spine

## Case Report

Eight years prior to admission, a 40-year-old female was referred to a local hospital for back pain. Computed tomography (CT) showed an osteolytic lesion with an ossified rim in the body of T11. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrated an irregular, heterogeneous mass in the same vertebra. Further diagnostic work-up and therapy were refused by the patient at that time.

The patient returned for follow-up at 3 years and again at 8 years. CT and MRI revealed continuous slow progression of the tumor. Positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) revealed ^18F-FDG uptake confined to the tumor, with a maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) of 5.7. No other foci of abnormal uptake were identified elsewhere in the body.

Hematologic evaluation revealed no detectable M-protein, and urinalysis showed no light chain protein. Bone marrow cytology and pathology demonstrated no obvious abnormalities. Tumor excision was performed using an anterior approach, followed by interbody fusion and titanium rod instrumentation. Based on pathological examination and immunohistochemical staining, a diagnosis of solitary plasmacytoma was established.

## Discussion

Solitary bone plasmacytoma (SBP) involving a vertebra is very rare and carries a high risk of progression to multiple myeloma. Despite the propensity of SBP to progress to multiple myeloma within 2–3 years [1], in this case the disease remained confined to the T11 vertebra for 8 years prior to tumor excision.
