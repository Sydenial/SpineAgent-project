# Spinal Hemangiopericytoma: An Institutional Experience and Review of Literature

## Abstract

Hemangiopericytoma is a rare tumor of the CNS with potential for recurrence and widespread metastasis, even outside the CNS, with rare involvement of the spinal cord. This case series presents five patients to evaluate the clinical presentation, radiological features, management, pathology, and outcome of spinal hemangiopericytomas. Between 2004 and 2013, five patients underwent surgery for spinal hemangiopericytoma. Histopathological data were reviewed in all cases and clinical and follow-up details were collected from the data available in our department.

There were three males and two females, including one pediatric patient. Three patients had dorsal spine involvement and two patients had cervical spine involvement. There were two patients with intradural extramedullary tumors, one patient with a pure intramedullary tumor, one with a pure extradural tumor, and one with both intra and extradural tumors. All of them presented with motor weakness. Gross total resection of the tumor was done in three patients. Four patients received post-operative radiotherapy. Histopathology showed anaplastic tumors in four cases with high MIB-1 LI. Most of them were positive for CD34, mic-2, and bcl-2. Three patients who underwent gross total resection improved significantly in the follow-up period. Two patients who underwent subtotal resection expired due to the spread of their disease.

## Introduction

Hemangiopericytoma is a rare tumor of the central nervous system (CNS) that has the potential to metastasize widely. Initially, it was characterized as angioblastic meningioma and later termed as hemangiopericytoma by Stout and Murray in 1942. However, in 2007, they were classified as tumors of the meninges of mesenchymal origin in the WHO classification of CNS tumors. These tumors account for 1% of all CNS tumors and represent 2–4% of all meningeal tumors. Brain is the most common location for these tumors, and spinal occurrence is rare. Till date, only 80 cases of spinal hemangiopericytoma have been reported, with most being intradural-extramedullary tumors. Intramedullary spinal hemangiopericytoma is extremely rare, with only five cases reported so far.

Liu et al. classified spinal hemangiopericytomas into three types and five subtypes: type I is extradural tumor (IA—intracanal, IB—extracanal), type II is intradural (IIA—extramedullary, IIB—intramedullary), and type III is intradural with extension into the extradural and paravertebral areas. In our center, we have found five cases of spinal hemangiopericytoma in the last 10 years, including both adult and pediatric patients. In this paper, we have evaluated their clinical presentation, radiological features, management, pathology, and outcome.

## Methodology

In this retrospective review, patients were identified from the biopsy reports of spinal tumors operated between 2004 and 2013. Five patients with spinal hemangiopericytoma were found. Details of the patients were obtained from the discharge summary and also from the available follow-up data.

## Results

From 2004 to 2013, five patients with spinal hemangiopericytoma underwent treatment at our institute. There were three males and two females, with a mean age at diagnosis of 34 years (12–50 years). Cervical tumors were found in two patients, while dorsal tumors were present in three. Both cervical tumors were located in the subaxial cervical spine, while all dorsal tumors were located below D7. The average tumor length was 4.3 cm. The MRI of the patient with intramedullary tumor showed an intensely enhancing soft tissue mass with cord expansion and proximal syrinx formation.

All patients presented with motor weakness of variable duration. The mean duration of symptoms before presentation was 5.62 months. Pre-operative contrast-enhanced MRI scans were evaluated in all cases. Tumors were found in extradural, intradural-extramedullary, and intramedullary locations. 

All patients underwent surgery as primary treatment. Although imaging features suggested a vascular nature of the tumor, pre-operative embolization was not used. The tumors were approached from the posterior aspect via laminectomy or laminoplasty. Gross total resection was achieved in three cases, while two patients underwent subtotal resections due to high vascularity and cord invasion.

Histopathological evaluation showed hemangiopericytoma in all cases. Four were anaplastic types, and one was a grade 1 tumor. MIB-1 labeling index was high in the anaplastic cases, with a mean of 16.5%. Post-operative contrast-enhanced MRI was done for all patients. Small residual tumor was found in one patient even after gross total excision. 

## Case Reports

### Case 1

A 50-year-old male presented with slowly progressive ascending quadriparesis for 4 months. MRI showed an enhancing mass lesion at the C4–5 level. The tumor was removed via C4–5 laminectomy. Histopathological examination confirmed a grade III anaplastic hemangiopericytoma. The patient received chemotherapy and radiotherapy. At the last follow-up, significant neurological improvement was noted, and there was no recurrence.

### Case 2

A 34-year-old male presented with progressive paraparesis for 6 months and early onset sphincter involvement. MRI revealed an intramedullary tumor at D8–10. Surgery was performed, and the tumor was removed subtotally. Histopathology showed an anaplastic hemangiopericytoma. Despite receiving chemotherapy and radiotherapy, the patient deteriorated and expired 2 years post-surgery.

### Case 3

A 37-year-old female presented with ascending spastic paraparesis. MRI showed a tumor at the D7–9 level, with both intradural-extramedullary and extradural components. The tumor was excised via D7–8 laminoplasty. Histopathology confirmed a grade II hemangiopericytoma. She received no adjuvant treatment and showed significant improvement with normalization of lower limb power at the last follow-up.

### Case 4

A 37-year-old male with a recurrence of cervical hemangiopericytoma 5 years after initial surgery. The tumor was removed subtotally due to dense scarring and high vascularity. Histopathology confirmed anaplastic hemangiopericytoma. The patient received radiotherapy and chemotherapy but succumbed to the disease after tumor regrowth.

### Case 5

A 12-year-old female presented with sudden onset paraplegia for 3 days. MRI showed an intradural extramedullary tumor at D11 to L1. The tumor was removed via laminectomy, and histopathology confirmed anaplastic hemangiopericytoma. Post-operative radiotherapy was given. At the last follow-up, the patient showed significant neurological improvement.

## Discussion

Spinal hemangiopericytomas are rare tumors with aggressive behavior and potential for widespread metastasis. They are often misdiagnosed pre-operatively as meningiomas or schwannomas. The goal of treatment is gross total resection, and radiotherapy may be beneficial for high-grade tumors or residual disease. Chemotherapy is rarely used, and its effectiveness remains unclear. Long-term follow-up is essential due to the risk of recurrence.

## Conclusion

Spinal hemangiopericytomas are rare and challenging to diagnose and treat, with a high recurrence rate. Strong clinical suspicion and increased awareness are crucial for preoperative diagnosis. Gross total resection is the treatment goal, and radiotherapy should be considered for high-grade tumors or residual disease. Recurrences are common even after complete resection, and long-term follow-up is required.
