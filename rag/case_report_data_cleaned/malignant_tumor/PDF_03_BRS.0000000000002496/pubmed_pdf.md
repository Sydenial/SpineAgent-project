# MRI Characteristics of Spinal Ependymoma WHO Grade II: A Review of 59 Cases

## Abstract

**Study Design**: Retrospective multicenter study.

**Objective**: Ependymoma review of MRI data of a large series of surgically proven cases.  
**Summary of Background Data**: Common spinal intramedullary lesions are most frequently ependymomas, and their pathology is well established. However, these types of tumors exhibit a variety of MRI findings.  
**Methods**: 59 patients with spinal ependymomas confirmed by pathology were included. MRI analysis was conducted using T1- and T2-weighted imaging sequences. The MRI characteristics of the tumors were studied, including intensity variations, enhancement patterns, and associated cysts.  
**Results**: All tumors were hypointense on T1-weighted MRI and 93% were hyperintense on T2-weighted MRI. Various enhancement patterns were observed on gadolinium-enhanced MRI.  
**Conclusion**: Spinal ependymomas exhibit a range of MRI characteristics, with distinct enhancement patterns and potential cystic changes that provide insights into their pathology. These findings are useful for improving diagnostic accuracy and surgical planning.

**Key Words**: spinal ependymoma, MRI, diagnosis, enhancement, cysts

## Introduction

Spinal ependymomas are common intramedullary tumors, often diagnosed via MRI. These tumors show a variety of MRI characteristics, making diagnosis challenging. The purpose of this study was to analyze MRI features of a large series of surgically confirmed spinal ependymomas.

## Materials and Methods

The study included 59 patients (35 males, 24 females) who underwent surgery for spinal cord ependymomas at the Nagoya Spine Group between June 2000 and December 2015. Surgical procedures were carried out by five board-certified spine surgeons. Detailed information was obtained from a prospective registry. The mean patient age was 51 years (range: 19-77 years).

The pathological diagnoses of all 59 cases were confirmed by two pathologists at each institution. All tumors were classified as cellular ependymomas by surgical biopsy, with a WHO grade II classification.

MRI was performed using T1-weighted, T2-weighted, and gadolinium-enhanced sequences. Image interpretation was carried out by two board-certified neurosurgeons and two radiologists who reviewed all images in consensus. MRI evaluation criteria included tumor localization, cystic components, syringomyelia, hemorrhage, signal characteristics, gadolinium-enhancement patterns, and surrounding edema.

## Results

Of the 59 ependymomas, 28 were located in the cervical spine, 34 in the thoracic spine, and 3 were conus lesions. The extent of involvement ranged from 1 to 6 vertebral segments. All tumors were hypointense on T1-weighted MRI, and 93% were hyperintense on T2-weighted MRI. The tumors were located centrally in all cases. 85% of cases showed surrounding cord edema. Cystic components were seen in 61% of cases, with 17% showing intratumoral cysts. A "cap sign" was present in 29% of cases, reflecting hemorrhage.

On gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted images, all cases showed enhancement. The enhancement pattern was homogeneous in 46% of cases, heterogeneous in 27%, rim-enhancing in 19%, and nodular in 8%.

## Discussion

Spinal ependymomas are most commonly diagnosed as WHO grade II lesions. They are typically well-circumscribed and amenable to complete resection. However, hemorrhage is frequently observed in these tumors, leading to MRI findings such as the "cap sign," which is indicative of hemosiderin deposition. The presence of cystic components, such as rostral or caudal cysts, also influences MRI characteristics.

The variability in enhancement patterns on gadolinium-enhanced MRI reflects different intratumoral components, which may correspond to tumor cellularity or the presence of hemorrhage. The presence of these features can guide the preoperative diagnosis and surgical planning.

## Conclusion

Spinal ependymomas present with a range of MRI characteristics that are crucial for accurate diagnosis and surgical management. The presence of features like hypointensity on T2-weighted images, hemosiderin deposition, and distinct gadolinium-enhancement patterns, such as the "cap sign," are important diagnostic markers for grade II ependymomas.
