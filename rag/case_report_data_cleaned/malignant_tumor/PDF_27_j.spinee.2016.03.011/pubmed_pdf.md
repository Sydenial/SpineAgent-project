# Multiple myeloma presenting with acute bony spinal cord compression and mechanical instability successfully managed nonoperatively

## Abstract

**Background Context:** Multiple myeloma (MM) with spinal involvement may present with spinal soft-tissue myelomatous extension into the spinal canal. These lesions are often managed with chemotherapy or radiotherapy alone, but patients with neurological compromise typically require surgical decompression with adjuvant therapy. Infrequently, patients with MM present with spinal cord compression due to bony collapse and kyphotic deformity, where the standard management is surgical decompression and internal fixation.

**Purpose:** This study aimed to report a high-risk myeloma patient with a mechanically unstable spine, acute spinal cord compression, and neurologic deficit who was treated successfully using nonoperative means.

**Study Design:** Case report.

**Methods:** A 37-year-old male patient with MM was referred to our tertiary referral spinal unit with acute bony spinal cord compression and neurological impairment. Computed tomography revealed lytic lesions of T2 and T3 and anterolisthesis of T1 on T2 producing mechanical instability, and magnetic resonance imaging confirmed extension of disease into the epidural space and cord compression. This was successfully managed with nonoperative treatment using a brace.

**Results:** Management in a brace restored clinical and radiological stability and normal neurological function.

**Conclusion:** Certain high-risk myeloma patients with a mechanically unstable spine, acute spinal cord compression, and neurologic deficit can be treated effectively in an appropriate brace when managed by a tertiary referral spinal unit.

## Introduction

A 37-year-old male with previously undiagnosed multiple myeloma (MM) presented with a 2-month history of upper thoracic back pain. He was subsequently diagnosed with light chain MM and commenced on appropriate chemotherapy and corticosteroid treatment for disease control, as well as radiotherapy to his spine for pain relief. Following an acute deterioration in lower limb motor and sensory function, he was referred to our tertiary referral spinal myeloma service.

Neurological examination revealed sensory deficit (1 of 2) in the right T7–T11 dermatomes and motor weakness (grade 4 MRC) in both lower limbs, graded ASIA D. He had no bowel or bladder dysfunction, and his Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) score for back pain was 7 of 10.

Computed tomography (CT) of the thoracic spine demonstrated multiple lytic lesions throughout, most extensive in the T2 and T3 vertebral bodies, resulting in significant loss of vertebral height and regional kyphosis. There was significant anterolisthesis of T1 on T2, causing marked narrowing of the anterior-to-posterior canal dimension. Subsequent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrated involvement of the posterior vertebral wall at T2 and T3 with extension of disease into the epidural space and associated cord compression. This patient had a Spinal Instability Neoplastic Score (SINS) of 18.

## Case Report

The patient was managed with a custom-made cervico-thoraco-lumbo-sacral orthosis (CTLSO) brace for a planned 3-month period and continued on chemotherapy and radiotherapy. He was monitored with regular clinical and radiographic assessments.

After 10 weeks in the brace, interval CT demonstrated significant anterior and posterior extraosseous bone formation with reconstitution of the T2 and T3 vertebral bodies and posterior elements. Bridging bone between the diseased vertebral levels conferred mechanical stability to the upper thoracic spine. At the 12-week follow-up appointment, brace treatment was discontinued. The VAS score for back pain was 0 of 10, with complete resolution of neurologic deficit (ASIA E). There was no obvious clinical deformity in the sagittal plane, and the patient had a pain-free functional range of motion of the spine.

## Discussion

Patients with MM commonly present with back pain from pathological vertebral compression fractures. Cement augmentation is a successful technique providing good analgesia in these cases [3,4]. A small cohort of patients with MM present with soft-tissue disease extending into the spinal canal, which may result in cord compression and neurological deficit [1]. The treatment of these soft-tissue epidural MM lesions classically involves chemotherapy and radiotherapy for those with no neurologic deficit [5–7], obviating the need for surgical decompression [8]. Surgical decompression is generally reserved for cases of acute neurologic deficit resulting from soft-tissue extension into the canal compressing the spinal cord or cauda equina [2,9].

Patients with MM presenting with bony spinal cord compression and neurological impairment traditionally require decompression and instrumented fixation to relieve neural compression and stabilize the spine [9]. This presentation commonly occurs when the spine is unstable due to significant vertebral body collapse, involvement of the facet joints and pedicles, and translation of the vertebral bodies. In our institution, a more conservative approach has been adopted for managing patients with MM and spinal involvement. Radiotherapy and/or chemotherapy can effectively decompress the spinal canal, and there is substantial potential for extraosseous bony healing to confer spinal stability [4].

Operative management may be accompanied by anesthetic complications, wound sepsis, wound dehiscence, iatrogenic axial neurological deficit, and metalwork failure due to poor bone quality. Avoidance of instrumentation is particularly relevant in patients receiving chemotherapy who are pancytopenic and in those planned for stem cell transplantation, as they are at high risk of secondary metalwork infection [4,9].

This case report describes a patient with significant spinal instability producing a maximum SINS score, including a lytic lesion at a junctional level, greater than 50% vertebral body collapse, bilateral involvement of posterolateral elements, and spinal malalignment due to translation and kyphosis [10,11]. The patient was treated as having an unstable spinal cord injury and managed with a CTLSO brace to provide relative stability and allow unaided mobilization. Following brace treatment, restoration of stability was demonstrated both clinically and radiographically. The patient achieved a pain-free functional range of motion with new bone formation in both the anterior and posterior spinal columns spanning the affected vertebrae, and neurological function returned to normal.

This case highlights the successful nonoperative management of a high-risk myeloma patient with a mechanically unstable spine, acute spinal cord compression, and neurologic deficit. This treatment strategy was safe and effective, avoiding surgery and its associated risks while producing sufficient extraosseous new bone formation to confer mechanical stability. These findings suggest a role for bracing as part of an overall nonoperative management strategy for mechanically and neurologically unstable vertebral fractures in MM, which should be considered only in tertiary referral spinal units with appropriate expertise.
