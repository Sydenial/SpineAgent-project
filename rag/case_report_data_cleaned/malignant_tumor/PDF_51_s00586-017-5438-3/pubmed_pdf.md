# Cryosurgery in the excision of a giant local recurrent sacral chordoma: a case report and literature review

## Abstract

Chordoma is a low-grade malignant tumor with recurrence and metastasis tendency that originates from embryonic notochordal remnants. The sacrococcygeal region is the most commonly involved site. The aim of this paper is to report the results of the use of cryosurgery in the excision of a giant recurrent sacral chordoma and review the pertinent literature.

A 64-year-old female patient with a locally recurrent sacral chordoma came to our attention after ten interventions performed at another institute. A surgical treatment was performed using argon cryosurgery.

In this case, it was not possible to perform a marginal or wide excision, but an intralesional resection with the removal of three major blocks was achieved. The fragments sent for histological analysis measured a total of $35 \times 30 \times 8$ cm with a weight of 4.260 g.

Given the gelatinous structure of the tumor and the possibility of contamination of the operative field, cryosurgery may be indicated when previous surgeries and the dimensions of the tumor mass do not allow a resection with negative margins, improving radiotherapy efficacy in the local control of the tumor.

## Introduction

Chordoma is a low-grade malignant tumor with recurrence and metastasis tendency that originates from embryonic notochordal remnants [1]. The incidence of chordoma is 0.08 per 100,000 individuals [2]. The sacrococcygeal region is the most commonly involved site, followed by the skull base and mobile spine [3]. It represents only 1–4% of all primary bone tumors [4], but accounts for 40% of all primary sacral tumors [2].

Sacral chordoma has a male-to-female ratio of 2–3:1, and the majority of affected patients are in the fifth to seventh decades of life [5]. It has a local recurrence rate of 43–85% [6–14], with most recurrences occurring within 3 years from surgery [5].

Local recurrence of sacral chordoma after surgery is mostly related to the level of the tumor and the margin of resection. In particular, S3 represents a watershed: oncologically appropriate resections above that level often include sacral nerves whose loss of function (e.g., sphincteric and sexual functions) is poorly tolerated by patients. Resections below that level are less disabling.

Regarding resection margins, intralesional and marginal resections are associated with a higher risk of local recurrence than wide resection [5, 15, 16].

The treatment of local recurrent chordoma involves both surgery and radiotherapy. When surgery is planned, wide en bloc excision should be performed whenever feasible. When wide resection is not possible due to tumor localization, partial resection must be performed, followed by high-dose radiotherapy to reduce local recurrence [17].

The present report describes a case of a 64-year-old female patient affected by a giant locally recurrent sacral chordoma treated with intralesional en bloc resection using argon cryosurgery.

## Case presentation

The patient was referred to our clinic because of a huge locally recurrent chordoma presenting as voluminous plurilobate formations within the buttock muscles bilaterally and in the right adductor region. Neurological examination revealed urinary and fecal incontinence, with preserved motor function in the lower limbs.

Additional minor chordoma localizations were present in the right paravertebral lumbar region and superficially in the subcutaneous layer of the gluteal region.

Over the previous 7 years, the patient had undergone multiple ineffective treatments at another institution, with continuous tumor relapse. Initial treatments included two radiofrequency ablations, followed by an intralesional excision. Histopathological examination confirmed chordoma, and adjuvant external beam radiation therapy (66 Gy in 33 fractions) was administered. Despite this, the tumor relapsed locally seven times, requiring repeated debulking procedures. The patient also underwent chemotherapy with imatinib without benefit.

The case was discussed during a multidisciplinary oncology meeting, and a further excision using intraoperative argon cryosurgery as a local adjuvant was proposed. Written informed consent was obtained.

The tumor extended along the posterior thigh bilaterally and transversely at the level of the sacrococcygeal region. Cryosurgical probes were used to facilitate identification of adequate resection margins. Due to tumor extent, marginal or wide excision was not feasible; an intralesional en bloc resection with removal of three major blocks was performed, including the terminal portion of the coccyx. The sciatic nerves were identified, isolated, and preserved bilaterally.

Histopathological examination confirmed chordoma. The surgical specimens measured a total of $35 \times 30 \times 8$ cm and weighed 4.260 g. Postoperatively, the patient experienced worsening fecal incontinence and weakness of the right anterior tibialis muscle. Bowel, bladder, motor, sensory, and sexual functions are among the most frequently compromised after sacral resection [18].

The early postoperative course was complicated by ischemic necrosis at the wound edges, which required debridement and application of vacuum-assisted advanced dressings. This complication was attributed to impaired skin blood supply following extensive subcutaneous excision rather than to cryoablation. The wound was revised two months later and healed uneventfully.

During this secondary procedure, cryosurgical excision was also performed on a small chordoma localization in the right paravertebral lumbar region. After wound healing, the patient received an additional course of proton therapy. A 6-month follow-up MRI showed no recurrence in the regions treated with cryosurgery.

## Discussion and conclusion

Cryosurgery was first used by Cahan for the treatment of benign and malignant tumors [19, 20]. Marcove later developed its application in bone tumors, suggesting alternating freeze–thaw cycles [21, 22].

De Vries et al. first introduced cryosurgery as a treatment modality for sacrococcygeal chordoma, reporting long-term disease-free survival in selected cases [23]. Subsequent studies demonstrated the usefulness of cryosurgery as an adjunct to resection, particularly in achieving better local control when margins are close or intralesional [24, 25].

According to the Chordoma Global Consensus Group, a history of piecemeal excision, prior high-dose radiotherapy, and tumor capsule violation exclude curative-intent resection. In such cases, debulking surgery combined with radiotherapy is recommended to achieve local disease control [2].

In the present case, multiple prior intralesional surgeries led to tumor cell dissemination and repeated relapses. Cryosurgery was used to support intralesional excision in a context where the surgical goal was local control rather than cure.

Chordomas have an expansile, lobulated structure with a gelatinous matrix. Cryosurgery, by solidifying the tumor mass, may reduce the risk of malignant cell spillage during piecemeal removal and improve local control when combined with adjuvant radiotherapy.

In conclusion, cryosurgery may strengthen surgical margins when negative-margin resection is no longer feasible, such as in cases of extensive prior surgery, large tumor burden, or previous high-dose radiotherapy. Ultimately, this approach may enhance the efficacy of radiotherapy in multidisciplinary treatment strategies.
