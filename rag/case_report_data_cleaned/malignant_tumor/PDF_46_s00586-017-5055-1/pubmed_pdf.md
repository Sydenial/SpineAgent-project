# Multiple intradural-extramedullary spinal ependymomas including tumors with different histological features

## Abstract

**Introduction** We report a rare case with multiple intradural-extramedullary spinal ependymomas with different histological features.  
**Case report** A 26-year-old female presented to our hospital because of difficulty in walking due to progressive paresis. Magnetic resonance imaging of the thoracic spinal cord showed multiple spinal cord tumors. Surgical resection of these tumors was performed, and different histological diagnoses, including World Health Organization (WHO) Grade III anaplastic ependymoma and Grade II ependymoma, were obtained from the resected specimens. Additional radiotherapy and chemotherapy were also performed, and a successful outcome has been maintained for at least 3 years after surgery.  
**Conclusion** Surgical resections and subsequent radiotherapy and chemotherapy for cases with multiple intradural-extramedullary ependymomas can result in a good postoperative course.

## Introduction

Spinal ependymoma typically occurs in a solitary fashion as an intramedullary tumor at the spinal cord level, or as an exophytic tumor in the conus medullaris or filum terminale [1, 2]. However, to the best of our knowledge, multiple intradural-extramedullary spinal ependymomas that are histologically confirmed to be composed of two subtypes of ependymomas have not yet been reported.

## Case Report

A 26-year-old female presented to our hospital complaining of difficulty in walking due to progressive paresis, pain in the trunk, and numbness in the lower limbs. She had no medical history or family history, including neurofibromatosis. At the first visit, a neurological examination revealed muscle strength of the lower limbs of grade 3–5/5 and hypesthesia in the trunk and lower limbs, with hyperreflexia of the muscle stretch reflex, indicating pyramidal tract signs.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the thoracic spinal cord demonstrated multiple spinal cord tumors with low signal intensity changes on T1-weighted, T2-weighted, and short-T1 inversion recovery images.

We performed laminectomies of T3 and T4 and gross total resection of two intradural-extramedullary tumors located at the upper thoracic level to confirm the histology of the tumors and improve the neurological status. Histological studies revealed proliferation of small cells with round-to-oval nuclei, formation of perivascular pseudorosettes, increased cellularity, numerous mitoses, and microvascular proliferation on hematoxylin and eosin staining. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated strong positivity for GFAP and vimentin, as well as dot-like and ring-like structures positive for epithelial membrane antigen. These histological findings were consistent with WHO Grade III anaplastic ependymoma.

Cranial MRI after the first operation showed no intracranial mass lesion. Although the neurological condition improved after the first operation, five weeks later we resected five residual tumors detected on MRI via T5–T10 laminectomies. All five tumors were also intradural-extramedullary, and gross total resection was achieved. Histological assessment revealed one tumor with WHO Grade III anaplastic ependymoma, similar to the initially resected tumors, and four tumors with WHO Grade II ependymoma, which showed moderate cellularity without mitotic figures or microvascular proliferation, clearly different from the initial tumors.

After the second operation, craniospinal radiation was added in consideration of possible nonvisible tumor dissemination, in addition to chemotherapy using temozolomide [11].

Three years after surgery, further improvement in neurological condition was observed, and the patient was able to walk without support. No apparent recurrence or dissemination was detected on gadolinium-enhanced MRI.

## Discussion

There have been only a few reports of multiple spinal ependymomas, especially multiple intradural-extramedullary ependymomas at the initial visit [3–5, 9, 10]. Furthermore, to the best of our knowledge, cases with multiple ependymomas consisting of tumors with different histological subtypes have not been reported. In this report, we described a case of multiple intradural-extramedullary ependymomas including WHO Grade III anaplastic ependymomas and WHO Grade II ependymomas.

Vural et al. reported a case of multiple intradural-extramedullary ependymomas in which all resected tumors were WHO Grade II, and demonstrated dissemination by genetic analysis [5]. In addition, anaplastic transformation has been reported in intradural-extramedullary ependymomas [6–9]. In the present case, two hypotheses regarding pathogenesis can be considered. First, a primary WHO Grade II intradural-extramedullary ependymoma may have disseminated to other spinal levels via cerebrospinal fluid, after which some tumors, including the primary lesion, underwent anaplastic transformation. Second, the tumors may have arisen from multicentric foci. Further genetic analysis is required to clarify the mechanism of tumor multiplicity.

Regarding treatment, surgical management has been adopted in all previously reported cases [3–5, 9, 10], although adjuvant chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy were used in some cases [3, 9, 10]. There are also reports supporting the activity of temozolomide in ependymoma and glioblastoma [11, 12]. In this case, surgery was first performed to improve neurological symptoms and obtain histological diagnosis, followed by additional resection of residual tumors, craniospinal radiation, and chemotherapy with temozolomide. Although this strategy resulted in a favorable outcome for at least 3 years, accumulation of further case reports is necessary to establish an optimal treatment strategy for multiple intradural-extramedullary ependymomas.

## Conclusion

Surgical resections followed by radiotherapy and chemotherapy for multiple intradural-extramedullary ependymomas resulted in a good postoperative course. This case provides useful information for establishing an appropriate treatment strategy for this extremely rare pathology.
