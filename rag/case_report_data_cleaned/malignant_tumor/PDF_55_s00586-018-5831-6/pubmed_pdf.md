# Lung Metastasis Regression with Increased CD8+ T Lymphocyte Infiltration in a Patient with Spinal Metastatic Leiomyosarcoma

## Abstract

**Purpose:** To explore the regression of lung metastasis with increased CD8+ T lymphocyte infiltration following total en bloc spondylectomy (TES) in a patient with metastatic uterine leiomyosarcoma.

**Methods:** A 44-year-old woman with metastatic uterine leiomyosarcoma of the lung and L1 vertebra underwent TES following bilateral three-level preoperative segmental artery embolization. Resected tumor-bearing lamina was frozen using liquid nitrogen and used as a tumor-bearing bone graft for spinal reconstruction.

**Results:** Tumor necrosis and obstructing material used in preoperative embolization were detected in the resected specimen of L1. Five days after TES, chest computed tomography scan demonstrated decreased solitary lung mass size without adjuvant treatment. Lobectomy was performed for the lung metastasis 42 days after TES. Infiltration of CD8+ T lymphocytes into tumor tissue was observed. Six months after TES, activities of daily living were normal with no evidence of local recurrence or distant metastasis. One year after TES, however, lung CT revealed another lung metastasis, and molecular-targeting therapy (pazopanib) was initiated.

**Conclusions:** This case demonstrated that preoperative tumor embolization combined with TES using tumor-bearing autograft provided both local radical cure and systemic antitumor immunological enhancement, though the long-term effect may be limited.

**Keywords:** Frozen autograft, Embolization, CD8, Leiomyosarcoma, Spondylectomy

## Introduction

Leiomyosarcoma is a malignant soft tissue sarcoma rarely arising in the uterus. Uterine leiomyosarcoma comprises only 1% of all uterine malignancies but has high recurrence rates (45–73%) even with aggressive management. Distant metastasis indicates late-stage disease and has limited treatment options, with a 10–35.8% 5-year survival rate. Skeletal metastasis occurs in 13.8% of cases. The spine is one of the more common skeletal sites and can be highly morbid and deadly. Surgical options for spinal metastatic leiomyosarcoma range from spinal decompression to en bloc excision. Postoperative radiation and chemotherapy have been used, but no definitive guidelines exist due to their controversial efficacy. Herein, we present a case of metastatic uterine leiomyosarcoma of the lung and L1 vertebra that was successfully treated with TES using tumor-bearing frozen autograft for reconstruction. After surgery, solitary lung metastasis regression was observed. CD8+ T lymphocyte infiltration into tumor tissue significantly increased in shrunk lung metastasis with slight infiltration in both L1 and the primary lesion, suggesting systemic immunological enhancement.

## Case Report

### History and Clinical Evaluation

A 44-year-old woman was diagnosed with a malignant tumor of the uterus. Three months later, she underwent total hysterectomy at another institution. Histopathological evaluation revealed uterine leiomyosarcoma. She complained of low back pain 2 years later. Lumbar radiographs revealed cortical erosion of the superior and inferior endplates of the L1 vertebral body, while the other lumbar vertebrae appeared normal. CT scan and T2-weighted magnetic resonance imaging revealed extensive destruction of the L1 vertebral body, causing pathological fracture and invasion of the tumor into the spinal canal. Chest CT scan revealed a 20×21 mm solitary left lung mass suspected of lung metastasis.

### Treatment

Preoperative embolization of bilateral segmental arteries at three levels was performed 3 days before TES. Combined left anterolateral retroperitoneal and posterior approach TES was performed without preoperative chemotherapy or radiation. The posterior approach was performed, and the lower half of the T12 lamina was removed to expose the L1 superior articular facet. The L1 vertebral body was then removed en bloc posteriorly. The excised tumor-bearing lamina was frozen using liquid nitrogen for 20 minutes and used as a tumor-bearing bone graft for spinal reconstruction.

### Pathological Findings of Resected L1

Histological analysis revealed metastasis of uterine leiomyosarcoma. Immunohistochemistry showed the majority of cells to be negative for αSMA, caldesmon, desmin, ER, and PgR, but positive for CD34, S-100, and EMA. Partial tumor necrosis and obstructing material used in preoperative embolization were detected in the resected specimen.

### Postoperative Course

No postoperative complications occurred, and the patient walked independently 2 weeks after surgery. Five days after TES, chest CT demonstrated decreased solitary lung mass size to 16×18 mm without receiving any adjuvant treatment. Forty-two days after TES, lobectomy of the solitary lung metastasis was performed. Tumor necrosis was detected in the resected lung metastasis, with approximately 18% of tumor necrosis. Histological evaluation revealed significantly increased infiltration of CD8+ T lymphocytes in the shrunk lung metastasis. Six months after TES, the patient was in normal daily activities with no evidence of local recurrence or distant metastasis. One year after TES, however, another lung metastasis was observed, and molecular-targeting therapy was initiated.

## Discussion

Spinal metastatic leiomyosarcoma is rare, with only a few case series and reports published. Due to its rarity, management is based on clinical case reports, clinical status, and patient survival. Radiation and chemotherapy have been used, but their efficacy is controversial. Survival from spinal metastatic leiomyosarcoma varies from weeks to up to 13 years. This case was expected to have a long survival due to single-level spinal involvement and a solitary lung metastasis. Therefore, TES of L1 and lobectomy of the solitary lung metastasis were performed.

Reconstruction using tumor-bearing autograft treated with liquid nitrogen has been found to be safe and effective with no reported local recurrences. This technique has been performed in TES at our institute since 2010 and eliminates graft harvest-site morbidity, decreases blood loss, and shortens surgical time. This case report is the first to show clinical evidence of cryoimmunology using this reconstruction technique in TES, combined with preoperative embolization.

The combination of preoperative embolization with TES using frozen tumor-bearing autograft may enhance systemic antitumor immune response. The mechanism involves tumor necrosis, which releases a large amount of tumor-related antigens, potentially stimulating the immune system. Previous reports have shown that embolization combined with cryoablation enhances immune responses in other cancers. This case demonstrates the potential of embolization-stimulated antitumor immunological effect for spinal metastasis.

## Conclusion

The combination of preoperative spinal tumor embolization and TES using tumor-bearing frozen autograft provided both local radical cure and systemic antitumor immunological enhancement in this case, although the long-term effect may be limited.
