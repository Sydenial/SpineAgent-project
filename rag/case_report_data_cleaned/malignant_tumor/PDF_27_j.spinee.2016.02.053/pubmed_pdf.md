# Circumferential Intradural Meningioma of the Thoracic Spinal Cord

## Abstract

**Background and Context:** There are very few reported cases of a meningioma circumferentially surrounding the spinal cord. To date, this entity has only been described at the conus medullaris and in the cervical region, with authors basing opinions on unusual extramedullary meningioma that completely encircled the thoracic spinal cord.

**Case Report:** A 40-year-old woman with progressive numbness of the lower limbs and spasticity of gait following a fall presented to our hospital. Magnetic resonance imaging of the spine demonstrated an abnormality at T6–T7 completely encircling the spinal cord. The patient underwent subtotal resection, with the anterolateral portion not accessible. Histology revealed a psammomatous meningioma (WHO Grade I). The patient showed clinical improvement, with residual numbness of her feet and right hemithorax.

**Conclusion:** This is the first reported case of an intradural extramedullary meningioma completely encircling the thoracic spinal cord. Achieving complete resection of this circumferential meningioma was not possible via a posterior approach. The optimum management of this condition is unknown; clearly, achieving symptomatic relief with adequate cord decompression is paramount. However, the long-term outcome and risk of recurrence in these cases, given their rarity and the difficulties in achieving complete resection, remain unknown.

## Introduction

Meningioma is a common form of spinal neoplasia, accounting for 25%–46% of cases [1]. The majority tend to be intradural, extramedullary, and within the thoracic region [1]. Typically well-circumscribed, discrete lesions, it is rare to encounter a meningioma that completely encircles the spinal cord. There are only three cases of full circumferential spinal meningioma reported in the literature: two in the cervical region [2,3] and one at the conus medullaris [4]. Here, the authors present the first case of a fully circumferential intradural, extramedullary meningioma in the thoracic spinal cord.

## Case Report

A 40-year-old woman with a known history of systemic lupus erythematosus presented to our hospital following recurrent falls. She had been experiencing progressive numbness in her lower limbs for approximately 18 months and numbness of the pelvis for approximately 4 months. Onset of the latter symptom coincided with episodes of both urinary and fecal urgency. She had also experienced significant weight loss of approximately 13 kg six months before presentation.

On examination, she was found to have a spastic gait but normal, symmetrical power in both lower limbs, with increased tone and bilaterally brisk deep tendon reflexes, extensor plantar responses, and bilateral ankle clonus. Sensory examination revealed a thoracic sensory level. Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated an enhancing lesion within the subdural space, circumferentially encasing the spinal cord at the level of T6 and T7 without involvement of the surrounding bone. Differential diagnoses included lymphoma or other primary or secondary spinal tumors. Less likely considerations were tuberculosis or an inflammatory process.

Staging computed tomography of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis revealed extensive calcification within the subdural space at the level of T6–T7, with no infiltration into the epidural space or destruction of adjacent vertebral structures. No other distant abnormalities were detected.

Given the unusual radiological appearances and clinical progression, the decision was made to proceed with urgent decompression, biopsy, and debulking. A midline durotomy was performed under microscopic vision. The dura was dissected free from a calcified layer beneath, which was divided longitudinally to reveal an associated extramedullary soft tissue lesion. This lesion was dissected free from the spinal cord. Frozen section histology supported the intraoperative diagnosis of meningioma. Posterolateral resection was achieved with piecemeal dissection of heavily calcified areas, resulting in decompression of the spinal cord. The remaining anterolateral portion of the tumor was left intact due to its adherence to the cord and the risk of neurologic injury. The dura was left open and repaired with a dural substitute and fibrin sealant.

The patient recovered well postoperatively without immediate complications. She received a short course of dexamethasone and was discharged home following physiotherapy.

Histopathology revealed a meningothelial tumor with dense psammomatous microcalcifications. No atypical or malignant features were observed, and Ki-67 nuclear labeling was very low. A diagnosis of psammomatous meningioma (WHO Grade I) was made.

At six months’ follow-up, the patient’s gait had markedly improved, although she continued to experience numbness of the right hemithorax. She returned to work and resumed her exercise regimen. Postoperative baseline MRI confirmed adequate decompression of the spinal cord with residual tumor anterior and lateral to the cord, as expected. The patient is being followed with annual spinal MRI, with further treatment options to be considered if tumor growth occurs.

## Discussion

A review of the literature identified three cases of meningioma with complete circumferential enclosure of the spinal cord [2–4] and two cases with partial enclosure [4,5]. Of the fully circumferential cases, two were located in the cervical region and one involved the conus medullaris and cauda equina. The present case represents the first reported instance of a fully circumferential meningioma of the thoracic spinal cord.

Due to the unusual radiological features and rarity of this condition, meningioma was not initially considered the most likely diagnosis. Infection, inflammatory disease, and lymphoma were considered based on the patient’s history and imaging characteristics. Computed tomography–identified calcification and intraoperative findings ultimately supported the diagnosis of meningioma.

The natural history of circumferential spinal meningioma is not well understood. Complete resection is generally optimal for spinal meningiomas and is achievable in most cases [1]. However, complete resection is considerably more challenging in fully circumferential lesions, and the risk of recurrence is likely higher when residual tumor remains.

Achieving complete resection in such cases may require an anterior thoracic approach, such as a transthoracic procedure, which carries significant morbidity. Given the rarity of this condition, it is unclear whether the risks of such invasive surgery outweigh the potential benefits. In cases where complete resection is not feasible, symptomatic relief through adequate decompression is paramount. Leaving the dura open has been suggested to reduce the risk of postoperative arachnoiditis and cord compression.

None of the reported cases, including the present one, received adjuvant radiotherapy. Given the patient’s young age, good neurological recovery, degree of decompression achieved, and extensive tumor calcification, radiotherapy was deferred and reserved for potential future recurrence.

## Conclusion

This case represents the first reported instance of a meningioma completely encircling the thoracic spinal cord. Optimal management of this rare condition remains uncertain. Achieving symptomatic relief through spinal cord decompression is a primary goal. While complete resection is desirable, it is often difficult to achieve in circumferential lesions; therefore, long-term surveillance with serial imaging and follow-up is required.
