# Electrochemotherapy to Metastatic Spinal Melanoma  
A Novel Treatment of Spinal Metastasis?

## Abstract
Electrochemotherapy (ECT) is a novel antitumor treatment that combines systemic bleomycin with electric pulses delivered locally at the tumor site. These electric pulses permeabilize cell membranes, allowing bleomycin to enter cells and increasing its cytotoxicity. This report evaluates the effectiveness of electrochemotherapy as a treatment for metastatic spinal melanoma. Clinical outcomes suggest that electrochemotherapy could be an effective, minimally invasive treatment for spinal metastasis, with no significant adverse effects reported.

## Introduction
Spinal metastases are the most frequent tumors of bone, and treating spinal tumors presents a significant challenge. While surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy are common treatment options, these interventions often come with substantial risks and side effects. Electrochemotherapy, which combines chemotherapy and electric pulses, offers a promising alternative that minimizes tissue damage and improves drug delivery. This case report discusses the use of ECT to treat metastatic melanoma in the spine.

## Materials and Methods

### Clinical Data of the Patient
A 51-year-old female with a history of nasal malignant melanoma and subsequent recurrences presented with low back pain. MRI revealed a lesion at the L5 level, and biopsy confirmed metastatic melanoma. The patient’s Karnofsky performance score was 80%. ECT was offered as a palliative treatment to prevent rapid disease progression.

### Operation
Under general anesthesia, a left L5 laminectomy was performed. Four needle electrodes were positioned around the L5 tumor under fluoroscopic and neuronavigator control. Systemic bleomycin was administered intravenously prior to electrical pulse delivery.

### Electrodes, Pulse Generator and Pulse Parameters
Four custom-made stainless steel electrodes were used, connected to the Cliniporator Vitae pulse generator. Electrical pulses of 3000 V and 50 A were delivered at a frequency of 4 Hz. Pulse duration was 100 μs, with 8 pulses delivered after the intravenous administration of bleomycin.

### Electric Field Monitoring
An oscilloscope and current monitor were used to ensure proper delivery of pulses to the tumor site.

## Results

### Tolerance of ECT
The patient tolerated the procedure well, with no significant changes in hemodynamic or cardiologic parameters. Mild back muscle contractions were noted after each pulse, but no adverse side effects occurred. The patient was discharged with only mild pain at the surgical site.

### Clinical Outcomes
Follow-up over 48 months showed significant improvement in pain (VAS from 10 preoperatively to 0 postoperatively) and disability (ODI from 18 to 3). The Karnofsky performance score improved from 80% to 100%. Histological examination post-treatment showed over 95% necrosis of the tumor.

## Discussion
Spinal metastases, especially from melanoma, present a serious challenge in oncology. While conventional treatments are often effective, they come with risks and side effects. Electrochemotherapy offers a less invasive alternative that improves local drug delivery. In this case, electrochemotherapy successfully treated metastatic spinal melanoma, with good clinical and histological outcomes.

Electrochemotherapy works by using electric pulses to temporarily permeabilize cell membranes, allowing chemotherapeutic agents like bleomycin to enter the cells and increase their cytotoxic effects. This technique has shown promise in treating various tumors, including melanoma. Despite the challenges in understanding the molecular mechanisms, electrochemotherapy has demonstrated high efficacy, with responses seen in up to 90% of cases for certain tumor types.

This study presents the first reported clinical case using electrochemotherapy to treat metastatic spinal melanoma. The outcomes suggest that electrochemotherapy may serve as a viable treatment for spinal metastasis, offering good efficacy with minimal side effects.

## Conclusion
Electrochemotherapy is a promising treatment for metastatic spinal melanoma, with significant potential as an adjuvant therapy for spinal metastasis, regardless of histological type. The results of this case suggest that electrochemotherapy should be considered as an alternative to more invasive treatments. Larger, well-designed clinical trials are necessary to confirm the long-term effectiveness of this technique.

## Key Points
- More than 90% of spinal tumors are metastatic, and treating them remains a challenge.
- Electrochemotherapy (ECT) uses electrical pulses to enhance the penetration of cytotoxic drugs like bleomycin into cancer cells.
- ECT may represent a novel and adjuvant treatment for spinal metastasis, regardless of histological type.
- The first reported case of metastatic spinal melanoma treated with electrochemotherapy shows promising results.
