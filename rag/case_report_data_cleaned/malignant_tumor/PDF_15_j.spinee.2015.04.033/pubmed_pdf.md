# IMAGES OF SPINE CARE

## Intraspinal Neuroblastoma

A 2-year-old girl was admitted because of being unable to walk and urinate for the last 2 days. Her medical history was unremarkable. The neurologic examination revealed hyperactive deep tendon reflexes, clonus, and decreased muscle strength of the lower extremities (grade 2 of 5). The spinal magnetic resonance imaging acquired to uncover possible spinal cord pathology showed a solid epidural mass located at the level of T4-T6 vertebrae, with a size of $7 \times 17 \times 36$ mm. The lesion was heterogeneous and hyperintense on T2-weighted images compared with the spinal cord (Fig. 1) and had a hyperintense rim surrounding it, which was considered hemorrhage on T1-weighted images (Fig. 2). The spinal cord was compressed and displaced forward by the lesion. At the level of T1-T8 vertebrae, there was expansion and T2 hyperintensity consistent with vasogenic edema. The spinous process of the T5 vertebra was invaded. The serum level of neuron-specific enolase was positive at 63.5 ng/mL. The bone marrow aspiration revealed uniform cells with large nuclei and thin cytoplasm, forming rosette formation. 

T5-T9 laminectomy and total resection of the lesion were performed. On histopathology, the lesion was confirmed as neuroblastoma. After surgery, the patient started to walk, and the loss of strength in the lower extremities improved. Immediately, chemotherapy was initiated. A control spinal magnetic resonance imaging acquired 3 months later after the surgery showed neither recurrence nor residue, but at the level of T4–T6, the spinal cord was thin and hyperintense on T2-weighted images, consistent with myelomalacia (Fig. 3). Currently, the patient is undergoing chemotherapy, and autologous hematopoietic stem transplantation is being considered.

### Figure 1
T1-weighted magnetic resonance image revealed an epidural mass at the level of T4-T6 vertebrae (white arrows). The lesion had a hyperintense rim considered to be hemorrhage (black arrow). Note the invasion and expansion of the spinous process of the T5 vertebra (asterisk).

### Figure 2
The lesion was heterogeneous and hyperintense on T2-weighted magnetic resonance images (black arrow), and the spinal cord had vasogenic edema at the level of T1-T8 (white arrows).

### Figure 3
T2-weighted image acquired 3 months later after surgery revealed no recurrence or residue, but myelomalacia of the spinal cord (arrows).
