# Cervical Subtotal En-bloc Spondylectomy of C6 Mesenchymal Chondrosarcoma

## Abstract

We present a case of C6 mesenchymal chondrosarcoma and discuss safe posterior to anterior approach subtotal en-bloc spondylectomy. A 29-year-old male consulted for severe posterior neck pain while exercising. CT scan demonstrated a primary osteolytic lesion on the left transverse foramen of C6, and MRI revealed tumor involvement of the C6 vertebra, encasing the left vertebral artery. Preoperative embolization of the left vertebral artery and tumor feeding arteries was performed. The operation involved posterior laminectomy from C5-C7, nerve root sacrifice, posterior disc removal, anterior discectomy, C6 subtotal spondylectomy with en-bloc resection of the mass, and C5-C7 anterior plate fixation. Postoperative recovery showed no recurrence of the tumor and no progression of neurologic symptoms up to one year of follow-up.

## Case Presentation

A 29-year-old male presented with severe posterior neck pain following exercise. Despite two months of medical and physical therapy, the pain persisted. Radiographs, CT scans, and MRIs confirmed the presence of a bony mass at C6. A biopsy under C-arm guidance diagnosed the tumor as mesenchymal chondrosarcoma. PET-CT revealed no distant metastasis, leading to the decision for a C6 subtotal en-bloc spondylectomy with sacrifice of the left vertebral artery, C6 root, and C7 root through both posterior and anterior surgical approaches.

## Diagnostic Imaging

Plain radiographs showed nonspecific features, but CT scans revealed an osteolytic lesion at the C6 left lateral mass and transverse process. MRI demonstrated a mass surrounding the vertebral foramen of C6, invading layers B, C, and F, sectors 4-6, with no cord compression.

## Historical Review

Chondrosarcoma, the second most common sarcoma of the skeleton after osteosarcoma, is less commonly found in the spine (4-10%) compared to the long bones, with mesenchymal chondrosarcoma being an extremely rare variant. It can occur in both bone and soft tissue, with reports of its presence in the maxilla, mandible, and CNS. Prognosis largely depends on histologic features, patient age, and the ability to perform an en-bloc resection with appropriate oncologic margins. The proximity to neurovascular structures complicates en-bloc resection in the spine, especially in the cervical region, which has a poor prognosis. However, total en-bloc spondylectomy is one of the most effective surgical techniques for spinal tumors.

## Rationale for Treatment and Evidence-Based Literature

This case presents cervical mesenchymal chondrosarcoma, classified as Enneking stage IIB. En-bloc resection in the cervical spine is challenging due to the complex anatomy, including vertebral arteries and cervical nerve roots. While en-bloc resection can be performed in cases where the tumor is confined to the posterior elements or the anterior and posterior columns are not both invaded, technical difficulties and the risk of significant neurologic deficit remain. Previous studies report successful en-bloc resections in cases of cervical chordomas and other tumors, but such procedures are rare and technically demanding. In this case, subtotal en-bloc resection was performed with posterior and anterior approaches, achieving an adequate tumor-free margin.

## Procedure

Preoperative neurointerventional radiology occluded the left vertebral artery and tumor feeding arteries using coil embolization to minimize bleeding. The surgical approach began with a posterior procedure, including C5-C7 laminectomy, C5 and C6 nerve root exposure, and root sacrifice. The anterior approach involved discectomy, C6 subtotal spondylectomy, and resection of the left vertebral artery.

## Outcome

Postoperative neurologic examination showed numbness from the left thumb to the medial forearm, but the remaining muscle strength was normal. Follow-up radiographs confirmed complete tumor resection and no joint instability. No recurrence or progression of neurological symptoms was observed at the one-year follow-up.
