# Neurofibrosarcoma of the Lumbar Spine With a Misleading Onset

## Case Report

A 40-year-old man presented with a history of slowly progressive pain localized to the medial surface of the right knee and hyposthenia of the right quadriceps muscle. The patient had previously been treated with an arthroscopic meniscectomy with no clinical improvement. Persistence of the symptoms led to a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) study of the lumbar spine, which revealed a large neoplasm arising from the foraminal portion of the L3 nerve root and extending into the right psoas muscle.

Further preoperative investigations, including angio-computed tomography, MRI, and electromyography, were performed. As the tumor appeared solitary, without evidence of metastatic spread or bony involvement, a radical surgical excision was undertaken. Postoperative MRI confirmed complete resection of the lesion. Histopathological examination established the final diagnosis of neurofibrosarcoma, also known as malignant schwannoma or malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor [1–7].

Following surgery, the patient was referred to the oncology department and immediately commenced on combined chemotherapy and radiotherapy.
