# A rare cause of spinal mass: Primary intramedullary spinal cord lymphoma

## Abstract

A 50-year-old female patient was admitted to our hospital with increasing back pain, weakness in the legs, and walking and urinating disorders. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a solid lesion in the right side of the T7 and T8 segments of the spinal cord, measuring 20 x 6 mm, located intramedullary. The lesion was isointense on T1-weighted images (WI), hyperintense on T2WI, and showed homogeneous enhancement on T1W contrast-enhanced (CE) series. The patient underwent surgical operation, and the lesion was confirmed histopathologically as diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. The probability of secondary lymphoma involving the spinal cord was excluded with abdominal and chest tomography.

Primary intramedullary spinal cord lymphoma (PISCL) is a rare disease, constituting only 1% of central nervous system lymphomas. It typically develops in the 4th and 5th decades and is more common in women than men. Risk factors include AIDS, organ transplantation, congenital immune deficiency syndromes, and Epstein-Barr virus. Clinical findings include pain, sensory, and motor disorders in the extremities. Non-Hodgkin lymphomas constitute 85% of all cases, with most being B-cell lymphomas. PISCL usually presents as a solitary lesion, often in the upper thoracic or lower cervical cord, and rarely in the lumbar segments. Conventional MRI findings can mimic demyelinating diseases. PISCL is isointense on T1WI and hyperintense on T2WI, unlike intracranial lymphomas. Homogeneous enhancement on T1 CE series is typically observed. Intramedullary spinal cord lymphoma, though rare, must be considered in the differential diagnosis of intramedullary spinal masses.

## Introduction

Primary intramedullary spinal cord lymphoma (PISCL) is an extremely rare condition, accounting for a small percentage of central nervous system lymphomas. Despite its rarity, it is important to consider PISCL in the differential diagnosis of intramedullary spinal masses. The pathophysiology, clinical presentation, and imaging characteristics of PISCL are crucial for early diagnosis and appropriate treatment.

## Case Report

A 50-year-old female patient presented to the hospital with complaints of increasing back pain, weakness in the legs, and difficulty with walking and urination. MRI revealed a solid intramedullary lesion located at the T7 and T8 spinal cord segments. The lesion was 20 x 6 mm in size, isointense on T1WI, hyperintense on T2WI, and demonstrated homogeneous enhancement on post-contrast T1WI. Histopathological examination confirmed the lesion as diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. Further imaging studies, including abdominal and chest tomography, excluded the possibility of secondary lymphoma involvement.

## Discussion

PISCL is a rare form of lymphoma that primarily affects the spinal cord. It is most commonly diagnosed in the 4th and 5th decades of life and occurs more frequently in women. The clinical presentation often includes back pain, motor and sensory deficits in the limbs, and other neurological symptoms. Imaging studies, particularly MRI, are critical for diagnosis, as PISCL can mimic other conditions such as demyelinating diseases. The treatment of choice typically involves surgical resection and chemotherapy, although the prognosis can vary depending on the extent of the disease and the patient's overall health.

## Conclusion

Primary intramedullary spinal cord lymphoma is a rare but important diagnosis to consider when evaluating patients with spinal masses. Early diagnosis through imaging and histopathological confirmation is essential for proper management and treatment planning. The condition requires a multidisciplinary approach, including neurosurgical intervention and oncological treatment.
