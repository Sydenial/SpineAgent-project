# IMAGES OF SPINE CARE

## Pelvic Ewing sarcoma: a radiologic and histopathologic correlation

An otherwise healthy 22-year-old service member developed an insidious onset of new right-sided sacroiliac (SI) joint and radicular pain of the lower extremity while deployed abroad. Upon return to the United States, the patient noted 40 lb of unintentional weight loss and progressive swelling of the right lower extremity. Initial radiographs displayed a pelvic lytic lesion with SI joint erosion. Computed tomography further demonstrated the destructive SI joint mass. Magnetic resonance imaging displayed a large heterogeneously enhancing soft tissue mass with areas of soft tissue necrosis. A computed tomography biopsy returned CD99 and FLI-1 positive, and negative for pankeratin, CD56, synaptophysin, CD45, leukocyte common antigen, CD3, and CD20. Fluorescent in situ hybridization was positive for EWSR1 rearrangement.

After completion of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (vincristine sulfate, cyclophosphamide, topotecan hydrochloride), the patient underwent right hemipelvectomy with partial sacrectomy (95% tumor necrosis noted). Microscopic sections revealed findings that were consistent with post-neoadjuvant Ewing sarcoma, with some perivascular sparing. The patient has successfully completed adjuvant chemotherapy and is scheduled for interval pelvic reconstruction. As typical in patients presenting with Ewing sarcoma after the second decade of life, the patient's initial symptoms were insidious harbingers of a pelvic mass.
