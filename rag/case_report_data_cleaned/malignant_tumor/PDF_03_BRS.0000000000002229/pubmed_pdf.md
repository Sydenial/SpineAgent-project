# Multi-level 3D Printing Implant for Reconstructing Cervical Spine with Metastatic Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma

## Abstract

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is a malignant neoplasm with a relatively favorable prognosis among all subtypes. Three-dimensional (3D) printing, an additive manufacturing technology, is the process of manufacturing products by accurately layering material under digital model control via a computer. Reported herein is the case of metastatic papillary thyroid carcinoma in the cervical spine (C2–C4) of a middle-aged female patient who underwent a spondylectomy of C2 to C4, which were reconstructed with 3D-printed cervical vertebrae.

## Introduction

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is a malignant tumor that often metastasizes to the cervical spine. The use of 3D printing for spinal reconstruction, specifically for multi-level cervical spine surgery, is still rare in the literature. This case report discusses the use of a personalized 3D-printed implant to reconstruct the cervical vertebrae after metastatic PTC destruction.

## Case Report

A 53-year-old female patient complained of neck and upper-extremity pain, dysphagia, and thumb and index finger paralysis. Muscle strength had deteriorated significantly. Imaging studies, including X-ray, CT, and MRI, revealed a large lesion involving the C2–C4 vertebrae. A biopsy confirmed the diagnosis of papillary thyroid carcinoma. The patient's neurological function was rated 8/17 on the Japanese Orthopedic Association (JOA) scale, and the decision was made to proceed with radical resection and reconstruction.

## Methods

### Surgical Design

A unique surgical design was employed for this case, using a personalized 3D-printed implant. The internal fixation system was based on a cervicalero-profile screws system, and a computer-assisted design (CAD) model was used to refine the screw positioning and ensure security. The implant was made of Ti6AL4V titanium, and the internal porous structure mimicked bone trabecular design to balance bone growth and biomechanical properties.

### Personalized Surgical Plan

Radical resection of the thyroid gland was performed first, followed by the removal of the metastatic lesion in C2, C3, and C4 vertebrae. The customized implant was then inserted, and lateral mass screw fixation was performed via a posterior approach.

## Results

The thyroid gland and 21 bilaterally cleaned lymph nodes were resected. A grey-red tissue mass (4 cm × 2.5 cm × 1.5 cm) was excised from the cervical vertebrae (C2–C4). After surgery, the patient remained ventilator-dependent and was transferred to the intensive care unit. Post-operative complications included unilateral vocal cord paralysis and weakness of the laryngeal muscles, which contributed to difficulty in expectoration. The patient was weaned from mechanical ventilation and returned to the orthopedic ward on post-operative day (POD) 5. By POD 11, the patient scored 11/17 on the JOA scale and was ambulating unassisted with a personalized skull–neck–thorax orthosis.

## Discussion

The favorable prognosis of thyroid carcinoma contributed to the decision for surgery and the positive post-operative outcome despite the Stage IV tumor node metastasis (TNM) classification. Conventional titanium mesh cages (TMC) were not suitable for the reconstruction due to the irregular shape of the cervical vertebrae and the risk of subsidence. 3D printing technology enabled the production of personalized implants with optimal mechanical and biological properties, including biocompatibility and osteogenic activity, which helped reduce the risk of implant subsidence.

The use of 3D printing in this case provided a tailored solution to an otherwise complex and unconventional problem in spinal surgery. However, challenges remain, such as the high-density shadow caused by the printed implant, which may obscure the observation of bone growth.

## Conclusion

This case demonstrates the successful application of 3D printing technology in spinal reconstruction for a patient with metastatic papillary thyroid carcinoma. The personalized implant facilitated the restoration of cervical spine stability and function, highlighting the potential of 3D printing in spinal surgery.
