# What are MRI findings of Spine Benign Metastasizing Leiomyoma? Case report with literature review

## Abstract

Benign Metastasizing Leiomyoma (BML) is a rare disease resulting from the metastasis of uterine leiomyoma to distant sites, displaying benign pathologic features. Spine BML is exceptionally rare, and there is limited information about its features and pathophysiology. We present a case of a 42-year-old woman who experienced right buttock and leg pain with paresthesia. MRI of the lumbar spine revealed a well-circumscribed mass in the posterior compartment of the L4 vertebral body, extending into the ventral epidural space and both foramina. The mass showed hypointensity on T1 and T2-weighted images and strong homogeneous enhancement on gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted images. Tumor removal was performed, and biopsy revealed the mass to be leiomyoma. A review of nine previous spine BML cases was conducted, revealing common clinical and MRI findings. Premenopausal women with a history of uterine myoma and myomectomy/hysterectomy were identified as predisposed to this condition. MRI findings in spine BML typically include low T1 and T2 signal intensities with strong homogeneous enhancement.

## Introduction

Benign Metastasizing Leiomyoma (BML) is a rare condition where uterine leiomyoma metastasizes to distant sites with benign pathological features. The most common sites of BML include the lung, skin, trachea, bladder, esophagus, liver, adrenal glands, skull base, and spine, often associated with a history of hysterectomy or myomectomy. The literature on spine BML is fragmented, making it difficult to suspect the diagnosis. This case presents spine BML and compares its clinical and radiologic features with previous reports.

## Case report

A 42-year-old woman presented with right buttock and leg pain with paresthesia, which had started 2 years earlier and worsened a week prior. MRI of the lumbar spine revealed a well-circumscribed mass in the posterior compartment of the L4 vertebral body, extending into the ventral epidural space. The lesion showed hypointensity on both T1- and T2-weighted images and strong homogeneous enhancement on gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted images. An osteolytic lesion was observed on the posterior aspect of the L4 vertebral body, extending into the spinal canal and both neural foramina. The patient had a history of uterine myomectomy 14 years prior but no history of malignancy. A whole-body Positron Emission Tomography (PET) scan was performed, showing no hypermetabolic activity, consistent with a benign lesion. Surgery was performed to remove the mass, and a frozen biopsy confirmed the lesion as a benign spindle cell tumor. Permanent pathology confirmed the diagnosis of benign metastasizing leiomyoma (BML).

## Discussion

Spine BML is difficult to diagnose before pathological confirmation due to its rarity. We reviewed nine previous spine BML reports and found common clinical features, such as a history of myomectomy/hysterectomy and a premenopausal age group. The mass typically involves the posterior element of the vertebral body, with bony destruction, neural foramen invasion, and canal encroachment. MRI findings commonly show low signal intensity on both T1 and T2-weighted images, with strong homogeneous enhancement on post-gadolinium T1-weighted images. The metastatic mechanism for BML is thought to involve the pelvic venous plexus and IVC route, which can spread to the lungs, spine, and other sites.

## Conclusion

This case report contributes to the understanding of spine BML by reviewing clinical and MRI findings. Spine surgeons should consider the possibility of spine BML in patients with typical clinical history and MRI findings, including a well-circumscribed mass on the posterior vertebral body with bony destruction, neural foramen invasion, and canal encroachment. Further clinical and imaging data collection, along with pathophysiological studies, are necessary for a more comprehensive understanding of this rare condition.
