# Giant Lumbosacral Ependymoma

## Abstract

A 52-year-old man presented with a 10-year history of low back pain. Two months before presentation, he developed bladder dysfunction. On examination, there was a reduced range of movement in the lumbar spine without other neurologic deficits. CT images of the lumbosacral spine showed lysis of the entire sacrum. Magnetic resonance (MR) imaging demonstrated a giant lumbosacral mass. Due to the extent of bone destruction, only decompression and subtotal removal of the tumor could be performed, and the patient was referred for local radiotherapy. Histological findings from the tissue removed during surgery showed myxopapillary ependymoma with a positive margin. At the last follow-up, the patient had no low back pain and had resumed full activities with normal bowel, bladder, and sexual function.

## Introduction

Myxopapillary ependymomas are slow-growing tumors that can attain very large sizes before clinical detection, often filling and expanding the lumbosacral canal and causing significant osteolytic destruction of the sacrum. Early, complete resection of myxopapillary ependymomas is associated with an excellent prognosis for long-term disease control. Close postoperative MR imaging monitoring of the surgical site is required, and radiation therapy is necessary for patients with incomplete tumor excision and/or recurrent disease.

## Case Report

A 52-year-old man presented with a 10-year history of low back pain. Two months prior to presentation, he developed bladder dysfunction. Upon examination, there was a reduced range of movement in the lumbar spine without any additional neurologic deficits. CT images of the lumbosacral spine revealed lysis of the entire sacrum. MR imaging demonstrated a large lumbosacral mass. Due to the extent of bone destruction, only decompression and subtotal tumor removal were performed. The patient was subsequently referred for local radiotherapy. Histological findings from the tissue removed during surgery indicated myxopapillary ependymoma with a positive margin. At the last follow-up, the patient reported no low back pain and had resumed full activities with normal bowel, bladder, and sexual function.

## Discussion

Myxopapillary ependymomas are slow-growing tumors, often reaching large sizes before detection. These tumors can significantly affect the sacrum, causing osteolytic destruction. Early detection and complete surgical resection are crucial for positive outcomes. For patients with incomplete resection or recurrent disease, postoperative MR imaging and radiation therapy are essential for disease control.
