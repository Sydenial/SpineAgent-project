# Primary Epidural Lumbar Ewing Sarcoma: Case Report and Review of the Literature

## Abstract

### Study Design
Case report.

### Summary of Background Data
Primary epidural Ewing sarcoma is a very rare entity. The best generally accepted treatment option in sarcomas is to achieve a gross total resection with safe margins followed by local radiotherapy and chemotherapy. A total resection with safe margins is a great challenge in neurosurgical patients.

### Methods
We present a previously healthy 17-year-old girl who complained of right sciatica with an epidural lumbar mass at L3–L4. She underwent complete resection of the tumor and a laminoplasty, which, in our experience, is a good way to preserve stability.

### Results
At surgery, an isolated and noninvasive lesion was identified. Histopathological confirmation of Ewing sarcoma was obtained by immunohistochemical study and EWSR1 gene rearrangement detection. Treatment with 6 months of chemotherapy resulted in no further identifiable lesions by PET and MRI imaging at 4 years postsurgery. The laminoplasty has remained stable.

### Conclusion
Primary epidural Ewing sarcoma is extremely rare. The detection of the EWSR1 gene rearrangement can help to diagnose these tumors. The decision on how to treat these patients is difficult and can hardly be based on data from the current literature because of the small number of patients. Laminoplasty procedures can be safely performed in the setting of sarcoma of the epidural space.

## Introduction

Ewing sarcoma is a primitive neuroectodermal tumor originating in the medullary cavity of the diaphysis of long bones. Initially, the histopathological diagnosis of Ewing sarcoma was based on the finding of small round blue cells on hematoxylin-eosin staining with no morphologic evidence of a cell of origin. Metastases usually affect the lungs, long bones, and bone marrow. As a result, systemic chemotherapy has become an important component of treatment.

## Case Report

A previously healthy 17-year-old girl consulted because of a 2-month history of right sciatica. The neurological examination was normal.

Lumbosacral MRI showed an extradural $6.4 \times 1.8 \times 2$ cm mass at L3–L4. No other primary tumors or metastases were detected. After this finding, the patient was proposed for surgery.

An L3–L4 laminoplasty was performed. At surgery, we found an epidural isolated mass with no visible extension to adjacent bony structures. Complete resection was achieved.

Histopathological examination showed osseous, cartilaginous, and adipose tissue extensively infiltrated by a mitotically active small blue round-cell neoplasm suggestive of Ewing sarcoma. The Ki-67 proliferative index was 40%, confirming the high mitotic rate. EWSR1 gene rearrangement at the 22q12 locus was detected by fluorescence in situ hybridization, confirming the diagnosis.

After surgery, the patient was started on standard adjuvant combination chemotherapy. No radiotherapy was administered.

At 4 years of follow-up, there have been no clinical or radiological evidences of local recurrence or metastases. The patient has also achieved a good stabilization of the spine.

## Discussion

Ewing sarcoma is the most frequent type of malignant bone tumor in children younger than 10 years, with the highest incidence in the second decade of life.

Patients with Ewing sarcoma typically present with localized pain or swelling of a few weeks or months of duration. Around 70% of cases present with neurological deficit; our patient did not. An association with 18q− syndrome has been reported, but it is exceptional.

The best generally accepted treatment option in sarcomas is to achieve a gross total resection with safe margins, followed by local radiotherapy and chemotherapy. A total resection with safe margins is a major challenge in neurosurgical patients. Studies have shown that patients with axial primary tumors have a worse outcome after treatment than patients with limb lesions. Although some authors have suggested that management should rely mainly on radiotherapy and chemotherapy, gross total en bloc resection has become essential for prognosis.

We consider that spinal radiotherapy should be reserved for residual lesions or tumors where surgery is not feasible, especially given the risk of neurologic sequelae from spinal cord damage.

It is presumed that most patients will have subclinical metastatic disease at the time of diagnosis, even in the absence of overt metastases. Therefore, modern treatment plans include chemotherapy.

Laminoplasty can be questioned in oncologic surgery; however, given the unexpected diagnosis, the clear plane separating the tumor from the bone, and the need to preserve spinal stability in young patients, we consider it a valid option.

Prognosis may vary according to the spinal level of origin. Lumbosacral lesions might have a worse prognosis due to later diagnosis. Despite its lumbosacral origin, our case showed a favorable outcome, similar to other reported cases managed early and aggressively.

To our knowledge, this is the only reported case in which EWSR1 gene rearrangement was used to establish the diagnosis of a primary epidural Ewing sarcoma. Ancillary molecular techniques are essential for confirming the diagnosis of such rare tumors.

## Conclusion

Children and young adults presenting with an epidural mass should be evaluated for possible primary epidural Ewing sarcoma. Detection of EWSR1 gene rearrangement is helpful for diagnosis. Treatment decisions remain challenging due to the low number of reported cases, but gross total resection combined with chemotherapy and spinal stabilization appears to be a reasonable approach.

## Key Points

- Primary epidural Ewing sarcoma without bone involvement is a very rare entity.
- Standard sarcoma management includes gross total resection with safe margins, which is particularly challenging in neurological regions.
- Lumbosacral epidural Ewing sarcomas may have a worse prognosis due to delayed diagnosis.
- Detection of EWSR1 gene rearrangement can help confirm the diagnosis.
- Laminoplasty can be safely performed in the setting of epidural space sarcoma.
