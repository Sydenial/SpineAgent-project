# Minimally invasive resection of extradural dumbbell tumors of thoracic spine: surgical techniques and literature review

## Abstract

**Purpose**: Dumbbell-shaped thoracic tumors represent a distinct type of tumor and involve both the spinal canal and the posterior thoracic cavity. Successful treatment for these tumors depends on gross total resection (GTR) via an open laminectomy, facetectomy, or transthoracic transpleural approach. In this case series, we report our experience with a minimally invasive method for the removal of extradural dumbbell thoracic tumors and present a related literature review.  
**Methods**: We retrospectively reviewed two patients with dumbbell-shaped thoracic tumors who underwent minimally invasive resection and unilateral transforaminal thoracic intervertebral fusion (TTIF) through a unilateral paraspinal muscle approach with a spotlight expandable tubular retractor. Clinical data, tumor characteristics, and outcomes were analyzed.  
**Results**: Two patients underwent successful minimally invasive treatment of their spinal neoplasms. There were no procedure-related complications. The efficacy in terms of neurological recovery, pain improvement, and operative variables (length of incision, operative duration, blood loss, and hospital stay) was better compared with prior published studies. Postoperative CT images demonstrated complete resection of the dumbbell tumor in both patients. Solid fusion was obtained after 3 months of follow-up, and there was no failure of internal fixation.  
**Conclusion**: If the medial border of the intracanal component of an extradural dumbbell tumor is near the midline of the canal and the pedicles of adjacent vertebrae are intact, minimally invasive resection of the tumor through a unilateral paraspinal muscle approach combined with unilateral TTIF is a good choice.

**Keywords**: Dumbbell-shaped tumor, Minimally invasive surgery, Pedicle screw fixation, Transforaminal thoracic intervertebral fusion, Surgical techniques.

## Introduction

Spinal dumbbell-shaped tumors are characterized by growing both inside and outside of the spinal canal through a connection via the foramen. Approximately 90% of these are nerve sheath tumors (NST) arising from the spinal nerve sheath, with neurofibromas and schwannomas making up the majority. The NSTs are most often seen in the cervical or lumbar regions, followed by the thoracic region. Typically, they are found on the dorsal sensory roots. More than half of these lesions are extramedullary intradural, about 25% are completely extradural, and a small percentage (15%) are both intra- and extradural. Very rarely, they are seen intramedullary (<1%).  
While most spinal NSTs (>90%) are benign, they can compress nerve roots and the spinal cord, leading to severe neurological symptoms, including pain or neurological deficits.  

Conventional gross total resection (GTR) for these lesions typically involves posterior, combined posterior and anterior, posterolateral, or transthoracic transpleural approaches. However, these operations have significant morbidity related to the surgical approach, potential blood loss, and extensive dissection. The risk of postoperative complications, such as injury to paraspinal tissues, is also a concern. A safe operative approach is crucial for successful resection of these tumors. The thoracolumbar paraspinal sacrospinalis muscle-splitting approach offers good exposure for the foramen and unilateral lamina. Recent studies have suggested that minimally invasive resection using expandable tubular retractors through the paraspinal muscle can yield satisfactory results.

The treatment of thoracic dumbbell tumors, particularly those located in the upper thoracic region, presents unique challenges that are not commonly encountered in other regions of the vertebral column. Due to their rarity, treatment for thoracic dumbbell tumors has not been methodically addressed in the literature. This report highlights the feasibility, safety, and efficacy of minimally invasive tumor resection and unilateral transforaminal thoracic intervertebral fusion (TTIF) via a unilateral paraspinal muscle approach with an expandable tubular retractor.

## Material and Method

## Case Report

### Case 1

A 46-year-old woman presented with a 2-year history of chest pain, stiffness, zonesthesia, and dyspnea, which were unresponsive to medical therapy. Neurological examination revealed no sensory loss but weak muscle strength in both lower limbs. MRI revealed an expansive, well-circumscribed, encapsulated dumbbell-shaped extradural lesion (35 × 25 × 15 mm) at the right T9-T10 level. The mass showed hyper-signal on T2-weighted images and hypo-signal on T1-weighted images. The tumor had both intracanal, foraminal, and extraspinal components, with no infiltrative or invasive lesion seen.

### Case 2

An 18-year-old male presented with chest pain, zonesthesia, and walking instability for 6 weeks, and dysuria for 2 days. Weak muscle strength was noted in both lower limbs, and there was sensory loss below the xiphoid. MRI showed an expansive extradural dumbbell-shaped tumor (45 × 35 × 35 mm) at the right T5-T6 level, with high signal intensity on T2-weighted images and isointensity on T1-weighted images. Neurological elements were compressed by the intracanal component of the tumor.

Inclusion criteria for both cases included: the medial border of the intracanal component being near the midline of the canal on MRI, and the pedicles of adjacent vertebrae being intact on CT.

## Operative Technique

Both patients underwent surgery using a minimally invasive approach with an expandable tubular retractor. The senior author (Y.T.G.) performed the operations. Patient consent and clinical data were obtained under the institutional review board guidelines.

### Anesthesia and Positioning

After sedation and endotracheal intubation, the patient was positioned prone on a radio-lucent operating table. An arterial line was inserted for blood pressure monitoring. The posterior thoracolumbar area was sterilized and draped after the skin was dried.

### Surgical Procedure

The precise location of the tumor was confirmed using a K-wire under anteroposterior intraoperative fluoroscopy. A 30-mm-long paramedian skin incision was made 2 cm lateral to the midline, allowing access to the ipsilateral thoracic extraforaminal space. The thoracic fascia was incised parallel to the skin incision, and a muscle-splitting approach was used to expose the unilateral lamina, superior articular facet, and transverse process of the involved vertebrae. Pedicle entry sites were located at the junction of the lateral border of the superior articular facet and the upper third of the transverse process.

Expandable tubular retractors were used to access the tumor and remove the hemilamina, facet joint, transverse process, and rib. Gross total resection (GTR) of the tumor was performed with attention to preserving the nerve root. The pleura was repaired if it was breached. A cage filled with autograft bone was inserted transforaminally into the intervertebral space after the disc and endplate cartilage were removed. Pedicle screws were placed through the paraspinal approach, and a rod was fixed over the screws to stabilize the spine.

## Clinical Follow-up

Both patients were assessed immediately after surgery, and at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months postoperatively. Pain intensity was measured using visual analog scale (VAS) scores. Neurological function was evaluated using the ASIA impairment scale.

## Result

Gross total resection of the dumbbell thoracic tumors was achieved without sacrificing any nerve root. The postoperative CT and X-ray images confirmed complete tumor removal and proper placement of the screws and cage. Solid fusion was achieved after 3 months, with no failure of internal fixation.

### Case 1

The length of the incision was 30 mm, with an operative duration of 150 minutes. Blood loss was 200 ml, and there were no complications. The patient showed complete resolution of chest stiffness, zonesthesia, and dyspnea. The VAS score decreased from 7 preoperatively to 2 immediately after surgery, and to 0 at 1-year follow-up. Histopathological analysis confirmed the tumor was a benign ganglioneuroma.

### Case 2

The incision length was 32 mm, and the operation lasted 170 minutes with blood loss of 250 ml. There was no need for closed thoracic drainage. The VAS score improved from 6 preoperatively to 1 immediately after surgery, and to 0 at 1-year follow-up. Histopathological evaluation revealed schwannomas.

## Discussion

Dumbbell-shaped thoracic tumors have unique anatomical challenges due to their involvement in both the spinal canal and posterior thoracic cavity. Conventional resection methods often result in significant morbidity, including spinal instability and neurological deficits. Minimally invasive techniques, using expandable tubular retractors, have shown promise in reducing tissue destruction, blood loss, and recovery time.  

In our study, we successfully performed minimally invasive resection of dumbbell thoracic tumors combined with unilateral TTIF. This approach offers several advantages, including reduced surgical trauma, shorter hospital stays, and improved postoperative recovery. The technique also minimizes risks associated with traditional approaches, such as nerve root sacrifice and spinal instability. However, further studies with larger sample sizes and comparison to traditional methods are necessary to confirm the long-term benefits of this technique.

## Conclusion

Minimally invasive resection of extradural dumbbell tumors through a unilateral paraspinal muscle approach combined with unilateral TTIF is an effective and safe surgical option when the medial border of the intracanal component is near the midline of the canal and the pedicles of adjacent vertebrae are intact.
