# Disappearance of Lung Adenocarcinoma after Total En Bloc Spondylectomy Using Frozen Tumor-Bearing Vertebra for Reconstruction

## Abstract

**Purpose**  
Total en bloc spondylectomy (TES) is a surgical procedure performed to achieve complete resection of an aggressive benign spinal tumor or a malignant spinal tumor. When reconstructing the spine after resection, we have been using liquid nitrogen-frozen resected spine bearing tumor as a bone graft, expecting an immunological response to tumor-specific antigen(s). The purpose of this article is to report a successful treatment case of lung adenocarcinoma metastasis with TES and this cryotherapy.

**Methods**  
A 59-year-old male presented with rapid progression of neurological deterioration of the lower limbs due to a spinal metastasis from T8 to T10. The primary lung adenocarcinoma had already been excised under thoracoscopy. The patient underwent TES with reconstruction using frozen tumor-bearing vertebra for the bone graft.

**Results**  
One month after surgery, a new nodule appeared at the right middle lobe of the lung. However, no biopsy of the newly emerged nodule was carried out, and the patient received no adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy. Six months after surgery, the tumor vanished. No local recurrence or metastasis of the tumor has been observed to date.

**Conclusions**  
TES with a liquid nitrogen-frozen tumor specimen could be a promising therapeutic option for cancer patients with spine metastasis.

## Introduction

Total en bloc spondylectomy (TES) is a surgical procedure performed to achieve complete resection of an aggressive benign spinal tumor or a malignant spinal tumor [1]. The outcome of this procedure has been reported to be favorable with regard to local control [2–4]. To improve patient survival, a new technique combining TES with cryosurgery has been used in our institution [5,6]. This procedure consists of resection of the vertebral body containing metastatic tumor, freezing of the tumor with liquid nitrogen, and reuse of the treated bone for grafting [7]. This approach is intended to induce tumor-associated cryoimmunology [8].

This surgery was approved by the ethics committee of Kanazawa University. We previously reported a case of metastatic thyroid cancer at T4 in which, after TES with cryosurgery, the serum thyroglobulin level decreased without any adjuvant therapies, indicating activation of antitumor immunity.

We herein present the case of a patient with metastatic lung adenocarcinoma from T8 to T10, whose newly emerged lung metastasis vanished after TES with cryosurgery. Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication of this case.

## Case Report

### History and Presentation

A 59-year-old male with rapidly worsening back pain was admitted to a nearby hospital. Magnetic resonance imaging revealed a spinal tumor involving T8, T9, and T10. Metastatic cancer was suspected, and a detailed systemic evaluation was performed. A lung tumor in the left upper lobe was identified on whole-body CT. The patient underwent excisional biopsy via thoracoscopy, and lung adenocarcinoma was confirmed pathologically.

The patient was referred to our hospital for treatment of the metastatic spinal tumor. During preoperative evaluation, he developed rapid neurological deterioration of the lower limbs, and emergency spinal surgery was performed.

### Operation

En bloc resection of the posterior elements of T8, T9, and T10 was performed using pediculotomy with a thread wire saw. The anterolateral aspects of the vertebral bodies were dissected, and the affected vertebrae were removed after posterior fixation with instrumentation. The excised vertebral bodies were frozen in liquid nitrogen for 20 minutes. The frozen bones were crushed and used as bone graft material for spinal reconstruction along with local bone. In addition, frozen tumor tissue was implanted subcutaneously in the axillary fossa, with the expectation that fragmented tumor tissue would provide tumor-specific antigens.

### Postoperative Course

The patient’s lower limb weakness improved immediately after surgery. One month postoperatively, a new nodule in the right middle lobe of the lung was detected on routine follow-up CT. Retrospective review of preoperative CT revealed that the nodule had already been present and had gradually enlarged over time. Metastasis was strongly suspected, and surgical excision was planned.

Consultation with two respiratory surgeons and a radiologist supported the diagnosis of lung metastasis. Careful observation was continued, and on CT imaging five months later, the lung nodule had completely disappeared. No local recurrence or instrumentation failure has been observed up to one year after surgery.

We routinely measure serum interferon-γ (IFN-γ) and interleukin-12 (IL-12) levels as markers of antitumor immunity before surgery and at 1 and 3 months postoperatively. In this patient, the preoperative IFN-γ level was 8.4 IU/ml and decreased to 5.8 IU/ml at 1 month postoperatively, but increased to 22.2 IU/ml at 3 months. Similarly, IL-12 levels increased from 3.6 pg/ml preoperatively and 3.4 pg/ml at 1 month to 18.1 pg/ml at 3 months postoperatively, suggesting activation of an antitumor immune response.

## Discussion

This report describes a successful case in which metastatic spinal and lung lesions were treated with TES using a liquid nitrogen-frozen tumor specimen. The most remarkable finding was the spontaneous disappearance of a newly emerged lung metastasis without any adjuvant therapy.

The incidence of lung adenocarcinoma is increasing. Although prognosis varies by histological subtype, the most important prognostic factor remains the tumor–node–metastasis stage [9]. Bone metastasis is known to negatively impact survival [10]. However, previous studies have shown favorable outcomes for patients with lung cancer metastasis to the spine treated with TES [11].

Current immunotherapy strategies for lung cancer include enhancing tumor immunogenicity through cancer vaccines and overcoming tumor-associated immunosuppression using immune checkpoint inhibitors [12]. In the present case, cryotherapy was used to induce systemic antitumor immunity by presenting tumor antigens via frozen tumor tissue. We previously reported a similar approach in metastatic thyroid cancer with lung metastases, although that patient also received adjuvant radioiodine therapy [5]. In contrast, the lung metastasis in the present case resolved without any additional treatment.

Cryotherapy as a systemic cancer treatment has gained increasing interest. Experimental studies have shown that cryoablation can induce tumor-specific immune responses and reduce metastasis in animal models [13,14]. However, successful reports in humans remain limited [5,15]. The present case suggests that cryotherapy may have potential as a systemic cancer therapy in humans.

This report has limitations. The pathological diagnosis of the newly emerged lung nodule was not confirmed, and it is possible that the lesion was inflammatory. However, progressive enlargement and multidisciplinary agreement supported the diagnosis of metastasis. In addition, the follow-up period was relatively short, and long-term outcomes remain uncertain.

## Conclusions

We reported a successful case of metastatic spinal tumor with newly emerged lung metastasis treated using TES and cryotherapy. TES with a liquid nitrogen-frozen tumor specimen may represent a promising therapeutic option for patients with spinal metastasis, offering both local tumor control and potential systemic antitumor effects.
