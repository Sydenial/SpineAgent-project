# Spinal Meningioma Causing Cervicothoracic Rotoscoliosis

## Case Report

An 86-year-old woman was admitted to our neurosurgery department with neck and thoracic back pain for 2 years. Neurological examination was normal. Magnetic resonance imaging was performed and demonstrated a meningioma at the cervical spinal cord. In addition, computed tomography showed cervicothoracic rotoscoliosis associated with the spinal meningioma.

## Discussion

Spinal meningiomas are the second most common intradural extramedullary spinal tumors. They can occur at any location throughout the spine, but the thoracic region is the most common site. Symptomatic spinal meningiomas typically present with motor and sensory deficits, pain, and, rarely, sphincter dysfunction.

The presence of a spinal cord tumor can cause rotoscoliosis. This may result from muscle denervation and weakness due to involvement of the anterior horn cells, or from a flexed posture that opens the spinal canal in an attempt to relieve symptoms. Complete surgical resection is performed in the vast majority of patients.
