# Spinal intramedullary metastasis as the first manifestation of lung cancer

## Abstract

A 60-year-old woman was admitted to our hospital with a complaint of back pain, lower limb sensory disturbance, and urinary difficulties of acute onset and rapidly progressive course. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the thoracal spine revealed an intramedullary spinal cord lesion located at the thoracic 11-12 level with enhancement and edema above and below the lesion. Incidentally, a large mass in the upper lobe of the right lung was observed on thoracic MRI. Further radiological evaluations showed no suggesting disease in other organs. Transbronchial needle aspiration confirmed lung adenocarcinoma.

## Introduction

Spinal intramedullary metastasis is a rare manifestation of lung cancer. It is often associated with advanced stages of the disease, with symptoms that can range from vague back pain to more severe neurological deficits. Early diagnosis and treatment are crucial for improving patient outcomes.

## Case Report

### Patient Presentation

A 60-year-old woman presented with acute onset and rapidly progressive back pain, lower limb sensory disturbance, and urinary difficulties. The patient had no significant past medical history.

### Imaging Findings

MRI of the thoracic spine revealed an intramedullary spinal cord lesion located at the T11-12 level, with enhancement and edema surrounding the lesion. A large mass was incidentally detected in the upper lobe of the right lung, raising suspicion for metastatic disease.

### Diagnosis

Transbronchial needle aspiration was performed on the lung mass, confirming the diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma.

## Discussion

Spinal intramedullary metastases are rare, and their detection often occurs at advanced stages of cancer. In this case, the patient's presentation with back pain and neurological symptoms prompted imaging studies that led to the discovery of both spinal and pulmonary lesions. The lung mass was confirmed to be adenocarcinoma, which is known to metastasize to the spine.

## Conclusion

Spinal intramedullary metastasis as the first manifestation of lung cancer is a rare but significant finding. Early identification through imaging and confirmation via biopsy is essential for appropriate treatment and management.
