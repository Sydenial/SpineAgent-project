# Successful Total En Bloc Spondylectomy of T7 Vertebra for Hepatocellular Carcinoma Metastasis

## Abstract

This case report describes a successful total en bloc spondylectomy (TES) for T7 metastasis after living donor liver transplantation (LT) for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Spinal metastasis from HCC has a poor prognosis, and few studies have examined the surgical outcomes. Despite high surgical morbidity and limited life expectancy, TES has been reported to yield satisfactory results in some metastatic spinal tumors. This report highlights the first case of TES for T7 metastasis after LT for HCC.

## Introduction

Spinal metastasis from hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is rare but associated with a poor prognosis. While liver transplantation (LT) remains the curative treatment for early HCC, its recurrence may lead to complications such as spinal metastasis. This case describes the successful treatment of a patient with solitary T7 spinal metastasis after undergoing living donor LT for HCC. 

## Methods

A 65-year-old male patient, who underwent LT for HCC two years prior, presented with progressive gait disturbance due to spinal cord compression at T7. Radiological and pathological examinations confirmed a solitary metastasis at T7. The patient underwent TES using a pedicle screw system and a mesh cage filled with frozen autografts. 

## Results

After surgery, the patient's neurological symptoms improved significantly. At three months post-surgery, a new metastasis at T4 was detected, which was treated with heavy ion radiotherapy. At 1.5 years post-surgery, the patient was able to walk without a cane and showed no evidence of recurrence.

## Case Report

A 65-year-old man, who had undergone living donor LT for HCC three years prior, presented with a 2-month history of back pain and rapid progression of lower extremity paralysis. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a solitary tumor at T7 compressing the spinal cord. The tumor was confirmed to be a metastasis of HCC via biopsy. CT imaging showed low-density areas at T7 with pathological fractures. The patient's PIVKA-I levels were elevated, a known tumor marker for HCC. There was no recurrence of HCC in the liver, and the patient did not have metastasis in other organs.

The patient underwent TES, which involved en bloc laminectomy of T7, insertion of pedicle screws at T5, T6, T8, and T9, and anterior reconstruction with a titanium mesh cage filled with frozen autografts. The operation lasted 5 hours and 10 minutes, with minimal blood loss. The patient's PIVKA-II levels dropped significantly post-surgery but rose again three months later when a metastasis was detected at T4.

## Discussion

The treatment options for spinal metastasis include radiotherapy and surgery. Surgical intervention is particularly effective in cases of neurological deterioration. However, there is debate over whether surgery should be performed in patients with HCC due to their poor prognosis. Previous studies have shown that the prognosis of patients with HCC spinal metastasis is generally poor, with a median survival of only 3 to 6 months. 

The Tomita and Tokuhashi scores, which assess the prognosis of metastatic spine tumors, are often used to guide treatment decisions. In this case, the patient’s Tomita score was 5, indicating that surgery was aimed at providing short- to mid-term palliation rather than a cure. Despite the poor prognosis, the patient had good liver function post-LT and no other metastases, which supported the decision to perform TES.

Several studies have demonstrated that TES can offer survival benefits in selected patients with solitary spinal metastases. This case, which involved a patient with a solitary spinal metastasis from HCC, shows that TES may be a viable option if liver function is intact after LT.

## Key Points

- This is the first reported case of a patient successfully treated with TES for T7 metastasis after living donor LT for HCC.
- Patients with good liver function after LT may be candidates for TES if they develop solitary spinal metastasis.
- The prognosis of patients with spinal metastasis from HCC is typically poor, but surgery may improve quality of life in certain cases.
