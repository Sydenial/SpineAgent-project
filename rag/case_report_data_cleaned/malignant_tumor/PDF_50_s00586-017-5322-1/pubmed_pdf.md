# Recurrent primary osseous hemangiopericytoma in the thoracic spine: a case report and literature review

## Abstract

**Purpose**: Primary osseous hemangiopericytoma (HPC) of the spine is exceedingly rare. HPC has malignant potential and can metastasize or recur locally. We present the first case of recurrent primary osseous HPC in the thoracic spine, successfully treated by total spondylectomy at three vertebral levels and spinal reconstruction.

**Methods**: A two-stage operation was performed for recurrent HPC using both anterior and posterior approaches at the T5–T7 vertebrae. Preoperative embolization of the tumor was done to prevent massive bleeding. Total spondylectomy (T5–T7) was performed to resect the tumor. Anterior spinal reconstruction and posterior instrumentation were performed, with abundant bone autograft and allograft used for sufficient bony fusion.

**Results**: Two years after surgery, the patient made a full recovery from symptoms. Bone union was complete, with no tumor recurrence or implant failure.

**Conclusions**: Total spondylectomy and spinal reconstruction with instrumentation may be useful for safely and adequately excising recurrent HPC of the spine. However, patients should be closely monitored for local recurrence and malignant degeneration after surgery.

**Keywords**: Hemangiopericytoma, Recurrence, Spine Myelopathy, Spondylectomy

## Introduction

Hemangiopericytoma (HPC) typically occurs in various soft tissues, such as subcutaneous tissues, pelvic fossa, retroperitoneum, and occasionally as a meningeal tumor in the cranial cavity. Primary osseous HPC is rare in the skeleton, and among these, primary osseous HPC of the spine is exceedingly uncommon.

Although HPC is a slow-growing tumor with a generally favorable prognosis compared to sarcomas, it has malignant potential and the ability to metastasize and recur locally. The best treatment for HPC is wide en bloc excision. However, in cases of spinal HPC, it is often difficult to perform an adequately wide excision due to the spine's anatomy and the risk of excessive bleeding. Furthermore, scar adhesion from previous surgeries and unclear tumor margins complicate safe excision in recurrent cases. As primary osseous HPC of the spine is rare and resection is difficult, the optimal treatment remains unclear.

This report presents the first case of recurrent osseous HPC of the thoracic spine treated by total spondylectomy at multiple vertebral levels and spinal reconstruction, and reviews the literature on primary osseous HPC of the spine.

## Case Report

### Clinical History

A 25-year-old male presented with numbness in the body trunk and bilateral lower extremities. Imaging revealed a spinal tumor at the T6 vertebral body compressing the spinal cord. He underwent emergency surgery for posterior decompression and tumor resection, but the tumor was not completely removed due to severe hemorrhage (11,000 ml). Histopathological analysis was inconclusive. Postoperatively, the patient's paraplegia improved, but numbness remained in the body trunk and lower extremities.

During follow-up, the residual tumor increased in size and compressed the thoracic spinal cord. At 30 years old, he underwent reoperation with arterial embolization at T5 and T6 levels. A two-stage operation using anterior and posterior approaches was performed. Tumor removal and spinal reconstruction were done via anterior approach, followed by laminectomy and posterior instrumentation. Blood loss was 6000 ml, and symptoms improved, though numbness persisted.

At 43 years old, he was referred to our department for further treatment due to tumor recurrence over a 13-year period.

### Examination

His gait was spastic and slightly unstable, but he could walk without assistance. Sensory examination revealed hypoesthesia in the body trunk and lower extremities. His patellar and Achilles tendon reflexes were exaggerated, but there was no muscle weakness. His neurological diagnosis was recurrent thoracic myelopathy. The modified JOA scale score for thoracic myelopathy was 4/11.

### Imaging Findings

Plain radiographs showed postoperative thoracic spine instrumentation. MRI revealed a recurrent tumor involving vertebrae T5 to T7, with compression of the spinal cord. CT scans showed bony destruction from T5 to T7. Preoperative angiography confirmed arterial supply to the tumor from T5–T7 segmental arteries.

## Operation

Total spondylectomy from T5 to T7 was performed using a two-stage procedure. In the first stage, laminectomy and pediculectomy were performed with implant removal, followed by spinal stabilization using a pedicle screw and rod system. Autograft from the iliac bone and allograft were used for posterior fusion.

In the second stage, vertebrectomy was performed via an anterior approach, followed by anterior reconstruction using a titanium mesh cylindrical cage with autograft and allograft. Blood loss was 1200 ml during each stage.

### Postoperative Course

The postoperative course was uneventful, and the patient's symptoms improved, though slight sensory changes remained. At 6 months post-surgery, CT revealed complete bone union with no implant failure. MRI showed adequate spinal cord decompression at the T6 level, and no recurrence was observed at the 2-year follow-up.

### Histopathological Findings

Histopathology revealed sheets of spindly cells surrounding ectatic capillaries (staghorn vessels). Tumor cells were mildly to deeply stained, with scarce mitotic figures. Immunohistochemistry showed positivity for vimentin and partial positivity for HHF-35 and αSMA. The Ki-67 index was less than 20%. The diagnosis was consistent with HPC.

## Discussion

Primary osseous HPC of the spine is extremely rare, with only 15 reported cases. Recurrent primary osseous HPC has been reported in only two cases. HPC is associated with a high risk of local recurrence unless gross total resection is performed. Prognosis is generally favorable if malignancy is not present.

In our literature review, patients with malignant HPC had worse survival outcomes. In the present case, the tumor was not malignant, and no recurrence or metastasis occurred after total resection in the third surgery.

Recent studies advocate for gross total resection for HPC, correlating with improved long-term survival. A meta-analysis showed a 69% 10-year survival rate for patients undergoing total resection, compared to 44% for subtotal resection. In our case, total resection achieved local control and prevented symptomatic recurrence.

HPC is a highly vascular tumor, making adequate resection difficult due to bleeding risks. Preoperative embolization can reduce intraoperative bleeding, improving the safety of tumor removal. In this case, preoperative embolization was performed in the second and third surgeries, reducing bleeding compared to the initial operation.

Spinal reconstruction after total spondylectomy at multiple levels carries a high risk of implant failure. Proper bone graft preparation and extended posterior fixation can reduce this risk. In this case, abundant autograft and allograft were used to secure spinal fusion, and no implant failure occurred.

## Conclusions

This is the first reported case of recurrent osseous HPC of the thoracic spine successfully treated by total spondylectomy and spinal reconstruction. Total spondylectomy with instrumentation may be effective in excising recurrent HPC and preventing recurrence, but careful postoperative monitoring is required to detect local recurrence or malignant transformation.
