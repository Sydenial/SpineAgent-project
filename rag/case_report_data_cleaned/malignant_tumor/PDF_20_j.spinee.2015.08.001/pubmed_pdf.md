# Endometrial cancer metastasis mimicking spondylodiscitis and psoas abscess

## Case Report

A 51-year-old female patient was admitted to the hospital with lumbago lasting for 3 weeks. Lumbar magnetic resonance imaging was performed and demonstrated a heterogeneous lesion with peripheral enhancement in the right psoas muscle, associated with bone involvement of the lumbar vertebrae. This finding was interpreted as a psoas abscess associated with spondylodiscitis.  

During this period, the patient underwent an endometrial biopsy because of vaginal discharge. Histopathologic examination revealed endometrial cancer. Percutaneous drainage of the presumed psoas abscess was subsequently performed, and histopathologic evaluation of the drained material demonstrated squamous cell carcinoma metastasis. After consultation with radiation therapy and medical oncology services, no additional therapy was recommended.

## Discussion

Endometrial cancer is the most common gynecologic malignancy in the United States and primarily affects postmenopausal women aged 40 years or older. When metastases occur, they most frequently involve the lungs and liver, while skeletal muscle involvement is exceedingly rare. Radiologically and clinically, such metastatic lesions may closely mimic infectious processes such as abscesses, particularly in patients without a previously known primary malignancy.
