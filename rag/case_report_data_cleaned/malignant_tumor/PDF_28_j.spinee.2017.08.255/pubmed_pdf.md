# Role of Intraoperative Radiotherapy in the Treatment of Sacral Chordoma

## Abstract

**Background Context**: Sacral chordoma is a rare entity with high local recurrence rates when complete resection is not achieved. Till date, there are no series available in literature combining surgery and intraoperative radiotherapy (iORT).

**Purpose**: To report the experience of our Centre in the management of sacral chordoma combining surgery and intraoperative radiotherapy (iORT).

**Study Design**: Retrospective case series.

**Patient Sample**: 15 patients with sacral chordoma resected in our centre from 1998 to 2015.

**Outcome Measures**: Overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), and rates of local and distant recurrence.

**Methods**: We retrospectively reviewed the records of all patients with sacral chordoma resected in our centre from 1998 to December 2015. Results between patients treated with or without iORT were compared.

**Results**: A total of 15 patients were identified: 8 males and 7 females. The median age was 59 years (range 28-77). iORT was applied in 9 patients, and 6 were treated with surgical resection without iORT. We observed a median DFS of 85 months in the iORT group, versus 41 months in the non-iORT group (p=0.05).

**Conclusions**: Multidisciplinary management of sacral chordoma seems to improve local control. The use of iORT is associated with improved survival and disease-free survival. Multicenter studies should be performed to confirm these findings.

## Introduction

Chordoma is a rare bone tumor (1-4% of all primary malignant bone tumors) arising from embryonic remnants of the primitive notochord. Its incidence is low, with approximately 0.1 per 100,000 persons per year. The sacrum is the most common location (40-50%), followed by skull base tumors (35-40%) and vertebral bodies (5-15%).

Chordomas are slow-growing and low-grade malignancies, typically presenting in the 5th to 7th decades of life, with a male predominance. They are often characterized by low metastatic potential, though recurrence in the lungs has been noted. The median survival time is approximately 6 years, with a 5-year survival rate of 70%, which drops to 40% at 10 years.

Due to the rarity of this disease, randomized controlled trials are difficult to conduct, and most studies are retrospective with small sample sizes, contributing to low scientific evidence. Surgery remains the cornerstone of treatment, although achieving a complete resection (R0) is challenging, with success rates ranging from 35-75%.

Intraoperative radiotherapy (iORT) has emerged as a potential adjunct, allowing for the delivery of high-dose radiation directly to the tumor bed during surgery, thereby minimizing the impact on surrounding healthy tissues. This technique has been shown to improve local control and survival outcomes for various malignancies.

## Objective and Methods

The aim of this study was to report the experience of our Centre in the management of sacral chordoma, combining radical resection with iORT.

We retrospectively reviewed our database and analyzed all patients with sacral chordoma resected from January 1998 to December 2015. The variables included in the analysis were: age, gender, primary chordoma or recurrence, gluteal involvement, surgical approach (posterior or combined), sacrectomy level (high or low), tumor margins (R0-R2), administration of iORT and/or adjuvant radiotherapy, local and systemic recurrences, disease-free survival (DFS), and overall survival (OS).

The surgical procedure typically involved radical surgery, which could be performed via a posterior approach or a combined anterior and posterior approach if visceral or vascular involvement was suspected. After tumor resection, a boost of radiotherapy was delivered to the tumor bed using a non-dedicated linear accelerator. Since 2013, a portable accelerator (LIAC 12 MeV by SORDINA) has been used in the operating room for iORT.

## Results

We analyzed a total of 15 patients (8 males and 7 females) with sacral chordoma, with a median age of 59 years (range 28-77). Thirteen patients had primary chordomas, and two had local recurrences. High sacrectomy (S1-S2) was performed in 8 patients, and low sacrectomy (S3-S4) was performed in 5 patients.

Sixty percent of the patients (n=9) received iORT, while the remaining patients (n=6) underwent surgery without iORT. The median follow-up period was 38 months (range 11-209 months). Six patients had local recurrence, and two had distal recurrence. The 5-year overall survival (OS) was 83%, and the 5-year disease-free survival (DFS) was 53%.

When comparing the groups treated with and without iORT, the iORT group showed a significantly better 5-year OS (100% vs. 53%, p=0.05). The median DFS in the iORT group was 85 months, compared to 41 months in the non-iORT group.

## Discussion

Surgery remains the primary modality for achieving long-term control of sacral chordoma, although the location of these tumors in the pelvic ring makes en bloc resection technically challenging. In our experience, radical surgery was feasible in nearly half of the cases, with extended resections often required to achieve negative margins.

Intraoperative radiotherapy (iORT) has shown promise in improving survival and local control by delivering high doses of radiation directly to the tumor bed. This method minimizes radiation exposure to surrounding healthy tissues, which is crucial when resecting tumors in the pelvis.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, the management of sacral chordoma in centers with extensive experience in sacropelvic tumors appears to improve local control and survival outcomes. The use of iORT in this context is associated with improved overall survival and disease-free survival. Further multicenter studies are needed to confirm these findings and refine treatment strategies.
