# Extranodal non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma with Multiple Vertebral and Hepatic Lesions without Involvement of Lymph Nodes

## Case Report

A 55-year-old man was admitted to the hospital because of severe lumbago. Laboratory findings, including soluble interleukin-2 receptor levels, were almost normal except for elevated alkaline phosphatase (367 U/L) and lactate dehydrogenase (511 U/L). Computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, and bone scintigraphy demonstrated bone lesions at Th11, Th12, and L4. Because of progressive paralysis of the bilateral lower extremities, the pedicle of Th7 was urgently removed. Multiple masses in the liver were also observed, without splenomegaly or enlarged lymph nodes.

Metastatic cancer to the bone and liver was highly suspected because the patient had no B symptoms associated with aggressive lymphoma, serum chemistry revealed elevated levels of alkaline phosphatase and lactate dehydrogenase that are frequently observed in bone tumors, and no other lesions in lymph nodes or organs were demonstrated. However, histologic examinations of the liver and the pedicle of Th7 led to a diagnosis of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. Bone marrow examination from the right posterior iliac crest revealed no invasion by lymphoma cells.

The patient received six courses of chemotherapy with rituximab. Primary lymphoma of the bone is a rare disease entity among malignant lymphomas. The long bones are preferentially involved, such as the femur and humerus [1]. In the present case, the lesions were located at Th7, Th11, Th12, and L4, with no other bone lesions. Extension of lymphoma cells to the spine from whole bone marrow infiltration was ruled out. Although the incidence of a single vertebral lymphoma is reported to be 1.7% of all primary lymphomas of the bone, multiple primary vertebral lymphoma without involvement of other bones is extremely rare, with only two cases reported in the literature [2,3].

Primary hepatic lymphoma is also rare, representing 0.016% of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma [4,5]. A previous report of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma involving both bone and liver without lymphadenopathy showed that the affected bones were widely distributed [6]. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma limited to vertebral bones with concurrent hepatic lesions. Clinicians should be aware that non-Hodgkin's lymphoma may mimic metastatic cancer to the bone and liver on imaging and clinical findings, and further accumulation of similar cases is expected.
