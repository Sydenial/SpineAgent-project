# Anterior retroperitoneal approach for removal of L5/S1 foraminal nerve sheath tumor—case report

## Abstract

BACKGROUND CONTEXT: Extradural lumbar schwannomas are a rare form of nerve sheath tumors (NSTs). The typical management approach for extradural foraminal NSTs is total gross resection, which involves a midline incision and muscle exposure, followed by laminectomy and facetectomy to access the tumor for resection. Following tumor removal, spinal fusion is often indicated to reduce postoperative deformity, pain, and neurologic deficits.

PURPOSE: We report the case of a 34-year-old woman who presented with a 2-year history of progressive dysesthesia and left foot drop. Magnetic resonance imaging revealed a lesion located in the lateral L5/S1 foramen. A novel anterior retroperitoneal approach was used to access the tumor via muscle splitting, retraction of the peritoneum medially, and the psoas muscle or iliac vessels laterally.

STUDY DESIGN/SETTING: This study is a case report of a novel approach for extradural lumbar schwannomas.

METHODS: The methods involve a description of the approach and reporting of clinical findings.

RESULTS: The schwannoma was successfully resected without requiring additional fusion surgery. The patient recovered uneventfully and was discharged on postoperative day 2.

CONCLUSION: We propose that the anterior retroperitoneal approach is a viable technique for resection of lumbar foraminal NSTs without the need for fusion surgery.

## Introduction

Neoplasms of the spinal canal comprise a range of tumors which may involve the spinal cord, the theca, and nerves. Nerve sheath tumors (NSTs) are the most frequent form, making up 25%–35% of primary spinal neoplasms in the Western population. These tumors can be classed according to their location, including intradural, combined intradural–extradural, and purely extradural [1]. In the intramedullary location, astrocytomas and ependymomas are more common, compared with extramedullary tumors which are more likely NSTs such as schwannomas, neurofibromas, and meningiomas.

Spinal extradural foraminal NSTs are rare neoplasms and are more commonly found in the cervical and thoracic regions than in the lumbar spine [1]. The typical management approach is total gross resection via a posterior approach involving midline incision, muscle exposure, laminectomy, and facetectomy. Following tumor removal, spinal fusion is often indicated to reduce postoperative deformity, pain, and neurologic deficits [2–4]. Various reports have demonstrated the safety and efficacy of minimally invasive posterior approaches [3,5,6]. We report a case of removal of an L5/S1 foraminal schwannoma through an anterior retroperitoneal exposure.

## Case Report

A 34-year-old healthy woman presented with a 2-year history of progressive dysesthesia and left-sided foot drop. Magnetic resonance imaging revealed a lesion located in the lateral aspect of the left L5/S1 foramen with deflection of the exiting L5 nerve root laterally. Nerve conduction studies confirmed an L5 nerve root lesion, with normal conduction velocities of the common peroneal nerve.

The patient was initially scheduled for a posterior approach with total facetectomy, resection of the lesion, and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion with pedicle screw fixation at an outside institution. A non-fusion anterior retroperitoneal approach was adopted to access the lesion for resection.

Under general anesthesia, a linear incision was made medial to the anterior superior iliac spine and lateral to the rectus abdominis. Using a muscle-splitting approach through the external and internal oblique muscles, the retroperitoneal plane was exposed. A retractor system was used to retract the peritoneum medially and the psoas muscle or common iliac vessels laterally to expose the L5/S1 foramen from an anterior direction.

The lesion was easily identified at the L5/S1 foramen, with the L5 nerve deflected laterally. Using microneurosurgical techniques, the capsule of the NST was incised and complete resection of the lesion was achieved. Histopathology revealed a schwannoma with no atypical features.

The patient recovered uneventfully and was discharged on postoperative day 2. She resumed her teaching duties 2 weeks following surgery. Imaging at 9 months’ follow-up demonstrated complete resection of the NST.

## Comment

Extradural foraminal schwannomas of the lumbar spine are exceptionally rare. Prior studies have reported a prevalence of 0.7%–4.2% among all extradural schwannomas [1,3]. The mainstay of treatment involves total resection of the lesion via a posterior approach, often necessitating laminectomy, facetectomy, and subsequent fusion to address iatrogenic instability [1,7,8]. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first reported case using an anterior retroperitoneal exposure for resection of a lumbar extradural foraminal schwannoma.

The posterior approach allows access to posterior spinal elements and facilitates simultaneous fusion if required [9–12]. However, disadvantages include longer operative duration, extensive paraspinal muscle dissection, nerve root retraction, and increased risk of instability requiring fusion [9–17]. Although minimally invasive posterior techniques have been introduced [3,18–21], they may not be suitable for all extradural schwannomas, particularly those extending into the foramen.

The anterior retroperitoneal approach provides direct access to the intervertebral foramen without the need for laminectomy or facetectomy [4,13,22–24]. This reduces the risk of iatrogenic instability and obviates the need for fusion. Additional benefits include reduced muscle dissection, lower blood loss, reduced postoperative pain, faster recovery, and shorter hospitalization [11,12,14].

However, this approach carries specific risks, including abdominal ileus, vascular injury, and psoas-related complications such as thigh weakness or bleeding [25,26]. Vascular surgical support should be readily available. Alternative approaches, such as the far lateral extraforaminal approach, may also be considered for select cases.

## Conclusion

Anterior retroperitoneal access represents a viable alternative to traditional posterior approaches for the removal of lumbar extradural schwannomas, allowing complete tumor resection without the need for additional fusion surgery.
