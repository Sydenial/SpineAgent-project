# Real-time Ultrasound-MRI Fusion Imaging for Locating Intraspinal Tumour in a Pregnant Woman

## Abstract

**Background:** Standard fluoroscopic guidance (C-arm fluoroscopy) has been routinely used for intraoperative localization of spinal tumours in patients who need to avoid radiation exposure. Fusion imaging of real-time ultrasound (US) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is a radiation-free technique that has been reported to have good localization accuracy in managing several conditions. 

**Clinical Presentation:** A 37-year-old pregnant patient, presented with progressively aggravating lower back pain for 20 days and was incapable of lying supine with lower extremities swelling for 1 week. She was referred to our hospital in her 18th week of gestation. Fusion imaging using volume navigation technique (VNT) based on real-time US/MRI was used to localize the intraspinal lesion, which was removed entirely via a minimally invasive interlaminar approach. Pathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of ependymoma of the conus medullaris. After the operation, her symptoms were relieved, and a healthy baby was delivered at the 40th week of pregnancy. 

**Conclusion:** This is the first case of using VNT-based fusion imaging of real-time US/MRI to guide the surgical resection of an intraspinal tumour. Further studies with a larger patient population are needed to validate this technique as an alternative to fluoroscopy in patients who need to avoid radiation exposure.

## Background

C-arm fluoroscopy has been routinely used for visualization of spinal structures for accurate localization of intraspinal tumours. However, like computed tomography (CT), it should be avoided in pregnant women to minimize radiation exposure to the fetus. On the other hand, fusion imaging of real-time ultrasound (US) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has already been used for percutaneous hepatic intervention, detection of prostate cancer, sacroiliac joint injections, etc., with good localization accuracy. However, there is still no data available regarding the feasibility of applying volume navigation technique (VNT)-based real-time US/MRI fusion imaging for accurate localization of intraspinal tumours.

## Clinical Presentation

A 37-year-old pregnant woman presented with progressively aggravated lower back pain and right lower limb pain for 20 days, incapable of lying supine with lower extremities swelling for 1 week. She was referred to our hospital in her 18th week of gestation. She had a history of two miscarriages and a previous caesarean delivery. The lumbar MRI at a local hospital revealed an L1 level intraspinal lesion.

Upon admission, the patient presented with forced sitting position, and neurological examination revealed normal muscle strength in her upper limbs but reduced superficial sensation below the L1 dermatome. Severe lower extremity edema was also noticed, although she was uncooperative for examination of muscle strength and tone in her lower limbs.

## Fusion Imaging

A Mylab Twice Ultrasonography system (Esaote, Genova, Italy), capable of fusion imaging (Virtual Navigator, Esaote, Genova, Italy), was coupled with a convex array transducer (1–8 MHz). After general anaesthesia, the magnetic field generator, located near the patient, and the electromagnetic position sensor, mounted on the US transducer, were activated. The first step of image fusion involved uploading the MRI data to the US machine. Then, image fusion between real-time US and MRI images was achieved through both plane registration and point registration (12th rib as a bony anatomic landmark). This registration process was repeated until optimal fusion was achieved (the registration error between real-time US and MRI images was within 3 mm).

## Surgical Procedure

After the induction of general anaesthesia and placement of fetal heart rate (FHR) monitoring, a left lateral decubitus position was used to avoid aortocaval compression by the fetus. A 5-cm skin incision was made in the midline centered over the L1. The interspinous ligament was kept intact, and part of the upper right L1 lamina was drilled to expand the interlaminar space to about $1.0 \times 1.5$ cm. The ligamentum flavum was first resected piecemeal, followed by dissection of the dura and the arachnoid membrane in a standard longitudinal fashion. A tumour, red-grey in colour, was identified, attached to the conus medullaris and filum terminale. After loosening of the cauda equina, the proximal tumour end was coagulated and cut off to stop tumour blood supply. Intraoperative debulking was performed using a cavitational ultrasonic surgical aspirator (CUSA). Tumour samples were sent for intraoperative pathology consultation, which confirmed the diagnosis of ependymoma. The remaining tumour was removed completely, and the dura was closed with titanium dural clips. The suture line was reinforced with fibrin glue to prevent epidural bleeding. The operation lasted about 95 minutes with a blood loss of about 50 mL.

MRI performed 3 days after the operation confirmed the complete removal of the tumour. After surgery, symptoms like lower extremity edema resolved the first day post-operation. The patient was discharged 3 days after surgery, and a healthy child was delivered via caesarean section at the 40th week of pregnancy.

## Discussion

C-arm fluoroscopy is the most commonly used intraoperative imaging modality during orthopedic and spinal procedures for accurate localization of spinal levels. The risk to a fetus from ionizing radiation is dependent on the gestational age at the time of exposure and the dose of radiation. Although there was no direct evidence showing deleterious effects on the fetus by radiation exposure from a single X-ray imaging on the spine, minimizing or eliminating this risk is essential.

On the other hand, MRI is the first-line diagnostic tool for intraspinal lesions, and there is no report of harmful effects on the fetus by MRI scans. One common technique for localization of an intraspinal tumour is placing a water-filled balloon (external marker) on the patient’s skin before the MRI scan. However, due to the mobility of the spinal column and back skin, this method is inaccurate and would lead to unnecessary trauma during surgery. Intraoperative MRI (iMRI) could be an optimal solution for patients who need to avoid radiation exposure but is expensive and limited to certain institutions. US, on the other hand, is a safe, non-invasive technique that does not generate ionizing radiation. VNT improves the sonographic spatial resolution and would be ideal for lesions with poor sonographic visibility, such as intraspinal lesions. 

Fusion imaging between preoperative MRI and US for brain lesion removal has been successfully adopted, and our case provides the first proof-of-concept that VNT-based US/MRI fusion imaging can accurately localize intraspinal lesions. This method faces challenges due to spinal column mobility and the difficulty in finding reliable external anatomical landmarks during registration between MRI and US in laminotomy.

## Conclusion

VNT-based fusion imaging of real-time US and MRI allows accurate localization of an intraspinal tumour in a patient who needs to avoid ionizing radiation from conventional fluoroscopy.
