# Successful non-surgical treatment for highly unstable fracture-subluxation of the spine secondary to myeloma

## Abstract

**Background Context:** Patients with multiple myeloma (MM) may develop rapidly progressive lytic spinal lesions with poor bone quality. In addition, these patients are often immunocompromised and therefore at increased risk of deep infection.

**Purpose:** To describe a patient presenting with an unstable fracture-subluxation of the thoracic spine secondary to myeloma, successfully treated with non-surgical management.

**Study Design:** Case report.

**Methods:** A 74-year-old Caucasian female presented with destructive myelomatous lesions of T9 and T10. Greater than 50% of the T9 vertebral body was involved, with subluxation and translation of T9 on T10 (Spinal Instability Neoplastic Score [SINS] 14). There was a single episode of transient paraesthesia affecting both lower limbs. The patient was in considerable pain, requiring large quantities of opioid analgesia. She was treated non-surgically in a thoracolumbar sacral orthosis (TLSO) for a period of three months, with strict bed rest for the first three weeks.

**Results:** A CT scan at three months demonstrated bony fusion and the brace was removed. The patient returned to normal activities five months post-treatment. Pain levels and patient-reported outcome scores improved significantly.

**Conclusions:** This case demonstrates successful non-surgical management of an unstable myelomatous vertebral fracture without neurological deficit. Surgical stabilization remains the treatment of choice for unstable vertebral fractures, and spinal surgical opinion should be sought in all cases.

## Keywords

Myeloma; vertebral fractures; non-surgical treatment; unstable fracture

## Introduction

Patients with multiple myeloma often develop numerous lytic vertebral lesions that may rapidly increase in size and ultimately result in vertebral fractures [1]. Outcomes following surgical fixation of unstable vertebral fractures in myeloma patients are often poor due to a combination of bone resorption and steroid-induced osteoporosis [4]. Surgical fixation may therefore carry an increased risk of failure [5]. A recent case report suggested that vertebral fractures in MM can be successfully managed non-surgically if the myeloma is adequately controlled [6].

We present the case of a patient with an unstable thoracic fracture-subluxation secondary to myeloma in whom surgical stabilization was considered likely to fail. The patient was successfully managed non-surgically and returned to her pre-fracture level of function. We discuss our management strategy and the rationale behind the chosen treatment.

## Case Report

### Clinical Presentation

A 74-year-old Caucasian female was referred to our specialist spinal myeloma unit in May 2015 with an unstable fracture-subluxation of T9/T10. She had been diagnosed with IgG lambda subtype MM 15 months prior to presentation and had commenced chemotherapy.

The patient initially presented to her local hospital in February 2015 with severe thoracic back pain. MRI of the spine demonstrated myelomatous infiltration of the thoracic vertebrae with extension of soft tissue tumor into the spinal canal and associated spinal cord impingement, without neurological deficit. She was treated with high-dose steroids, bortezomib (Velcade), and melphalan, but did not receive radiotherapy.

Over the following two months, the patient’s paraprotein levels improved; however, her back pain deteriorated significantly. By April 2015 she required 60 mg of slow-release morphine twice daily. Repeat MRI demonstrated a fracture-subluxation of T9/T10 with no residual soft tissue mass in the spinal canal. She experienced a single transient episode of numbness and paraesthesia in both lower limbs but had no persistent neurological deficit.

A CT scan confirmed a fracture-subluxation of T9/T10. The SINS score was calculated as 14, indicating an unstable fracture requiring stabilization. At presentation, the patient had an EQ-5D score of 0.56, an Oswestry Disability Index of 42, and a Roland-Morris Disability score of 12. Her medical history included hypertension, hypothyroidism, previous venous thromboembolism, and hypercholesterolemia. Prior to her MM diagnosis, she had been independently mobile.

### Treatment

The patient had no ongoing neurological deficit and had recently completed high-dose chemotherapy and prolonged steroid therapy. Due to concerns regarding fixation failure, poor bone quality, impaired wound healing, and increased infection risk, a non-surgical management strategy was chosen.

She was admitted to a spinal ward and immobilized in a rigid clamshell TLSO. Management included strict bed rest with log-rolling for three weeks. Prophylactic low-molecular-weight heparin was administered daily, and bisphosphonate therapy was initiated. A repeat CT scan after three weeks demonstrated no further displacement, and the patient was allowed to mobilize while wearing the TLSO.

### Outcome

A repeat CT scan three months after immobilization demonstrated satisfactory bony fusion, and the TLSO was removed. At five-month follow-up, the patient had returned to all normal activities with minimal pain and markedly reduced analgesic requirements. Imaging demonstrated stability across T9 and T10 with evidence of extra-osseous bone formation contributing to spinal stability.

## Discussion

The SINS score has been shown to reliably assess stability in pathological vertebral fractures. In this case, the fracture-subluxation at T9/T10 yielded a score of 14, classifying it as unstable and typically indicating surgical stabilization. Surgical treatment has been shown to be effective in this patient group; however, fixation in lytic and osteoporotic bone is technically challenging. Furthermore, this patient was immunocompromised following chemotherapy and steroid treatment, increasing the risks of poor wound healing and deep infection.

Minimally invasive spinal stabilization techniques have gained popularity due to perceived benefits of faster recovery and reduced wound complications. Although considered in this case, concerns regarding screw cutout due to poor bone quality led to abandonment of this option.

Recent guidelines for spinal myeloma management suggest that unstable pathological fractures or fractures with extensive bony destruction may be considered for treatment with external orthoses when appropriate [2]. In this case, cement augmentation was not suitable due to fracture configuration. As the patient’s myeloma responded well to chemotherapy, non-surgical management was pursued.

Close collaboration with hematology ensured adequate disease control throughout treatment. By three months, sufficient bony fusion had occurred to permit brace removal. By five months, the patient had resumed full activity with minimal pain. Imaging demonstrated stability between T9 and T10, with adjacent vacuum phenomena indicating motion restriction and adequate fusion.

## Conclusion

This case demonstrates successful non-surgical management of an unstable thoracic fracture-subluxation secondary to spinal myeloma. While surgical stabilization remains the gold standard for unstable vertebral fractures, selected patients may be managed non-operatively when surgical risks are high and disease control is adequate. Further studies are required to clarify the role of non-operative treatment in unstable vertebral fractures associated with multiple myeloma.
