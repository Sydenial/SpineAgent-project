# A Multilobulated, Pan-Columnar Cervical Spine Lesion Causing Myelopathy

## Case Report

A 45-year-old man presented with a history of progressive weakness of all four limbs. Magnetic resonance imaging showed a mildly enhancing, multilobulated lesion from the C5 to C7 levels arising from a partially destroyed C6 vertebra. The lesion extended into the prevertebral and paravertebral regions, displacing the trachea and oesophagus anteriorly. The intervertebral discs were preserved. There was a significant epidural component with associated spinal cord compression.

Computed tomography demonstrated lytic changes involving all three spinal columns at the C6 level, along with bilateral widening of the neural foramina. The patient underwent radical debulking of the lesion with C5 and C6 corpectomy, fusion, and anterior plating, followed by posterior fixation from C3 to T1. Histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of chordoma, and the patient was subsequently referred for radiation therapy.

## Discussion

Chordomas occurring in the lower cervical spine are rare. Given their insidious nature and aggressive local behavior, radical debulking followed by postoperative radiotherapy is recommended. For multi-columnar spinal involvement, total spondylectomy combined with piecemeal excision has been associated with improved prognosis.
