# Remarkable efficacy of temozolomide for relapsed spinal myxopapillary ependymoma with multiple recurrence and cerebrospinal dissemination: a case report and literature review

## Abstract

Myxopapillary ependymomas, classified as WHO grade I, are typically slow-growing but can result in cerebrospinal fluid dissemination or local recurrence. This report describes a case where temozolomide (TMZ) showed remarkable efficacy in treating a recurrent spinal myxopapillary ependymoma. A 26-year-old female, initially diagnosed with a spinal myxopapillary ependymoma at the L5 level, underwent multiple surgeries due to recurrence. After aggressive adjuvant therapy with TMZ for one year, the disseminated metastatic tumor at T11/12 completely disappeared. Six years post-treatment, MRI shows no recurrence. This case suggests that TMZ could be a potential adjuvant therapy for multiple recurrent myxopapillary ependymomas.

**Keywords:** Spinal cord tumor, Microscopic surgery, Chemotherapy

## Introduction

The World Health Organization (WHO) classifies ependymomas into four categories: subependymomas (WHO grade 1), myxopapillary ependymomas (WHO grade 1), ependymomas (WHO grade 2), and anaplastic ependymomas (WHO grade 3). Myxopapillary ependymomas usually occur in the terminal filum of the spinal cord and are slow-growing. Despite their classification as grade I tumors, some myxopapillary ependymomas cause poor prognoses due to cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) dissemination or local recurrence. Klekamp reported recurrence rates of 6.6%, 19.0%, and 37.0% after 1, 10, and 20 years, respectively. 

Adjuvant therapy is recommended to prevent recurrence. Radiotherapy is advised when piecemeal resection occurs to reduce local recurrence and CSF dissemination. Chemotherapy has been suggested but its effectiveness has not been established for myxopapillary ependymomas. This report highlights a case where TMZ showed significant efficacy in treating recurrent spinal myxopapillary ependymomas.

## Case report

### Initial surgery at L5

A 26-year-old female presented with back pain and muscle weakness in the bilateral lower extremities. MRI revealed an intradural tumor at the L5 level. Total resection, including the tumor capsule, was performed. Pathological examination confirmed myxopapillary ependymoma. MIB-1 proliferation index was 8.3%, and no tumor was found at the surgical margin. Postoperatively, her symptoms improved significantly.

### Second surgery at L4

Two years post-surgery, the patient experienced back pain and leg pain. MRI showed tumor recurrence at the L4 level. Another gross total resection was performed, and the MIB-1 index was 9.3%. Local radiation therapy was administered at L4–5 with 50 Gy in 25 fractions. Follow-up MRIs were taken annually.

### Disseminated tumor at T11/12

Four years after the initial surgery, MRI showed a new tumor at the T11/12 level, diagnosed as a disseminated metastatic tumor via CSF flow. Local radiotherapy was performed, but the tumor size did not change. MRI was followed up every six months.

### Third surgery at L3

Six years after the initial surgery, a new tumor was found at the L3 level. The patient complained of mild leg pain, and rapid tumor growth was noted. Gross total resection was performed, with no paresis. The MIB-1 proliferation index was 10%. 

### Adjuvant therapy after the third operation

Given the multiple recurrences, TMZ was administered with local radiotherapy at L3. TMZ treatment included 42 days of daily therapy, followed by 6 cycles of 5-day adjuvant treatment. During the first cycle, there were no side effects, but a mild decrease in white blood cells occurred after the second cycle. MRI showed no recurrence at the lumbar lesion, and the disseminated metastatic tumor at T11/12 disappeared. Six years post-TMZ, follow-up MRI shows no recurrence in the brain or spine.

## Discussion

This case demonstrates the effectiveness of TMZ for recurrent myxopapillary ependymoma. Despite gross total resection in the first surgery, recurrences occurred. TMZ, combined with radiotherapy, was successfully used after multiple recurrences, resulting in the complete disappearance of the disseminated metastatic tumor. 

TMZ alkylates DNA, inducing O6-methylguanine, which leads to cell death. It has shown efficacy in glioblastoma treatment, and recent studies suggest it can be effective for lower-grade spinal gliomas, including myxopapillary ependymomas. This report is the first to demonstrate TMZ's success in treating recurrent myxopapillary ependymomas.

While TMZ has proven effective in similar tumors, its use in myxopapillary ependymomas is still under investigation. The patient’s complete response to TMZ suggests it could be a promising therapy for multiple recurrent cases, especially when surgery and radiotherapy have failed.

### Toxicity risks

TMZ is generally well-tolerated, but adverse effects, including neutropenia, have been noted. In this case, the patient's white blood cell count dropped but recovered without significant issues. The potential risk of delayed toxicity and teratogenicity requires careful consideration, especially in young female patients.
