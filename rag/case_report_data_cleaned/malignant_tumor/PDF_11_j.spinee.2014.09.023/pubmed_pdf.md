# A Rare Case of Extramedullary Myxopapillary Ependymoma

## Case Report

A 58-year-old man was admitted to the neurosurgery outpatient clinic with the complaint of backache radiating to the pelvic region in the prone position. There was no discomfort in the erect position.

On neurologic examination, there was mild motor weakness in the feet. Other findings were normal.

Magnetic resonance imaging of the lumbar spine showed a posterolateral intradural extramedullary mass compressing the spinal cord at the L1–L2 levels (Figure). The mass demonstrated low signal intensity compared with medullary tissue on T1-weighted images and slight hyperintensity on T2-weighted images. The lesion showed well-defined and homogeneous enhancement on contrast-enhanced T1-weighted images. Based on these findings, the lesion was initially prediagnosed as a schwannoma.

The mass was totally resected during surgery performed by the neurosurgery team. Histologic examination revealed a low-grade glial tumor. The final diagnosis was myxopapillary ependymoma, World Health Organization Grade I.
