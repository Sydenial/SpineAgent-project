# A rare case of malignant nonfunctional retroperitoneal paraganglioma with C3 spinous process metastasis

## Case Report

A 39-year-old man was admitted to our department with a 5-month history of a stiff neck and pain in the cervical spine. Physical examination demonstrated mild tenderness and percussion pain on the back of the neck, normal sensation and strength of the limbs, decreased sensation in the right shoulder, intact range of motion of the cervical spine, and a negative Hoffmann’s sign.

Lateral plain radiography and three-dimensional computed tomography (3D-CT) of the cervical spine showed destruction of the C3 spinous process, vertebral plate, and left lateral mass. Contrast-enhanced T1-weighted magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrated an elliptical occupying lesion with high signal intensity in the C3–C4 appendicular region. Computed tomography angiography of the cervical arteries revealed intact carotid and vertebral arteries without evidence of tumor invasion. The patient’s blood pressure was normal.

The patient underwent debridement, decompression, bone graft fusion, and internal fixation. No significant blood pressure fluctuations occurred intraoperatively. Postoperatively, cervical pain and decreased sensation in the right shoulder were significantly relieved. Histopathological examination revealed that the cervical lesion was a paraganglioma.

Three months later, a retroperitoneal mass measuring $10 \times 7 \times 5$ cm was incidentally discovered during abdominal CT evaluation. Postoperative pathological analysis confirmed that the retroperitoneal tumor was also a paraganglioma. Six months later, the patient again complained of neck stiffness. Coronal 3D-CT demonstrated progressive destruction of C3 and the left lateral mass, with invasion of the left C3 pedicle and part of the vertebral body. Radiotherapy was recommended.

## Discussion

We considered the cervical appendicular tumor to be a metastasis originating from the retroperitoneal paraganglioma. Paragangliomas are generally defined as tumors arising from extra-adrenal paraganglia [1]. They are typically benign and are often associated with catecholamine hypersecretion [2]. However, malignant nonfunctional paraganglioma with osseous metastasis to the cervical spine is extremely rare.
